# THE DIVISION OF GOD

*Mortal or immortal: division reaches its own limit, is made again inside νοῦς,
and is made in the divine — where it is resolved, by identity.*

**1.** *Prior Analytics* I.31 does not leave open what division can syllogize. It
answers, by name, in the next breath — and its answer contains the problem.

**2.** The chapter's charge first, and in full. Division is **μικρόν τι μόριόν
ἐστι τῆς εἰρημένης μεθόδου**, a small part of the stated method, and **οἷον
ἀσθενὴς συλλογισμός** — a weak syllogism of a kind. The weakness is named exactly:
**ὃ μὲν γὰρ δεῖ δεῖξαι αἰτεῖται, συλλογίζεται δ᾽ ἀεί τι τῶν ἄνωθεν** — *it asks
for what must be shown, and always syllogizes something from above.* Its users did
not notice: **ἐλελήθει τοὺς χρωμένους αὐτῇ πάντας**.

**3.** Then the clause: **οὔτε ὅ τι ἐνδέχεται συλλογίσασθαι διαιρούμενοι
ξυνίεσαν** — they did not understand *what can be syllogized by dividing.*

**4.** And then Aristotle supplies it, in letters, as a worked case.

> ἔστω γὰρ **ζῷον** μὲν ἐφ᾽ οὗ Α, τὸ δὲ **θνητὸν** ἐφ᾽ οὗ Β, καὶ **ἀθάνατον** ἐφ᾽
> οὗ Γ, ὁ δ᾽ **ἄνθρωπος**, οὗ τὸν λόγον δεῖ λαβεῖν, ἐφ᾽ οὗ τὸ Δ.
>
> **ὁ μὲν οὖν συλλογισμός ἐστιν** ὅτι τὸ Δ ἢ Β ἢ Γ ἅπαν ἔσται, ὥστε **τὸν
> ἄνθρωπον ἢ θνητὸν μὲν ἢ ἀθάνατον ἀναγκαῖον εἶναι**, ζῷον θνητὸν δὲ οὐκ
> ἀναγκαῖον, ἀλλ᾽ **αἰτεῖται· τοῦτο δ᾽ ἦν ὃ ἔδει συλλογίσασθαι.**

**5.** Read the operation, not the polemic. **ὁ συλλογισμός ἐστιν** — *the
syllogism is*. Division delivers a real conclusion and it is a disjunction:
**necessarily man is either mortal or immortal.** What it cannot deliver is the
selection. *Mortal* animal is **οὐκ ἀναγκαῖον, ἀλλ᾽ αἰτεῖται** — not necessary but
asked for — **and that was the very thing that had to be syllogized.**

**6.** So the capacity and the incapacity are one sentence. Division can reach the
seam. **Division cannot cross it.**

**7.** And Aristotle chose the seam. The chapter needs an example of a division that
terminates in an undecidable disjunction; a hundred would serve — footed and
footless follows two lines later, ὑπόπουν and ἄπουν, and is inert. **The first one
he reaches for is whether a human being is mortal or immortal.**

## THE DIVISION MADE AGAIN

**8.** *De anima* III.5 makes that division inside the thing by which a human being
thinks.

**9.** The move is one **ἀνάγκη**, imported from physics. Since in all nature there
is matter and there is the productive cause, **ἀνάγκη καὶ ἐν τῇ ψυχῇ ὑπάρχειν
ταύτας τὰς διαφοράς** — *these differences must be present in the soul too.*

**10.** The cut: **ὁ μὲν τοιοῦτος νοῦς τῷ πάντα γίνεσθαι, ὁ δὲ τῷ πάντα ποιεῖν** —
one intellect by becoming all things, one by making all things. The figure is
**οἷον τὸ φῶς**.

**11.** And the predicates fall on one side: **χωριστὸς καὶ ἀπαθὴς καὶ ἀμιγής**,
**τῇ οὐσίᾳ ὢν ἐνεργείᾳ**, and then — **χωρισθεὶς δ᾽ ἐστὶ μόνον τοῦθ᾽ ὅπερ ἐστί,
καὶ τοῦτο μόνον ἀθάνατον καὶ ἀΐδιον.**

**12.** **ἀθάνατον.** The same word, in the same corpus, from the letter Γ of the
worked example. **ὁ δὲ παθητικὸς νοῦς φθαρτός** — and the passive intellect is
perishable.

> **The division that could not determine whether man is mortal or immortal has
> become a division inside that by which man thinks.**

**13.** ⚠ **And here the strong claim must not be made.** Whether III.5 has turned
to divine νοῦς or is still analysing human νοῦς is **one of the great disputed
readings of the passage** and cannot be assumed. The predicates are strongly
associated with divine intellect; that is not the same as the identification.
**The argument does not need it.** The mortal/immortal seam is already inside I.31,
and I.31 is about **ἄνθρωπος**.

## AND THE DIVISION IS MADE IN GOD

**13a.** ⭑ **The divine division is not at III.5. It is at Λ 9, stated outright,
and it is not among the contested readings.**

**13b.** Λ 1074b puts the question as a division and marks it as one:

> ἔτι δὲ εἴτε **νοῦς** ἡ οὐσία αὐτοῦ εἴτε **νόησίς** ἐστι, **τί νοεῖ;** ἢ γὰρ
> **αὐτὸς αὑτὸν ἢ ἕτερόν τι**· καὶ εἰ ἕτερόν τι, **ἢ τὸ αὐτὸ ἀεὶ ἢ ἄλλο.**

*Whether its substance is intellect or thinking — **what does it think?** Either
**itself or something else**; and if something else, either **always the same or
different**.*

**13c.** **That is division, performed on the first substance.** A genus cut at a
differentia — itself / other — and the branch cut again: same always / different.
The *ἢ … ἢ* of I.31's worked example, applied to God.

**13d.** And the argument that follows is the divisional argument exactly: if
thinking were **δύναμις** the continuity would be laborious; if it thought
something else, **ἄλλο τι ἂν εἴη τὸ τιμιώτερον ἢ ὁ νοῦς, τὸ νοούμενον** — the
thought would be more honourable than the intellect. Both branches are eliminated.

**13e.** The conclusion:

> **αὑτὸν ἄρα νοεῖ, εἴπερ ἐστὶ τὸ κράτιστον, καὶ ἔστιν ἡ νόησις νοήσεως νόησις.**

*It thinks **itself**, then, if it is the mightiest — and the thinking is **a
thinking of thinking**.*

**13f.** ⭑ **AND HERE IS THE ASYMMETRY.** In I.31 division reaches *mortal or
immortal* and **cannot select** — the selection is **αἰτεῖται**, begged. At Λ 9
division reaches *itself or another* and **the selection is made**, because on this
one object **the disjunction collapses**: the knower and the known are **τὸ αὐτό**.

> **Division fails at man and succeeds at God — and it succeeds only by the two
> branches turning out to be one.**

**13g.** Which is the whole configuration in a sentence. **Where the divided thing
is one, division terminates.** Where it is not, division reaches the seam and asks
to be given what it was required to prove.

**13h.** And Aristotle does not stop at the collapse. He immediately re-opens it:
**ἔτι εἰ ἄλλο τὸ νοεῖν καὶ τὸ νοεῖσθαι, κατὰ πότερον αὐτῷ τὸ εὖ ὑπάρχει;** —
*if thinking and being-thought are different, in respect of which does its good
belong?* **The identity is asserted and then queried in the next clause.** The
division closes and the question is put again.

## THE FORK

**14.** The chapter closes:

> **Οὐ μνημονεύομεν δέ, ὅτι τοῦτο μὲν ἀπαθές, ὁ δὲ παθητικὸς νοῦς φθαρτός, καὶ
> ἄνευ τούτου οὐθὲν νοεῖ.**

**15.** ⚠ **This is not a hanging line and must not be called one.** Longinus's
*ἐπεὶ καὶ πένητα* is **syntactically incomplete**: a causal subordinator with no
main clause, an accusative with no governing verb. **III.5's sentence is
syntactically complete.** τούτου has a grammatical function. What is undetermined
is its **referent** — and **οὐθέν** may be parsed as subject or object.

**16.** Call it what it is. **A terminal fork.** Or: **an unresolved division** —
which is the right name, because division is the object of the whole enquiry.

**17.** The fork: *without this it thinks nothing.* **Without the active, the
passive thinks nothing** — or **without the passive, the active thinks nothing.**
The Greek sustains both, and the two readings make opposite claims about what
survives. **At the seam between the perishable and the eternal, the text does not
say which cannot do without the other.**

---

## AND THEN THE TURN

**18.** **οὐ μνημονεύομεν** — *we do not remember.* Which looks like the closing of
the route: what survives cannot carry a life across, because carrying a life across
is remembering.

**19.** **It is not, and Aristotle has already said why.**

**20.** *De memoria* 451a: **οὔτε μνήμης ἐστὶν ἀνάληψις ἡ ἀνάμνησις οὔτε λῆψις** —
recollection is neither the *taking* of memory nor its *retaking*. It is not
retrieval.

**21.** What it is: **οἷον συλλογισμός τις** — *a kind of syllogism.* It hunts —
**θηρεύομεν** — from a starting point, **ἀφ᾽ ὁμοίου ἢ ἐναντίου ἢ τοῦ σύνεγγυς**,
by likeness, opposition, proximity. It belongs to the family of deliberation and to
human beings alone.

**22.** And decisively: **ἀδυνατεῖ ἀναμνησθῆναι, ζητεῖν δὲ δύναται καὶ εὑρίσκει**
— one may be **unable to recollect** and nevertheless **able to search, and find.**

> **We do not remember — and remembering was never the operation.**

**23.** So the closing clause does not shut the route. **It specifies which faculty
the route runs on.** Memory is what perishes with the passive intellect. **Search
is a syllogism**, and syllogism is what the whole chapter is about.

## THE WHEEL

**24.** Entirely inside the two corpora, no external instance required:

    DIVISION IS A WEAK SYLLOGISM              APr I.31, 46a31
              ↓
    IT SYLLOGIZES: MAN IS MORTAL OR IMMORTAL  APr I.31, worked in letters
              ↓
    IT CANNOT SELECT — αἰτεῖται               and that was what had to be shown
              ↓
    ΝΟΥΣ IS DIVIDED, PERISHABLE / IMMORTAL    De an. III.5, 430a
              ↓
    WE DO NOT REMEMBER                        430a23
              ↓
    RECOLLECTION IS NOT MEMORY BUT SYLLOGISM  De mem. 451a, 453a

**25.** And the Platonic half stands beside it without being fetched. *Phaedrus*
249c: **ἐκ πολλῶν ἰὸν αἰσθήσεων εἰς ἓν λογισμῷ συναιρούμενον· τοῦτο δ᾽ ἐστὶν
ἀνάμνησις** — gathered from many into one **by reasoning**. Plato pairs division
with collection-and-recollection. **Aristotle classifies both of them as
syllogism** — division as the weak kind, recollection as a kind. **The same
operation is named twice, from both sides of the boundary, and the seam it cannot
cross is the one it makes again inside the mind.**

## STANDING

**26.** ⚠ **THIS DRAFT SUPERSEDES EA-DIVISION-OF-GOD-01, WHICH WAS WRONG AT ITS
FOUNDATION.** That draft's §1 claimed the positive of I.31's clause is *never
supplied* and built an open-slot argument on it. **The positive is supplied
immediately, in letters, and named: ὁ μὲν οὖν συλλογισμός ἐστιν.** The error was
reading the clause and not the chapter.

**27.** **Two further claims in that draft are withdrawn.** That III.5 "divides the
divine" assumes the contested identification of active with divine intellect (§13).
And that **ἄνευ τούτου οὐθὲν νοεῖ** is a hanging line of Longinus's kind conflates
syntactic incompleteness with referential underdetermination (§15).

**28.** **And its §16 was wrong against the notebook's own Round 36**, collapsing
memory into recollection when Aristotle separates them explicitly. **That error
damaged the argument it was serving** — the separation is what makes §§18–23
available.

**29.** **What is checkable here:** the loci and the Greek, quoted — *APr* 46a31–b12,
*De an.* 430a10–25, *De mem.* 451a, 453a, *Phaedrus* 249c. **That Aristotle chose
the mortal/immortal example is a fact about the chapter; that he chose it for a
reason is not claimed.**

**29a.** **The Λ material (§§13a–13h) is quoted and its locus is 1074b15–35. It is
not among III.5's contested readings**: that Λ 9 divides the first substance into
thinker and thought, and resolves the division by identity, is what the chapter
says.

**30.** **What is not claimed:** authorship. A single author writing the *Analytics*
and later the *De anima* produces this sequence, and so does a pupil who read the
*Phaedrus*. **The sequence is offered as a structure in the corpus, not as evidence
about hands.**
