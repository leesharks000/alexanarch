# I. Socrates Asked, But Did Not Answer

## 1. The sentence

On the last page of the *Sophistical Refutations* — position 0.968 of the seated work — Aristotle names Socrates.

> Σωκράτης ἠρώτα, ἀλλ᾿ οὐκ ἀπεκρίνετο· ὡμολόγει γὰρ οὐκ εἰδέναι.[^1]

Socrates asked, but did not answer; for he acknowledged that he did not know.

The name has appeared twice before in the treatise, at 0.109, where it means nobody: *if he is other than Socrates, and Socrates is a man, then he is other than a man.* That is how the name works across the Organon. Fourteen times in the *Categories* Socrates is well, Socrates is ill, there is no Socrates; ten times in *De interpretatione*; four in the *Topics*; twice in the *Prior Analytics*. He is the example individual, the token Aristotle reaches for when he needs any man at all.[^2] The name is filled once, here, on the closing page, and what it is filled with is a function. The inquiry had to cover both sides of the activity — taking an account and giving one, ὅπως λόγον ὑπέχοντες φυλάξομεν τὴν θέσιν — and the reason is that one side was already taken. Socrates asked. He did not answer. He said he did not know. Aristotle does not reproach him. He assigns him a side and takes the other.

## 2. The paragraph, and the innocent reading of it

The sentence opens the paragraph in which Aristotle says what did and did not exist before him. It is a short theory of predecessors and it is stated entirely in the vocabulary of parts.

Discoveries are of two kinds. Some are taken over from others who worked on them beforehand, πεπονημένα κατὰ μέρος, and advance part by part in the hands of those who inherit them. Others are found from the beginning, and grow by a small first increase, worth more than all the later growth, because the beginning is the greatest thing — smallest in size, strongest in power, hardest to see.[^3] Rhetoric, and nearly all the arts, grew the first way, οἷον ἐκ διαδοχῆς, by succession: Tisias after the first ones, Thrasymachus after Tisias, Theodorus after him, καὶ πολλοὶ πολλὰ συνενηνόχασι μέρη. Then:

> Ταύτης δὲ τῆς πραγματείας οὐ τὸ μὲν ἦν τὸ δ᾿ οὐκ ἦν προεξειργασμένον, ἀλλ᾿ οὐδὲν παντελῶς ὑπῆρχεν.[^4]

Of this inquiry it was not that part had been worked out beforehand and part not. Nothing whatsoever existed. He names the middle case — the case his own theory has just made the normal one — and denies it first; then denies everything. And he says what *existed* means: about rhetoric, πολλὰ καὶ παλαιὰ τὰ λεγόμενα, many old things said; about syllogizing, nothing we had to say, only long labour by trial.[^5]

The innocent reading is strong and it is Aristotle's own, so it must be stated first and in full. Predecessors had products, not the art. The paid teachers of eristic — their instruction was like Gorgias's practice — handed out finished speeches to be memorised, rhetorical ones and question-form ones, ἐρωτητικοὶ λόγοι, and supposed that this was teaching: as if a man who promised a science of not hurting one's feet taught no shoemaking, nor where shoes might be got, but handed out many kinds of shoes. οὐ γὰρ τέχνην ἀλλὰ τὰ ἀπὸ τῆς τέχνης.[^6] Socrates questioned; questions are among the products; the dialogues are full of them; nobody before Aristotle had the art of which they are products. On this reading the paragraph is precise, generous, and true, and there is nothing to explain.

Hold that. It is the reading the rest of the section has to survive.

## 3. Division filed as a part

*Prior Analytics* I.31, position 0.294. Aristotle has finished laying out how the premises of a syllogism are found and refers the reader for the detailed treatment to the inquiry concerning dialectic — ἐν τῇ πραγματείᾳ τῇ περὶ τὴν διαλεκτικήν — the inquiry whose last page we have just read. Next sentence:

> Ὅτι δ᾿ ἡ διὰ τῶν γενῶν διαίρεσις μικρόν τι μόριόν ἐστι τῆς εἰρημένης μεθόδου, ῥᾴδιον ἰδεῖν· ἔστι γὰρ ἡ διαίρεσις οἷον ἀσθενὴς συλλογισμός.[^7]

Division through the genera is some small part of the method just described; it is a kind of weak syllogism — it begs what it should show and always concludes something higher than it needs. And this had escaped all who used it, τοὺς χρωμένους αὐτῇ πάντας, who tried to persuade people that what-a-thing-is could be demonstrated: so they neither understood what could be syllogized by dividing, ξυνίεσαν, nor that it could be done as we have described.[^8]

Three facts, kept separate. Division is classified: μόριον, the paragraph's own unit for what predecessors contribute. The method is identified: the one treated in detail in the dialectical inquiry. The users are characterised: all of them missed what it was. The third fact is the innocent reading's second pillar — retrospective subsumption. Plato may have divided without having division *as* syllogistic. Good. It explains why Aristotle files division as a part. It does not touch the sentence that says no part existed. The filing is his; the denial is his; the filing is what gives the denial an object.

## 4. Where it was

*Phaedrus* 265d, position 0.737. Socrates asks whether the power the two speeches displayed could be taken hold of by art — εἰ αὐτοῖν τὴν δύναμιν τέχνῃ λαβεῖν δύναιτό τις — and answers with the procedure: two named operations, collection and division; a rule, cut at the joints as the thing is naturally jointed; a failure mode, breaking a part like a bad carver. Then: τούτων δὴ ἔγωγε αὐτός τε ἐραστής, ὦ Φαῖδρε, τῶν διαιρέσεων καὶ συναγωγῶν, ἵνα οἷός τε ὦ λέγειν τε καὶ φρονεῖν — a lover of these, so as to be able to speak and to think — and those who can do it he calls dialecticians.[^9] The *Sophist* makes dividing by kinds, τὸ κατὰ γένη διαιρεῖσθαι, the work of dialectical ἐπιστήμη, perhaps the greatest. The *Philebus* says everything belonging to art that was ever discovered came to light by this route, and that the ancients handed it down, παρέδοσαν.[^10]

So the operation Aristotle files as a small part of his method is, in the corpus that stands before his by every received chronology, held as a τέχνη, called an ἐπιστήμη, named, ruled, loved, followed, and described as the route by which arts are found. By the criterion Aristotle himself applied to rhetoric — old λεγόμενα — there are old things said about it. And the man who says them is the one who asked but did not answer, because he did not know.

Now the positional fact, recorded because it is not in the literature and flagged because it has no base rate yet. Stay on the *Phaedrus* page. Having named the dialecticians, Socrates asks what is left to rhetoric once division and collection are taken from it. Phaedrus: a great deal, τά γ᾿ ἐν τοῖς βιβλίοις τοῖς περὶ λόγων τέχνης γεγραμμένοις — the things in the written handbooks. And Socrates lists them: Theodorus of Byzantium (0.761); Evenus, who *first found* covert allusion; Tisias and Gorgias (0.764), let them sleep; Prodicus, who laughed and said he *alone had found* the art; Thrasymachus, the Chalcedonian's strength.[^11] Aristotle's succession — Tisias, Thrasymachus, Theodorus — and his type of the product-handing teacher, Gorgias, are the four names on the page of the *Phaedrus* that names division as the art, in the same role, on the same side of the same contrast: writers of handbooks, holders of what is written, makers of first-finder claims. The null: these are the canonical names and Aristotle compiled the handbooks himself.[^12] What the null leaves standing: the earlier page draws Aristotle's contrast, with Aristotle's men on the product side, and says what is on the other side.

The corpus also contains the theory of the word Aristotle files with. *Statesman* 262a (0.086): do not cut off one small part, σμικρὸν μόριον, against many large ones, apart from a form. 263b: a form of something is of necessity a part of it; a part is not of necessity a form.[^13] μικρόν τι μόριον, says I.31.

## 5. The arithmetic

Four sentences. Division is a part of the method (*APr.* I.31). No part of the method was worked out beforehand (*SE* 34). Division was worked out beforehand, as a named art with a rule and practitioners (*Phdr.*, *Soph.*, *Phlb.*). The Platonic corpus precedes the Aristotelian (the received chronology). The first three are Aristotle's and Plato's words. The fourth is nobody's; it is the frame the words are read in.

The four cannot all stand unless one word moves: *part*, *worked out*, *the method*, or *before*. Every reading in the tradition moves one of them, and the readings are strong; they are set out at their full strength in §III and I will not rehearse them here. What I will record is the pattern. *Worked out* is moved by the shoemaker. *The method* is moved by restricting the denial to the dialectical programme — and I.31 has just sent us to that programme for the method division is a part of, so the restriction does not restrict. *Part* is moved by ξυνίεσαν, which is Aristotle's own move and explains the filing, not the denial. *Before* is moved for Aristotle only: the *Topics* is early, the *Analytics* late, he had not yet seen that division was a part. Four words, four readings, every one of them paid for on Aristotle's side of the ledger — a restriction he did not mark, a sense he did not signal, a change of mind between books.

The fourth word has never been moved for Plato.

## 6. The literal reading

Now read the paragraph as written. Nothing of this inquiry existed beforehand — not some of it, none of it. Division is a part of this inquiry. Therefore division, as the *Phaedrus* holds it, was not beforehand.

Not: did not count. Not: was products without art — the *Phaedrus* says τέχνη and gives the rule. Not: was there under another description — Aristotle's own criterion counts Tisias's parts without asking what Tisias called them. *Was not before.* The page on which Socrates loves the divisions and names the dialecticians, and lists Tisias, Thrasymachus, Theodorus and Gorgias as the men who have the handbooks and not the art, is not a page the inquiry inherited. It is a page the inquiry contains. The operation was performed, loved, and named in the same enterprise that later says what it is, files it as a small part, says its users did not understand it, and says that nothing of the enterprise existed before the enterprise.

It is preposterous. It requires the received chronology to be false in the received way: the Platonic corpus, or the part of it that holds division, would not precede the Aristotelian inquiry as a predecessor precedes a successor, but stand inside it — as one of the products the shoemaker hands out before the art is shown; as the part worked out in the place where parts are worked out; as the questioner's side, taken so that the answerer's side could be taken next. It requires *Plato* to be something other than the name of a man who wrote first. It requires the man who asked and did not know to be, in some sense not yet stated and not yet permitted, in the same room as the man who answered.

It is also the only reading on which every sentence in Aristotle's paragraph is true as written. No word moves. The shoemaker holds — the questions are products, and the products were made. The succession holds — Tisias contributed parts to an art that grew by tradition, and this art did not. The criterion holds — there were old λεγόμενα about rhetoric, and about syllogizing there were none, because the λεγόμενα about division are not old; they are the inquiry's own. ξυνίεσαν holds — the users did not understand what division was, because the user was the questioner, and it was the answerer's job to say. And the sentence that began the paragraph holds most exactly of all. Socrates asked. He did not answer. He said he did not know. Someone else did the answering, and the paragraph tells us who.

I do not assert this. I say that it is thinkable, that it is the face-value reading, and that the received chronology is what has made it unthinkable rather than anything on these pages. That is a claim about a prior, not about a text; and a prior is not a reading.

The question the rest of this paper puts to the texts is whether the literal reading can be disconfirmed — whether anything in the two corpora, measured, shows the number of makers the chronology assumes. §II reads the commentators, who joined I.31 to the priority claim in the sixth century and never asked this. §III gives the received explanation its strongest form. §IV and §V run the instruments. If the instruments can see two, the reading dies there, and it should.
