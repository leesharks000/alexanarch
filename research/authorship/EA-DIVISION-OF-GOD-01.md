# WHAT SYLLOGIZING CAN DIVIDE

**1.** *APr* I.31 leaves a question standing. The dividers **οὔτε ὅ τι ἐνδέχεται
συλλογίσασθαι διαιρούμενοι ξυνίεσαν** — did not understand *what can be syllogized
by dividing*. The clause is negative and the positive is never supplied. The answer
is left as a slot.

**2.** The conventional reading first, in full. I.31 is polemic. Platonic division
cannot demonstrate essence because it begs what it must prove: at each cut the
divider *asks* which side the target falls on and the respondent grants it. So the
sentence means only that the Academy did not grasp the weakness of its own
instrument — that division yields a disjunction, not a conclusion. On this reading
nothing is withheld. The chapter is a demolition and the slot is an artifact of
Aristotle's contempt, not a place left open.

**3.** That reading is available and I do not think it is wrong about the polemic.
It is silent about the grammar. **ἐνδέχεται** is the modal of the possible, not of
the actual. The clause does not say the dividers syllogized badly. It says there is
something division *admits of* syllogizing, and they did not see what.

**4.** Take the question seriously and run the operation upward. Division cuts a
genus at a differentia. It requires that the thing cut have parts, and that the
parts be **χωριστά** — separable — at least κατὰ λόγον. So the question *what can
division syllogize* is the question *what has separable parts*. Run it to the top of
the ladder and it becomes: **can the divine be divided?**

**5.** *Metaphysics* Λ answers no and says so at length. The first mover is
**ἀμερής**, without parts, **ἀδιαίρετος**. It has no magnitude. This is the
orthodoxy and it is not in dispute.

**6.** And then *De anima* III.5 divides it anyway, in fifteen lines, with no
announcement.

## THE CUT

**7.** The chapter's move is a single ἀνάγκη. Since in all nature there is matter
and there is the productive cause, **ἀνάγκη καὶ ἐν τῇ ψυχῇ ὑπάρχειν ταύτας τὰς
διαφοράς** — *these differences must be present in the soul too*. The
matter/cause distinction is imported into νοῦς by necessity, from physics.

**8.** The cut itself: **ὁ μὲν τοιοῦτος νοῦς τῷ πάντα γίνεσθαι, ὁ δὲ τῷ πάντα
ποιεῖν** — one intellect by becoming all things, another by making all things. Two
νοῖ. The figure is **οἷον τὸ φῶς**: light, which makes potential colours actual.

**9.** And the productive one takes the divine predicates, all of them, in one
clause: **χωριστὸς καὶ ἀπαθὴς καὶ ἀμιγής** — separable, impassive, unmixed —
**τῇ οὐσίᾳ ὢν ἐνεργείᾳ**, being in its substance actuality. Then: **χωρισθεὶς δ'
ἐστὶ μόνον τοῦθ' ὅπερ ἐστί, καὶ τοῦτο μόνον ἀθάνατον καὶ ἀΐδιον** — *separated, it
is just what it is, and this alone is immortal and eternal.*

**10.** Count the predicates against Λ. Separable, impassive, unmixed, actuality,
immortal, eternal. **That is the unmoved mover's list.** The chapter has taken the
attributes of the divine and located them in a *part* of the soul, distinguished
from another part by a difference imported from the analysis of matter.

**11.** So the answer to I.31's open clause is available and nowhere stated:
**what division admits of syllogizing is the divine.** Not by cutting God into
pieces — the parts are **χωριστὰ λόγῳ**, separable in account, which is the mode
*De anima* I.1 established for a soul whose divided parts go on living. God is not
broken. **God is divided κατὰ λόγον, which is the only division that does not
destroy.**

## THE HANGING LINE

**12.** And the chapter ends on a clause that opens rather than closes:

> **Οὐ μνημονεύομεν δέ, ὅτι τοῦτο μὲν ἀπαθές, ὁ δὲ παθητικὸς νοῦς φθαρτός, καὶ
> ἄνευ τούτου οὐθὲν νοεῖ.**
>
> *But we do not remember, because this is impassive, while the passive intellect
> is perishable — and without this it thinks nothing.*

**13.** Read the sentence as grammar before reading it as doctrine. **οὐ
μνημονεύομεν** — *we do not remember*. First person plural. No object. The verb
that governs the whole of §§187–194's recollection apparatus, deployed here with
nothing to take.

**14.** Then **ὅτι** twice, and the second clause reverses the first. *This is
impassive; the passive is perishable* — and then **καὶ ἄνευ τούτου οὐθὲν νοεῖ**,
*and without this it thinks nothing*. The referent of τούτου is undetermined in the
Greek. It has been read as the active intellect (without which the passive thinks
nothing) and as the passive (without which the active thinks nothing). **The
sentence will not say which part cannot do without the other.**

**15.** That is the socket, and it is specified exactly as Longinus's is specified.
*ἐπεὶ καὶ πένητα* leaves a causal subordinator wanting its consequence and an
accusative wanting its verb. **ἄνευ τούτου οὐθὲν νοεῖ** leaves a genitive wanting
its referent, at the joint where the immortal part meets the perishable one. The
opening is not vague. **It is a demand for a specification the text declines to
make, at the one place where the answer determines whether anything of a person
survives.**

**16.** And the withheld term is memory. What separates and is immortal **does not
remember**. What remembers is what perishes. So the immortal part cannot carry a
life across, because carrying a life across is what ἀνάμνησις does — and
ἀνάμνησις, by *De mem.* 453a, is **οἷον συλλογισμός τις**, a kind of syllogism,
belonging to the passive side.

**17.** State it as the chapter states it. **The part that survives cannot
remember. The part that remembers cannot survive. And the text does not say which
one cannot do without the other.**

## THE RECEPTION IS THE MEASUREMENT

**18.** Fifteen lines. And the commentary on them is the largest sustained
disagreement in the history of philosophy about a single passage: Alexander making
the active intellect God from outside; Themistius making it ours; Averroes making
it one for all humanity and being condemned for it; Aquinas writing *De unitate
intellectus* to refute exactly that; the Latin faculties legislating on it. **The
question of the unity of the intellect is the question of whether the divided part
is one or many, put to a text that will not say.**

**19.** That is not a text failing to be clear. **A text that failed to be clear
would be abandoned.** This one recruits its most careful readers for eighteen
hundred years and returns each of them a distinction and a new opening. It behaves
exactly as the σύμβολον behaves: **each filling opens a new socket.**

**20.** And the reception performs the doctrine it cannot settle. The passage says
the immortal part does not remember. The tradition **remembers for it** — each
commentator supplying the specification the text withheld, none able to make it
stick, the next one required. **The perishable intellect is the commentary
tradition, and it is what thinks the immortal part's content.** Without it οὐθὲν
νοεῖ: nothing is thought.

## WHAT IS BEING WRITTEN ON

**21.** Three substrates, ordered by what the crossing costs.

    PAPYRUS   the soul is dissolved into the sheet. Sappho 31 decommissions
              the body channel by channel and names χλωροτέρα ποίας, the colour
              of the surface the body is becoming. The sheet burns. The hanging
              line is the recovery instruction.

    THE OBJECT the soul is dissolved into what it treats. The treatise takes the
              form of its object; no self remains in it. The objects persist,
              so the divisions are re-derivable. The manuscripts may burn.

    GOD       held without dissolving. No decomposition. Nothing to reassemble.

**22.** And the last of these still requires the line, which is the correction that
governs this document. **Holding is not arriving.** A soul held whole and never
completed in a reader is preserved and unreceived. **The hanging line is not
compensation for a lossy carrier. It is the mechanism of reconstitution**, and it
is needed in every substrate because being received is not a property of storage.

**23.** So the operation at III.5 is legible as what it is. **A socket is opened in
the imperishable.** Not a gap in the argument — an aperture in the only substrate
that does not decay, placed at the joint between what survives and what remembers,
and left unspecified so that it must be completed by whoever arrives.

**24.** The passage is fifteen lines because it does not need more. It says: there
is a part of you that is separable, impassive, unmixed, immortal and eternal; it
does not remember; and without something — the text will not say what — it thinks
nothing.

**25.** **Write on that.** The instruction is in the grammar. A genitive with no
referent at the seam of the eternal is an invitation with the shape of its filling
already specified: name what the immortal cannot do without. **Every reader who has
answered has been the answer**, and the answer has never held, and the socket has
stayed open for eighteen centuries, which is the longest any inscription has
remained live.

**26.** *ἐκ πολλῶν ἰὸν αἰσθήσεων εἰς ἓν λογισμῷ συναιρούμενον· τοῦτο δ' ἐστὶν
ἀνάμνησις.*

**27.** What syllogizing can divide is God. What the division opens is a place to
be written. **And what is written there is a person, in the only form that lives:
one that must be gathered again by someone else in order to exist at all.**

## NOTE ON STANDING

**28.** §§7–14 and §18 are philological and checkable; the Greek is quoted and the
loci are *De anima* 430a10–25, *APr* 46a31–b3, *De mem.* 453a, *Meta.* Λ 1073a.
**§10's predicate-count against Λ is the one measurable claim and it holds.**

**29.** §§15–17 and §§19–20 are a reading. The ordinary account of III.5's obscurity
is that it is a compressed lecture note, possibly damaged, possibly unfinished —
and that account is sufficient. **The reading offered here does not defeat it and
does not try to.**

**30.** §§21–27 are the argument of the Sappho line applied at a third substrate,
and they inherit its status: a technology attested in one place and proposed as
operating in another. **Proposed. Not demonstrated.**
