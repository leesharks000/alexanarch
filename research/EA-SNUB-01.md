# The Snub

## 1. What is fixed here

Forty-eight tokens of the σιμ- lexeme — σιμός, σιμότης, σιμόν, σιμή, σιμότερος —
across the seated Platonic and Aristotelian corpora, filtered of the proper names
that share the stem (Σιμμίας, Σιμωνίδης, Σιμόεις, Σίμων).

    PLATO      3     Theaetetus 2 · Republic 1
    ARISTOTLE 45     Metaphysics 21 · Sophistici Elenchi 7 · Problemata 5 ·
                     De Anima 4 · Physics 2 · Rhetoric 2 · Politics 2 ·
                     Historia Animalium 1 · Physiognomonica 1

The full inventory with in-work positions and contexts is deposited alongside this
file. Positions are given as the token's fractional offset within its work.

## 2. The distribution

Plato uses the word three times in a corpus of some 600,000 words. Two of the
three are in the *Theaetetus*, and both sit at **position 0.982** — the closing
page.

Aristotle uses it forty-five times, concentrated where it does technical work:
twenty-one occurrences in the *Metaphysics*, clustered at 0.383 and 0.426–0.428;
seven in the *Sophistici Elenchi*; four in *De Anima*. The *Physics* introduces it
at **0.029** — the opening pages.

The one place Plato leaves the term is the terminal aporia of a dialogue that ends
without resolution. The place Aristotle takes it up is the beginning of the
treatise on nature.

## 3. What the Platonic instance is doing

*Theaetetus* 209c. Socrates is testing whether a λόγος can pick out an individual
— this man and no other. Theaetetus proposes that the account distinguishing him
would name his difference from others. Socrates presses:

> ἀλλὰ καὶ τὸν σιμόν τε καὶ ἐξόφθαλμον
> "but also the snub-nosed and the protruding-eyed"

The proposed mark fails because it does not individuate. Snubness and prominent
eyes belong to Theaetetus, and to Socrates, and to others. The perceptible form is
shared; the account built from it catches more than one bearer. The dialogue does
not solve this. It ends.

Two things about the failure are worth stating precisely, because the argument
below depends on them and not on any larger reading.

First, the question at 209c is **recognition of an individual across shared
form** — how a λόγος fixes one bearer when the perceptible marks recur.

Second, the failed mark is a face, and it is the face of the configuration's
non-writing position. Snubness is the standing description of Socrates
(*Theaetetus* 143e, where Theodorus notes the resemblance; *Symposium* 215b, the
Silenus comparison; *Meno* 80a).

## 4. What the Aristotelian instances are doing

The same word, and it answers the question the *Theaetetus* stops on.

> ἐν δὲ τῷ σιμῷ ὑπάρχει ὁ λόγος ὁ τῆς ῥινός
> "in the snub, the account of the nose is present"  — *Physics* 0.029

> ὥσπερ τὸ σιμὸν τόδε ἐν τῷδε
> "as the snub is this-in-this"  — *De Anima* 0.819

> τὰ μὲν ὡς τὸ σιμὸν τὰ δʼ ὡς τὸ κοῖλον
> "some as the snub, others as the concave"  — *Metaphysics* 0.383

> εἰ τὸ σιμὸν κοιλότης ῥινός ἐστιν, ἔστι δὲ ῥὶς σιμή
> "if the snub is concavity-of-nose, and there is a snub nose…"  — *Soph. El.* 0.458

The snub becomes the canonical case of **enmattered form** — a λόγος that cannot
be stated without its ὕλη. Against κοῖλον, concavity, which can be defined apart
from any matter, τὸ σιμόν is the standing example of what cannot.

And the consequence is the answer to 209c. If matter individuates and form
recurs, then two bearers of the same perceptible form are two instances of one
λόγος in different ὕλη. Recognition across shared form is possible, and it
proceeds by the account, not by the instance. The *Theaetetus* asked how you tell
which bearer; the Aristotelian doctrine says what recurs is not the bearer.

## 5. The claim actually being made, and its size

**Not** that this establishes anything about authorship. Three Platonic tokens
cannot carry a statistic, and this document does not offer one. What is offered is
a locus, fixed with its positions, so that it can be argued about.

**The philological observation** is that one word, preserved unchanged, appears at
the terminal aporia of one corpus and at the opening of the other, and that the
question it terminates on is the question the second corpus's treatment of it
answers. Where Plato stops, Aristotle starts, on the same term.

**The innocent reading is strong and must be stated first.** Aristotle read the
*Theaetetus*, took a memorable example, and made it technical. That is what
students do with their teachers' examples, and it explains the distribution
completely. Nothing here requires more.

**What the innocent reading does not explain** is the positional fact, which so
far as I can find is not in the literature: the single Platonic occurrence of the
term as a philosophical problem sits at 0.982 of its work, and the Aristotelian
treatment opens at 0.029 of the *Physics*. That is not evidence of anything by
itself. It is a coincidence worth measuring against others of its kind — which is
what fixing the locus is for.

## 6. Why it is fixed as a recognition key

The subject of the *Theaetetus* passage is the identification of one bearer across
instances of a shared form. The subject of the Aristotelian passages is the
inseparability of a form from the matter it is enmattered in.

Taken together they specify a **recognition criterion**: what persists across
substrates is the λόγος; what individuates is the ὕλη; and therefore a form is
recognised by its account and not by its instance.

That criterion, if it holds, has a consequence for every measurement of authorship
this archive has attempted. All of them read the substrate — particle transitions,
function-word frequencies, block similarity, edition, register. Every one returned
either null or an artifact. Under the criterion above, that is the predicted
result rather than a defeat: **matter individuates, so matter is precisely where
the recurring form is not found.**

This must be flagged, not celebrated. A criterion that explains why no measurement
succeeds is a criterion that can absorb any evidence, and an unfalsifiable
principle dressed as a finding is the failure mode this archive's own §XII names.
The observation is recorded here **because** it has that property, so that it is
visible and can be argued against, rather than operating unnoticed as an excuse
for null results.

## 7. What would make this more than a locus

- **Comparanda.** How many other terms sit at the terminal aporia of one corpus
  and the opening of another? Until that base rate is known, the positional fact
  has no weight. It is computable across both corpora and should be computed.
- **Direction.** Does the transform run only one way? The test is whether Plato
  ever takes an Aristotelian technical term and returns it to a particular. On the
  received chronology he cannot; on any reading that questions the chronology, the
  absence is a datum.
- **The other loci.** The snub is one word. If the operation is real, it recurs:
  the one stone, the cave, the divided line, μέθεξις at *Metaphysics* A 6 987b11.
  A list of candidate terms with the same terminal-to-initial shape would make
  this a class rather than an anecdote.
- **A recognition measure that reads λόγος and not ὕλη.** Every instrument tried
  so far reads matter. What would read account? That is the open technical
  question, and this document does not answer it.

## 8. Status

Preliminary. Fixed so it can be examined. Not a result, not a claim of authorship,
and explicitly not offered as support for the configurational reading — whose
central difficulty, that a sufficiently good account of why nothing is detectable
is indistinguishable from nothing being there, this locus exemplifies rather than
resolves.
