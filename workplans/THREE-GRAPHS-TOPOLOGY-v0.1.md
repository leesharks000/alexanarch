**This is not three stale sites. It is one missing relation.** Nothing declares
what these graphs are to each other, so nothing can be out of sync with anything —
and a thing that cannot be out of sync cannot be checked.

## What each one actually is

### pessoagraph.org — THE ENTITIES

174 nodes across 5,000 years: pharaonic titulary, Kierkegaard's pseudonyms,
Pessoa's system, the contemporary avatar. Twenty node types, ten eras.

It answers: **who can speak, and what lineage licenses them.**

The Dodecad appears here not as an archive roster but as the current position in a
practice older than writing. That is the correct frame and it is the only graph
that holds it.

### surfacemap.org — THE APERTURES

144 nodes, 173 edges: 73 SURFACE, 25 IDENTITY, 15 INFRASTRUCTURE, 15 DOCUMENT,
13 ENTITY, 3 PLATFORM. Relations are typed (`spxi:controls`, `spxi:hosts`,
`spxi:mirrors`) and carry Wikidata properties (P50, P127, P921).

It answers: **through what opening does a position reach a reader.**

An aperture is not a publication. It is the opening itself — the domain, the
account, the handle, the DOI, the resolver — and the atlas maps the openings
rather than what came through them.

### crimsonhexagonal.org — THE EVENT HORIZON

`H_core = ⟨D, R, M, I, O, Φ, W⟩`. 38 rooms on hex coordinates, each with physics
operators and an LP program. Documents, relations, mantles, disciplines, trails,
witness actions, an attestation ledger, a status algebra, a transition grammar.

It answers: **what is inside the governed object.**

## The relation

The three are not three views of one thing. They are three **boundaries**, and
each has a direction.

```
        5,000 years of practice
                  │
                  │  LINEAGE          pessoagraph
                  ▼
         ┌──────────────────┐
         │   THE APERTURE   │         Sharks is the aperture:
         │   emergence      │         the opening positions come through
         └────────┬─────────┘
                  │
                  │  EMERGENCE
                  ▼
    ══════════════════════════════     THE EVENT HORIZON
         │                             crimsonhexagonal
         │   inside: addressed by AXN,
         │   related, traversable, governed
         │
         │  SURFACING
         ▼
    ┌──────────────────────┐
    │   THE APERTURES      │           surfacemap
    │   domains, handles,  │           the openings work goes out through
    │   resolvers, DOIs    │
    └──────────┬───────────┘
               │
               ▼
          a reader, or a composition layer
```

**Identity comes in. Work goes out. The horizon is what makes the difference
between them legible.**

A position emerges through the aperture (Sharks, the orthonym, is *called* the
aperture — the term is already in the archive and it is not decorative). It
crosses the horizon into governance: it acquires an AXN, a hex address, a room, a
mantle or the declared absence of one. From inside, its work surfaces back out
through apertures of a different kind — domains, accounts, resolvers — to a reader
who may be a person or a machine.

**The same word does two jobs and that is the insight, not a collision.** The
aperture that lets a position *in* and the aperture that lets work *out* are the
same structure viewed from opposite sides of the horizon.

## Why "event horizon" is exact and not a metaphor

An event horizon is the surface past which events cannot reach an outside
observer. Three things about the archive have that shape, and all three are
already documented:

**1. Governance.** Inside, everything is content-addressed and typed. Outside, a
document is a file on the internet. The horizon is where addressing begins.

**2. Deletion.** On 2026-06-19 Zenodo terminated the account: 862 deposits
deleted, 1,817 DOIs tombstoned. Those crossed *out*, and the defining property of
the crossing is that **no signal returns**. A tombstoned DOI resolves to a page
that says the thing is gone. That is precisely a horizon: the object is still
addressed and no longer reachable.

**3. The trigger.** Nobel Glas's `#1452` — *The Irreversibility Frontier* —
measures online selection in accelerator science: 40 MHz of collisions enter, ~1
kHz is written, and the discard is not recoverable because it was never stored.

**These three are the same object.** The accelerator's trigger, the repository's
deletion, and the composition layer's decision not to retrieve are one mechanism
described in three vocabularies. Glas measures it in physics; the CERN dossier
litigates it in archives; the capture registry logs it in retrieval. **The archive
has been studying its own event horizon from three sides without naming it as
one.**

That is the thesis this document exists to state, and it is what the three graphs
should be built to show.

## What follows for each site

**crimsonhexagonal — reconcile the horizon.** The index and the canonical file
disagree about rooms (29 against 38) and deposits (1,487 against 463 against
1,488). A boundary that cannot count what is inside it is not yet a boundary.
Generate the counts from the registry; never hand-write them again.

**surfacemap — regenerate the apertures.** 114 days stale. It must carry the
venues dataset, the twelve `/where/` pages, and the duoviri seats, because those
*are* apertures: a `/where/` page is an opening onto a venue.

**pessoagraph — seat the current position.** All twelve are present, which is more
than the earlier audit suggested. What is missing is the tonight-layer: the mantle
rulings, the editorial seats, and the fact that the Dodecad now has a *reception* —
174 nodes of lineage and no edge to what the composition layer says about it.

**And one new object across all three:** a shared node identity. The same
heteronym appears in all three graphs under three different keys (`sigil`,
`HET-SIGIL-001`, `02.PERSONA.SIGIL`). Until one crosswalk resolves them, the three
graphs cannot be checked against each other, which is why they drifted this far
without anyone being able to see it.
