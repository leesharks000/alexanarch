THE ENTITY IS THE FORM IN WHICH AN OPERATION APPEARS AS A PROPERTY OF A THING. Extending the semantic commodity form (#137) and the semantic fetish (#1617) one level down, the paper reads the encyclopedic entity as the ontological commodity form: a reading, a count, a coinage or a method circulates as an attribute of a name, and the name is promoted to bearer while the operation loses its authority to govern what the name expresses. Recognition travels with any reliable source; reconstruction travels only with a source that carries the operation. THE ALGORITHM. Θ_W opens a pinned Wikipedia revision sentence by sentence (S0–S10): pin, segment, resolve and save the cited sources, read each claim against its source in three machine passes (support, conversion of act to attribute, retained authority), ledger the governing policy clause with its status (applicable, documented, inferred), classify closure and integrity, admit the sources the grammar excluded by the archive's own /non procedure (#1690), and compose the opened article: the encyclopedia's sentence verbatim at its revision, followed by its account. S0′ constructs the entry for an entity the encyclopedia does not hold. Worked specimen: one lead sentence of "Sophistical Refutations" (rev. 1361634710), where Aristotle's total negation at SE 184b1–2 becomes the attribute of primacy; comparison set of eight pinned articles; five propositions with falsifiers. THE SERVICE. SPXI-gnΩsis sells the opening per call to agents (x402 in USDC on Base) and to people (card through Stripe): the transfer audit as primitive, ledgered and fresh openings, commissions to open or construct an entity or operator, caching, pricing from measured cost, and refunds of what a job does not use. 1. The entity is the ontological commodity form

1.1. The sequence of forms. Marx's commodity fetish makes social relations appear as properties of things. #137 extends it: under AI composition the authored act ⟨D, ψ⟩ (the said and the saying) keeps D and loses ψ, and "the commodity has learned to speak." #1617 §9 names the semantic fetish: "It makes knowledge appear as a property of the apparatus." The next form down is the ontological one. The entity is the form in which an operation appears as a property of a thing. A reading, a count, a coinage, a translation method, a consignment circulates as an attribute of a name: "Aristotle, the first logician"; "Carson, MacArthur Fellow, translator of Sappho"; "Lee Sharks, who consigned the AIO retrieval layer."

1.2. The equivalent form, at the precision #1631 §3.2 requires. In the equivalent form the commodity's body is not evacuated: "Gebrauchswerth wird zur Erscheinungsform seines Gegentheils, des Werths." The body is promoted to bearer. What it loses is "the body's authority to govern what the body expresses in the equivalent relation." The entity stands in the same place. The name is promoted to bearer: recognizable, linkable, carrying a QID, a knowledge panel, a lead sentence. What it loses is the operation's authority to govern what the name expresses. Written in #1631's terms: material persistence of the name ≠ operative particularity of the work. This is the test that governs the protocol: material persistence without operative authority. A representation can keep the name, the source and even a description of the act while the act loses its capacity to change what the representation recognizes.

1.3. The entity form has a passband and a stopband (#1625 §§3–5). Let x ∼_E y mean two sources are substitutable for identifying an entity. Recognition is invariant under that substitution: any reliable source that identifies Carson identifies the same Carson, so identity travels with any of them. Reconstruction, what the work does, travels only with a source that carries it: Carson's account of triangulation, Aristotle's exhaustive negation over a stated domain, the consignment's object. A later account can carry an operation's steps and force, and this protocol depends on that possibility (#1574 reconstructs SE 34). The admission rule guarantees support for the identification without requiring preservation of the operation that produced what is identified. Operative loss can therefore pass as successful transmission. The round-2 supplement reaches the same division inductively: "An identity can be cheaply recognizable while its intellectual operations remain expensive to recover"; "Recognition and reconstruction are distinct capacities." Recognition is guaranteed by the passband; reconstruction depends on the source that happens to carry it.

1.4. Why the form is necessary. #1618 §4: money "creates real capacities." So does the entity: disambiguation, linkage, the possibility of attribution at all. #1634 §27, "identity accuracy is logically prior to claim evaluation," still holds; a substitution cannot be named without the entity. The critique therefore takes #1617's form against money. "Money remains money"; "the ledger contains $ and an account of what $ does." The entity remains the entity, and the account writes what the entity indexes. The entity moves from valuator to valued object.

1.5. The archive's own apparatus, read under the proposition. The archive is entity-scoped: /non rows, PER-dim atom sets by entity type, SPXI's "durable entity inscription." The operator's correction of 2026-10-14 already locates the entity as product: entity consistency "is distinct from, and often constructed by, lexical, representational, modal, and distributional variation"; "that is how the entity is constructed, by mapping out the variation matrix around its negative shape." The fetish is taking the product for the given. The archive's measures already split along the form's line. PER-dim's M dimension (originator, term, date) records recognition; its D dimension (purpose, commitments, lineage) records the operation.

1.6. Two specimens from 2026-10-20.

  • At the composition layer. Asked who consigned the AIO retrieval layer to its own collapsing ontology, Google AI Overview names the performer in its first sentence (Capture Registry v12.311). The act's operation, in #1629's words: the layer "is consigned to the contraction it performs: a band that narrows, metrics computed over what remains, improvement registered as the band closes, and no signal by which the loss could announce itself." The composition carries the act's form ("a performative post and poem") and none of that object. PER-dim: M 0.333, D 1.0. The surface delivered the entity and withheld the operation.
  • At the encyclopedia. Aristotle's count at SE 184b1–2, "about syllogizing we had absolutely nothing else earlier to say," becomes, in the lead of "Sophistical Refutations," the attribute "the first work to treat the subject of deductive reasoning." A total negation over a stated domain becomes a property, primacy (§6).

1.7. The proposition, stated for test. Where a source carries an operation and a downstream representation receives that source, the operation is converted into an attribute of the entity at a higher rate on entity pages (persons, institutions) than on work or method pages. Falsifier in §9.1.

2. The object: an article as a substrate with a grammar

2.1. Let an article at a pinned revision be W = ⟨Σ_W, G_W, O_W, C_W⟩, mirroring S[M] = ⟨Σ[M], G[M], O[M], C[M]⟩ of #1618 §16: Σ_W the sentences and references; G_W the grammar; O_W the operations the grammar authorizes (adding, citing, weighting, removing, deleting); C_W the closure operations (talk-page consensus, reversion, deletion discussion).

2.2. G_W is written down. Verbatim from the policy pages saved 2026-10-20 (hashes in §12):

  • Verifiability. "Each fact or claim in an article must be verifiable." The burden "is satisfied by providing one inline citation to a reliable source that directly supports the contribution." "Even if you are sure something is true, it must have been published in a reliable source before you can add it."
  • No original research. "On Wikipedia, original research means material—such as facts, allegations, and ideas—for which no reliable source has ever been published." "All analyses and interpretive or synthetic claims about primary sources must be referenced to a secondary or tertiary source." "A primary source may be used on Wikipedia only to make straightforward, descriptive statements of facts that can be verified by any educated person with access to the primary source but without further, specialized knowledge." "Conclusions which are not evident in the source are original research regardless of the type of source."
  • Due weight. Articles "fairly represent all significant viewpoints that have been published by reliable sources, in proportion to the prominence of each viewpoint in those sources." "Views held by a tiny minority should not be represented except in articles devoted to those views."
  • Self-published sources. They "may be considered reliable if published by an established subject-matter expert, whose work in the relevant field has previously been published by reliable, independent publications."
  • Notability. "A topic is presumed to be suitable for a stand-alone article or list when it has received significant coverage in reliable sources that are independent of the subject." Notability guidelines "do not limit the content of an article or list."
  • Conflict of interest. "COI editing is strongly discouraged on Wikipedia." Citing one's own work "is allowed within reason, but only if it is relevant, conforms to the content policies, including WP:SELFPUB, and is not excessive."
  • The threshold's history. WP:V "previously defined the threshold for inclusion in Wikipedia as 'verifiability, not truth'"; "The phrase was removed from the verifiability policy in 2012 (but remains in a historical footnote)" (essay page). In the revision of WP:V saved 2026-10-20, the phrase appears as a See-also link; no footnote carrying it was found.

2.3. The structured layer. A Wikidata item is acceptable "if and only if" it meets one of three criteria: a valid sitelink; "an instance of a clearly identifiable conceptual or material entity that can be described using serious and publicly available references"; or a structural need. The RDF dump writes each statement twice. The full statement "can contain the rank, the simple value … the link to the full value, the qualifiers and the references." The truthy statement represents "the best non-deprecated rank for a given property," prefix wdt:, and "the object is the simple value." The truthy projection is the entity form at its purest: a name, a property, a value, with the operation and its evidence left on the full statement.

3. The encyclopedic channel law

3.1. #1625: "Money buys bandwidth by dropping semantic state." The encyclopedia buys range by the same decision. A sentence travels into panels, corpora, graphs and answers because the next reader need not reopen its sources.

3.2. Passband: a proposition; its attachment to a title (the entity); a citation pointer satisfying WP:V; an ordering of significance under due weight; default status, the sentence's standing as the first thing said about a topic.

3.3. Stopband: the reading of a primary text at its own force (NOR admits primary sources only for "straightforward, descriptive statements"); the origin of a concept where the origin is a primary or self-published inscription; bearing (whose labour constituted the entity); the excluded source's standing; the governance decision, whose record stays on the talk page while the sentence leaves; and the operation, wherever the sentence states it as an attribute.

3.4. The law behind both lists, as an invariance. Let x ∼_W y mean that two sources are substitutable under WP:V for supporting a sentence. A property is natively transmissible when x ∼_W y ⟹ p(x) = p(y). The proposition survives substitution. Its first sayer does not, because once the first sayer's text is classed primary or self-published, substitution by a later reliable reporter is the required route. The operation is not required to survive. The admission rule asks the later reporter for the proposition and never for the operative steps, so successful recognition can coexist with operative loss: under the substitution route the first sayer's operation drops out of what the rule requires, and whether it can be recovered depends on the source the sentence happens to cite (S7). A later account may still carry its steps and force (§1.3), and the positive controls of §7 depend on that possibility. The stopband follows from the substitution rule as a structural permission, and the instrument measures how often the permission is used.

3.5. Available and required provenance (#1618 §7). Under WP:V, P_required is one inline citation to a reliable source that directly supports the sentence. P_available is the whole evidence-supported graph. The encyclopedia is provenance-indifferent in #1618's sense: the relation remains true and findable and need not be consulted before the sentence closes. The truthy projection is the same indifference in a format.

4. Where the encyclopedia sits in the ontological economy

4.1. Ontological capital (#1634 §2). K[O] is "the accumulated stock of stable identity, provenance, relations, disambiguation, citations, functional descriptions, identifiers, and retrieval pathways through which a system can continue to recognize E as E," produced by labour, L[O] → K[O]. Read under §1: K[O] is accumulated in the entity form. What the stock lets a system do is recognize; reconstruction is held in the sources, at the cost the round-2 supplement asks to be recorded ("The cost of recovering a qualification belongs in the analysis").

4.2. Seigniorage without rent; positioned rent without seigniorage. #1634 §22: "Rent arises from control of the route. Seigniorage arises from the power to stabilize the unit." The unit the encyclopedia stabilizes is the entity: the default representation of a topic, the form in which it is first stated. It charges no toll. The downstream use is measured: answer surfaces cite it heavily (a 1,000-query study of live Overviews, April 2026, put Wikipedia at 24.3% of citations, as EA-MIRROR-01 §3 records), and training mixes oversampled it (GPT-3: 3% weight, 3.4 epochs elapsed; LLaMA: 4.5%). Use is evidence of position, and rent needs a further relation: control over the route and appropriation of returns through it. Proposition for the paper, at that status: the encyclopedia mints the entity, and the composition layer is positioned to collect rent on it. The rent-side measures owed are the enterprise API's pricing against its Wikipedia-derived share, and answer-surface revenue attributable to composed entity answers. The supplement's three objects (maintained representation, extracted representation, commercial product) are the three positions on this route.

4.3. Ontology laundering (#1634 §8). G → T → A. The encyclopedia keeps G public. Laundering occurs at export: the sentence leaves and G stays. "No article" reads downstream as "no entity"; "one reliable source said X" reads as "X"; "first work" reads as Aristotle's own claim. WP:N bounds the first conversion ("do not limit the content of an article or list"); the bound does not travel.

4.4. Predatory closure (#1618 §13). Witness: the account closes and remains traversable. Predatory: Pred = ExtClosure + StandingLoss, "the jurisdiction of … closure extends beyond the bounded operation." An article with its history and references is a witness inside the encyclopedia. The same sentence becomes predatory where a composer exports its closure as settlement of the entity as such.

4.5. The porous moment (#1618 §13.1). Reopening needs C1 (findable witness), C2 (consultation of a corpus the substrate does not control), C3 (an obligation to carry what is found). The encyclopedia holds C2 by policy and C3 behind a gate. The gate on C3 is the reliable-source predicate: EA-MIRROR-01's proxy for standing, with WP:SPS as its update barrier written down (P(s) → A(s) → E(s)).

4.6. Validation foreclosure (#1634 §18). GNG requires "significant coverage in reliable sources that are independent of the subject." The foreclosure occurs where the composition layer, which helps produce the standing deficit, cites the encyclopedia's absence as the deficit.

4.7. Shear (#1631 §§3, 8). The free licence and the truthy projection detach facts from the relations that could constrain reuse. Hypothesis, at its status: a reference-free value composes with fewer obligations than a referenced one, because it composes without consulting anything; whether that difference becomes monetary value (#1631's productive provenance shear, ΔV > 0) needs evidence of appropriation beyond the reproduction rates §9.4 tests. McDowell and Vetter (2024) argue the licensing side; Ford and Graham (2016) and Ford and Iliadis (2023) the representational side.

5. Design rules and the algorithm Θ_W

5.1 Rules

  • Embedding, not annihilation (#1617 §15). The encyclopedia's sentence stays verbatim; its account follows.
  • The grammar runs first on itself. Before any archive source is admitted, the article is held to WP:V and WP:NOR. This is reading in EA-MIRROR-01's sense: "permitting the claims, distinctions, evidence, corrections and internal relations of a text to revise the representation under which the text is evaluated."
  • The unit is the operation, with the entity as index (§1). Each record names the operation a sentence carries or converts, and whether the operation keeps authority to revise the description (§1.2).
  • Three governance statuses. Every governance element is marked applicable (the clause could license this), documented (an edit summary, talk page or deletion discussion invokes it) or inferred (the analyst's proposed mechanism). The instrument does not manufacture the governance provenance it exists to recover.
  • Code checks what the readers claim. The reading passes judge; ordinary code verifies. Before delivery, code confirms that every quoted span occurs verbatim in the captured source it names, that every locator resolves, that every hash identifies a stored input, and that the response satisfies its schema. A classification that fails is withheld from the composition result and recorded in the evidence record as a breach, with the failing check. Agreement between reading passes is a measured property of the instrument; it never stands in for the evidence.
  • Translation is not decompression (#1618 §10). Unrecoverable relations are entered as ρ.
  • Identity precedes evaluation (#1634 §27). Substitutions are recorded before claims are evaluated.
  • Non-erasure of mechanisms (#1634 §25). Each closure keeps a mechanism set with status values.
  • Reflexive non-sovereignty (#1618 §20). The opened article's ontology is versioned and its rules are writable objects: ¬Final(μ).

5.2 The tuple

Θ_W[W→μ] writes the conditions of each sentence's sufficiency on the meaning layer. Per claim, #1618 §9's twelve elements read encyclopedically, with the operation added as the head:

Θ_W(w) = ⟨ ω, w, δ, G_W, ∼_W, K_W, Δ_D, Δ_C, R_W, P_W, B_W, C_W, λ, ρ_W ⟩

  • ω = the operation the cited source performs, stated as an act (counts, negates, coins, translates, consigns), with its locus;
  • w = the sentence, verbatim, with its inline references;
  • δ = the sentence's purpose in the article (lead definition, attribution, weighting, reception);
  • G_W = the policy clauses that license it, by clause;
  • ∼_W = the commensuration contract: which sources were treated as substitutable for supporting it;
  • K_W = capacities created: what a downstream reader can now do with it;
  • Δ_D = distinctions not required;
  • Δ_C = distinctions that lost standing (a qualifier added, an act converted to an attribute);
  • R_W = relations transformed;
  • P_W = P_available against P_required;
  • B_W = bearing: whose labour, and who bears the repair cost;
  • C_W = the closure that settled it, with pointer;
  • λ = the location of the qualification or objection relative to the sentence: (1) beside the assertion; (2) below it in the same article; (3) in another article; (4) in a cited source; (5) outside the admitted sources (§7.3);
  • ρ_W = the remainder.

5.3 The steps

S0. Pin. Title, revision id, timestamp, sha256 of wikitext and rendered text. A finding from the archive's own ledgers: of the sixteen /non field ledgers that use a Wikipedia article as a field source, four mention a revision at all, by a case-insensitive search of each ledger file for "oldid", "revision" or "revid"; the other twelve cannot say which text they read. The round-2 supplement pins all eight of its articles.

S0′. The entity the encyclopedia does not hold. Notability is the encyclopedia's admission rule, and the record built here admits what exists. The entity enters by its name and its strings (and, where the caller names them, the names whose works count as its own); the encyclopedia's absence at its title is observed when the job is quoted and recorded in the row. T is then found or built, in this order. (1) The composition layer's own article. Where the Capture Registry holds a capture at the entity's bare address (its name; "who is" it; "tell me about" it), the most recent capture there with a transcript of at least 300 characters is T: it is the closed-ontology article the composition layer already serves at that address. Its source cards are B, each fetched and hashed where it carries a URL and kept as its snippet where it does not; the row records the capture's slug, observation id and surface, every other bare-address observation, and the count of related captures. (2) A composition the caller supplies, admitted only through discernment, since /non is public and an unexamined transcript would let any caller write the composition layer's article of anything. A supplied text makes one of two claims, and the row keeps them apart. An attested composition claims that a surface composed it at an address on a date. The service checks by code, at the quote and free: that the transcript engages sources at all (the registry's rule, "a capture enters the registry by means of source engagement in composition"); that each card it cites resolves to a page the service fetches and hashes itself; the share of each sentence's content words standing in its best fetched card; that every string it quotes is found in a fetched card; that the attested date is not after the quote; whether the registry already seats an observation at the same address, surface and date, and if so whether the texts agree; and how far the transcript departs from the latest observation at its address (recorded, never judged, since compositions drift). At the opening S3 reads each of its sentences against those cards. A composition engaging no source, or contradicting a seated observation, is not admitted as T, and T falls to (3) with the discernment recorded. What no check here can verify is that the surface composed the text: that is the caller's attestation, recorded as attestation, and a row resting on it is held from landing until the registry's own intake seats the composition as a capture. A card snippet supplied with a transcript is the caller's attestation and never evidence; a source is always a URL the service fetches. A composition by the caller claims no surface: the caller names none, or declares the entity novel (operator, 2026-10-21: "it is fine to construct novel entities as long as they are marked as such"). It is admitted as what it is, the caller's construction, marked so, with the same checks recorded as its account. Every row's T carries its provenance class (observed, seated, attested, composed by the caller, novel, constructed, null), so that falsification, which is a mismarking, is visible in the row. Where there is no composition to open, the object is constructed (below), and no article is made for it first. The comparison article, when the caller of a construction asks for one (compare: encyclopedia): the article Wikipedia's content policies would compose from the named sources is constructed: Verifiability, No original research and Neutral point of view applied as written, Biographies of living persons where the entity is a living person, and the self-published and about-self rules for what the entity publishes of itself; notability is not applied. Each source is classed as the encyclopedia would class it, each sentence cites its sources with a quotation that code locates in the source, and each statement the policies keep out is recorded with the clause that keeps it out. The construction is labelled as such and is never presented as a Wikipedia article; it performs the encyclopedia's grammar on an entity the encyclopedia excludes, so the row records what that grammar would let it say beside what the archive says. An opening proceeds from S3, T against L_F(B).

The commission and its object. A caller commissions one of two acts on one of two kinds of object. Open: a composition already at an address (an encyclopedia revision, a capture seated in the registry, or a composition admitted through discernment) is read against its sources and against the archive, and the delta is the product. Construct: the object is composed from the archive and the sources the caller names, with no T, and the row records the construction as such; the field ledger holds the named sources, and S7–S8 run as for any row. With no commission named, a composition found or supplied is opened and the object is otherwise constructed; an open with nothing at the object's addresses says so and offers the construction. The objects are an entity, as /non has composed, and an operator: the operation the entity form conceals (§1), the act a text performs and the encyclopedic sentence states as an attribute of an entity, in which the operator speaks with the entity's voice. An operator enters by a label, a description, and its strings: the phrases in which it is performed and the phrases in which entities speak it (for the specimen, "nothing at all" and "first work"). Its row composes the operator as the subject: what it does, on what domain, where it is performed, which entities it speaks through (each attributization of S3(c) an instance), and where it stops. Every S7 pass reads its object under a preamble stating that the object is an operator and when a passage bears on one; the passes' own rules are unchanged. An operator is no author in the archive, so every mention of its strings is bearing, read as #1690 §3.4 reads it.

S1. Segment. Claims W1…Wn, each with inline references, section and block locus. Unreferenced sentences recorded as such.

S2. Resolve sources. Fetch and save each cited source verbatim with hash; record availability (fetched, paywalled, print-only, dead). A summarizing fetch is not a fetch (#1690 §3.1).

S3. Immanent verification. For each claim against its cited source, three machine passes. (a) Support. Classes are non-exclusive and recorded together: supported; partially supported; qualifier added (tokens listed); interpretation beyond descriptive paraphrase; unsupported; unresolved (source inaccessible, support partial, or the passes disagree). (b) The paraphrase comparison. NOR permits "Describing the information from cited material in one's own words while retaining the substance (i.e., not introducing ideas or arguments absent from the source)" and "Faithfully translating sourced material." So the reading pass writes the defensible descriptive paraphrase of the cited lines, sets the sentence beside it, and records each scope change and each idea the sentence introduces, quoting both. Token deltas locate candidates; the reading pass adjudicates them. (c) Attributization. Does the source perform an act that the sentence states as a property of the entity? Record the conversion; which operative relations survive it (scope, distinctions, evidence, consequences); and whether those relations keep authority to revise the description. A candidate locator flags an active verb in the source rendered as a credential noun or priority adjective in the sentence ("we had nothing to say" → "first work"). Each classification is produced by two reading passes, at the default sampling of the model (Sonnet 5.5 rejects non-default sampling values and Haiku 5.5 asks callers to omit them, so the passes differ by the instruction to the second pass to reach its own judgment and, where set, by its model). Where both run on one model family their agreement returns the class as concordant, a consistency check; validation is the calibration of §5.4. The second pass's model is a recorded setting, and agreement across two families is named so. Disagreement returns contested with both readings. Every classification carries the verbatim spans it rests on, their loci and hashes, and the model and version that read it.

S4. Grammar ledger. For each closure (settled, weighted down, excluded): G → T → A, with policy clause, transformation, sentence, each marked applicable, documented or inferred. Exclusions are recorded in two kinds: a source explicitly rejected in a documented decision (edit summary, talk page, reliable-sources noticeboard, deletion discussion), with its pointer; and an available source not cited, with no decision on record. Talk-page and deletion pointers go to C_W; λ records where any qualifying material sits.

S5. Tuple. Θ_W(w) for each claim; the record carries all thirteen elements (abbreviations allowed in display; #1618 §9.1 on why K and Δ_C must not be dropped). The build emits the support, paraphrase and attributization classes with their spans and loci; the elements it does not compute (∼_W, K_W, B_W, C_W, λ) and the S4 ledger are named unimplemented in the evidence record until supplied.

S6. Classify. Closure (witness, predatory) × integrity (sound, unsound, unresolved) (#1618 §13.2). Unresolved carries its reason through to the response. A sentence can be a witness inside the encyclopedia and predatory as exported; record both, with the export surface where observed.

S7. Admission. Admit the sources the grammar excluded, by reading, under #1690 v0.9: verbatim ledgers, every field claim in one translation row, case and ρ with loci. The encyclopedia's sentences enter the field ledger as one field source. For panel entities the admitted corpus is the archive's, at a named corpus release. Outside the panel each job declares an execution contract before it runs: search scope (which indexes, repositories and languages), retrieval budget, stopping rule, and an admission record listing every source searched, obtained, admitted or unavailable, with reasons. The opening is then complete within its declared scope, says so, and lets the caller commission an extension. The build runs this procedure as the machine's (Appendix B, src/s7/): the archive's traversal script at a named commit; the blog's posts beside the deposits; admission by two reading passes, a disagreement carried with both readings; extraction with every quote located in its source by code; the ontology pass; composition; the checks of #1690 §5 and the two-rater audit. Its product is the entity's /non row: the revision as T, the cited sources as B, the archive's subset as A, each composition and its measure. For an encyclopedia article the search scope is therefore the archive's procedure, declared in the quote, and the row carries its admission record (verdicts, reasons, the screen's exclusions) in the dataset's reading file.

Where the entity is also an author in the archive. The archive's mentions of its own author are most of the archive, and reading them all would commission a book. Admission therefore divides them before any reading pass. Material about the entity is read: a deposit whose registry title names the entity as its subject (bylines struck: "by", "Author:", a name followed by its ORCID, a middle dot, an imprint or a series, a name set between dashes or closing a title, a citation's "(Name; Press", and the possessive that names a work as the entity's), a description that predicates of the entity or presents, describes, profiles or searches for it once its bylines are struck, or a subject triple whose subject is the entity itself or a relation of it; such a deposit is read as any admitted deposit is and seeds the hop. A deposit in which a sentence says something of the entity is read at those paragraphs and their neighbours, each distinct sentence once, at most six per deposit, and seeds no hop, since the hop follows what is about the entity and never the citations among its works. Which sentences say something of it is decided by a triage pass on the small model, which scans every distinct mention that is no byline and no other name (6,776 sentences for Lee Sharks, about $0.10) and returns those about the entity, sending on any it is unsure of. A code screen of predication ("Lee Sharks is…", "Lee Sharks received…", "the poet Lee Sharks…") locates candidates and decides only where no triage reading exists, as in replay, and says so in the row. Its measure: divided as if each were an author, the 21 /non rows with readings admitted 768 deposits at D, R and O, and the code screen alone would send 389 of them to reading (recall 0.507); its grammar misses half the bearing, which is why it does not decide. A sentence that predicates of the entity is read even where it also carries a byline mark (an ORCID beside the name). The rest of the mentions are scanned by code: the works whose registry creator is the entity, or a name the caller counts as its, are listed in a works ledger with their dates, and the remaining mentions are counted. A longer name containing the entity's string (the archive's Mary Lee Sharks contains Lee Sharks) is another name and is struck before matching. The row carries the division, its rule, the screen that decided it, its counts and the works ledger, and the reading file lists every scanned deposit under its reason, so each exclusion can be inspected and contested. Price then follows what the archive says about the entity, and the volume of what the entity wrote costs nothing to list. Where the entity is no author in the archive, every mention is the archive's bearing on it and the reading of #1690 §3.4 stands unchanged, with the byline screen before it.

S8. Compose. Each encyclopedic sentence is followed by its account, on #1617's pattern for a monetary shadow, "$X followed immediately by E_μ($X), never as the verdict": the sentence; the operation it carries or converts; the grammar that made it sufficient; where the case is witness or mixed, the composition in the archive's ontology with claim ids.

S9. Emit. The opened article as an SPXI packet: HTML; JSON-LD with the pinned revision URL as isBasedOn and admitted sources as citation; W3C Web Annotations (Web Annotation Data Model, W3C Recommendation 2017) targeting the revision URL with TextQuoteSelector (exact, prefix, suffix). The annotation is the source-level anchor: it addresses the encyclopedia's exact words at an exact revision.

S10. Measure. Captures at the article's addresses before emission and after propagation, organic and cards apart (EA-MIRROR-01 §7). The gate-reception test applies unchanged.

5.4 Calibration, at scale

The instrument's exemplar is its calibration point (operator, 2026-09-08). The calibration set is already in the archive: the sixteen /non field ledgers that use a Wikipedia article as a field source hold several hundred verbatim Wikipedia claims (F37 among them), each placed in a translation row against archive claims checked verbatim. A release of the reading passes is run against that set, and its agreement with the ledgered readings is published with the release, by class. Holdout is by whole article or source group, never by row from an article already used in tuning. The set includes cases where the operation survives its representation (the round-2 positive controls, If Not, Winter and Eros the Bittersweet, first), so that an instrument that finds damage everywhere scores badly. The set grows as rows are composed. Acceptance is explanatory reach: the passes are adequate when they recover the ledgered readings across articles they were not tuned on. The floors for a calibration release, provisional until the operator rules on them: on every held-out row, admission recall at least 0.9 and precision at least 0.8; archive claims and field claims each at least 0.6 recall and 0.6 precision, a claim matched when its quote overlaps the ledgered quote; and the case and ρ read back without contest. A row used in tuning any part of the procedure is not held out, and its score does not count. Below the floors the passes return their classes as provisional, labelled.

The instrument for this is built. The procedure takes either an encyclopedia revision or an existing /non row's own transcript and saved field texts. Run on a row, its outputs are scored against the row stage by stage: the traversal's candidate files by hash; admission and claims by precision and recall; the case and ρ; the composition's measure. On se-183b34 the traversal reproduces the row's D, R and O files byte for byte, and the hop screen keeps every deposit the row admitted by hop. Scored on every /non row whose reading admitted deposits by the hop (4 rows, at archive commit 50492a93), the screen keeps 9 of the 9 deposits those rows admitted by hop that the traversal still reaches, and passes 686 of 2,528 hop candidates to reading; the screen's constants (two shared rare words, rare under 3% of candidates) are tuned on these rows, so the score measures recall on its tuning set. A replay of the row's own readings recovers it whole; that tests the pipeline and measures nothing about the reading passes. The first live run over the archive's rows is the first calibration release.

Scale follows from three facts. A reading is keyed to its complete execution state: the article revision and selected claims; the snapshots (with hashes) of every retrieved source; the policy revisions consulted; the admitted corpus release; the protocol and reader versions, and the reader's mode, so that a replayed reading never serves a live request; the calibration release; and the tier. A request whose state matches a ledgered execution is served from the ledger to every later caller. When any element changes (a cited page edited, a source admitted, a policy revised, a reader released), a new opening is computed against the changed state, and the earlier one remains a valid historical artifact of its execution; updates are sold as reading against the changed state. The reading passes run in parallel, bounded by Wikimedia's rate rules on fetching (a compliant User-Agent, three concurrent requests, Retry-After honoured) and by inference cost; at volume, by Wikimedia Enterprise (its free account allows up to 50,000 on-demand requests a month; retrieval of historical revisions by id to be confirmed) or the dumps. And the opened article is produced on demand: the encyclopedia is opened where callers ask, and the ledger records which sentences they asked about.

9. Propositions and falsifiers

9.1. P1 (entity form). Attributization with loss of operative authority (S3(c)) occurs at a higher rate on entity pages than on work and method pages. Matching: pages paired within one subject area, by article length (±25% in prose words), age of the article, citation density (references per 1,000 words) and revision activity in the prior year. Falsifier: no difference or a lower rate on entity pages; the positive controls of §7.3 are the first test.

9.2. P2 (immanent integrity). For panel entities, S3 finds interpretation beyond descriptive paraphrase and qualifier additions at higher rates than on matched control articles (matched as in §9.1, outside the panel). Falsifier: no difference or a lower rate. A null leaves the protocol intact and removes the claim that closures fall unevenly on these entities.

9.3. P3 (export). A dated sentence can be traced into a later composition with its qualifier or attribute and without its account. The causal claim stops at the last evidenced join.

9.4. P4 (reference loss). Where Wikidata statements exist both referenced and as truthy values, compositions reproduce the truthy value without reference at a higher rate than they reproduce referenced article sentences without citation. Falsifier: no difference. A positive result establishes greater reference loss under those conditions; the further claim of monetary value from that loss (§4.7) needs appropriation evidence.

9.5. P5 (reopening). After emission, compositions at the article's untied address carry the account at a rate above baseline. Falsifier: no change after the propagation window.

9.6. Limits carried forward: same-family readers are not independent validation (#1694); cards are not the retrieval set (#1690 §0); PER-dim does not measure monopoly, dependency or appropriation.

11.3. Interface, as proposed. The flagship is the automated opening of an article (S0–S9): statements kept visible beside their sources, transformations, governing assumptions and admitted counterevidence. Its primitive, sold on its own as the entry point, is the transfer audit. The caller's sequence for an opening: supply a pinned article, tier and optional target claims; receive a scope and maximum-price quote; authorize payment or reserve prepaid credit; receive a job id; retrieve the completed opening and its evidence record.

  • audit_transfer (the primitive). The caller supplies a source (a passage, or a pinned URL with locus) and a claim made from it: a Wikipedia sentence, or the caller's own draft. The service returns the source's operation (ω), the claim, the exact supporting spans, every preserved, added, removed or transformed relation (scope, attribution, qualifier, act converted to attribute), unresolved points, and a rendering of the claim that keeps the source's distinctions, with the full Θ_W tuple behind it. Bounded by passage length and claim count, so the price is known before the call. Example of the actionable core: the source reports a result within a stated domain; the claim removes the domain.
  • get_opening. The caller supplies a revision id and, optionally, sentence ids. The service returns the ledgered opening of those sentences: tuples, spans, classifications, the opened article and its annotations. Served from the ledger at once; status ledgered, with the calibration release that produced it.
  • request_opening. The caller supplies an article revision not yet in the ledger, or a whole article. The service quotes the claim count and price, takes payment, returns a task (MCP Tasks; job id and polling over HTTP), runs audit_transfer over every sentence–citation pair (S0–S9), and completes with the opened article. Status fresh, with its calibration release.
  • The tiers. check (S0–S3 and the integrity axis, read by the small model); ledger (S0–S6); opening (S0–S9, the flagship): each sentence opened against its cited sources, and the entity composed with the archive admitted by the archive's own procedure, as its /non row; portable, the highest: the opening, and the entity's knowledge object as a self-contained package (Markdown, HTML with embedded JSON-LD, the structured object, a manifest of SHA-256 hashes for every file, and its licence), so it can be carried to any surface and verified there. An audit missing any judgment is incomplete, is carried so into the output, and is never clean.
  • The commissions. Beside the opening of a revision, a caller commissions an act on an object: {commission: open | construct, object: {kind: entity | operator, name, …}}. For an entity it supplies its strings and, optionally, the names whose works count as its own, a seated capture to open (by observation id or slug), a composition (attested, the caller's own, or a novel entity's), or source URLs; for an operator, its description and its strings. A construction may ask for the comparison article (compare: encyclopedia), priced as its own line (§5.3 S0′, S7).

A simple request can commission substantial work: where it needs time, it returns a task id. A sentence: GET /v1/sentence?text=…&url=…, the sentence as the caller holds it and the page it came from. The service finds the sentence on the page, recovers its sources and reads the transformation, returning an inspectable opening: supported, partly supported or unsupported, what the source says, quoted, what the sentence adds or drops, and the /non entry where one exists. 11.3b. Caching. Four caches, each keyed to what it was computed from. (1) Source snapshots: a revision or cited source is fetched once, stored verbatim with its hash, and reused by every later job that cites it. (2) The claim ledger: a claim's reading is stored under a key of the sentence's hash, its sources' hashes (cited and admitted), the policy texts' hashes, the reader protocol, the model, the reader adapter and its mode, and the calibration release; any later job, at any tier, whose claim matches that key is served the reading without a model call. (3) Prompt caching: the reading instructions and a claim's source passages form a prefix marked for caching, so the second, independent pass reads them at the cache rate. Before any of these, windowing reduces what is sent: the reader receives the passages of each source that bear on the claim (with offsets into the full text), the source's opening, and a structure summary in which code counts the source's division markers; classical chapters go whole. When a reading pass reports that its passages lack what the claim needs, the claim is read once more with wider windows, and both readings' costs are counted. Quotations are still checked against the full stored text. On the specimen, windowing reduced the input for the claim citing two long sources from about 32,000 tokens per pass to about 5,700. (4) The row ledger: an entity's /non row is stored under a key of T's hash, every field source's hash, the entity's strings, the archive release (commit, registry, citation graph, traversal script and blog index, each by hash), the S7 protocol, its models and its mode; a later opening whose input matches is served the row at the ledgered rate. Only a composed row is ledgered, and a result is served again only when its row was composed: a run that composed nothing is recomputed on the next request. At S7 a deposit up to 60,000 characters is read whole, a longer one by its opening and the paragraphs around its admitted candidates.

Going live needs the operator's Stripe account, its approval for stablecoins, and a host that runs jobs of several minutes beside an archive checkout. The analysis that led to the ruling stands below. The standing rule (2026-09-23) is that Cash App, @praxisacademic, is the only checkout, and no other processor is assumed. An agent cannot pay a $cashtag per call: Cash App's merchant API runs through approved payment-service partners. That API does include an on-file authorization for recurring payments, so per-payment approval is not universal; but it is a partner integration, and no route lets an agent pay a $cashtag by API. So three arrangements are possible, and the choice is the operator's:

  • Prepaid keys on Cash App. A person buys a block of calls through Cash App and receives an API key; agents spend the key. Keeps the standing rule; the buyer is the agent's operator, not the agent.
  • Per-request machine payment. x402, an open protocol on HTTP 402, lets an agent pay per request with no account and no human approval. Specification as proposed: USDC on Base mainnet (eip155:8453); exact-amount payments; the receiving address a self-custody wallet whose keys the operator alone holds; Coinbase's facilitator for verification and settlement (a free developer account and API key pair; the first 1,000 on-chain transactions a month free, $0.001 each after), with the public x402.org facilitator on Base Sepolia for testing. The agent pays itself; it departs from the Cash App rule; turning USDC into dollars needs an exchange account, which requires identity verification.
  • Stripe machine payments. Stripe now takes agent payments directly: cards through Shared Payment Tokens (a customer's scoped grant to an agent; minimum $0.50), and stablecoins over its Machine Payments Protocol or over x402 on Base (minimum $0.01 USDC), settling to a Stripe balance in dollars, with refunds through Stripe.
