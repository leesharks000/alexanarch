Integrity Lock Certificate: The Refutation Triad — Mutual Anchoring is an integrity-lock deposit by Lee Sharks, dated 11 June 2026. An integrity lock is the archive's mechanism for making a set of documents mutually dependent: each anchors the others, so that removing one leaves detectable absence in the rest. The Refutation Triad locks three works whose argument only holds together, and the certificate records the anchoring rather than asserting it. The governing protocol is the Crimson Hexagon Mutual Anchoring Framework, and the certificate cites its precedents by DOI — a genre that accumulates a chain of prior certificates. The mechanism is the practical form of the archive's non-erasure doctrine: a document that can be deleted without trace is a document a platform can delete quietly, and mutual anchoring is designed to make quiet deletion impossible.

Recursive defense

## Recursive defense

To challenge the referee report's empirics is to invoke the Tailguard, which grants the empirics _arguendo_ and still defeats the recommendation. To challenge the Tailguard as ideological is to invoke the referee report, which is purely technical and stands without the Tailguard. To challenge the disambiguation as territorial is to invoke its timestamps, which are facts of the public record, and the referee's §9, which derives a diligence finding from them without any conduct allegation. To challenge the priority record is to challenge CERN-operated infrastructure timestamps. Each document's weakest face is another document's strongest; the architecture holds by mutual anchoring, not by accumulation.
