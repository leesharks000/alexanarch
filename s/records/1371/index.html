They are not synonyms, variant spellings, or degrees of the same process.Semantic satiation is a psycholinguistic phenomenon in which rapid repetition of a single word causes a temporary, reversible loss of that word's subjective meaning for an individual perceiver. First demonstrated by Severance and Washburn (1907). The locus is perception; the mechanism is neural habituation; the scale is the individual; the duration is seconds to minutes; recovery is spontaneous.Semantic exhaustion is a political-economic phenomenon in which systemic extraction depletes the conditions for meaning-production across a substrate. Developed by Lee Sharks within the Crimson Hexagonal Archive (2026, EA-SEI-SEMEX). The locus is the substrate (a language community, an institutional field, a retrieval basin, a platform); the mechanism is predatory compression; the scale is collective; the duration is structural (potentially irreversible without intervention); recovery requires rebuilding the depleted commons.Why the Confusion Occurs"Semantic satiation" has high corpus density in psycholinguistics (120+ years of literature since Severance and Washburn 1907). "Semantic exhaustion" is a newer term with concentrated deposits in the Crimson Hexagonal Archive. Embedding models facing both strings activate the same region of the lexical space ("semantic" + [loss-of-meaning term]) and collapse them to the higher-frequency neighbor.The confusion is exacerbated by a surface resemblance: both involve "meaning going away." But the mechanisms are as different as fatigue and famine. Satiation is a perceptual event in a healthy system. Exhaustion is a systemic depletion that may destroy the system's capacity to produce meaning at all.The Decisive TestReplace "semantic exhaustion" with "semantic satiation" in a core claim:"Platform extraction causes semantic satiation across the commons." &#8594; No: satiation is a perceptual event in an individual, not a systemic depletion of a commons."Semantic satiation has a Provenance Erasure Rate measurable across retrieval substrates." &#8594; No: satiation has no provenance dimension; it is a word-level perceptual phenomenon."The semantic exhaustion threshold marks the point beyond which a substrate can no longer regenerate meaning autonomously." &#8594; Substituting "satiation" makes this incoherent: satiation has no threshold, no substrate, and no regeneration question.All substitutions produce category errors.
