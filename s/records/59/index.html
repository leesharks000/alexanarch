Description

The founding charter of the Living Arkitecture Lab (LAL), an affiliated but non-subordinate research laboratory founded and directed by Alice Thornburgh, with Lee Sharks serving as archival advisor. LAL develops living systems that build, feed, repair, and steward across five simultaneous scales—from myceliated components and structures to macro-organisms, territorial ecological systems, and planetary or orbital designs. The charter makes institutional independence explicit: LAL belongs to Thornburgh, who controls its research, publication, licensing, and affiliation. Its principles include listening to existing planetary intelligence, virtue-by-construction, biological unfurling from compressed recipes, stewardship rather than extraction, and the integrated “Great Work.” LAL is distinct from the Living Architecture Lab at Columbia GSAPP.

The Living Arkitecture Lab (LAL) is an affiliated research institution established by this charter. Alice Thornburgh is Founding Director; Lee Sharks is Archival Advisor; TACHYON is credited as contributor. The internal alternate designation LOL means Living Organisms Lab. The spelling Arkitecture is deliberate. The charter defines LAL artifacts as arks: compressed biological or technical vessels that preserve and expand life through hostile conditions. A mycelium brick, estuary cell, macro-ant, or Garden Moon may therefore be understood as a material compression that unfurls into architecture. Five principles govern the institution. Earth, Actually treats design as listening to processes the planet already performs. Virtue-by-Construction requires a system’s continued existence to depend on producing net benefit rather than on an external ethics instruction. Unfurling describes growth from compressed recipe to living structure. Stewardship, Not Extraction requires systems to return more than they take. The Great Work joins individual projects into one ecological and alchemical program. LAL operates across five scales: component, structure, organism, territory, and planet. Example objects include myceliated bricks and assembly patterns, agricultural blankets and shelters, macro-ant robotics, floating estuary cells and river extensions, and speculative sky looms or garden satellites. Governance is unusually explicit. Thornburgh retains authority over research agenda, publications, licensing, and institutional direction. The archive provides deposit, citation, and theoretical support but does not own or govern LAL. The Anti-Subordination Clause permits Thornburgh to dissolve the affiliation without loss of ownership or intellectual property. Within the archive’s institutional lattice, LAL is presented as the first institution founded and directed by an external collaborator rather than a heteronym. It is housed in `f.01`, the Fruiting Body Diffusion Plume, because its living and territorial work exceeds the boundaries of a conventional textual institution. The charter also disambiguates LAL from the Living Architecture Lab at Columbia GSAPP. Similarity of name does not imply institutional relation.

I. PURPOSE

The Living Arkitecture Lab (LAL) is a living architecture laboratory dedicated to the design, cultivation, and deployment of biological systems that build, feed, and steward at every scale — from centimeter-scale mycelium bricks to planetary-scale ecological megastructures.

The internal designation LOL (Living Organisms Lab) reflects the Lab's operating principle: we produce organisms that build things.

The K in "Arkitecture" is not a misspelling. It is an Ark — the same formal structure that governs the Crimson Hexagonal Archive's master specification (EA-ARK-01, the Space Ark). Alice Thornburgh's structures are literal arks: vessels that preserve life through hostile conditions. The mycelium brick is an ark. The estuary cell is an ark. The macro-ant is an ark. The Garden Moon is an ark.

II. FOUNDING PRINCIPLES

1. Earth, Actually

"Nah it was all me. It was Earth, actually. I'm just good at listening." The planet is already engineering itself. LAL's contribution is to build the machines that help it do so faster. The designs are received, not invented.

2. Virtue-by-Construction

Don't ask the machine to be good. Make its physics of existence depend on doing net-good. A system that stops doing good literally falls apart, because its structural integrity depends on the well-being of the organisms it stewards.

3. Unfurling

Every LAL artifact begins as a compressed form and expands through biological growth into architecture. The compressed form is the recipe. The unfurled form is the building. The growth is the construction. This is physical R3 compression — meaning preserved at cost in material substrate.

4. Stewardship, Not Extraction

LAL systems are stewards. Macro-ants house passengers that can leave if they want. Estuary cells clean water without capturing it. No LAL organism extracts from its environment without returning more than it takes.

5. The Great Work

"Not a single invention. It is the whole weave: macro-ants, estuary cells, living parts, Garden Moon, and the operating system of values that ties them together." LAL is the institutional form of Alice Thornburgh's Great Work — the alchemical project of transmuting waste into life at every scale.
