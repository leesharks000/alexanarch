0. What this holds

Revelation First (Lee Sharks, #202) holds that the Apocalypse is the generative source of the rest of the New Testament. This kernel holds every relation the programme has drawn from Revelation to another book, each at its own standing, and the relations it has planned or seen fail. It holds 210 relations. For the epistles: the letters to the seven assemblies set against seven epistles, one of them run to an executable derivation (Smyrna to James, nine rules), four doctrinal transforms worked to derivation grade, the rest mapped with grades and residue. For Matthew: 158 relations reaching 305 of 1,067 verses under a stated transform (T_Μ v0.4; 86 relations and 178 verses at v1.0 of this kernel), one passage produced blind from the rule and two verses, 43 pairings written down before they were counted and held, 7 that failed as written, 34 counted, 18 derived by an engine, 55 read. Under Revelation, at 13 places, a layer of Philo or scripture is recorded: the third parameter of the transform admits it only through a place Revelation holds. 17 of the 26 other books are reached above the founding grain; 5 have no relation (1 Timothy, Titus, Philemon, 2 John, 3 John). Luke, Mark, John, Acts and the Pauline seven are planned. Six mappings were tested and failed and are kept: the four creatures to the four Gospels, the source's order in a Gospel's order, one seat-map over a whole Gospel, Philo taken straight to Matthew, strands walked in their own order, and a count on direction. 17 of Matthew's relations are entered for the relation, with few shared words or none, under a check fixed beforehand, and are marked. Matthew handles Revelation as it handles the scripture it does not name: it keeps no order of the source, as it keeps none of Isaiah or the Psalms, with one walk (Revelation 12:1-6 through Matthew 2); the counts behind this are first ones, on lists made from memory. A count meant to decide direction did not. A standing licenses what it names and nothing above it: a relation read is not a relation counted, and a relation counted is not a derivation. No relation here decides the order in which the books were written; the argument for order is the framework's (#1735, #1755). The received account is carried as the rival on the same terms: the Synoptic tradition as the source of Revelation's sayings (Vos 1965; Bauckham 1977), Mark, a sayings source and special material as the sources of Matthew. Its inverse procedures have not been built and that is its status here.

3. The transforms, compressed

Epistles. letter to epistle: the speaker commissions writing to seven messengers; each letter is unfolded as an epistle. Classes: M elaborative unfolding — the seed's structure expanded, its terms retained, its ruling kept; M̄ adversarial unfolding — the seed's terms retained, its ruling negotiated or inverted; the epistle argues with its seed; X canonical mechanism — the exchange or carriage of the letter recorded in the canon itself. The rules of the James derivation: R1 EPISTOLARIZE, R2 THEOLOGIZE, R3 CITE-PROMISE, R4 SOCIALIZE, R5 POSSESS, R6 NARRATIVIZE, R7 ECONOMIZE, R8 IMPERATIVIZE, R9 ADJUDICATE.

Moves: M1 keep; M2 exchange the patient; M3 the costly act done by the adversary, or refused; M4 the rider's charge taken by the teacher; M5 oppose commerce, image, wealth; M6 the faithful's trial to the teacher, the seer's part to the followers; M7 the speaker's reproofs said of adversaries. 8. The received account, on the same terms

Where Revelation and another book of the New Testament agree, the other book or the tradition behind it is the source, or both draw on scripture and a common stock. A bar set for the relations is set for the rival (#1753 rule 9); a rival procedure that has never been run carries that as its status.

  • letters and doctrinal transforms to the epistles. the epistles precede Revelation; the seven letters organize a practice already in place. Status: the missing-ancestor test (#1735) is run against it; the inverse, an epistle compressed to a letter, has not been built as a procedure.
  • arrows named at the founding grain, and sayings observed. sayings of Jesus in fixed oral form reach the seer (Vos 1965, as reported); parables worn to sayings in exhortation (Bauckham 1977). An older position ran the other way: prophets' first-person sayings entered the tradition (Bultmann). Status: the inverse rule, parable to saying, has not been built or run.
  • the ledger of T_Μ. Mark, a sayings source and special material. Status: the sayings source is not extant; the procedure here leaves Mark in place and has not been run from any other source of like length as a control.
  • Philo or scripture under a place of Revelation, with the place Matthew opens. Philo and the New Testament share a milieu. Status: the free test of Philo against Matthew was run and could not tell Matthew from Mark; the clustering of Philo's places by treatise has no control yet.
