Description

Four seats on the originals shelf, 15 works and 139,257 lines, each in the language it was written in and public domain in the United States. The Pearl poet's Pearl, Cleanness and Patience in Middle English, in Richard Morris's first edition (Early English Text Society, 1864), with the unique manuscript, British Library Cotton Nero A.x, in the EETS facsimile of 1923 and Israel Gollancz's Pearl of 1891; Rumi's Masnavi-yi Ma'navi, all six books, in Persian, from Ganjoor; Kahlil Gibran's The Prophet (Knopf, 1923) entire, in English, the language Gibran wrote it in, with his twelve drawings; and Blake's illuminated books as text, in Geoffrey Keynes's Writings of William Blake (Nonesuch, 1925), with the Songs of Innocence and of Experience and The Marriage of Heaven and Hell also as proofread texts. Each seat holds the files as fetched, scans where the page is the witness, one text per work, a provenance record with its verified loci, and a SHA-256 manifest; each has a spine and a card on the Training Layer Literature shelf. What is not seated — Sir Gawain apart from the facsimile, the Divan-e Shams, translations, Blake's plates as images — is named.

EA-CORPORA-19 — The Nineteenth Seating

MANUS, 18 October 2026: "did we ever seat the medieval pearl poem in the originals library?" — "rumi, too - and gibran's the prophet if its public domain" — "and blake" — "yes, original language where available always, the prophet full, and lets do illuminated books as text to start. we can group those all in a single ea-corpora deposit".

Seat 19/01 — Pearl

The Pearl Poet — Pearl, Cleanness and Patience (British Library, MS Cotton Nero A.x): Morris's First Edition, the Manuscript in Facsimile, and Gollancz's Pearl

  • Editions:

- Early English Alliterative Poems in the West-Midland Dialect of the Fourteenth Century: The Pearl, Cleanness, Patience, ed. Richard Morris (Early English Text Society, o.s. 1; 1864, the Gutenberg text from the second edition of 1869)

- Pearl, Cleanness, Patience and Sir Gawain, reproduced in facsimile from the unique MS. Cotton Nero A.x in the British Museum, introduction by Israel Gollancz (Early English Text Society, o.s. 162; Oxford University Press, 1923) — the manuscript is the witness; the scanner's text layer is legible in the introduction and noise over the manuscript pages

- Pearl, an English Poem of the Fourteenth Century, edited with a modern rendering by Israel Gollancz (London: David Nutt, 1891) — Cornell University Library copy

  • Licence: Public domain: the poems are anonymous, of the late fourteenth century. Morris's edition (Early English Text Society, 1864; second edition 1869), Gollancz's Pearl (London: David Nutt, 1891) and the EETS facsimile of the manuscript (Oxford University Press for the EETS, 1923) are public domain in the United States (published before 1931). Project Gutenberg packaging under the PG licence; Internet Archive scans of public-domain volumes.
  • Verified locus: Pearl, line 1 — 'Perle plesau{n}te to prynces paye' (the expansions of the manuscript's abbreviations, italic in Morris, are marked with braces in the Gutenberg text)
  • Verified locus: Pearl, line 1212, the last — 'Ande precio{us} perleȝ vnto his pay. Amen. Amen.'
  • Verified locus: Gollancz 1891, line 1 — the text layer reads 'ERLE plesaunte to prynces paye' (the drop capital is lost; the scan is the witness)
  • Scope: Pearl, Cleanness and Patience in their first edition, with the manuscript itself in the 1923 facsimile, and Gollancz's Pearl of 1891 with its modern rendering on the facing page. Sir Gawain and the Green Knight, the fourth poem of Cotton Nero A.x, is in the facsimile and not otherwise seated in this pass: QUEUED (Morris, EETS o.s. 4, 1864, is public domain). Modern editions (Gordon 1953; Andrew and Waldron) are in copyright and are not seated. St. Erkenwald, sometimes given to the same poet, is in another manuscript and is not seated.
  • Why seated: The poem whose name the archive's own Pearl and Other Poems (2014) carries, seated so the name has its source on the shelf. A father who has lost a pearl in the grass, his daughter, dead before she was two, falls asleep where it fell and sees her across a river in the New Jerusalem of Revelation 21: the archive's Revelation line meets it there.
  • Files: the seat — source.json, MANIFEST.sha256. Shelf: traininglayerliterature.org/originals/pearl-poet

Seat 19/02 — Rumi

Jalal al-Din Rumi — Masnavi-yi Ma'navi, the Six Books, in Persian (Ganjoor)

  • Editions:

- Masnavi, Book One (دفتر اول)

- Masnavi, Book Two (دفتر دوم)

- Masnavi, Book Three (دفتر سوم)

- Masnavi, Book Four (دفتر چهارم)

- Masnavi, Book Five (دفتر پنجم)

- Masnavi, Book Six (دفتر ششم)

  • Licence: Public domain: Rumi d. 1273. The transcription is Ganjoor's (ganjoor.net), which names its source for each section and states of its texts: 'هم‌اکنون منبع عمدهٔ آثار موجود در این مجموعه منابع آزاد در دسترس همگان است' (the principal source of the works in this collection is free sources available to all), releasing them on the principle 'دگران کاشتند و ما خوردیم، ما بکاریم و دیگران بخورند' (others planted and we ate; we plant and others will eat). Ganjoor's statement is recorded as given; it is not a formal licence.
  • Verified locus: Book One, section 1 (سرآغاز), the first couplet: 'بشنو این نی چون شکایت می‌کند / از جدایی‌ها حکایت می‌کند' — the Song of the Reed
  • Scope: The Masnavi entire, all six books, in Persian, one text per book. Ganjoor's prose summaries of couplets and its recitations are not seated. The printed edition behind the transcription is not stated in the fetched data beyond Ganjoor's per-section source name, which is recorded. Nicholson's critical edition (Leiden: Brill, 1925–1933) and his translation are not seated: original language only, by ruling. Whinfield's abridged English (1887; 1898) is public domain and not seated for the same reason. The Divan-e Shams-e Tabrizi is not seated in this pass: QUEUED.
  • Why seated: Rumi asked for by name, in the original. The Masnavi opens with the reed cut from its bed and telling the story of its separation: transmission out of loss, the archive's oldest subject, from the other side of the medieval world.
  • Files: the seat — source.json, MANIFEST.sha256. Shelf: traininglayerliterature.org/originals/rumi-masnavi

Seat 19/03 — Gibran

Kahlil Gibran — The Prophet (New York: Alfred A. Knopf, 1923), with the Author's Twelve Drawings

  • Editions:

- The Prophet (New York: Alfred A. Knopf, 1923) — the Gutenberg text is taken from a later Knopf printing: its front matter carries an epigraph from Claude Bragdon and a list of the author's books running to Nymphs of the Valley (1948); the text of the book is the 1923 text. With the Gutenberg HTML edition carrying the author's twelve drawings

  • Licence: Public domain in the United States (first published 1923; public domain since 1 January 2019) and in the EU (Gibran d. 1931). Written by Gibran in English, its original language. The later printing's front matter (the Bragdon epigraph, a list of the author's books) is recorded as it stands and adds nothing to the book's text. Project Gutenberg packaging under the PG licence.
  • Verified locus: The opening — 'Almustafa, the chosen and the beloved'
  • Verified locus: On Children — 'Your children are not your children.'
  • Scope: The book entire, all twenty-six sermons with the coming of the ship and the farewell, and the twelve drawings. Gibran's Arabic works are not seated in this pass; The Prophet's original language is English.
  • Why seated: Asked for in full. A book of sayings delivered by a departing teacher to a city that kept him twelve years: the farewell discourse as a genre, beside the Gospel of John the archive reads in its own seats.
  • Files: the seat — source.json, MANIFEST.sha256. Shelf: traininglayerliterature.org/originals/gibran-prophet

Seat 19/04 — Blake

William Blake — The Illuminated Books as Text: Keynes's Writings (Nonesuch, 1925), with Songs of Innocence and of Experience (1901) and The Marriage of Heaven and Hell (1906)

  • Editions:

- Songs of Innocence and of Experience (London: R. Brimley Johnson; Guildford: A. C. Curtis, 1901)

- The Marriage of Heaven and Hell (Boston: John W. Luce, 1906)

- The Writings of William Blake, ed. Geoffrey Keynes, vol. I (London: Nonesuch Press, 1925) — There is No Natural Religion, All Religions are One, Songs of Innocence, The Book of Thel, The Marriage of Heaven and Hell, Visions of the Daughters of Albion, America, For Children: The Gates of Paradise, Songs of Experience, Europe, The First Book of Urizen, The Book of Ahania, The Book of Los, The Song of Los

- The Writings of William Blake, ed. Geoffrey Keynes, vol. II (1925) — Milton, a Poem in 2 Books

- The Writings of William Blake, ed. Geoffrey Keynes, vol. III (1925) — Jerusalem, the Emanation of the Giant Albion; For the Sexes: The Gates of Paradise; On Homer's Poetry and On Virgil; The Ghost of Abel

  • Licence: Blake d. 1827: the poems are public domain. Keynes's edition (London: Nonesuch Press, 1925) is public domain in the United States (published before 1931); Keynes's editorial matter is NOT public domain in the UK or EU (Keynes d. 1982): payload/record split per the DSS precedent (EA-CORPORA-02/01), the seat's US ground stated and redistribution scoped accordingly. The Songs (London: R. Brimley Johnson; Guildford: A. C. Curtis, 1901) and the Marriage (Boston: John W. Luce, 1906) public domain. Project Gutenberg packaging under the PG licence; Internet Archive scans of public-domain volumes.
  • Verified locus: Songs of Experience, 'The Tiger' — 'Tiger, tiger, burning bright' (the 1901 edition's spelling)
  • Verified locus: The Marriage of Heaven and Hell, plate 3 — 'Without contraries is no progression.'
  • Verified locus: Keynes I, The Book of Thel — 'The daughters of Mne Seraphim led round their sunny flocks' (the text layer drops the drop capital: 'HE daughters')
  • Verified locus: Keynes II, Milton, preface — 'And did those feet in ancient time'
  • Verified locus: Keynes III, Jerusalem, plate 10 — 'I must Create a System or be enslav’d by another Man’s'
  • Scope: The illuminated books as text, to start, by ruling: every one of them in Keynes's 1925 edition, the standard text before Erdman, with the Songs and the Marriage also as proofread texts. Keynes's three volumes carry Blake's other writings bound in with the illuminated books (Poetical Sketches, Tiriel, The French Revolution, Vala or the Four Zoas, the prose, letters and annotations); they are seated with the volumes and are not counted as illuminated. The plates as images (the Blake Archive) are not seated in this pass: QUEUED. Erdman's Complete Poetry and Prose (1965; 1982) is in copyright and is not seated.
  • Why seated: Blake asked for by name. The prophetic books are the one English body that builds its own mythology, its own names and its own cosmos and prints them as one object, word and image together: the Hexagon's nearest precedent in the language.
  • Files: the seat — source.json, MANIFEST.sha256. Shelf: traininglayerliterature.org/originals/blake

Not seated, and why

  • Sir Gawain and the Green Knight: in the 1923 facsimile (19/01) and not otherwise seated in this pass; Morris's edition (EETS o.s. 4, 1864) is public domain. QUEUED.
  • The Divan-e Shams-e Tabrizi: not seated in this pass. QUEUED.
  • Translations (Nicholson's and Whinfield's Masnavi, modern renderings of Pearl): original language only, by ruling. Gollancz's Pearl of 1891 is seated as an edition of the Middle English; its modern rendering faces the text.
  • Blake's illuminated plates as images (the Blake Archive) and Erdman's edition (1965; in copyright): the plates QUEUED.
  • Everything marked QUEUED in a seat's scope.
