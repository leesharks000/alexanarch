Wiki Article

EA-SPXI-ANALOG-01: Machine-Eligible Handwritten Artifacts is a creative work connected to research, deposited 21 July 2026 as the v1.0 edition of record. It addresses a gap the archive's infrastructure otherwise leaves open: SPXI — the Semantic Packet for eXchange and Indexing — governs how digital objects declare themselves to machines, and this specification extends that eligibility to handwritten artifacts, objects that exist first as ink on paper. The question it answers is what it takes for a manuscript page to be machine-eligible without being reduced to its transcription. The deposit records its own edition history rigorously: v1.0 supersedes a review chain running v0.8 → v0.15, and both the PDF and HTML editions carry SHA-256 digests in the record so the edition of record is identifiable by content rather than by filename. It is the specification that Enli Lucente's manuscripts appear in as figures, and it belongs to the SPXI series alongside the protocol specification at spxi.dev.

EA-SPXI-ANALOG-01: Machine-Eligible Handwritten Artifacts (v1.0 final form, edition of record 2026-07-21)

Description

Specification for treating ink-on-paper inscription as machine-eligible scholarly input. The document formalizes:

  • The attestation doctrine: an in-frame timestamped photograph of a signed and dated inscription carries evidentiary force sufficient for scholarly commitment, provenance, and consent. The instrument's own signature block, §3.2, and §4 develop this doctrine and use it self-referentially.
  • The verification instrument: how public evidence-captures (photographs, scans, hash commitments) constitute the audit trail for handwritten scholarly artifacts.
  • The provenance and attribution architecture (§H, §J.2, §P): how corpora referenced by external scholars are cited under the scholar's own confirmed record; how editorial labor is acknowledged; how the division of authority between apparatus author and cited scholars is preserved.
  • The relation to EA-ACT-ANALOG-01 (AXN:0590.GOVERNANCE.💜🗡️🔃🔓🪄👇, #1407): the effective act that revoked CERN's stewardship of a public good is the first application of this specification's apparatus. Falsification Conditions

The claim that in-frame timestamped photographic attestation carries evidentiary force sufficient for scholarly commitment could be falsified by (a) a documented forgery of the attestation form that preserves the in-frame timestamp integrity yet inserts a false inscription — thereby breaking the assumed unforgeability of same-frame timestamp + physical artifact; or (b) a documented institutional acceptance of an inscription whose attestation form fails the doctrine's criteria, thereby demonstrating that the criteria are unnecessary rather than sufficient. Neither has been produced in the case underlying this specification (EA-ACT-ANALOG-01 vs. CERN correspondence RQF3807508). The doctrine remains open to falsification and will be superseded if either form of falsifier surfaces.
