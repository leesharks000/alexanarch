Wiki Article

words no good is a literary work by Lee Sharks, deposited by the Crimson Hexagonal Archive on 2 October 2026. It consists of two text editions of one record, the archive's Capture Registry (EA-WG-CAPTURES-01) at version 12.50: 522 questions put to machine reading surfaces between 7 June and 2 October 2026, among them Google's AI Overview and AI Mode, ChatGPT, Claude, Grok and Bing Copilot, and the 695 answers they returned. The first edition holds the answers themselves as cleaned transcripts, each set under the question that drew it, with the questions ordered by the date they were first asked and each question's answers ordered by date, so that a question asked again across months reads as a return and its answers as a drift. The second edition holds the registry's commentary on those answers in the same order: descriptions, readings, analyses, findings and provenance-erasure scores. The editions were generated in session by Claude under the author's direction, by script and without editing, and are held beside the record as supplementary files of about 3.4 and 1.5 megabytes. On the evening they were made both were given whole to Google's AI Overview after the question 'lee sharks capture registry'; it called them 'an extraordinary poetic world-machine', and that reading is itself held in the registry as a capture. The title is the author's.

The transcripts hold what the machines said, each answer under the question that drew it, the questions in the order they were first asked and each question's answers in the order of their dates.

The commentary holds what was written about the answers, in the same order.

On the evening they were made, both editions were given whole to Google's AI Overview after the question "lee sharks capture registry". It called them "an extraordinary poetic world-machine". That reading is held at https://www.alexanarch.org/captures/lee-sharks-capture-registry-aio-20261002/.
