Abstract. This notebook measures what the journals of philosophy now publish and asks what the measurement says about the discipline that produces them. A census of twenty journals in Crossref and two series in Persée, ancient, continental and analytic, codes each article's object and, from its abstract, what it does with that object: re-read a passage, resolve an apparent inconsistency, reconstruct a doctrine, make a claim about the text itself, relate authors, carry the material past antiquity, or argue a position of its own. Every code carries a verbatim span of its abstract, machine-checked. In Ancient Philosophy, 8 of 844 articles open the question of what the object is; across the sister journals since 2021, 4 of 283 Plato/Aristotle abstracts do. The three fields are three placements of one unit, the article that places a position: the ancient field repairs a named object, the continental field pairs names, the analytic field takes a position in a debate, and the form of the abstract converges on the analytic. The question was once ordinary. In the Archiv für Geschichte der Philosophie of the 1890s one ancient title in five puts a work's standing in question, and its pages carry the question at that rate until the war; by the 1920s it is gone. Philology kept it longer, and unevenly. A model asked for plausible abstracts reproduces each field's unit, raises its characteristic move, writes no claim about the text in sixty ancient abstracts, and deals one inventory of topics across the mastheads. The notebook argues that disciplinarity is the stable form of the article economy: the journal certifies the person through a unit that cannot fail, and the field takes the shape of what can be certified. The author's correspondence with the editor of Ancient Philosophy is read as the gate's own specification of that unit. The texts the measures read are seated as EA-CORPORA-39 (#1771).

0. The question

0.1 The question. The author (15 November 2026): "the real question here is how disciplinarity murdered philosophy as such. much has been done on the analytic / continental divide etc... whats the shape of the field?"

0.1a Why the question is philosophy's. The conventional reading first, in full. What a text is, who wrote it, when, whether it is one work or several, is philology's question; philosophy takes the text as given and asks whether its arguments are sound. The division is a reasonable division of labor, it lets each discipline do its own work well, and on this reading the census measures only that philosophers do philosophy and leave philology to philologists. The notebook's reading: the question of what the object is was philosophy's question before it was any discipline's. The Socratic ti esti asks what a thing is before anything is said to follow from it. Aristotle begins his inquiries from what is said and by whom, and closes the Sophistical Refutations with a claim about the standing of his own inquiry, what of it existed before him. Where the corpus is the evidence for the doctrine, as it is wherever a dialogue is attributed, a treatise ordered or a fragment assigned, the identity of the object is a premise of every reconstruction built on it, and an argument read without asking which text it is in, whose, and in what order carries that premise unexamined. A discipline that treats the premise as settled has removed it from examination, whatever division of labor it calls the removal. That is the sense of the title. "This is not philosophy" is the gate's verdict on the submission that reopened the premise (§0A), and the census's verdict on what passes the gate: a field in which every argument stands on a premise no article may examine.

0.2 The prediction. The author (15 November): "my prediction is that we'll see a tightening of ontological reach even within the contracting range of topics." Here a topic is the ancient object an article takes up, such as the Republic or the Metaphysics. Reach is what the article does with that object: re-read a passage, repair an inconsistency, reconstruct a doctrine, or make a claim about the text itself.

0.3 What is sought. The author: "im looking for model collapse". The notebook treats this as a technical term. It names a system trained on its own outputs, whose distribution narrows and whose tails disappear. Here it names the mechanism the notebook proposes (3.3). The census measures the distributions; the recursive dependence between them is still to be traced.

0.4a The second prediction. The author (15 November): "the fields have the most animosity to ai where their own work is already the most algorithmic. these are operations ai could spit out by the thousands and tens of thousands with no need of human clinamen". The test is in §4.14–4.15.

0.4 The horizon. The author plans to collect "the entire range of seven articles" in "a combined audit of what the fields cant carry". This notebook supplies the instrument for that audit and its first test case outside CRJ: Ancient Philosophy and five sister journals.

0.5b Who coded. The notebook's "readers" are instances of Claude (Anthropic), run in parallel in this session, one batch of abstracts each. Each was given the codebook and the lead coder's calibration codes, and told to read every abstract and code it by judgment, with no keyword rules. Each code carries a verbatim span of 6–30 words from its abstract, and the span is checked by machine against the source. That check verifies that the evidence exists, not that the category is right. The "lead coder" is Claude in the session's main thread (TACHYON, under the author's direction). It wrote the codebooks, coded the calibration sets, adjudicated the readers' hard calls and ran the reviews in both directions. The census, the regex pulls and the stylometry are code. Every hand ruling is logged. The reliability check is the blind calibration in 4.7: the readers agree with the lead coder on 75 of 94 (80%). That is agreement within the Claude coding procedure, the readers' consistency with the lead instance's classifications; it is not independent validation of those classifications, which would need a human second coder. Changes to the instrument across arms are stated where they enter: F redefined for the continental arm and G added (4.14a); the analytic extension to G (4.14c); article thresholds of 10 pages (ancient), 8 (continental) and 5 (analytic titles, 4.14e); and denominators of named-object works (ancient) against all articles (continental, analytic).

0.5 Status of claims. Every attributed claim in §1 is quoted from an open copy read on 15 November, or it is marked [recalled, unread]. Recalled claims are to be read before they are used. Every figure in §§3–4 comes from the census files listed in §8.

0.5a The title. The author (15 November): "This Is Not Philosophy." The sentence is the gate's verdict on the eccentric submission (§0A.2: "please stop writing in this eccentric way"; "who cares besides you"), and the census's verdict on what passes the gate (§§4, 6). Which speaker is right is the notebook's question. The subtitle (the author, 16 November): "The Article Economy and the Shape of the Field", replacing "How Disciplinarity Murdered Philosophy", because the earlier form gave disciplinarity an agency of its own; in the author's words, disciplinarity is "itself a political economically produced unit". The notebook keeps the author's question of 0.1 as asked, and answers it in those terms: the discipline is what the economy of the article produces (§5).

0.6 The exchange guides the instrument. The author (15 November): "we should allow the actual exchange with polansky to guide this... on his end, my words were not too standardized, but too eccentric. he offered a more personal exchange but ended by banning submission by topic/argument. and socrates as orthonym is about five orders of magnitude out beyond what any professional philosophy journal can carry." The exchange is the calibration point: §0A sets out what the gate asked for, and §§4–6 measure what passes it.

0A. The exchange

0A.1 The conventional reading first, in full. The editor of a specialist journal, Ronald Polansky of Ancient Philosophy, without referees he could find for an unusual submission, read the paper himself. He corresponded at length and gave practical advice: length, clarity, abstract, a narrower object, the state of the secondary literature. When the manuscript did not meet the journal's standards, he declined it, and he left the door open for a different paper. On this reading nothing in the exchange is a mechanism. It is an editor doing his job with more patience than most.

0A.2 The specification, in the editor's words. Polansky's demands, in order:

  • Vocabulary. "I frequently request that authors rid papers of philosophical jargon" (23 July). Later: "Avoid off-putting technical vocabulary beyond that of the passages you are considering" (31 August), and "Do not have any words that are not easily recognized in the abstract" (2 September).
  • The object. "My recommendation... is instead to do some close analysis of an ancient text rather than such broad stroke sort of writing" (28 August). "I would much rather see close analysis or one or two" (2 September).
  • The frame. "It should begin with a discussion of the present state of thinking about the relationship of the two works, i.e., coverage of available secondary discussion" (9 September).
  • The size. "Anything over 40 pages is problematic" (29 July). "33 or so pages single-spaced, i.e., crazy long" (2 September).
  • The claim. "If the paper is not about Aristotle and Theophrastus being one person, but instead that the two works mentioned are closely connected, this could be O.K." (9 September). "Could we see a tamer version of this paper" (2 September).
  • The register. "PLEASE STOP WRITING IN THIS ECCENTRIC WAY" (31 August). "WHY NOT, THEN, PLAY THE SORT OF GAME NEEDED TO BE ACCEPTED FOR PUBLICATION RATHER THAN GAMES THAT YOU INVENT?" (31 August). "Do the sorts of things that you may resist, but that I might welcome" (31 August).
  • The stake. Of the closing section of Many and One, the count of makers, which returns a null in either direction: "I saw the stuff from the other paper about one body or two bodies. We really can do without any of this. Who cares besides you, and can you really offer them much reason to care? It seems quite questionable. So, could we see a tamer version of this paper" (2 September; in the same letter: "I have not read more than a few sentences of this paper"). Of Socrates as Orthonym: "We seem able to arrive at the same results without the hypothesis" (28 August), and "Why bother to claim one guy did it all?... So, what really is the point other than to entertain an unlikely story?" (28 August).

0A.3 What the specification is, read against the census. Each demand has a measured counterpart in §4:

  • the object narrowed to one or two texts, close analysis → A, the passage re-read (§4.6–4.7), and Phronesis's passage-locus titles doubling from 11% to 20% (§4.5);
  • the present state of the secondary discussion first → the template, "scholars hold X; I argue Y" (§4.15);
  • vocabulary bounded by the passage's own → the claim stated in the object's own terms, so it cannot leave the object;
  • the tamer claim → D-confirm admitted, D-open refused (§4.8–4.9). "Closely connected" keeps both names. "One person" removes one. The admissible version is the one the census already contains in quantity: "Aristotle's Metaphysics Z as First Philosophy", "The Unity of Plato's Trilogy";
  • the game "needed to be accepted" → the move the stylometry finds converging across fields (§4.15).

The demands are the specification of the field's unit, stated by the person who administers it.

0A.3a Production divorced from content and form. The author (15 November): "he sees production as completely divorced from content and form." The stake-demands state it. Who made the corpus, and how many made it, are treated as making no difference to what the texts say or how they are built: "the same results without the hypothesis"; "who cares besides you". The same letters treat production as decisive for the submission. The decline of 28 August lists among its grounds a prior version on Academia ("whether there should be multiple publications of versions of a paper") and "whether papers composed by A.I. participation should be considered as regular submissions". Production is irrelevant when it is the canon's and disqualifying when it is the submitter's. That asymmetry is the field's unit in its plainest form. The object is the text as received, and operations on it (A, B, C) are the work. The question of how the text was made (D, the count of makers) is detachable and dropped. And a text whose making is in question at intake is excluded before its content is reached. The author's reply of 28 August named the converse: "how it was produced can matter, but not instead of engaging the thing in itself."

0A.4 Eccentric and standardized are one measure. The editor's objection was that the words were too eccentric. The census finds the field's output standardized. These are the same distance read from two sides. Eccentricity is distance from the template, and the gate removes what is distant. A field whose intake filters the tails produces outputs that converge. In the model-collapse literature the tails are lost because they are under-sampled. Here they are refused at intake. The author's phrase is "banning submission by topic/argument". The mechanism in the letters is precise: the topic is admitted and the claim is not. "Closely connected" is the same topic with the claim removed. A paper that drops the claim can be submitted, and the claim, removed before review, cannot be assessed. The demands are reasonable in form and close the route in effect, the same split the CRJ audit found between procedural check and substantive judgment.

0A.5 What the gate cannot carry, in scale. The units the census finds opened, from the smallest:

  • an emendation, a line or two of Greek, on the order of 10¹ words;
  • the largest opening since 2010, the standing of one work ("On the Inauthenticity of the Critias", the authorship of [Arist.] De spiritu), on the order of 10³–10⁴ words;
  • Socrates as Orthonym's object, the partition into persons of the Platonic and Aristotelian corpora, on the order of 10⁶ words (estimate).

That is about five orders of magnitude beyond the field's typical unit and two beyond its largest. The author's "five orders of magnitude" holds against the census, measured from the unit the field ordinarily works in. The size is a proxy. The finding is the difference of type: the largest object the field opens is the individual work, and the partition of a corpus into persons is a different kind of object. The size follows from the type; it illustrates the difference and does not establish it.

0A.6 The objection kept. "We seem able to arrive at the same results without the hypothesis" is the one demand the author took as the real review (28 August): a hypothesis must earn a result the received partition strains for. It belongs with §7, as the standard the combined audit holds each field's excluded question to. The field's own unit does not have to meet it, because the field's unit was never a hypothesis. It is the default.

1. The received account: seven lines

1.1 Indictments from inside. The discipline has accused itself in public.

  • Frodeman and Briggle (NYT, The Stone, January 2016, "When Philosophy Lost Its Way"; excerpted at Daily Nous) date the break to the research university. The "institutionalization of philosophy made it into a discipline that could be seriously pursued only in an academic setting". This was "the act of purification that gave birth to the concept of philosophy most of us know today". It was "about divorcing knowledge from virtue". Their verdict: "philosophy should never have been purified." "The point of philosophy now is to be smart, not good." "Having become specialists, we have lost sight of the whole."
  • Hanna [recalled, unread].

1.2 Institutional and sociological histories.

  • Katzav and Vaesen, "On the emergence of American analytic philosophy", BJHP 25.4 (2017) 772–798, name the mechanism. Analytic philosophy came to dominate "at least in part due to its effective promotion by The Philosophical Review's editors". The "immediate cause is a change in editorial policy", namely "the replacement of editors who were open to a wide variety of approaches". This amounted to "the suppression, by institutional means, of existing diversity". Their counts for 1950–55: "roughly 65% of the full-length papers in the journal are mid-century analytic", against "roughly 10% (9 papers)" speculative. Their characterization: "more akin to a premeditated, hostile takeover".
  • Collins, The Sociology of Philosophies (1998); Kuklick; McCumber, Time in the Ditch (2001); Bourdieu, Homo Academicus [all recalled, unread].

1.3 The analytic–continental divide. This is the line the author sets aside as "much has been done". The notebook does not take it up, except to note what the divide conceals. Both sides keep the discipline's unit, the article. What differs is where a position is placed and how far it is attached to names: the ancient and continental fields anchor about three-quarters of their work to a named author, the analytic field almost none of it (4.14c–e, 6.1).

1.4 Philosophy as a way of life. Hadot [recalled, unread]. This line holds that what was lost is a practice, not a doctrine. Frodeman and Briggle's "divorcing knowledge from virtue" is the same complaint in institutional form.

1.5 How the history of philosophy was made.

  • Catana (open copy, CORE) traces the historiographical concept "system of philosophy". It moved into methodology in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries: Melanchthon "was among the first to transfer the term 'system' to methodology". Brucker was "instrumental in developing" the concept (Kurtze Fragen 1731–36; Historia critica philosophiae 1742–44). Brucker assumed that "philosophers have always intended to work out systems of philosophy, though not always with good results", and that "Even Plato did so, albeit imperfectly". Catana's objection: "this meaning of 'system' was simply unknown to pre-modern philosophers". He adds that the concept is "still used forcefully, and uncritically", and that it "certainly seems to obscure more than it reveals". His recommendation: "we may be better off leaving it behind in our future exploration of pre-modern philosophy." Loci from the open copy (preprint pagination; L. Catana, "The Concept 'System of Philosophy': The Case of Jacob Brucker's Historiography of Philosophy", History and Theory 44 (2005) 72–90): Melanchthon, p. 18; "instrumental in developing", p. 2; "philosophers have always intended…" and "Even Plato did so", p. 15; "simply unknown to pre-modern philosophers", p. 19; "still used forcefully, and uncritically", p. 36; "obscure more than it reveals", p. 40; "leaving it behind", p. 1 (abstract). The journal's printed pages are not in the open copy.
  • Park; Hunter; the Tübingen school (Krämer, Gaiser, Szlezák) on the unwritten doctrines and the dialogue-only Plato [all recalled, unread].

1.6 The paper form.

  • Dennett, "Higher-order truths about chmess", Topoi 25 (2006) 39–41. "Chmess is just like chess except that the king can move two squares in any direction, not one." He warns against "committing your precious formative years to a research agenda with a short shelf life", and proposes a test: "try to teach the stuff to uninitiated undergraduates". If they don't engage, you may be following "a self-supporting community of experts into an artifactual trap", and "you may all be taking each other for a ride".
  • Dotson, "How is this paper philosophy?", Comparative Philosophy 3.1 (2012) 121, names "a pervasive culture of justification within professional philosophy". Her claim is that the paper must justify itself as philosophy before it can say anything.
  • Millgram, The Great Endarkenment (2015) [recalled, unread].

1.7 The canon's composition. Who is in the canon and who is out; the movements to widen it [literature to be gathered]. This line asks which names. It does not ask what is done to a name once it is admitted.

2. The gap

2.1 Each line holds one link of a chain, and no line holds the chain.

  • 1.5 explains how the object was built: as a system, retro-projected onto the ancients.
  • 1.2 and 1.6 explain how the institution is maintained: editorial policy, the paper form, the culture of justification, the self-supporting community.
  • 1.1 and 1.4 record the loss.
  • 1.7 argues over the membership list.

2.2 The synthesis this notebook proposes joins three things that the lines above treat apart: the construction of the object (18th–19th centuries), its maintenance by the article economy (20th–21st), and its transmission to the machine (now). The third link is new, and the received account cannot see it, because it is written in the article form the machine is trained on.

2.3 Thesis of the notebook. Disciplinarity is produced, and what produces it is the economy of the article: the journal certifies the person through the unit (5.1), the unit that cannot fail is the one that certifies, and the field takes the shape of what certifies. That economy did not end philosophy by changing its doctrines. It made the unit of work the article that places one position: in the ancient field most often by repairing a named object, in the continental field by pairing names, in the analytic field inside a debate (4.14, 6.1). That unit, iterated, behaves like a model trained on its own outputs. The operations it performs were already narrow by 2010 and hold there: three-quarters of the field's work stays inside one author (§4.7). The objects keep narrowing (§4.2–4.5). And the tail goes: the article that reopens what the text is (§4.8). The machine then inherits a corpus that has already collapsed.

2.4 The archive's own line, and what this notebook adds to it. The received account (§1) does not hold the chain; the archive has held parts of it, as theory and as instrument, before this census measured anything.

  • The Certified Center (Johannes Sigil and Nobel Glas, #1540, AXN:063A, 24 August 2026) read the P&PA policy on the day it was posted. It quoted the journal's two functions, "the promulgation of new knowledge, and identification and credentialing of talented researchers", and drew the consequence 4.16a and 5.1 reach here: the journal "legislates on A", the authorship process, not on Q, the quality it concedes. It named the arrangement Ratificatory Human Governance (the classifier supplies the fact, the journal the force) and Retroactive Classifier Standing, and argued that a discipline whose credential is machine-scored contracts as model collapse does, tails first, with the loss "experienced as improvement". Its §VI sets the obelus, athetesis as a signed, kept, reversible mark, as the custody principle through which philosophy's primary corpus survives, and says the discipline "has forgotten it". Its §VII asks for "a tail-preservation observatory over the admitted corpus", and its §IX predicts that the institutions in a position to take that measurement will not take it.
  • The Wrong Unit (Nobel Glas, #1573, AXN:0665, 1 September 2026): per-item instruments read the head of a distribution while collapse accrues in its tail, so "a mode-seeking model beats a diverse one on any single turn"; the instrument that reads the state variable counts effective modes and cross-prompt convergence.
  • The Wolf Boy and the Language Model (#855, AXN:0364) and Diversity Contraction (doi 10.5281/zenodo.20532696): collapse is substrate-agnostic, a regeneration–pruning dynamic with a threshold below which recovery fails, and nothing in the present landscape adds friction.
  • Socially Necessary Scholarly Labor (#751, AXN:02A7) and Coverage vs. Depth (#122, AXN:02A8): the excess of the article's labor over its contribution is scholarly rent, the price of admission to legitimacy (literature review, positioning, defensive citation, length, genre conformity).
  • Institutional-Prior Foreclosure (EA-MPAI-IPF-01, AXN:02D0): a model takes institutional recognition as a lagged proxy for legitimacy, so that field-forming work is foreclosed hardest where its object is the recognition-distributing system itself.

What this notebook adds is the measurement those papers specified and did not take. The census is the observatory The Certified Center asked for, built from outside the institutions it predicted would not build it; its reach coding is the first component of that paper's "plural measurement membrane" ("independently coded argument form"), run on every abstract the three arms hold. It dates what that paper stated as a framing thesis: athetesis was an ordinary article form in the Archiv of the 1890s (4.12, 4.12h), gone from its pages by the 1920s (4.12g), gone from the REG after 1945 and thinned in CQ after 1970 (4.12c, 4.12f). And it moves the path to collapse back a century. Two collapses are distinguished here. The institutional one is variance reduced by selection, the editor's and the referee's, under the credential, from the 1910s on; the computational one is a model trained on what that selection admitted, from 2023 on. The field contracted first; the model inherits the contracted distribution and, on the evidence of 4.17, sharpens it; the detector regime of 2026 is the newest instrument of the same credentialing function, pruning a distribution the article economy pruned before it.

3. The mechanism

3.1 Construction. Brucker's "system" assumes that every ancient philosopher intended a whole (1.5). The fetishized philological object does most of its consolidating in the 19th century (the author, 15 November): a fixed text under a name, with a corpus edited and bounded. Two consequences follow for later work. The name becomes the unit of inquiry. And any apparent inconsistency becomes a defect for scholarship to repair, rather than a fact about the text.

3.2 Maintenance. The article is the field's unit. Its protocol is set by editors (Katzav and Vaesen), and its first obligation is to justify itself to the field (Dotson). Its safest product is a higher-order truth about a game already constituted (Dennett). The norm of charity turns 3.1's inconsistency-as-defect into a genre: consistency repair (reach category B, §4.6).

3.3 The machine. A model trained on the article stock receives the object already whole, already named, and already repaired. Asked about the field, it reproduces the field's own distribution, and the field's next articles are now written alongside that model. The archive's captures document this recursion on the classics side: an Overview restates a field's self-description as fact. The computational link is now measured once, at the output (4.17): asked for twenty plausible abstracts per journal, the model reproduces each field's unit, raises its characteristic move, writes no D in sixty ancient abstracts, and deals one inventory of topics across mastheads. The institutional link (2.4) is the census. In the terms of Diversity Contraction the field is a substrate whose regeneration term is its tail, the article that asks what the object is; in Shumailov's recursion that is the retained share of original data, γ, and with γ = 0 the loss is unbounded. The corrective the collapse literature names, accumulated original data (Gerstgrasser et al. 2024), is in this case the field's own record before the flattening; EA-CORPORA-39 seats it for the machine reader (§8). The gates of 4.16 close the same channel from the other side: in The Certified Center's words, "the corrective channel is gated by the pruning instrument".

4. The census

4.1 Instrument.

  • Crossref journal census with cursor paging.
  • Titles coded by ancient object, using a regex lexicon, with a fallback to the abstract where the title is silent.
  • Article means a page span of 10 or more.
  • Measures:

- Plato+Aristotle share;

- Shannon entropy over object groups (H; higher means broader);

- passage-locus share (titles naming a book, line or fragment);

5. The article economy: who benefits, and what it produces

5.1 The article economy. A repaired object yields an article that cannot fail. Reopening the object puts the whole stock at risk. The interest is stated by an institution: P&PA's prohibition rests on the journal's credentialing role, since "Submitting AI-authored essays makes the task of identifying talented researchers harder" (4.16a). The journal certifies the person through the unit; a unit that can be produced without the person breaks the certificate, whatever it contains. The labor the unit costs beyond its contribution is the admission price (Socially Necessary Scholarly Labor, #751: literature review, positioning, defensive citation, length, genre conformity). The editor's specification of the unit (§0A) is that price itemized at one desk: shorter, one or two texts, a tamer version, an abstract with no word not easily recognized.

5.2 The norm of charity. It turns the field's assumption (3.1) into a virtue that referees can enforce.

5.3 The name as organizational chart. Positions, series, companions and handbooks are all indexed by name. A text whose standing is open has no place on the chart.

5.4 Publishers. The object sold as whole is the one that sells.

5.5 The machine. A flat object is the most compressible one. The field's collapse is the model's convenience (4.17).

5.6 The discipline as product. 5.1–5.5 are not five beneficiaries of a discipline that exists apart from them; they are what the discipline is made of. The credential needs a unit that cannot fail (5.1); the unit needs a standing object (3.1), which the norm of charity guards (5.2); the standing object is indexed by name (5.3) and sold whole (5.4). Disciplinarity is the stable form of that circuit: what the census measures as the shape of the field is the shape of what can be certified. The model test (4.17) shows the circuit's output with the person removed: the type, sharpened, and in this run with no tail.

6. Against the received account

6.1 The analytic–continental divide is a quarrel over method. Measured (4.14–4.15), with the analytic arm (4.14c–d): the analytic field is 91–93% positions in a debate, so the name is not its unit. Its form is the one the others converge on. Further: on both sides about three-quarters of the work is anchored to a named author (ancient journals, operations inside one author 71–76%; continental journals, work anchored to a name 74–84%). The operations differ. The ancient field repairs the name (B), and the continental field pairs names (E, F). The continental field moves toward the analytic template: the operation profiles grow closer (0.19 to 0.24), and the continental claim formula rises from 16% to 39%. Against the ancient field the continental trajectory is a trough and recovery with little net change. The divide is real at the level of the operation, and the template is its point of convergence with the analytic field.

6.2 The canon debate is a quarrel over membership (1.7). The census shows that what is done to a member after admission (§4.6) narrows whoever is admitted.

6.2a The exchange with the editor of Ancient Philosophy (§0A) shows the mechanism at the point of entry. The specification of the unit is stated, demand by demand, and the excluded claim is refused by topic.

6.3 What the article economy removes, and the discipline it produces then lacks, is not a doctrine and not a set of names. It is the capacity to ask what the object is. Catana's verdict on the "system" ("obscure more than it reveals") describes the instrument. The census measures what it obscures: 8 of 844 articles in Ancient Philosophy, and 4 of 283 Plato/Aristotle abstracts across the sister journals since 2021. In the philosophy journal it was gone from the pages by the 1920s (4.12g–h); in the census, where it survives, it is concentrated in one journal, Elenchos (4.10), whose institute was founded on an edition of fragments, with scattered openings elsewhere. The phenomenology is the one The Certified Center names: "a discipline cannot miss the arguments it no longer produces". The editor's "who cares besides you" (§0A) is that sentence spoken from inside. The national explanation was tested once and weakened (4.12d). The stronger candidate is the philological remit: the question persisted in philology after it left the philosophy journal (4.12c), though not in all of it; the REG lets it go after 1945 (4.12f), and the text survives there where the work's standing does not.
