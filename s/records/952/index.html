Its surviving metadata places the operators in biblical, Septuagintal, and apocalyptic traditions. The edition used a New Testament and Septuagint register rather than classical Attic Greek and reportedly linked BRIDE to Song of Songs 8:6 and BEAST to Revelation 11:7. The abstract describes a theological adaptation of Johannes Sigil’s preface, a complete Part I, and BRIDE and BEAST sections combining archetypal descriptions with kernel specifications. Transformation is framed through metanoia and incarnational practice: the operators are enacted in the body rather than held as abstract concepts. The planned full edition would have included all eight operators and a broader Septuagint apparatus. That full edition is not present, and neither is the partial facing text. The Alexanarch record preserves only the captured metadata and its evidence of the work’s former registration.

## Description (as recorded by DataCite)

Koine Greek-English facing edition of the Mandala operator system. Positions the eight recursive transformation operators within biblical, scriptural, and apocalyptic literature traditions. Uses New Testament/Septuagint Greek register (not classical Attic), includes scriptural cross-references (Song of Songs 8:6 for BRIDE, Revelation 11:7 for BEAST), and frames operators as biblical hermeneutics rather than New Age spirituality.

Features Johannes Sigil preface adapted for theological context, Part I complete translation, Part II with BRIDE and BEAST operators (archetypal + kernel layers), and theological apparatus using concepts of metanoia (transformation) and incarnational theology (operators work in the body, not thought alone).

This partial edition includes Johannes Sigil's preface, Part I complete, and Part II with BRIDE and BEAST. Full edition will include all eight operators plus Parts III-VIII with full Septuagint apparatus.

Part of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive seven-language fortress architecture.

Biblical/scriptural positioning within Christian Orthodox and apocalyptic traditions.
