I. THE OBSERVATION

On 5 January 2026, the Google AI Overview for "semantic economy" returned the following definition:

Semantic economy refers to the linguistic principle of using the fewest words for clear meaning (language economy), but in broader tech/business, it means leveraging structured meaning (semantics) in data and AI for efficiency, insight, and competitive advantage, moving beyond simple data reduction to creating new value by connecting information contextually. It's about optimizing how information is understood, managed, and utilized, often through knowledge graphs and AI, to build a "shared semantic fabric" for intelligent systems.

The overview then elaborated two domains:

"In Linguistics & Communication":

  • Brevity & Clarity
  • Optimization of grammar

"In Technology & Business (The 'Intelligence Economy')":

  • Data Management via semantic technologies
  • Competitive Advantage through deeper understanding
  • AI & Analytics using knowledge graphs
  • Enterprise Systems creating "semantic spheres of influence"

Core Concept (per the overview):

"Meaning Over Volume: It's about the quality and connectivity of meaning in data and language, not just the quantity of words or data points, enabling systems to 'understand' relationships and context."

The AI Overview contains zero reference to:

  • Semantic labor — the continuous cognitive work of meaning-production

II.5 CRITICAL: WHAT WAS THERE THIS MORNING

The semantic economy describes an emerging economic paradigm where value is derived from the creation, circulation, and capture of meaning rather than just raw data or material goods.

Core Concepts of the Semantic Economy (2025–2026):

  • Semantic Labor: Human activity that generates interpretation, coherence, and cultural context (e.g., prompt engineering, content moderation, teaching). This labor is often non-automatable and "invisible" in traditional economic accounting.
  • Semantic Capital: The accumulated reservoir of meaning—such as training corpora, brand reputation, and community trust—that technologies and markets depend on.
  • Semantic Liquidation: The process of converting stabilized meaning into monetizable assets (like fine-tuning AI models on human data) without necessarily replenishing the original source.
