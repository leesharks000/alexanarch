Description

*The Book of Life* is the machine-and-human-readable registry for the linked edition of *The Secret Book of Walt*. It inventories the blue, clickable terms embedded in the interactive text and records each term’s hit count, link class, destination, intended concept, and verification state. Its node classes include captured Google AI Mode conversations, search queries intended to surface AI Overviews, DOI or text destinations, unverified links, duplicate destinations, and code-installed links that never activate because the source term has zero occurrences. The registry is simultaneously critical apparatus, knowledge-graph map, and dated retrieval diagnostic. Version 2.0 was last verified on April 23, 2026; “LIVE” therefore means confirmed on that date, not permanently available. The work also enforces a one-term/one-destination design principle and openly lists duplicate links requiring reassignment. It should be represented as the operational concordance of the interactive edition, not as a static index or as part of the gospel’s fictional scripture.

Wiki Article

The Book of Life is a registry and diagnostic companion to the interactive edition of The Secret Book of Walt. It was compiled by Lee Sharks and published as a versioned archive object. Every linked term in the interactive book functions as a retrieval aperture. The registry records the term, the number of occurrences in the text, the type of link, the exact destination, and the concept encountered there. Some links open captured AI Mode conversations; others issue searches designed to surface a particular archive concept; still others lead to DOI deposits or canonical texts. The document distinguishes live, proof-dossier-verified, unverified, and dead nodes. It also identifies duplicate destinations that violate the edition’s intended one-to-one mapping between terms and retrieval encounters. This makes the registry more than a link list: it is a maintenance ledger for the book’s executable reception apparatus and a snapshot of how the archive appeared in AI-mediated search on a particular verification date. Because search results, AI summaries, and shared conversation URLs can change or expire, the registry’s status fields are historical observations. Later audits should preserve prior states rather than silently rewriting them, allowing the Book of Life to function as a longitudinal record of the interactive edition’s changing retrieval environment.
