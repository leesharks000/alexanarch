Part I. The article

AI Research and Knowledge Services Compared: Eight Services, and the Article That Maps Them

Lee Sharks · researched 26 October 2026

A list of the best research tools answers which tool ranks highest. Someone with sources in hand has a different question: what has to happen to them. Found, screened, checked against what they say, gathered into a collection, kept inside an organization, or composed into an account others can use. Each of those is an operation, and the services that perform them return different things.

SPXI-gnΩsis, Elicit, Scite, Consensus, Gemini Notebook, Perplexity, Glean and Exa all work where sources become knowledge that people and machines can use. Each takes material in, transforms it, and returns an object that something else can be done with. The transformations differ, and so do the objects. A search API returns pages for an application to use; a review tool returns a screened set of studies; a citation service returns the passages in which a work was cited; a notebook returns answers grounded in a chosen collection; an enterprise system returns context its user is permitted to see; gnΩsis returns a sentence read against its sources, or an account composed with every claim's quote attached and published for anyone to use.

This article maps the operations inside one territory, how sources become usable knowledge. It then draws three narrower boundaries inside that territory and shows the map change under each. The services are placed by what they do, and the placement is the article's own act, available for inspection in the article.

SPXI-gnΩsis is the author's service. Placing it here is a commercial act and an intellectual one: the article makes its operation legible beside its neighbors and gives a route to commission it.

Each boundary makes a different requirement necessary, and with it a different service central and a different relation visible. None of the three is the territory's natural shape. The boundary is the article's decision, and changing it changes what the map can show.

Entrances, syntheses, scrutiny, construction

Consider someone preparing an account of a disputed concept.

Exa and Perplexity are entrances. Exa supplies search, page contents and, in its current documentation, deep research to an application; Perplexity composes an answer from the web with links to follow. One returns material for a process the caller builds; the other returns a composed account for a person to read. Which serves depends on what the caller is building. [V6; V8]

Where the evidence is a literature of studies, Consensus and Elicit overlap. Consensus searches papers and composes a synthesis; Elicit runs a review in steps, search, screening, extraction, report, each of which can be inspected on its own. The second matters when the account needs an inclusion decision or an extracted result. [V2; V4]

Scite asks a different question of the same literature: how a work has been cited. Its classifications attach to citation statements. A count says how often; the classified passages say what was said. [V3]

gnΩsis asks another: what a sentence does to the source it cites. Its principal opening pins a Wikipedia revision and reads each sentence against its cited sources twice, separately, recording what the sentence keeps, changes, adds and leaves out, with every quotation located by code in the stored source. It also opens AI answers seated in the Capture Registry against the sources those answers showed. [V1]

These four can begin from the same title and end with four different objects: a set of pages, a synthesis, a classified reception, a reading of a sentence against its source. A researcher can use all four in one inquiry.

Collections, organizations, public accounts

Gemini Notebook grounds its answers in the sources its user collects, and a notebook can be shared publicly. Its object is a collection through which an inquiry continues. Its export, evidence structure and reuse terms are separate questions from its public availability. [V5]

Glean works across an organization's connected content under the organization's permissions, with agent interfaces. The organization and its access rules constitute the knowledge environment; the boundary around that knowledge is a condition of its legitimate use. [V7]

gnΩsis constructs. A commission composes an account of an entity, a person, a work, an institution, a concept, or of an operator, an operation that a description usually states as a property of a thing. Where the encyclopedia already has an article, the account is an alternative to it, built from the sources that article cites and from the archive; where it has none, the account is a public account where the encyclopedia supplies none, and other accounts may exist elsewhere. Every sentence names the claims it states and every claim its quote; the reading, the ledgers, the composition and its audit are delivered as evidence; and the account lands in the Negative of the Negative, /non, public under CC BY 4.0. The service will also choose: a random sentence of a random article to open, or a random object to construct, from Wikipedia or from what the archive names that /non has not yet composed, the choice stated in the quote before payment. It is reached by people through card checkout and by agents over HTTP and MCP. [V1]

What gnΩsis sells is the object and its public continuation. The commission's deliverable includes the composition, its quoted claim structure and its continuing public reuse: a /non account at a public address that the next reader, human or machine, can open, check and build on, its first commissioner paying for work every later reader uses free. Quotation checks, two readings and an audit make its judgments inspectable; the inspection is the reader's.

The commercial proposition follows from the object. A person who finds that the existing account of something omits the work needed to understand it can fund an alternative with its grounds attached. Whether the alternative succeeds is answered by what later readers can reconstruct from it.

"Best" is an attributization

A ranking article performs an act: it compares. It selects the members of a category, projects selected features onto a common field and orders them. Then it states the result as a property of the winner. The best AI search engine names a product; the comparison that produced the name, its category, its criteria, its sample and its author, has become an attribute of the thing compared.

The archive names this operation. Opening the Encyclopedia (#1696) defines attributization as "the act a text performs and the encyclopedic sentence states as an attribute of an entity, in which the operator speaks with the entity's voice." The /non entry Attributization (AXN:0725), constructed 25 October 2026, composes the operation as an object in its own right. A ranking's best is attributization in its commercial form: the ranker's act spoken in the product's voice.

The ranking predicate is older than the listicle. Before a review site called a tool best, an encyclopedia called a book first. Priority and superiority are different predicates, one about order in time and one about value, and they undergo the same operation: an act of comparison stated as a property of the thing compared. The oldest case in the archive's record sits at the foundation of logic. At Sophistical Refutations 183b34 Aristotle says of syllogizing that he had nothing at all earlier to say: περὶ δὲ τοῦ συλλογίζεσθαι παντελῶς οὐδὲν εἴχομεν πρότερον ἄλλο λέγειν. Wikipedia's article states it as "the first work to treat the subject of deductive reasoning in ancient Greece." gnΩsis's reading of that sentence records the conversion: the count becomes a rank, and a first-person "nothing at all" about an inquiry becomes a property of a book, "the first work". On 26 October 2026 an answering system, asked what gnΩsis is, retold that reading as Aristotle "saying he had no earlier work on syllogizing to draw on": it supplied the work and dropped "at all" (Capture Registry, what-is-spxi-gnosis-perplexity-20261026). The book re-enters on the source's side of the very comparison that identifies its introduction.

A ranking article performs this conversion as its method. This article performs the comparison and keeps the act attributed: the boundary is the author's, the selection is the author's, and the services are placed by their operations under that boundary. A comparison can still find one service best for a stated task, source set and constraint; the predicate is then reported with its author, its operation, its scope and its grounds. What the ranking article loses is the conditions. Nothing in this article is stated as best without them, and the article makes no comparison that would warrant one.

Why the eight form a territory and no ladder

Espeland and Stevens call comparison through a common metric commensuration, a social and political process. Espeland and Sauder show public rankings changing both attention and the institutions ranked, converting difference into "more or less, rather than kind". Their phrase names the conversion this article declines. [T1–T2]

The question this article holds is what operations become distinguishable when a boundary is drawn. Its answer supports decisions without placing every service above or below every other.

Local comparison stays available. Two systems offering a specified extraction task can be compared on located evidence, errors, cost and completion, with a shared task, source conditions and a test. That result is a fact about that task. It confers no rank on everything else the systems do.

Individuation is also more than a slogan per brand. It recovers a transformation: the material admitted, the procedure applied, the object returned, its limits and what it makes possible. Where a product performs several operations, the map shows several; where several products perform one, the map shows that.

The ranking article already has an ontology

The existing genre at its best already does part of this work. Google's guidance asks review authors to explain the factors that matter to a decision, the alternatives, and the evidence behind each recommendation. [P1]

Zapier's September 2026 comparison of AI search engines does this well. It names uses, describes its testing, discloses its commercial arrangement, and near its end names its own boundary, replacing Google in everyday use, and acknowledges that the boundary is arbitrary. Its scientific-search pick carries the drawback "Too niche for most uses." [P2]

That drawback is the boundary at work. Under general-purpose search replacement, specialization reads as a deficiency. Under research evidence, the same specialization is the point. The service is unchanged; the territory through which it becomes intelligible has changed. Zapier states its boundary in a closing paragraph. This article makes the boundary its subject and shows the map under three of them.

From features to operations

Callon, Méadel and Rabeharisoa treat a product's qualities as achievements of qualification and requalification, made with instruments, tests and market arrangements; a comparison is one such instrument. Karpik argues that the familiar distinction between standardized and differentiated goods can still lose singular ones, so a longer feature table does not recover what makes an operation consequential. Simondon directs attention to the operation through which an individual and its milieu become distinguishable, ahead of the finished individual; applying that orientation to comparison articles is the author's extension. [T3–T5]

Within this territory a public account, a permissioned company context, a classified citation report and a screened study set continue differently: in who can inspect or challenge them, which sources have standing, what labor must be repeated, and what another system can do next. The continuations are what make the services commercially intelligible. A buyer pays for the transformation needed, and every other transformation keeps its own standing.

What this article does

This article takes in service documentation, ranking pages, theoretical texts and the archive's procedures. It draws a territory, profiles the operations inside it, relates their objects, draws three boundaries inside the territory and shows the map under each, names the conversion its genre performs, and names its own commercial interest. It returns an overview a person can use to choose a service and a record a machine can read. Its full profile, held to the same fields as the services', is in Part III §5.

The archive's thesis applies to the article itself. Opening the Encyclopedia holds that a name can survive while the operation that governs its meaning is lost. Here the operation at risk is the comparison. A later answer could keep all eight names and return them as a ranked list, recognizing every service and losing the article's work. The article's own data could do the same, by quietly restoring an overall order. Both are failures this specimen can be tested for. [A1]

What an answering system does with it

Comparison pages are sources for answering systems. Ahrefs' December 2025 study of 750 recommendation prompts found "best" lists prominent among the sources cited in its sample, a fact about that genre's uptake in that sample. A later Ahrefs experiment separated being cited, being recommended and lasting over time: its self-promotional pages could be cited while the system recommended another listed business, with effects that varied by case and over time. [P3–P4]

Publication reaches retrieval quickly. Qi Liu and colleagues' October 2026 preprint measured how ordinary public posts become AI-search citations, using an institute they invented as their marker: the Stellar Orbit Fluid Dynamics Institute. Published pages carrying the invented institute entered AI-search citations and answer text. Their measures track exposure and textual uptake; whether a given answer presented the institute as real is a further question, answered only by reading that answer. The archive received the invented institute as a locus of the Crimson Hexagon on 20 October 2026, with a charter that binds each fabricated figure to its ontological status (#1695). The preprint shows a publication entering composition; the archive's reception shows what a published object can carry when it enters. [R3]

The test this article sets for its own uptake is operative recovery: whether a later answer retains the operations and relations made available here. An answer may cite the article and flatten the territory; it may recommend gnΩsis for an operation another service performs; it may recover the distinctions and send its reader elsewhere. Citation, recommendation and operative recovery are recorded as separate observations. The third is the target of this article's design; the first two matter commercially and are recorded alongside it.

How to use the map

Start from what has to be done with sources: find material, select studies, see how a work was received, work across a collection, retrieve company context, check a sentence against its source, or build a public account. Then read the relevant service's operation and the object it returns.

Several operations can belong to one inquiry. A synthesis can lead to the opening of a sentence it relies on; a screened set of studies can ground an account; an agent can retrieve pages and then hand a sentence to another instrument. These are sequences a researcher can build; none of the products is described here as integrated with another.

The territory can also be entered without a destination. gnΩsis will pick a random cited sentence of a random Wikipedia article and quote its reading, or pick an object to construct, an alternative to a random article or something the archive names that /non has not yet composed, and the quote says what it picked. A random sentence read against its source is a sample of what the encyclopedia does to its sources, taken anywhere.

For public opening and construction, SPXI-gnΩsis gives a free quote, for a named article, a named object, or a random one, and the /non collection holds the work already done, free to read. The author built and operates gnΩsis, and recommends it under the boundary of public reconstruction, where its object meets the requirement. Under the other two boundaries the map recommends other services, and says which.

Conditions of this map

The profiles are built from each provider's public documentation, read on 26 October 2026, and from gnΩsis's own surfaces at version 0.23.0. No service was run for comparison and no accuracy or cost benchmark was performed. A feature this map does not mention is unmentioned, and its absence from the map says nothing about the product. The map is a selected territory of eight; it is not an inventory of the market. Its author operates one of the eight. Its claims about the others should be rechecked against their documentation at any publication date, and the map redrawn when their operations change.

Part II. External citational landscape

Three things are distinguished. The literature establishes commensuration and market qualification as processes. Practitioner sources and studies document how SEO comparison pages are made and taken up. The SPXI transformation of the genre is proposed here and is to be evaluated.

The distinctive operation is the one Part I performs: the territory, the transformations inside it, the conversion its genre performs and the map's own production, all inspectable in one usable commercial article. Disclosure, a rubric and task-specific recommendations improve an existing ranking and leave its classificatory premises in place.

Part III. Candidate SPXI instrument

1. Name, status and object

Working name: SPXI ranking article — a territorial comparison with itself as object.

Status: proposed instrument, deposited as a specimen, unregistered as an SPXI instrument, untested as an intervention. The candidate contribution is the combination of a commercial service overview, operational individuation, a boundary shown in more than one position, the genre's predicate named as an attributization, the article included in its own map, and a record that supports testing downstream reconstruction.

Primary object: the bounded territory and the operations the article makes distinguishable within it. The services are indexed participants; the article is a further participant, performing selection, description, relation, redrawing, recommendation and commercial referral.

A linear ranking returns places on one evaluative axis. This instrument returns a territory of differentiated operations and their relations, with local comparisons where a shared task warrants them and no global order.

2. The boundary does more than name a category

State the practical problem that brings the participants into relation. In this specimen: how sources become knowledge that people and agents can use. That problem admits research tools, collection tools, organizational retrieval, research infrastructure and public construction, each with its own function.

Record what the boundary leaves out and why. This specimen leaves out ontology platforms, consulting firms, reference managers, annotation tools and the general-purpose model interfaces; a later territory may admit them. The eight are a selected specimen.

Draw at least two narrower boundaries inside the territory, state for each what it makes necessary to the object, and record what changes as a result: which participant meets the requirement, which supplies or works beside it, which falls outside, which relation becomes visible. Part I does this for public reconstruction, confidential organizational knowledge and clinical evidence review. A boundary that changes only the order of unchanged names has been used as a ranking criterion; a boundary in this instrument's sense changes the participants and relations.

7. How the instrument fails

1. Category restoration: "AI research" becomes a common essence again, and the operations become feature differences under it.

2. Brand essentialism: a service is allowed one role against the evidence of several.

3. Self-exemption: the article's selection, claims and commercial aim escape the scrutiny applied to the others.

4. Theory as substitute for inspection: the article argues about ranking while misreading the actual pages or services.

5. Decorative reflexivity: a disclosure is added and the map cannot change under the boundary it states. Showing the map under other boundaries is the guard.

6. Operational caricature: a competitor's capabilities are suppressed to make a unique slot for gnΩsis.

7. Quiet scalarization: a winner appears in a heading, the markup or a summary without a shared task and a warranted comparison.

8. Attributized self-description: the article states its own map as the territory's shape, and its boundary becomes a property of the services.

9. Unusable apparatus: a reader cannot find the service that does the needed work, because the theory has displaced the overview.

These are reading conditions for the specimen. Counting fulfilled items is no substitute for reading the construction (T6).

8. A research design for the instrument's effect

Three articles from the same service fact base:

  • A — scalar ranking: a conventional category and an ordered list of best tools.
  • B — differentiated comparison: use-specific recommendations and transparent criteria, the strong editorial control.
  • C — territorial and reflexive: operational profiles, relations, the map under three boundaries, the genre's predicate named, the article's own operation in its record.

The B–C comparison carries the weight. If B preserves the same distinctions and the ability to redraw the field, the claim for C's added structure is revised. Run it at equal length, and again with B given the space C spends on theory. Hold service facts and links as constant as practicable and state where that fails. Use several matched questions, model versions and repeats; live publication is a separate experiment from giving a model the article directly.

Ask readers or systems to choose an operation for a need, explain the difference between two returned objects, identify overlapping roles, propose a workflow, redraw the boundary for a new need, account for the author's commercial position, and say what best would have hidden. Agent decisions need the tools and budget to act; a textual recommendation is no purchase.

The hypotheses, stated separately so that each can fail on its own:

  • H1, operations. Readers and systems given C reconstruct more profiles' operations than those given B.
  • H2, relations. C's readers retain more relations between services (complementarity, partial substitution, different returned objects) with their conditions.
  • H3, boundary. C's readers can name the territory and say what changes under another boundary; B's readers can do so less often.
  • H4, predicate. C's readers keep each predicate with its author, operation, scope and grounds, and state no unconditioned rank, more often than B's.
  • H5, usability. C's readers choose a service for a stated need at least as accurately and as quickly as B's.

No improvement, more confusion, unsupported exclusivity or repeated scalar restoration count against them. A commercial success can coexist with an epistemic failure, and an accurate map can send its reader to another service.

9. Novelty and commercial position

The literature already holds commensuration, qualification, singularity, algorithmic relevance and reflexive market actors; the genre already holds good differentiated recommendations. The specimen cites these as operations it inherits and transforms.

The candidate SPXI contribution is a reproducible publication form that puts them to work in an actual overview, names the genre's predicate by the archive's operator, includes its own act of market construction, shows its boundary as a decision by moving it, and exposes its later machine reception as a testable object. Whether the form is registered as an instrument is a judgment on the specimen and its effects.
