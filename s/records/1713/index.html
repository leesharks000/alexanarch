Description

Six seats on the originals shelf that hold no text: the wanted, each a declared absence naming the works, their rights, why they are not held and when or how they open. Hypatia of Alexandria, whose works are lost or survive inside other texts, with the witnesses wanted; Philip K. Dick, the novels, the late trilogy and the Exegesis, with thirteen stories already open; Allen Ginsberg, Howl to Death & Fame; Anne Carson, Nobel laureate of 2026, from Eros the Bittersweet to The Gender of Sound; Tao Lin, the books and the mandalas; Paul Klee, in the public domain and held back, in the operator's words, by "too many files and too many image copyright landmines". Deleuze and Guattari, declared at 27/07, are referred to and not seated twice.

EA-CORPORA-28 — The Twenty-Eighth Seating

MANUS, 27 October 2026, after the twenty-seventh seating entered Deleuze and Guattari as a declared absence: "and a declared absence/ wanted seating: hypatia, d & g, pkd, allen ginsberg, anne carson, and tao lin - including the mandalas. and paul klee. too many files and too many image copyright landmines."

The seating holds no text. It is the shelf's list of the wanted, each seat a declaration of what the shelf would hold, who holds it, why it is not held, and when or how it opens. The absences are of three kinds. Hypatia's works are lost, or survive inside other authors' texts, and what can be seated are the witnesses. Philip K. Dick, Allen Ginsberg, Anne Carson and Tao Lin are in copyright, two of them living. Paul Klee's work is in the public domain where he lived and died, and is held back by its number and by the rights claimed in its photographs. Deleuze and Guattari, named in the request, were declared at 27/07 (#1712) and are not seated twice.

Hypatia is entered as an absence of a different kind from the others of this seating. Her works are not held back by copyright; they are lost, or they survive inside other texts under other names. The Suda names a commentary on Diophantus, a commentary on Apollonius' Conics and an Astronomical Canon, and the heading of Theon's commentary on the third book of Ptolemy's Almagest credits the text to an edition "revised by my daughter Hypatia, the philosopher". What can be seated are the witnesses: Synesius' letters to her, the historians who record her life and death, and the texts in which her editing is believed to stand.
