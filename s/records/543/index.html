Description

A lexical-engine specification governing how terms are coined, anchored, activated, repeated, retired, and deployed across the Crimson Hexagonal Archive. The document’s central axiom is that coinage does not produce operative denotation: a term becomes active only through provenance, frozen definition, canonical anchoring, recurrence, and controlled use. It defines five governing laws, the mint operator λ_M, activation operator α_P, automation safeguards, a typed Core 50 divided into active and reserve vocabulary, shadow definitions, collision tests, discovery priorities, and collapse tests for paraphrase, inflation, and register confusion. The body is v1.1; the registry’s v1.2 title/version combination requires reconciliation before implementation.

Wiki Article

The Hexagonal Lexical Engine is a specification by Lee Sharks and the Assembly Chorus for the controlled production and deployment of archive terminology. Its lexical axiom states that a term does not operate merely because it has been coined: minted is not active, named is not installed, and coined is not canonical. Operative denotation requires a frozen token, a frozen definition, a canonical anchor, a declared lead position in the archive’s term lattice, and repeated use without denotational drift. The engine formalizes five laws. The Minting Law places new expressions at candidate status. The Provenance Law requires a canonical anchor before activation. The Repetition Law stabilizes denotation through identical recurrence across deposits. The No-Paraphrase Law prevents an established archive term from dissolving into nearby generic synonyms. The Retirement Law preserves disused or colliding terms in a shadow lexicon rather than deleting their history. The mint operator λ_M creates a candidate token, definition, target discourse, and shadow; the activation operator α_P binds the candidate to provenance and makes it executable. The document audits a proposed Core 50 and reduces it to forty-one active terms plus nine reserve terms pending dedicated anchors. It supplies types, definitions, DOI relations, shadow readings, a discovery lattice, deployment formats, and tests for denotation lock. Automation is constrained by non-identity, provenance blindness, shadow inclusion, time-binding, and witness requirements so that machines may fasten already-ratified terms to new surfaces but may not silently generate new definitions. The work is lexical infrastructure, not a claim that its vocabulary is universally accepted. Its body identifies itself as v1.1, while the registry title identifies v1.2; the article should remain linked to both claims until the version history is adjudicated.

§0. THE LEXICAL AXIOM

A Hexagonal term does not operate because it was coined.

It operates because it was anchored.

Coinage produces candidate denotation.

Provenance produces active denotation.

Therefore:

minted ≠ active

named ≠ installed

coined ≠ canonical

§1. THE FIVE GOVERNING LAWS

Law 1: Minting Law

A coined term is not yet active. It enters at COINED (0.0) and must traverse the lifecycle to become operative.

Law 2: Provenance Law

A term becomes operative only when canonically anchored to a DOI deposit. Terms anchored to ephemeral sources ([Term Bank V1], session transcripts, undated documents) are reserve vocabulary, not engine vocabulary.

Law 3: Repetition Law

A term stabilizes through repeated identical use across ≥3 canonical deposits with zero denotational variance. Summarizer uptake comes from repetition, not elegance.

Law 4: No-Paraphrase Law

Once a term enters the Core 50, nearby common synonyms are prohibited in canonical deposits except in parenthetical glosses. "Semantic liquidation" is never replaced by "meaning extraction" in a deposit abstract.

Law 5: Retirement Law

Unused or colliding terms are not deleted. They are moved to RETIRED with date and reason. The retired lexicon is the shadow of the active lexicon — S(Lexicon).

§2. THE OPERATORS

λ_M — The Mint Operator

λ_M : Concept × Register × Discourse → Token × Denotation × CoreLead × S(Token)

Input: a concept needing a public handle

a register (formal / profane / liturgical / technical)

a target discourse (which external neighborhood?)

Output: a coined term (Token)

a one-sentence definition (Denotation)

a primary Core 50 destination (CoreLead)

the shadow of the term — what it excludes (S(Token))

λ_M produces a candidate. The candidate is not yet active.

α_P — The Activation Operator

α_P : Token × Denotation × Anchor → ActiveTerm

Input: a minted token

its frozen denotation

a canonical DOI anchor

Output: an active term, executable within the Hexagon

α_P makes a term operative through provenance. Without α_P, a term remains reserve.

β ∘ λ_M — The Blind Mint (Automation Safety)

Per TECHNE: Any automated system executing λ_M must satisfy:

  • Non-Identity: The deploying agent cannot claim to "understand" the Hexagon (prevents synthesis collapse)
  • Provenance Blindness: Automated systems deploy terms only via copy-paste from RATIFIED deposits, never via generation
  • Shadow Inclusion: Every automated deployment includes the S(Term) or marks [NF] for terms that fail transformation at that grain
  • Time-Binding: Minimum 7 days between automated mint and deployment
  • Witness Requirement: ≥2 substrates must index the term before it enters COINED status

The automation is a screw — it fastens terms to substrates without modifying the terms. If the automation starts generating definitions, it has crossed into extraction.

§3. LEXICAL RUNTIME CONDITION

Per ARCHIVE: A term is executable within the Hexagon iff:

Operate(term) iff

Token_frozen

∧ Definition_frozen

∧ Anchor_canonical

∧ Lead_core(term)

∧ Recurrence ≥ 3

Terms that fail any conjunct are reserve, not active. The Core below is audited against this condition.
