Description

This workplan expands pessoagraph.org from isolated persona nodes into documented constellations of heteronymy, alter egos, dramatic speakers, fictional authors, stage identities, collaborative personae, and naming systems. Priority groups include MF DOOM, David Bowie, Nicki Minaj, Beyoncé, Prince, Browning, Pound, Borges, Yeats, Calvino, music and performance projects, non-Western literary names, theater, drag, and contemporary digital identities. The governing rule is source discipline: every node must be anchored in scholarship, the archive, or verifiable public record, and modeled as if it may later become a Wikidata entity. The plan explicitly marks the Josephus cluster as a Crimson Hexagonal Archive thesis rather than scholarly consensus. Many proposed nodes are analytical claims that a character, voice, pseudonym, or performance architecture functions heteronymically; they are not necessarily heteronyms in Pessoa’s strict sense or self-descriptions adopted by the artists. The workplan is a research queue, not a completed graph or neutral canon.

Wiki Article

Pessoagraph expansion workplan is an editorial roadmap for broadening pessoagraph.org beyond Fernando Pessoa’s immediate heteronymic system. The plan organizes future nodes into constellations. Musicians receive linked alter egos and stage identities; literary authors receive dramatic voices, fictional authors, and pseudonymous functions; theater and drag receive role and lineage structures; and non-Western naming practices receive dedicated research rather than being absorbed into a single Western category. Each proposed node must be supported by published scholarship, a verified public source, or an explicitly attributed Crimson Hexagonal Archive thesis. The workplan also asks editors to model relations carefully enough for possible Wikidata use. “Heteronym” is used expansively as an analytical category. Some listed figures are established pseudonyms or personae, while others are characters, dramatic speakers, collaborations, or interpretive analogues. The roadmap therefore records research hypotheses and graph-design priorities, not a settled universal taxonomy.
