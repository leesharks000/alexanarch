Description

This record is the address and provenance note for the Crimson Hexagonal Archive’s lexical-minting registry. The JSON dataset, rather than the short Markdown body, is the canonical work. It contains term entries linked to defining deposits, extracted definitions, type classifications, and entity-relation triples. The body preserves two different counts. Its opening historical description says 12,015 minted terms across 555 deposits, pruned from 16,877 raw extractions. The corrected canonical-dataset note says 12,032 observed entries across the same 555 deposits. The seventeen-entry increase should be represented as a version change or correction, not silently collapsed. The record is also a major availability-integrity artifact. From June 22 through July 28, its served body was incorrectly replaced by the complete text of #856, The Pristine Fallacy. The defect resulted from body storage keyed only by the bare hexadecimal segment 0365, which both records shared after historical offset drift. The full AXN values distinguished them, but the resolver did not. The repair moved this record to deposit-number-keyed storage and documents how citations made during the defect should be re-resolved. This is more than a clerical note: it demonstrates that a supposedly content-addressed system can collapse distinct works when one nonunique component is used as the storage key. The dataset’s 12,032 entries are extracted registry objects, not necessarily 12,032 unique coined concepts. Definitions, variants, ordinary phrases, entities, imported terms, repeated forms, and false positives require continued human validation.
