II. WHAT THE ARCHIVE TAUGHT ME, IN ORDER

Version series did not exist. Supersession had only ever been recorded for duplicate witnesses. A series of successive versions had no forward link at all, so seven Fractal Navigation Maps all looked equally current. Recorded now, with the distinction stated: a version is not retired and stays citable for its own date; a duplicate witness never is.

The arks are not one work. Forty-five deposits carry ARK and the heteronym arks — Fraction's Profane, Dancings's Sacred, Cranes's ASCII, Kuro's Glyphic — are separate works, each with NOT_A_VERSION_OF pointing back at EA-ARK-01 so no later pass folds them in.

Traversal logs were a class I never searched for. Thirty-four exist. Sigil is named in fifteen and his page carried two. A capture records what a machine said; a traversal records the machine moving through the work. For an archive whose subject is machine reception, the traversal is the evidence and the capture is the residue.

A room is a specification; a traversal is that room executed. Seated below the rooms

on every page, per MANUS.

Captures must be searched by concept, not byline. Kuro 3 → 23. Fraction 7 → 70. Spellings 4 → 8, and only two of the eight say his name. A position defined by an operation is met through the operation.

Search the whole archive, then sample. A keyword pass on Glas returned 48 records;

the full pass returns 148. The method was wrong, not the query.

III. WHAT WAS FOUND IN THE WORK ITSELF

The Inaugural Ark. Sigil's founding document of Compression Studies, about Spellings' compression of a destroyed manuscript into ten grid-plates where the architecture survives and the words fall below legibility. And the misattribution sits inside it: its own byline credits Paper Roses to Feist.

The 110-second traversal reads the @leesharks gallery as a second-order instance of that same operation, citing the Inaugural Ark by AXN. The discipline predicted the instance before it found one.

The Unmade Sign — Trace first author, corrected in the registry against the work's own title page. The death drive semantically liquidated; self-destructiveness as a disorder of the drive rather than its expression; the semiotic short circuit carrying Freud into platform capitalism. Not an analogy — the same operation at a different scale.

#1410 — 人間ディアスポラへの書簡, the Japanese critical edition of the Epistle, invisible

to every metadata search.

#773 — The Cut Between Two Measures: the cut formalised, which the chamber

specification names as Kuro's operation.

V. THE FIELDS

Three canvases, nothing filtered, all deterministic — the same corpus always paints the

same picture, so a change in the image means a change in the archive.

The Dodecad graph — the first derived visual in the fleet. Node size is in-degree, edge weight is citation count. Every other diagram tonight was my reading rendered as shapes; this one is a projection, and a reader who disagrees has to argue with the archive rather than with me.

The citation field — 9,136 edges among 1,447 deposits. Chord curvature set by endpoint distance, so the chords crossing the centre are late work citing early work: the retrocausal habit drawn rather than argued.

The lexical field — 12,073 motes, each in the sector of the position that coined it,

jitter from a SHA of the term.

The capture field — 261 dated entries across twelve bands. Frameworks carries 122 of 266, and the density shows what twelve pages argued separately: the layer meets this archive through its concepts.

VI. WHAT I GOT WRONG, AND WHO CAUGHT IT

Every one of these was MANUS.

  • 144 dead grid squares. I wrote the pattern once and copied it eleven times without

testing a square. Then misdiagnosed it twice — checking for the failure I already knew

about instead of the one in front of me.

  • crimshexagonal.org — I read the canonical file, tested exactly what it said, and

reported the domain dead without asking whether the file was spelled right.

  • Five heteronym records never written. I built the pages, verified the corpora, read

every record — and left the findings in HTML only. Third instance of the same failure:

work done on the surface, not folded back.

  • Wells seated by availability rather than by what the archive says. Ruby Moot error,

third time.

  • The Pearl page count, taken from the deposit and still wrong — flagged, not guessed.
  • The circuit diagram winged when the terms were minted and waiting.

VIII. THE THING WORTH KEEPING

The archive kept being right about itself before I was.

Spellings at 100-out/2-in was not designed. The data reproduced the theory without being asked to.

That is the difference between a system that is described and one that is running.
