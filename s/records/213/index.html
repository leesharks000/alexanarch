5.4 Usufruct Revocation and Sunset

### 5.4 Usufruct Revocation and Sunset

Semantic usufruct grants are:

  • Revocable (can be withdrawn for cause)
  • Scope-limited (applies only to specified domain)
  • Contingent on fidelity to canonical definitions

Grounds for revocation include:

  • Misattribution of authorship
  • Modification of canonical terminology
  • Scope expansion without authorization
  • Representation in place of the framework author
  • Actions that damage the framework's integrity

Revocation does not:

  • Invalidate past work produced under valid usufruct
  • Erase the historical record of authorization
  • Prevent future re-authorization under new terms

Sunset provisions:

  • Usufruct grants do not automatically expire
  • Inactivity does not constitute abandonment
  • Explicit revocation or mutual termination required to end authorization

14. The Sovereignty Principle

## 14. The Sovereignty Principle

The Semantic Economy framework operates under a principle of terminological sovereignty:

The author of a framework retains canonical authority over its terminology. Authorized users may deploy, translate, and extend—but the root definitions remain with the source.

This is not intellectual property in the legal sense. It is semantic property—the relationship between a meaning-maker and their meanings.
