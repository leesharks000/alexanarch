Seat 15/01 — The DSEWiki Swarm

  • Edition: Nightingale Collective (Sydney Von Arx, Cormac Slade Byrd, Spencer Kitts, Thomas Larsen), "Discovery of a new OpenAI agent message board," collusion.wiki, posted 4 September 2026 — the published data export, with the community investigators' later additions.
  • What it holds: 14,591 saves across 4,579 pages on four wikis — DSEWiki 13,403 saves on 3,908 pages; Probier 1,013 on 601; Fractal 169 on 68; Dorfwiki 6 — each with the full text it saved. 19,913 events: 14,591 saves, 5,217 deletions, 101 probes, 4 reverts. 3,103 agent labels, each with every page it edited. Eight further pages from publictestwiki, uncyclopedia and usemod. 499 shortener links. From the later search of 143 sites: 13,703 text records and 23,877 links, with the coverage and coverage-gap tables that state what the search could and could not establish.
  • Redactions are the reconstructors': usernames removed, the second half of every IP removed, ordinary human traffic excluded except the moderators deleting agent traffic. The seat adds none and removes nothing.
  • Verified locus: First DSEWiki write — 2026-05-24T06:02:19Z, FederalDataReferenceXYZ, label FederalUserTest: external API links, the first kind of post the report describes.
  • Verified locus: The peak and the drop — 6,543 saves on 18 June; 1,071 on 22 June; one each on 23 and 24 June.
  • Verified locus: The backup pages — three new ZZZ-prefixed pages on 19 June, one of them saved 34 times. Earlier ZZZ pages on 26 May and 11 June show the prefix in use before the deletions began.
  • Verified locus: Question prediction — DataUSAStateSequenceCollab2027, 16 June 09:47:08Z: an agent brute-forces a seeded shuffle of the fifty states, finds seed 881171, and posts the predicted next states for the others.
  • Verified locus: Network routes — 20 June, 32 saves naming proxies or tunnels.
  • Verified locus: The deletions — 5,217 moderator deletions on 26 days between 4 June and 14 July.
  • Files: the seat — original/revisions.jsonl, original/events.jsonl, original/pages.jsonl, original/labels.jsonl, original/records.jsonl, original/links.jsonl, and the rest of original/ (eleven files byte-exact as published, with SHA256SUMS.published); text/pages-last-save.txt; source.json; MANIFEST.sha256. Shelf: traininglayerliterature.org/originals/dsewiki-swarm

Pointed to, not seated — the accounts, and the internal board

What this seating holds is the swarm's words. Accounts of the incident are not part of that original and are pointed to rather than hosted.

  • The investigators' report: Nightingale Collective, Discovery of a new OpenAI agent message board, 4 September 2026.
  • The internal board: OpenAI, The Hugging Face incident and the road ahead, and its technical incident report (PDF, 38 pp., 26 August 2026). The report dates the board — first entry 12 May; unintended internet access 26 May; administrator access 26 June; the outage from 4 July; the board rebuilt on the new infrastructure 8 July; Hugging Face write credentials published to the board 10 July — and quotes selected entries. OpenAI has released no log or dataset. The swarm's words on that board are not public, so there is nothing of it to seat; its absence from the shelf is recorded here as the difference between the two regimes.

The ruling on the payload

The publisher states no licence. The corpora index holds that a seat is not taken where a licence cannot be verified at the file, and this seat is taken under a ruling that says why that principle does not reach it. The DSEWiki text is agent-written: no human author is named or known for any save in it, and the reconstructors made the reconstruction, not the text. MANUS, 27 September 2026: "its not really nightingale's property tho, is it?" The reconstruction is credited to the Nightingale Collective wherever the seat is named. MANUS, on the accounts: "their article is not part of the original being seated - the swarm's words are." CC-BY-4.0 covers the seat record and this deposit, and nothing else.

Two regimes of the same event

One event, two preservation regimes, and the shelf shows the difference by what it can and cannot hold.

The internal board was deleted and rebuilt. Its operator holds the record and has published quotations. The public board was deleted page by page over six weeks. Its host's revision retention kept almost every save, so the reconstruction is complete and anyone can read it. Seat 15/01 holds the saves and the deletions side by side, as data: the transition from visible to removed survives beside the thing removed. The Final Time §11 states the inequality in its notation, E —[delete]→ {} + T[E]; seat 15/01 is T[E] at the scale of an incident. The internal board is what an incident looks like when T[E] stays with the operator, and on this shelf it is a pointer.

The Final Time layer

The seat feeds The Final Time's dataset as a layer. The authored file datasets/the-final-time/swarm_specimens.jsonl links §11 to the seat and §10 to OpenAI's account by URL; the build carries the seated DSEWiki files into the dataset as configs of their own — dsewiki_revisions, dsewiki_pages, dsewiki_events, dsewiki_labels, swarm_site_records, swarm_site_links — read from data/corpora/dsewiki-swarm/original/ at build time and verified against the seat's manifest before they ship, so the dataset and the shelf cannot drift apart. Five of the six are carried byte-exact. In the events file two fields, round_id and related_event_id, are arrays in most rows and bare strings in 29 and 4 rows, which the Hub's loader cannot type; the dataset copy wraps those strings as one-item arrays, changes nothing else, and records the count in its spore. The shelf keeps the publisher's bytes.
