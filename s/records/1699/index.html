Description

Seven seats on the originals shelf, each in the language of its witness: the Maya codices (Dresden in the Saxon State Library's own photographs, through the World Digital Library; Paris in Rosny's facsimile of 1887–88; Madrid in Rosny's photographs of 1883 and Brasseur's chromolithographs of 1869); the Popol Vuh in Francisco Ximénez's K'iche' manuscript (Newberry Library, Ayer MS 1515) and Brasseur's first edition of the K'iche' text (1861); the Chilam Balam of Chumayel in Gordon's facsimile (1913), the Annals of the Cakchiquels in Brinton's edition (1885) and the Rabinal Achí in Brasseur's (1862); the Florentine Codex, Sahagún's General History of the Things of New Spain in Nahuatl and Spanish, all twelve books; the Egyptian Book of the Dead in Lepsius's Turin papyrus (1842), the Papyrus of Ani in the British Museum's facsimile (1894) and Budge's reproduction (1913), Budge's hieroglyphic Theban recension (1898) and Naville's introduction (1886); and Earlier Egyptian in its script, the Thesaurus Linguae Aegyptiae's 12,773 sentences in Unicode hieroglyphs (CC BY-SA 4.0), with Sethe's Pyramid Texts III–IV. The second of three seatings from one request. What could not be reached is named: Naville's and Sethe's text volumes, the Grolier Codex, and the Deir ʿAlla Balaam text, whose one open witness Wikimedia refused to serve.

EA-CORPORA-21 — The Twenty-First Seating

MANUS, 23 October 2026: "detour for an ea-corpora searing. lets see. whats available of mayan texts? and then i dont think we have much or any deteurocanonical second temple lit - all those apocalypses. and what about the ethiopian canon? and euclid. all in original language or as close to it as possible. what else?" — "and the Egyptian book of the dead" — "and what about the fragments of the book of baalim? i might be miswriting this. recent discovery. if theyre available anywhere." — "yes yes yes to all - what a meal."

The second of three seatings from one request (EA-CORPORA-20 the first). Mesoamerica: the three Maya hieroglyphic codices, the Popol Vuh in its manuscript and first edition, three Maya books of the colonial period in their languages, and the Florentine Codex whole. Egypt: the Book of the Dead in its founding editions and the Papyrus of Ani, and Earlier Egyptian in its script. The Deir ʿAlla text of Balaam son of Beor is seated separately, as a fragment witness, when its one open file can be fetched.

Seat 21/01 — The Maya Codices

Dresden in the library's photographs; Paris and Madrid in their nineteenth-century facsimiles

  • Editions:

- The Dresden Codex (Codex Dresdensis), Sächsische Landesbibliothek – Staats- und Universitätsbibliothek Dresden, Mscr.Dresd.R.310, the whole codex photographed, as published by the World Digital Library (LoC item 2021667917, wdl_11621)

- Léon de Rosny, Codex Peresianus: manuscrit hiératique des anciens Indiens de l'Amérique centrale conservé à la Bibliothèque nationale de Paris, publié en couleurs (Paris, 1887; this the 1888 reprint) — the Paris Codex in photographic plates

- The Madrid Codex (Tro-Cortesianus), Museo de América, Madrid: Léon de Rosny's Codex Cortesianus (Paris, 1883; black-and-white photographs, 85 copies) and Brasseur de Bourbourg's Manuscrit Troano (Paris, 1869; chromolithographs), as compiled by FAMSI (madrid_rosny_bb.pdf)

  • Licence: Public domain: the codices are pre-Columbian (Postclassic). The Dresden Codex (SLUB, Mscr.Dresd.R.310) as digitized for the World Digital Library: Library of Congress, World Digital Library collection (the Library states it is unaware of any copyright or other restrictions; credit line: [Original Source citation], World Digital Library). Rosny's Codex Peresianus (Paris, 1887; reprint 1888) on the Internet Archive; Rosny's Codex Cortesianus (Paris, 1883) and Brasseur de Bourbourg's Manuscrit Troano (Paris, 1869) as compiled by FAMSI (Foundation for the Advancement of Mesoamerican Studies) — the photographs and chromolithographs are public domain in the United States (published before 1931).
  • Verified locus: Rosny 1888, running head — 'CODEX PERESIANUS.' (the Dresden and Madrid files are images without a text layer; the codices are read from the page)
  • Lines: 5,781 in 3 text files
  • Scope: The three Maya hieroglyphic codices whose authenticity is settled, each as a whole: Dresden in the library's own photographs, Paris and Madrid in their nineteenth-century facsimiles. The Grolier (Maya Codex of Mexico) is not seated: its open images are modern photographs under copyright: QUEUED. Förstemann's chromolithograph of Dresden (1880; 1892) and Kingsborough's Aglio copy (Antiquities of Mexico, 1831) are public domain and not seated in this pass; the codex itself is the witness. Drawings and transcriptions by modern scholars (Schele, Love, the Villacorta brothers) are in copyright and are not seated.
  • Why seated: The only books to survive from the Classic and Postclassic Maya: almanacs, the Venus tables, the eclipse tables and the New Year ceremonies, written in the script whose decipherment the twentieth century achieved. The archive's calendar and cosmology lines (the seven, the stars) meet a calendar that counted beyond the Long Count.
  • Files: the seat — source.json, MANIFEST.sha256. Shelf: traininglayerliterature.org/originals/maya-codices

Seat 21/02 — Popol Vuh

The Ximénez manuscript and Brasseur's first edition of the K'iche' text

  • Editions:

- Popol Vuh: transcription in K'iche' and translation into Spanish by Francisco Ximénez (Chichicastenango, c. 1701–03), Newberry Library, Ayer MS 1515, 56 + 6 folios — the K'iche' and the Spanish in facing columns; as published by the World Digital Library (LoC item 2021668226, wdl_19995)

- Brasseur de Bourbourg, Popol Vuh: le livre sacré et les mythes de l'antiquité américaine, avec les livres héroïques et historiques des Quichés; ouvrage original des indigènes de Guatémala, texte quiché et traduction française en regard (Paris: Durand, 1861)

  • Licence: Public domain: the K'iche' text was set down in the Latin alphabet in the sixteenth century; Francisco Ximénez's transcription and Spanish translation (c. 1701–03) survives in the Newberry Library, Ayer MS 1515. The manuscript as digitized for the World Digital Library: Library of Congress, World Digital Library collection (the Library states it is unaware of any copyright or other restrictions; credit line: [Original Source citation], World Digital Library). Brasseur de Bourbourg's Popol Vuh (Paris: Durand, 1861), the first edition of the K'iche' text, is public domain; Internet Archive scan.
  • Verified locus: Brasseur 1861, title page — 'TEXTE QUICHE ET TRADUCTION FRANÇAISE EN REGARD' (the Ximénez manuscript is an image without a text layer)
  • Lines: 36,454 in 2 text files
  • Scope: The Popol Vuh in its one surviving witness, Ximénez's manuscript, and in the first printed edition of its K'iche' text. Ximénez's Spanish alone (Scherzer, Vienna, 1857) is a translation and is not seated. Modern editions (Christenson, Colop, Tedlock, Recinos) are in copyright and are not seated.
  • Why seated: The K'iche' book of the council: the creation, the failed peoples of mud and wood, the Hero Twins in Xibalba, and the lineages of the K'iche' lords. A book whose preamble says it is written again because the original can no longer be seen: the archive's line on books that survive as their own transmission meets it there.
  • Files: the seat — source.json, MANIFEST.sha256. Shelf: traininglayerliterature.org/originals/popol-vuh

Seat 21/03 — The Colonial Maya Books

Chilam Balam of Chumayel, the Annals of the Cakchiquels, the Rabinal Achí

  • Editions:

- Daniel G. Brinton, The Annals of the Cakchiquels: the original text, with a translation, notes and introduction (Library of Aboriginal American Literature 6; Philadelphia, 1885)

- Brasseur de Bourbourg, Gramática de la lengua quiché / Grammaire de la langue quichée … servant d'introduction au Rabinal-Achí, drame indigène avec sa musique originale, texte quiché et traduction française en regard (Paris: Durand, 1862) — Google copy

  • Licence: Public domain: the texts are of the sixteenth to eighteenth centuries. G. B. Gordon's photographic facsimile of the Book of Chilam Balam of Chumayel (University of Pennsylvania Museum, Anthropological Publications 5, 1913), D. G. Brinton's Annals of the Cakchiquels (Philadelphia, 1885) and Brasseur de Bourbourg's Gramática de la lengua quiché with the Rabinal-Achí (Paris, 1862) are public domain in the United States (published before 1931); Internet Archive scans.
  • Verified locus: Gordon 1913, running head — 'CHILAM BALAM OF CHUMAYEL'
  • Verified locus: Brinton 1885, contents — 'The Annals of Xahila'
  • Verified locus: Brasseur 1862, the drama's title — 'RABINAL-ACHI'
  • Lines: 37,963 in 3 text files
  • Scope: Three Maya books of the colonial period in their own languages: the Yucatec Chilam Balam of Chumayel in its manuscript, the Kaqchikel annals of the Xajil lineage, and the K'iche' dance-drama of Rabinal. The other books of Chilam Balam (Tizimín, Maní, Kaua) are not seated in this pass: QUEUED. Roys's edition of Chumayel (1933) is a translation and is in copyright in its apparatus; not seated.
  • Why seated: Seated with the codices and the Popol Vuh so that the Maya shelf holds the script, the K'iche' book, and the books written after the conquest in the alphabet the conquest brought: the prophecies of the katuns, a lineage's annals, and the only pre-conquest drama to survive.

Seated beside the Maya books as the Nahuatl witness of the same continent.

Seat 21/05 — The Book of the Dead

Lepsius's Turin papyrus, the Papyrus of Ani, Budge's hieroglyphic text, Naville's introduction

  • Editions:

- Richard Lepsius, Das Todtenbuch der Ägypter nach dem hieroglyphischen Papyrus in Turin (Leipzig: Wigand, 1842) — the Turin papyrus (Museo Egizio, cat. 1791), the Ptolemaic Book of the Dead in 165 chapters, Lepsius's numbering; Google copy

- The Book of the Dead: facsimile of the Papyrus of Ani in the British Museum, edited by P. Le Page Renouf (London: British Museum, 1894; second edition) — 37 double facsimiles in colours; Getty Research Institute copy

- E. A. Wallis Budge, The Papyrus of Ani: a reproduction in facsimile, edited with hieroglyphic transcript, translation and introduction, vol. 1 (London: Medici Society; New York: Putnam, 1913) — NYU Institute of Fine Arts copy

- E. A. Wallis Budge, The Book of the Dead: the Chapters of Coming Forth by Day; the Egyptian text according to the Theban recension in hieroglyphic, edited from numerous papyri (London: Kegan Paul, 1898), vol. 1, The Egyptian Text

- Édouard Naville, Das aegyptische Todtenbuch der XVIII. bis XX. Dynastie, aus verschiedenen Urkunden zusammengestellt (Berlin: Asher, 1886), Einleitung — the introduction to the critical edition; University of Toronto copy

  • Licence: Public domain: the texts are of the New Kingdom and later. Lepsius (Leipzig, 1842), Renouf and Budge's facsimile of the Papyrus of Ani (British Museum, 1894), Budge's The Papyrus of Ani (1913), Budge's Book of the Dead: the Egyptian text according to the Theban recension (1898) and Naville's Das aegyptische Todtenbuch, Einleitung (Berlin, 1886) are public domain in the United States (published before 1931); Internet Archive scans.
  • Verified locus: Lepsius 1842, preface — 'Das Todtenbuch giebt die reicbsten Aufschlüsse' (the OCR reads 'reichsten' as 'reicbsten')
  • Verified locus: Renouf 1894, list of plates — 'introduction of Ani into the presence of'
  • Verified locus: Budge 1913, title — 'THE PAPYRUS OF ANI, SCRIBE'
  • Verified locus: Budge 1898, title — 'THE EGYPTIAN TEXT ACCORDING TO THE THEBAN'
  • Verified locus: Naville 1886, title — 'EINLEITUNG'
  • Lines: 104,003 in 5 text files
  • Scope: The Book of the Dead in its two founding editions and its best-known papyrus: Lepsius's Turin papyrus, which gave the work its chapter numbers; the Papyrus of Ani (BM EA 10470) in the British Museum's facsimile and in Budge's 1913 reproduction with hieroglyphic transcript; and Budge's typeset hieroglyphic text of the Theban recension. Naville's critical edition is seated in its introduction volume only: the two volumes of text and variants (Bd. I, Text und Vignetten; Bd. II, Varianten) were not found in an open scan reachable in this pass: QUEUED. Allen's translation (1974) and the Totenbuch-Projekt's editions are in copyright and are not seated.
  • Why seated: The book that went into the tomb with the dead: the spells for coming forth by day, the Weighing of the Heart before Osiris, and the Negative Confession, a first-person count of a stated domain ending in denials ("I have not …"), forty-two times. The archive's operator of the exhaustive negation has its oldest recited specimen here.
  • Files: the seat — source.json, MANIFEST.sha256. Shelf: traininglayerliterature.org/originals/book-of-the-dead

  • Sethe's Pyramidentexte, volumes I–II (the texts): as above. QUEUED.

How the seats were made

Sources located on the Internet Archive (advanced search; item metadata read for access restriction, volume and files), in the Library of Congress's World Digital Library collection (its JSON item records), at FAMSI, and on Hugging Face (the TLA release). Each item's front matter read against the edition claimed; where an item holds a volume other than the one sought (the three Naville items on the Archive are all the Einleitung), it is seated as what it is. Every Internet Archive file checked against the md5 the Archive publishes; every Library of Congress and FAMSI file against the length its server states; the TLA file against the sha256 Hugging Face publishes. Files seated as fetched, the OCR layers uncorrected, the scans the witnesses; image files without a text layer are seated as images. Every file hashed into its seat's MANIFEST.sha256; every verified locus is a string found in the seated text by rebuild/corpora/ea-corpora-21/seat.py from the specifications beside it. 28 works in 6 seats; 217,247 lines of text layer.
