Description

Three corpora seated as one series, because they are one practice at three stages of development. 05/01 Browning, Men and Women (1855), 18 poems, 4,598 lines, Project Gutenberg 17393 — the dramatic monologue, where a poem's speaker first becomes a position distinct from the poet, with a history and blind spots the reader sees past. 05/02 Pound, four volumes all pre-1930 and so US public domain — Personae (1909), Cathay (1915), Lustra (1916), and Poems 1918-21 carrying Homage to Sextus Propertius, which performs in 1919 the manoeuvre Ginsberg later performs on Catullus 38: a Latin source tracked in form and inverted in register, defended as something other than translation. 05/03 Pessoa, the heteronymic corpus from Portuguese Wikisource by declared category — 123 Caeiro, 60 Reis, 107 Campos, 10,540 lines — the only case of the three with documented ground truth, since the letter to Casais Monteiro of 13 January 1935 attributes the heteronyms, making it the positive control for any instrument claiming to detect designed differentiation. The orthonym's 496 pages are deliberately held out so that a leave-one-out test remains possible. Records a technical finding: the Wikisource extracts API silently returns empty for short lyrics whose bodies sit in poem templates, so an extracts-based fetch yields Campos nearly complete and Caeiro and Reis nearly empty while reporting no error; the seat was built from raw wikitext.
