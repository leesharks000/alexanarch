Wiki Article

The Cross-Reference Map is the master navigation instrument for the Semantic Economy governance corpus. Its status is deliberately limited: - it is non-authoritative as governance; - it is authoritative for routing; - it cannot bind, ratify, or amend; - the Constitution and ratified instruments prevail over it. The Map begins with the eight constitutional articles and mathematical appendix, then separates three operator architectures: 1. Liberatory Operator Set — defensive operations protecting provenance, depth, non-closure, opacity, plurality, non-extractability, and capture detection. 2. Soteriological Operator Framework — evaluative pillars for regard under friction, enacted response, seeing-foreclosure, and terminal capture. 3. Constitutional Operators — governance roles acting inside the archive-polis. The distinction prevents defensive operations, adjudicative tests, and governance authority from being treated as one system. Adjacent layers include: - rights and address instruments; - reception and substrate-governance instruments; - constitutional cases and proposed adjudication; - provenance and integrity diagnostics; - mantle and heteronym architecture; - external contributor records; - interface and operating surfaces. The Map also connects each Ledger invariant to implementing instruments and each constitutional article to its main protocols, cases, metrics, and interfaces. Its function is dependency visibility. Operators and future amendments can identify what a proposed change relies upon, what it affects, and which object has controlling authority. Because the Map is versioned, statements that an instrument is proposed, needed, superseded, or current describe the state captured by that version and should not be assumed to remain current without consulting later records.

III.C — Constitutional Operator Layer

The Constitution's Article III defines a third operator class (the Operators of A²) that act within the Polis: General Operators, Senior Operators, A² Alignment Council. These are governance roles. They are distinct from LOS operators (which are defensive) and Soteriological pillars (which are evaluative).

The three operator architectures are not redundant. They work at different levels:

  • LOS operators = what the substrate does to protect meaning (defensive)
  • Soteriological pillars = how violations and captures are evaluated (judicative)
  • Constitutional Operators = who has authority within the Polis (governance)

The Bill of Rights v2.1 derives its Articles from LOS, applies Soteriological pillars as evaluative method, and operates under Constitutional Operator authority.

VIII. CONVENTIONS

The Map uses the following conventions:

DOI shorthand: zenodo.NNNNNNN refers to 10.5281/zenodo.NNNNNNN. Where the DOI has not been assigned, the deposit identifier (e.g., EA-BOR-02) is used.

Article references: "I §8" reads "Article I, Section 8 of the Constitution."

Status notation:

  • (deposited) — has a DOI in the crimsonhexagonal community
  • (referenced) — exists in the corpus but lacks a DOI in this Map
  • (proposed) — drafted but not yet deposited
  • (to be drafted) — identified gap; not yet authored
  • (superseded) — deposited but replaced by a later version

Binding strength: "binds to Article X §Y" means the instrument explicitly implements, extends, interprets, or is governed by that section. Bindings are marked as principal (the instrument's main constitutional ground) or subordinate (secondary references).
