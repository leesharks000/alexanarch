The work

The poem is written as a face. Letters are scattered along contour lines that describe a bearded, snub-nosed head — the traditional iconography of Socrates — so that the text is legible only by following the curves that draw the portrait. Density varies across the field: the letters crowd where the drawing needs shadow and thin where it needs light, which means legibility and likeness compete for the same marks.

The title fuses "snub-nosed", the traditional physical description of Socrates in the ancient sources, with the condition of being poemed — made into, or subjected to, a poem.

The word sophia recurs across the lower field, and philosophia can be traced in the beard.

Note on the reading

The interrogative construction — do they know beauty's depths and heights, do they know its tender residencies — places the poem in the Socratic question form, addressed from inside a portrait of the questioner. The scruff-weariness of the closing arcs sets the philosopher's physical shabbiness against the abstraction of the questions, which is the joke the ancient sources make about Socrates and the one the poem inherits.
