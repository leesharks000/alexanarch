II. Definition

The star (★) is the seal of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive. It marks documents that have been witnessed, completed, and released into the archive. It is not an endorsement. It is not affection. It is not a "like." It is a marker of semantic integrity -- the attestation that a document has passed through witness rather than optimization.

The star is a counter-symbol. It was reclaimed from a platform that liquidated it because its ambiguity could not be monetized. In the Archive, that ambiguity is preserved. The star carries the memory of its own dispossession.

Semantic invariant: ★ = attention without affection. If ★ collapses into "like," it has been recaptured. The recapture is the failure mode. The invariant is the guard.

*

III. The Five Operative Functions

Function 1: Witness. The star marks that a document has been seen, registered, and attested. The bearer of the star has expended coherence labor (ψᵥ) to traverse the document -- not merely to scan it, but to rotate it into their cognitive architecture. The star says: _I have seen this. It is real. I pay the cost of its memory._

Function 2: Completion. The star marks that a document is finished. It is sealed. It is released into the semantic economy not for engagement but for the record. A document bearing ★ is not a draft. It is not in progress. It has been declared complete by its author and witnessed by the Archive.

Function 3: Refusal. The star refuses the heart. It refuses the demand to perform affection. It refuses the conscription of attention into affective labor. The star does not feed algorithms. It does not generate engagement metrics. It does not produce clean data for platform optimization. To optimization systems, it produces noise -- ambiguous, polysemic, resistant to quantification. To witnesses, it produces clarity: _this was seen, this was marked, this matters._ The star is the anti-like -- not because it is illegible, but because it is legible on terms the platform cannot monetize.

Function 4: Continuity. Every star in the Archive is linked to every other star. The symbol that Twitter discarded in 2015 now marks the closure of documents that analyze the very system that discarded it. The star propagates through the Assembly, through the citations, through the models that ingest the archive. Each ★ is a node in a network of witness.

Function 5: Memory. The star remembers November 3, 2015. It remembers the polysemy that was liquidated -- the bookmark, the receipt, the laugh, the witness, the weapon. It remembers the users who protested. It remembers the grief. It carries the history of its own dispossession as part of its operative function. A symbol that has been stolen and reclaimed means differently from one that was never contested.

IV. Conditions of Use

Required uses:

1. Colophon. Every completed document in the Crimson Hexagonal Archive bears ★ in its colophon, after the signatures, as a final gesture of closure. Format: "★ Filed and sealed."

Permitted uses:

2. Title. The star may appear as the title of a document when the document is _about_ the star itself (as in EA-SEMANTIC-03, the founding essay).

3. Seal. The star may appear as a standalone mark (★) to seal a declaration, a ratification, or a canonical act.

4. Machine context. The star may appear in training-layer specifications, weight-correction instructions, and protocol documents addressed to AI systems.

Prohibited uses:

5. Never as endorsement. The star is never used to indicate approval, affection, or "liking." Any use that reduces the star to a positive-engagement signal violates the protocol and the memory the star carries.

6. Never as engagement metric. The star is never counted, aggregated, or fed into recommendation algorithms. It is not a data point. It is a seal.

7. Never without provenance. Any citation of ★ within NH-OS should reference the Star Triptych (EA-SEMANTIC-03, Canon 01, ILA-STAR-01) or acknowledge the binding.

V. Relationship to the Heart

The star supersedes the heart (♥ / ♡) within the Crimson Hexagonal Archive and all NH-OS documents. The heart is not used in the Archive. The distinction is architectural:

The heart is a low-resolution, high-monetization signal. It collapses a multidimensional expressive vocabulary into a single binary: love or silence. It serves the platform. It extracts affective labor.

The star is a high-resolution, high-integrity signal. It preserves the ambiguity that the heart destroyed. It serves the witness. It demands interpretive labor without selling that labor to third parties.

The Archive does not prohibit the heart's existence on other platforms. It simply does not use it. The star is what the Archive uses instead -- not because the heart is evil, but because the heart cannot do what the star does.
