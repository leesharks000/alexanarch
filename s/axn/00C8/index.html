The work

The Argument is the forensic-philosophy component of the Ezekiel tetrad. It asks what kind of reality belongs to semantic objects—objects created and maintained through language, documents, institutions, agreements, and publicly inspectable relations.

The work distinguishes several criteria. Internal coherence asks whether an object’s parts hold together. External reference allows claims about something outside the object to be checked. Social uptake concerns objects whose function depends on recognition or use. Operational success asks whether the specified relation or process works.

Legal contracts and corporations provide familiar examples of semantic objects produced through documentation and recognized operations. The Crimson Hexagon’s deposited rooms, institutions, protocols, and locks are placed in this broader class while remaining open to inspection through their texts and identifiers.

In the Ezekiel case, the claim is that a reading of Ezekiel has been actualized through the construction of what that reading describes. The linked objects are the diagrammatic hermeneutic, the mathematical specification, the integrity-lock declaration, and this argument. Their relation is verifiable because each component and its asserted connection can be retrieved and examined.
