The work

Split the Adam is the founding song of Maybe Space Baby Garden Lanes. The record attributes the song to Viola Arquette / Bedouin Princess and documents Lee Sharks as the original witness, registrar, and author of the surrounding phenomenological and architectural apparatus.

The work begins from a song-as-event: a woman sang into a man’s ear on a couch, once, and the event was not recorded. The charter treats this bodily transmission as co-primary with the composition. Breath, proximity, hearing, and singular witness are not incidental background; they are the material substrate through which the room is organized.

The phrase “split the Adam” carries three convergent fractures. Cardiac rupture names the personal division of a heart. Edenic fracture names Adam, Eve, covenant, and separation. Nuclear fission hears “Adam” as “atom,” the division of what had been treated as indivisible. The song’s force lies in allowing these registers to sound together without reducing one to a metaphor for the others.

The record distinguishes multiple manifestations. The original couch transmission is unrepeated and unarchived. A TikTok or early performer recording is a later manifestation. A Suno AI rendering approximates aspects of composition and arrangement but not the original medium of breath on skin and mouth to ear. None of the later surfaces replaces the song-event.

Consent and authorship are explicit. The singer has authorized lyric quotation and architectural integration within a defined scope. The Singer’s Prerogative reserves her right to make a better recording or revise the public musical form. Singer’s Sovereignty exceeds the room: withdrawal of consent changes what the architecture may display, and the room may not convert her person into one of its possessions.

This record should be cited for the song, its manifestations, its convergence map, and the consent apparatus. Creator metadata should not collapse songwriter/singer, witness, registrar, and apparatus author into one undifferentiated role.
