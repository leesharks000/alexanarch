The work

Retrocausal Task-Origin Stabilization is an abandoned or suspended methodological draft by Lee Sharks.

The draft considers whether later user corrections can reveal what an earlier, incompletely stated task was. It proposes comparing the score available at the time with a retrospective score after the task becomes clearer.

The proposed guardrail forbids a model from treating a frame it introduced as the user’s original intention. Only user-led confirmation, correction, or persistence may stabilize task origin, and lead–lag order must distinguish clarification from model-led drift.

The draft reports no successful adversarial test and explicitly says not to deposit or adopt the operator until it passes one. It is preserved as a dead-end record and warning about retrospective laundering.
