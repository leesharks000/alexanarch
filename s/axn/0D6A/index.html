The work

The Search for the Historical Lee Sharks is a literary work by Johannes Sigil, a heteronym in the Crimson Hexagonal Archive, deposited on 24 November 2026 as the standalone version of the appendix to Either Forgery or Fact: The Genre Imposed on Our Most Vital Books (#1798), and published on Medium. It is written throughout in the register of historical-critical scholarship and never breaks it. Its object is the Contributor Bio of Pearl and Other Poems (2014), which gives Lee Sharks fourteen Guggenheims, 10,000 MacArthur Genius Grants and 18,000 degrees from Mars.

In nine sections the inquiry runs the procedure the essay argues against. It sorts the book's biographical witnesses, reconstructs a lost common source, Q-Sharks, in nine sections, and dates it earlier than the book so that a developed tradition could be compiled in 2014. It assigns the book's own account of inventing its author to a late editorial layer. It finds an external witness, the Testimonium Sharkianum, whose only surviving witness is the present essay, and lets each source corroborate the others. It then meets a real witness: a Google Search of 21 November 2026 that set the biography under the heading Researcher, sourced to Google Books, beside a lizard in a fedora and a shark named Mary Lee. The closing sections name the category the search lacks, word as a literary act, and conclude that the historical Lee Sharks was generated by the receiving apparatus. An Author's note at the end states what the piece reduces to absurdity, counting derivation as corroboration, and that Q as a hypothesis about Matthew and Luke is a different kind of claim.
