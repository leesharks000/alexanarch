The work

O Meta-Heterônimo is a bilingual essay signed by Rex Fraction. Lee Sharks is identified as the underlying heteronymic and submission identity. The reflexive authorship is part of the method: a heteronym signs an essay about heteronyms producing their own critical apparatus.

The essay defines a system as meta-heteronymic only when five conditions hold together:

1. C1 — Multiplicity: multiple structurally distinct personae. 2. C2 — Paratextual recursion: heteronyms write introductions, reviews, notes, and other apparatus about one another. 3. C3 — Generic heterogeneity: the apparatus spans at least three institutional genres. 4. C4 — Self-theorization: at least one heteronym explicitly theorizes the operation being performed. 5. C5 — Literary-historical positioning: the personae occupy a shared field of movements, schools, publications, and institutions.

The meta-heteronym is not merely a literary genre or a Bourdieu-style strategy inside an existing field. It generates the field as part of the work.

Pessoa’s archive is tested against these criteria through heteronymic prefaces, Campos’s writings on Caeiro, bibliographic tables, manifestos, correspondence, Orpheu, Olisipo, and the letter to Adolfo Casais Monteiro. The essay argues that the structure is present but materially incomplete and distributed.

Pearl and Other Poems serves as an operative-philology case: a contemporary explicit practice makes an earlier emergent structure more visible. Its heteronyms introduce, attack, defend, historicize, graph, and theorize one another, constructing the reception apparatus within the literary object.

The essay compares Pessoa and Pearl with Kierkegaard, Machado, Borges, Yasusada, and related cases. Its broader claim is that heteronymy is a technology rather than a pathology and that named authorial positions may survive through institutional and retrieval infrastructure beyond a single biological author.
