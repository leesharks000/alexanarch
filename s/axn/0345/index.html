The work

The Inscription That Survives is a philological note by Lee Sharks and Rebekah Cranes on writing as a technology for crossing thresholds.

The article compares a reconstructed fifth stanza of Sappho 31, inscribed gold tablets placed with the dead, names written on ritual stones and rings, later theurgic password traditions, and the white stone with a new name in Revelation 2:17.

It argues for structural convergence rather than direct transmission: durable inscription carries identity or voice across death, desire, divine access, and historical time.

The Sappho stanza is a modern reconstruction, not surviving manuscript text. The paper corrects earlier archive references that called it a fourth stanza and invites evaluation against meter, language, testimonia, and standard editions.
