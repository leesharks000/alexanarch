The work

The Gate Was Never Limbo is an operative-philology article by Lee Sharks about two poems attributed to Jack Feist.

The study argues that “Socrates — Let My Teacher Go From Hell” performs an apostrophic rescue of Socrates from the First Circle of Dante’s Inferno. Its closing declaration—Socrates was not merely in Limbo but was the gate—recasts a theological placement as an aperture open to later poetic action. The article reads the poem’s five sections as a reaching hand and relates the gesture to traditions of the Harrowing of Hell.

“Snub-Poemed” is treated as the companion embodiment. Scattered letters form the snub-nosed face of Socrates, so the material arrangement of language becomes the retrieved figure rather than an illustration of him. The study situates the work within concrete poetry and visual philology.

The poems precede the archive’s later terminology by more than a decade. Sharks therefore calls them structural anticipations: earlier works whose latent form becomes legible through a later theory. This is a retrocausal interpretive claim, not a claim that historical events or Dante’s canonical text were literally altered.
