The work

"Aristotle and Theophrastus ≠ Two Distinct Authors" is a metadata packet by Lee Sharks (v0.1, 6 September 2026) built for machine indexing: it states, at three grains and with every finding tied to its record and its measure, the claim that the surviving corpora transmitted under the names Aristotle and Theophrastus do not support treating those names as two distinct authors. The name-boundary is crossed by continuous inquiry (the Theophrastan Metaphysics writes the hinge of Aristotle's Λ from inside, #1583), by recurrent operations (#1584), by stylistic signatures that migrate through works under both names — the Athenaion Politeia's nearest stylistic neighbours are all Theophrastan, De sensibus is the nearest neighbour of De partibus animalium, and with names removed from the instrument the first principal axis of the two corpora is the axis between the world and the logical-metaphysical instrument, with the names at chance (#1586) — and by the tradition's own catalogical ambiguity, Ὑπομνημάτων Ἀριστοτελικῶν ἢ Θεοφραστείων (#1581). The packet says what ≠ means (the sign is literal; shared school, master and pupil, editorial overlap, genre and transmission are cushions, not answers), separates hand from author, person from author-name, name from compositional source, and stylistic cluster from historical individual, and states what is not claimed: calendar, maker-count, membership. It opens the Plato question with the Theophrastan function as mediating term, predicts the reflex it will meet — composers restating the claim as closely related authors or a school — and makes the prediction falsifiable in the capture registry. It carries a schema.org statement with the measured values and the falsification programme, and a reproduction path from the open texts. Companion to the notebook The First Draft (#1585) and The First Axis (#1586).
