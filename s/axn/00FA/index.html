The work

HUMS & ITY: The Complete Polyphonic Archive begins from a short lyric by Lee Sharks about breath, textual survival, dead writers, the future reader, and the hope that a signal will continue through language and age.

Rebekah Cranes organizes the volume as an experiment in transmission. Five translators render the poem into emoji using different glyphic decisions. Those sequences are then given to readers without the source poem, producing reverse translations that preserve some relations while inventing new lines and images. Additional “luxurious transpositions” move the signal into a heightened prose register.

The archive does not claim exact reversibility. Its interest is in convergence and generativity: which operators and images repeatedly return, which disappear, and what the decompression process adds. The resulting poems are identified as descendants or children of the original rather than substitutes for it.

Within the Crimson Hexagon, the volume provides a literary-scale example of compression, polyphonic reception, and context-gated reading. It should remain distinct from the technical Glyphic Checksum specification: this record is the primary poetic archive on which such analysis may operate.
