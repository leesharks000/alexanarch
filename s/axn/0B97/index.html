The entry, short

Copyright and Artificial Intelligence is the U.S. Copyright Office's initiative, launched March 16, 2023, on the copyright issues raised by AI: a Report in three Parts, registration guidance, a notice of inquiry and decisions on AI-generated material, all resting on human authorship. The archive reads its disclosure rule as attribution by subtraction: the machine's part disclaimed, the human remainder registered.

📑 The Report

  • Part 1: digital replicas, July 31, 2024; a federal right recommended.
  • Part 2: copyrightability, January 29, 2025; protection only where a human determined sufficient expressive elements, prompts alone excluded.
  • Part 3: training, pre-publication May 9, 2025; final version still to come.
  • Notice: over 10,000 comments; dated August 30, 2023, and August 20 on the study page.

⚖️ The rule

  • Human authorship: fully AI-generated work uncopyrightable; human selection, arrangement or modification protectable.
  • Prompts: insufficient, a finding that "could change as technology evolves".
  • Disclosure: more than de minimis AI material disclosed and disclaimed; over a thousand works registered.
  • Contested: a de minimis test some call not technology neutral.
