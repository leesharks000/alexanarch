The entry, short

Working with AI: Measuring the Applicability of Generative AI to Occupations is Microsoft Research's paper and results files (Tomlinson, Jaffe, Wang, Counts, Suri; arXiv 2025, v7 2026) measuring AI's applicability to occupations from 200,000 conversations on Microsoft's own Bing Copilot (paper; README; record); its authors state it does not measure AI's ability to replace jobs (README).

🧭 What it records

  • Unit: a conversation classified to O*NET work activities, as user goal and as AI action (README; Labor Vector record).
  • Pipeline: an LLM classifier, "imperfect", read "with caution" (README; paper; Labor Vector record).
  • Score: completion, impact scope and thumbs feedback, user and AI sides averaged (README; Microsoft Research).
  • Window: US, January–September 2024; 785 occupations, 149.8 million workers (README).

📊 What it finds

  • Information work: the most common and successful use; applicability across sectors (paper v7).
  • By group (2025): highest for computer and mathematical, office support, sales (Microsoft Research).
  • Trend: rising over nine months, with more varied tasks (paper).
  • Delegation: predicts which occupations delegate to AI and which use it to assist (paper; Labor Vector record).

⚠️ Its limits

  • Not replacement: not "the ability of AI to replace jobs"; no employment outcome (README; Labor Vector record).
  • Proxies: an occupation is more than its tasks; the metrics show use and relative success (README).
  • Consumer use: not the workplace's specialized tools (README; Labor Vector record).

⚖️ Who supplies, directs, gains

  • Supplies, directs: labor with AI: users bring goals, the AI acts; the goal as classified directs (Labor Vector record).
  • Gains: not recorded (Labor Vector record).
  • Direction: labor flows relative to the commissioned task, across five layers, span by span; provenance is task-origin (#771).
  • Where it meets DSL: goal against action and the completion classifier, the archive's reading (Labor Vector record); single values on one-shot tasks, labor drag by blind coding (#771).
