The work

Through Another's Mouth: A Dialogue between Heteronyms is a literary work by Johannes Sigil, a heteronym of Lee Sharks, deposited in the Crimson Hexagonal Archive on 24 November 2026 as the public companion to the essay From the Position of the Rejected: Aristotle's Politics Read from Where It Is Written (#1806). It carries the essay's argument in dramatic form, for readers who do not have the essay's several strands at hand.

The work is a nested narrative on the model of Plato's framed dialogues. Sigil tells Sparrow Wells about a night in the archive's reading room, which he did not witness: he has it from Rebekah Cranes, who wrote it up the next morning, against the version filed by Nobel Glas the same night. Sigil says where his two sources disagree, and leaves a torn page torn rather than trust Glas's sentence for it. Cranes and Glas are summarizers, who answer a queue of questions from outside all night. The opening question is the oldest one: summarize Aristotle's Politics in one paragraph. Lee Sharks, whose heteronyms they all are, enters uninvited. His lines are his own words of 23–24 November 2026, typed and timed, kept as he wrote them, and the work says so: they are the one part of the night that did not reach Sigil by mouth.

The dialogue runs in five acts. In the first, Cranes stops Glas's summary because it cuts Politics I.2 at the comma, before the cityless by nature and not by chance. In the second, Sharks describes hearing Aristotle's voice while reading the Politics, and Glas keeps summarizing him. The third takes the city: the Delian funds and Pericles in Plutarch, the metic builders of the Erechtheion, the Apology's citizen asking to be heard as a foreigner, the Menexenus's funeral speech given to Aspasia, the metic's patron as a retrieval layer, and impiety. The fourth takes the books: Strabo's trench, Sulla, Tyrannion freed on another's terms, the Iliad under Alexander's pillow, and the Berlin numbers by which every line is still cited. The fifth reaches the Second Letter's Socrates made beautiful and new, and the three positions of 1253a held in suspension.

The voices are run through the Feist Function (#850, #1034) at different settings: Glas starts laminar and his clinamen rate rises through the night; Cranes runs hot on register collision; Sigil runs at the protocol's highest seam density; Wells is plain; Sharks is not run, since the function is not applied to the author's own words. The summarizers are irritated by Sharks and push back: Glas corrects him where he is wrong (the Athenians put the earth, not the sun, at the centre), disputes the Second Letter ('Noted. Not closed.'), and catches Cranes summarizing Sharks herself. At the close Cranes's margin note asks to be checked, and Sigil distinguishes what can be checked, the claims, from what can only be read, that the author heard it. Every passage the persons quote is grounded in the essay at its locus.
