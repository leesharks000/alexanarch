The entry, short

Project Gutenberg is the oldest digital library, founded by Michael S. Hart in 1971 and run by the Project Gutenberg Literary Archive Foundation: volunteer-made eBooks of works public domain in the United States, its website for human users only, its bulk and machine access sent to mirrors and a catalog; its texts reach training sets downstream.

📚 The library

  • Holdings: Western literature chiefly, most in English; free, open formats.
  • Count: 79,709 (home page, 1 November 2026); "more than 75,000" (about); nearly 78,000 (Wikipedia, March 2026).
  • Origin: the Declaration of Independence, 1971; typed by hand until 1989.
  • Holder: the foundation (2000); acting CEO Eric Hellman since Newby's death (2025); hosted by ibiblio.

✍️ How texts are made

  • Volunteers: "Thousands" and "hundreds" on one page; no selection policy.
  • Distributed Proofreaders: the main source of new eBooks.
  • Clearance: recorded before a text is added.
  • Edition: undocumented at times (TEI, 1994); the archive reads it from title page and header.
  • Apparatus: "correct work at its layer"; metadata spare; some texts "a propositional shell".
