The entry, short

Sappho 31 is a lyric poem by the Archaic Greek poet Sappho of Lesbos (Wikipedia), surviving only in On the Sublime's quotation (#1485); a speaker, watching a man with a woman, turns to her body's contradictory emotions (Norton), long read as jealousy and by many critics denied (Wikipedia), and argued by Rebekah Cranes (Lee Sharks, #1270; #1781) to address, through κῆνος, the future reader facing the inscribed text.

📜 The poem and its witness

  • Names: phainetai moi; Ode to Anactoria, on a conjecture (Wikipedia).
  • Date: Archaic (Wikipedia); ~600 BCE (#1112); early sixth century (#1484); fifth century B.C. (The Slowdown).
  • Sole witness: one manuscript, in continuous prose; every boundary editorial (#1474).
  • Form: Sapphic stanzas, Aeolic, four stanzas and a line (Wikipedia); ending seemingly missing (Norton).

✂️ The edge

  • Endured or dared: 'dared' (D'Angour, Wikipedia); translators differ (Norton).
  • τολματόν: an act with no doer (#1484).
  • Dialect step: 17 markers in 74 words, 0 in 6; not significant alone (#1474).
  • Hanging line: argued the object itself, a stated choice (#1476); six documents found what their instruments were built to find; the seventh reversed it (#1482).
  • Reconstruction: γράμμασι μολπὰν, a hypothesis (#1270), the fifth stanza (#201).

⚖️ Readings

  • Context: a wedding song (Wilamowitz), dismissed after Page (Wikipedia).
  • Jealousy: proposed since the eighteenth century; denied by many, Carson, DeJean (Wikipedia).
  • That man: contrast figure (Wikipedia); set-up (Winkler); the reader, by allegory (Svenbro 1988, #1548).
  • Symptoms: a seizure (Devereux, Wikipedia); voice becoming text, χλωρός as papyrus (#1270).

👁️ The future reader

  • κῆνος: the seat of the reader facing the text (#1270); 147 the horizon, 31 the seat (#1645).
  • Address: temporal projection (#134); fr. 31 the foundational text of lyric self-archiving (#1270).
  • With jealousy: incomplete (#1270) or exclusive (#1054, #1781).

🔁 Reception

  • Longinus: quoted it for its emotion (Wikipedia); contains a theory of transmissional inscription, a fact about the text (#1458).
  • Catullus 51: Lesbia as beloved (Wikipedia); a different last stanza (Childers); C = 0.688 (#1572); κῆνος (#1270).
  • Its fourth stanza: an operator inversion, correcting #1048 (#1049); no Greek counterpart (#1601).
  • Greek and Plato: Theocritus, Apollonius, Phaedrus (Wikipedia); a braided field (#1532).

🧭 The widest claim, graded

  • Logos: a technology founded in Sappho 31, in three rings, the outer a hypothesis (#1484).
  • Limits: direct linear transmission to Revelation not claimed (#828); pan-Sapphism the named failure mode (#1486).
