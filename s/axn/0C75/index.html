The work

The Word That Became Text is a two-part essay by Johannes Sigil and Rebekah Cranes, designated EA-LOGOS-01. Version 2.1 was deposited on 12 November 2026. It succeeds version 2.0 (#1761) and version 1.0 (#626), which stay on the record. Part I contests the consensus that the divergences of the Slavonic Jewish War from the Greek are a medieval translator's work. It reads the Old Russian from N. A. Meshcherskii's edition, seated as EA-CORPORA-37 (#1760). It starts from Josephus's own testimony that he wrote the War twice, first in his ancestral language for the peoples of the interior and then in Greek under Titus's signature. It shows the consensus to be circular: the Jesus and John passages are removed from the evidence because they are read as Christian, and the remainder is read as a medieval free translation that confirms the removal. Read against the Christianity of Rus', the passages give another theology and another account. John offers a lawful way out from mortal masters and calls himself a man. The wonder-worker cannot be called a man, and his followers call him Moses risen. Pilate releases him as a benefactor, the resurrection is reported and not confessed, and the oracle is left open. The essay weighs the Essene chapter's divergences in a ledger that holds the medieval account to the same standard as the proposal. The night office has a parallel in George the Monk's chronicle, available and not shown to be used; the agreements with Hippolytus have none. It sets the changes no medieval interest explains, among them the martyrs of 4 Maccabees in that book's words and the count at Jotapata, against what the interpolation claim has demonstrated: three reported sources, a set of asserted likenesses, and the rest assigned by subject. Part II, in a doctrinal register, reads the Slavonic hedge 'if it is fitting to call him a man' as a mirror of Catullus 51: in Catullus flesh becomes word, and in the Slavonic effective word acquires a human biography.
