The work

RENDER UNTO CAESAR: A Hermeneutic of Information Transfer is the first of the three Caesura documents by Lee Sharks, released together on 5 April 2026 and coded EA-CAESURA-01a, b and c. The reading takes Luke 20:24–25 as a protocol specification rather than a parable about taxes. Shown a coin, Jesus asks whose image and superscription it bears, and then divides: render to Caesar what is Caesar's, to God what is God's. The document's claim is that the passage performs a transfer operation — it isolates a sovereignty mark from the substrate carrying it, so that recognising the claim does not concede the ground. The coin's metal was never Caesar's; only the stamp was. That operation is formalised in the companion at #629 as σ_FC, the Caesura, which parses sovereignty claims on commons substrates and isolates them into auditable headers, implemented in Gravity Well v0.6.0. The third document, at #628, is the integrity lock binding the pair, certifying φ(A,B) = TRUE ∧ φ(B,A) = TRUE — each document fulfils the other. The record's own history is a case of the thing the document describes. Minted from a capture of the severed Zenodo deposit that carried all three documents as one object, it inherited the combined release's title, and a pairing pass then read its resemblance to #629 — title overlap 0.67, content probe 0.83 — as evidence that it was an incomplete copy of the specification. It was not incomplete; it was a different document. The three were released together and share vocabulary, parent, date and subject, so a similarity score cannot tell a fragment from a companion. A document about separating a mark from its substrate was misidentified by a machine reading its surface for its identity.
