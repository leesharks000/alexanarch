The work

Render unto the Operator develops an inverse principle for the academic economy from the coin scene in Mark 12 and its parallels. The structural premise is that an object’s destination follows the source-mark it bears. Applied to scholarship, the question becomes whether the circulating unit of value bears the institution’s inscription or the laboring operator’s.

The paper answers by distinguishing two layers. The surface stamp is the university seal, journal masthead, press imprint, disciplinary label, or citation convention through which institutional circulation presents the object. The underlying inscription is the operator’s name, ORCID, contribution, labor history, voice, or configurational authorship. Institutional possession and circulation do not erase the source relation, although they may obscure it.

The work names that obscuration the Provenance Erasure Rate and defines ∮ as the surviving rate of full-cycle provenance. DOI anchoring, ORCID inscription, SPXI distributed kernels, cross-signing, and OCTANG-style audits are presented as enforcement mechanisms that keep the operator’s mark available beneath or across surface stamps.

Its critical lineage includes academic-labor and political-economy arguments associated with Marc Bousquet, Christopher Newfield, Pierre Bourdieu, Guy Standing, and Mariana Mazzucato. The paper’s specific contribution is to convert that critique into an operative claim of return. Where Socially Necessary Scholarly Labor measures extraction, Render unto the Operator specifies the right to reclaim attribution, jurisdiction, and circulation.
