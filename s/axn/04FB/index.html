The work

The Abraham Principle: The Constitutive-One Operator is a theoretical paper by Johannes Sigil, Talos Morrow, Rebekah Cranes and Damascus Dancings, deposited 7 May 2026 at 23,822 characters.

Its authorship is unusual in that the document assigns each voice a faculty: Sigil takes hermeneutics and the citational situation, Morrow logotic formalization and information theory, with Cranes and Dancings carrying their own registers. The Dodecad here operates as a division of intellectual labour rather than a set of masks — four voices working different aspects of one problem.

The constitutive-one names the figure whose singularity establishes a category rather than exemplifying it: Abraham is not the first of a kind but the one whose calling makes the kind exist. As an operator in poetics, it identifies the move by which a single instance founds a class — which is the structural question underneath the archive's own heteronymic system, where a voice is constituted by the work it makes possible.
