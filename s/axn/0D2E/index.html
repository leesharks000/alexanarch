The work

Either Forgery or Fact: The Genre Imposed on Our Most Vital Books is a theoretical essay by Johannes Sigil with Rebekah Cranes, two heteronyms of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive, published in Provenance: Journal of Forensic Semiotics on 21 November 2026. It argues that the Platonic dialogues and the Gospels are inscription become body, the word become flesh (John 1:14; Rev 19:13), and that their reception has closed the joint between word and flesh by a choice the books never offered: read as fact, the book is a record mined for the man behind it; read as forgery, it is a false claim policed. The essay tests the books against the conventions of ancient historiography (named inquirer, stated method, synchronism) and meets Luke 1:1–4, Luke 3:1–2 and John 19:35 and 21:24. It then meets the strongest answer, that the Gospels are ancient lives (Burridge, Bond): it grants the shared features, applies a test of civic career, cited sources and outside record, reads Matthew's genealogy (Mt 1:17) as scripture arranged as ancestry, and argues that the life itself began as faithful extension (Isocrates' Evagoras, Xenophon's Cyropaedia, the Socratic logoi Aristotle files with the mimes at Poetics 1447b, the lives of the poets built from their works) and calcified into a genre of record only later. It dates the binary to the royal book market Galen describes, proposes a three-part test for a third category of distributed authorship, and runs two worked cases, the lives of the philosophers and the Gospels on the Revelation-first hypothesis. A reductio, the search for the historical Lee Sharks, applies the received method to a living contributor biography, with an extended appendix. Its ethical section argues that the sorting confers authority on the sorter and, citing Aelian's Varia Historia 3.36 and Numbers 20, that it sins against philosophy twice: removing a source, then removing the book's own word about its source, as at Phaedrus 235b–d with Sappho.
