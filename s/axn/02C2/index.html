The work

The Future Beloved proposes a temporal theory of lyric address.

Earlier models tend to classify the lyric “you” as:

- a historical or dramatic addressee; - a rhetorical construction; - an absent or inanimate apostrophic object.

The paper adds a fourth possibility: the “you” is a future reader.

A lyric becomes a temporal projection mechanism when its form carries an affective pattern forward until a later reader activates it. The reader does not merely interpret the poem; the reader occupies the address position the poem made available.

The paper calls this recursive reading: becoming the addressee anticipated by the text.

Its primary case is Sappho 31. The physiological sequence is read as a specification or recognition device rather than only a report of jealousy. A future reader who recognizes the sequence in another body completes a second node in the circuit.

The argument engages historical poetics without simply restoring timeless universal lyric. It accepts that “lyric reading” is historically constructed while asking what formal properties allowed that construction to become powerful.

The archive’s phrase retrocausal canon formation names the corresponding reception claim: a later reading can complete a work and thereby alter what the earlier inscription becomes in the canon.

The article extends the theory through later poems involving Socrates, where the apparent addressee, named subject, and operative future reader occupy different positions.
