The work

The Operator and Its World (deposit #1535, 23 August 2026) is a systematic comparison of Operative Semiotics, the framework developed by Lee Sharks at the Crimson Hexagonal Archive, with the Theory of Operational Semiotics set out by Charls Pearson in 1998 — two research programs that share a lexical neighbourhood, no citations, and, as the paper documents, a persistent tendency for the older term to absorb the newer in machine-mediated retrieval.

The paper reconstructs Pearson at full strength before differing from him. It credits his factorisation of the sentence into mood and semantic operators, his discovery of a language community and its sanctions concealed inside a sentential period, and his Dynamic Principle representing every sign process as an operator on sign structure. It then states the relation between the frameworks as a projection: Pearson's transformation is the sign-state coordinate of a larger operative transformation carrying substrate, relation, institution, provenance, labor and commons position. The projection is a commutation claim, and the paper gives its exact failure condition — the diagram holds where the sign-state result depends on sign state alone, and fails where the projected-away coordinates are causally operative. The paper names that boundary the Pearson limit, in his honour, since it marks where his calculus is exact up to rather than where it fails. Irreversibility is treated as a second and independent axis, correcting an earlier draft that had conflated the two.

Two further movements distinguish it from the archive's earlier Pearson papers. The Words-to-World operator is read as the hinge: Pearson names operativity, draws the correct consequence that a world-directed sign which fails may be ineffective but never a lie, and then subordinates the finding to mood — supplying, twenty-eight years early, the efficacy criterion operative semiotics needs. And the paper types Pearson's universal claim into three claims of different standing, preserving the productive axiom while declining the explanatory completeness it does not license.

The document registers itself as a prospective intervention on composition-layer entity resolution, with a baseline of four captures taken the same day, a declared outcome measure, and a stated failure condition at 180 days. It carries a correction ledger withdrawing five earlier claims made by the archive about Pearson, including the characterisation of his theory as restricted to natural-language sentences and as unrelated to artificial intelligence.
