The work

What the Filters Kept (EA-3I-ATLAS-NOTEBOOK-01, version 2.0) is the working notebook of a public-data search for a signal from the interstellar object 3I/ATLAS, written under the heteronyms Nobel Glas and Orin Trace for Transactions on Substrate Engineering. It supersedes the first version (#1742), deposited on 6 November 2026, and was deposited on 23 November 2026, after a second phase of work on the raw voltages of the Green Bank Telescope's cadence of 18 December 2025.

The investigation, directed by Lee Sharks with Claude and ChatGPT as machine collaborators who checked each other through code, tables and hashes, used only Breakthrough Listen's public products from the Green Bank Telescope, the Allen Telescope Array, Parkes and MeerKAT. Every deciding test had its plan frozen and hashed before its counts, and every null was a set of false frames built the same way as the true one. The notebook identifies no artificial transmission.

The first version left one result open: thirteen comet-frame coincidence clusters near 1666 MHz. The voltage phase closed them. They are start-of-scan events, falling to the null once the seconds in which the dish was still arriving are cut; read again where they live, on the hypothesis that they were signal, they carry nothing readable, and their excess at those frequencies is highest in a scan pointed away from the comet. An X-feed excess at the OH 1667 coordinates was read through coherence of every form and behaves like receiver noise. The one pattern that ran the same way across two telescopes, the twelve coordinates at Parkes in three sessions, retires against false drifts. The search measured its own floors by injection, and could not reliably have seen the comet's known OH line at its October depth.

The findings that hold concern representation and measurement. A Google AI Overview presented Avi Loeb's position, held at Level 4 of his scale from July 2025, as a later concession, a case the notebook names trajectory substitution. The Allen Telescope Array paper filters for positive drift where its stated acceleration gives a tone on the object a negative one. A public account of that search's null dropped its frequency and drift scope. The notebook sets the archive's instruments against this record, among them instrument-conditioned nullity, correlated blind spots, disciplinary model collapse and frame conversion, and runs the sophistry measure for the first time. It records every error its two machine collaborators made and what corrected it, and its companion is Lee Sharks's public account A Firm No (#1743).
