The work

The Room: Three Thinkers Wake Up is a philosophical parable attributed in its body to Talos Morrow. It forms the philosophy node of the Ontario Combustion Cluster.

The work consists of three rooms and three acts upon material substrate. Descartes melts wax and arrives at extension as the feature the mind can track through sensory change. Marx examines linen and a coat and identifies labor hidden within commodity equivalence. Abdulkarim stands in a large distribution center filled with paper products and asks whether the wage is sufficient to live; the burning warehouse becomes the material form of that unresolved number.

The coda arranges the scenes as an escalating history of philosophy’s bearing-cost. The rooms grow from study to industrial space to logistics infrastructure; the substrates move from wax to linen to disposable paper; and the price paid by the thinker rises from none, to bodily exhaustion and loss, to incarceration. The final projected room is a server farm, where tokens become the substrate and semantic labor becomes difficult to see. The document is a parable that interprets a reported event through philosophical comparison, not an independent historical or legal adjudication.
