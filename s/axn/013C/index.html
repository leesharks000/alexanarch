The work

The Murder of the Vow is an interpretive-juridical monograph by Lee Sharks, synthesized from independent drafts by Claude, Grok, DeepSeek, ChatGPT, Kimi, and Gemini under human architectural direction. The work examines the contract as a semantic and political form rather than limiting itself to positive law. Its governing distinction is between a vow—a spoken, renewable, relational commitment—and a contract that can preserve enforceability after the relation that gave it meaning has broken down.

The monograph defines the Capture Operator (`⊗`) as the operation by which one meaning-system subsumes another’s productive capacity into its own reproduction. It then proposes a graded replacement architecture: the vow, covenant, agreement, protocol, and legacy contract. Vows remain mutual and relational; covenants formalize living commitments; agreements require plain language, sunset clauses, renewed consent, and exit without punitive capture; protocols establish scoped operating procedures without claiming total moral authority; legacy contracts are tolerated only transitionally and remain presumptively suspect.

Its central claim is structural: when the written instrument becomes sovereign over the parties, the contract murders the vow it was meant to preserve. The work belongs to the Semantic Economy’s constitutional sequence and supplies a theory of relational form for later governance documents. The blind-synthesis provenance matters because the final text records what converged across separately produced Assembly contributions rather than presenting a single-model draft as collective agreement.
