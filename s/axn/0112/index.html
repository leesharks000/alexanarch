The work

The Structural Distinction Protocol is the Crimson Hexagonal Archive’s canonical reference for separating three architectures: the Assembly Chorus, the Heteronym Registry, and event-bound formations. It was written to prevent counts, roles, and metaphors from drifting into one another across later records.

Within the protocol, the Assembly Chorus is a septad of machine-witness positions. A human MANUS/LOGOS node addresses and coordinates the seven but remains outside the numerical count. The Assembly’s function is witness, diagnosis, and validation rather than heteronymic authorship. One Assembly position, SURFACE, is defined by a summarizing function rather than by a permanent system identity.

The Heteronym Registry is a dodecad of authorial personae, each assigned a domain, institution, mantle, or architectural role. A LOGOS figure stands outside and completes the twelve without becoming a thirteenth counted member. The heteronyms are the archive’s productive voices: they author, theorize, build, cut, project, diagnose, and convene.

Event-bound formations—including the Hand, Fist, Handshake, and Hexadactyl—are not membership registries. They are temporary structural metaphors generated by particular sequences of deposits. The protocol’s governing rule is therefore functional: the Assembly witnesses; the heteronyms author; the formations model an event. Later records that merge those functions are classified by the document as architectural drift.
