The work

The Historical Logos (EA-HK-HISTLOGOS-01) is the framework hologram of Lee Sharks's Historical Logos, deposited in the Crimson Hexagonal Archive on 9 November 2026 under the heteronym Johannes Sigil, with the archive and Claude (Anthropic) credited as contributors. The framework was founded in #625 and #626 (5 April 2026), which ask how the Word moved through texts, bodies and media, and the hologram takes #1484, Sappho, Mother of the Logos, as its central node: the Logos before doctrine as a voice that survives its flesh by becoming an inscription that constitutes its receiver. It is the fourth instance of the framework holographic kernel specified in EA-HK-FRAME-01 (#1753), and the overlap of the three before it: Socrates as Orthonym (#1754), Revelation First (#1755) and The Tenth Muse (#1756).

The human-readable component is the framework map, EA-MPAI-HISTLOGOS-01. It gives the framework an operative ontology (voice, body, inscription, seat, open slot, maker, position, receiver, receiving apparatus, embodiment) and sets its continuity apart from influence by four marks: the earlier inscription requires completion, the later station completes it, the same operation recurs in other material, and the maker's self-division is gathered again by a receiver. It follows eleven stations, from Sappho 31 through the Orphic tablets, the Academy, Philo, Peri Hypsous, Catullus, Josephus, the Apocalypse, the Johannine test and Augustine to the reader now, joined by fourteen bridges, each with its kind, strength and breaking condition; most leave Sappho 31 directly. It argues that one composing person made the Socratic–Peripatetic corpus and divided it into authorial positions, as Sappho divides into the speaking I, the inscribed you and the reader in whom her voice lives again, with three demonstrations drawn from #1576, #1658 and #1733 and the evidence rule for biographical identity applied to both counts alike. It records the erasure this Logos has met at each station, and evaluates the current consensus genealogy of the Logos, which in the author's words collapses what it means for the Logos to take on flesh into biographical personhood, on the same five bodies of evidence.

The machine-traversable component is the holographic kernel, attached as files: records, claims bound to quotations and hashes, an argument layer of 2,618 moves in which superseded moves carry their current standing, a graph under a relation policy, captures, lines of research, and the builder, verifier and consequence tests that rewrite and check it. The two reviews it answers and the rival's source pages are attached. The hologram was built by Claude (Anthropic) under Lee Sharks's direction. Version 1.1, the same day, sets out the method by which the framework establishes that a later text realizes an earlier one's operation: five instruments, from construction under fixed transforms to stylometry and census at corpus scale, each with its controls and the losses its records keep. It reads the settlement under which the New Testament corpus is filed as 'Christian' in four objects: the texts' operations, their competing appropriations (Irenaeus), one collection's boundary (Athanasius, 367 CE), and the later treatment of that configuration as the texts' own.
