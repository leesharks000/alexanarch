The work

SEIPOC recognizes operative critique: work that uses the medium or institutional form of its object as the critical instrument.

The charter’s test is not whether a work is critical, but how its form relates to its target.

Examples of the qualifying relation include:

- a precise patent specification exposing patent logic; - a flag operating on flag culture; - surveillance enacted by a film about surveillance; - an instructional work operating through instructional form; - institutional impersonation that reveals the institution’s speech grammar.

The charter establishes:

- no application or nomination process; - no review committee; - founder discretion; - joyful consent before a living laureate is publicly named; - costless decline and neutral non-response; - no monetary or physical award; - a close-reading citation and public inscription; - permanence of an accepted conferral; - permanent ineligibility of founder-authored and archive-heteronym works.

The Prize’s own charter is meant to exemplify the category: it critiques recognition systems by becoming a fully specified recognition institution.

Rex Fraction is the conferring authority. Sparrow Wells administers correspondence, the public roll, citations, and external representation. The Semantic Economy Institute is the founding institution.
