The work

EA-CORPORA-36 is the thirty-sixth seating of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive's originals shelf, deposited under Rebekah Cranes, the standing authority on the EA-CORPORA deposits, on 7 November 2026. It seats the reception of Sappho and the history of the future reader as public texts in their original languages, for the revision of Closing the Fragment and the Sappho Future Reader framework, whose groundwork is Research Notes Towards the Future Reader (#1548). Part A holds twenty-three seats: the translators, editors and critics of Sappho from John Hall's English Longinus (1652) and Boileau's Traité du sublime through the Spectator, Pope, Fawkes, Welcker, Merivale, Mure, Swinburne, Higginson, Symonds, Renée Vivien, Bliss Carman, Wharton, O'Hara, Stacpoole, Wilamowitz and David M. Robinson to Edwin Marion Cox (1925); the remembrance fragments in the books that carry them (Dio Chrysostom with fr. 147, Plutarch with fr. 55, Stobaeus, the Oxyrhynchus Papyri XV); the Greek Anthology's epigrams on Sappho; Smyth and Jebb; and the Wikipedia sentence on Sappho writing for future readers, pinned at one revision. Part B holds nine seats of future-reader texts the library lacked, from Ennius in Cicero, Dante, Chaucer, Shakespeare's sonnets and Milton through Wordsworth, Keats, Shelley, Stendhal, Dickinson and Baratynsky to the Sandia report on markers for the Waste Isolation Pilot Plant (1993). Part C cross-references twenty-two loci in texts the library already held, Homer, Herodotus, Thucydides, the Phaedrus, Horace, Ovid, the Hebrew Bible, Nietzsche and Whitman among them. Part D holds open scholarship in copyright by reference, each work fetched and hashed with its loci verified: D'Angour, Finglass, Garner and three Bryn Mawr reviews, with pointers to Lardinois 2008 and the Center for Hellenic Studies editions. Part E declares sixty-six works behind rights-, pay- and access-walls. Each seat is hashed into its manifest, and seventy-seven loci are verified in the text layers.
