The entry, short

ORCID is a nonproprietary persistent identifier for the authors and contributors of scholarly communication, run since 2012 by ORCID, Inc., a nonprofit, to resolve author name ambiguity; the archive holds its heteronyms' works under one shared iD and states in more than one way whose iD it is.

🔑 What it does

  • Identity: one persistent identity per person, tied to a name, likened to a DOI for a person.
  • Names: not unique, changing, variously written; it separates holders from namesakes and from their own variants, and a record can carry other versions of the name.
  • Record: a CV hosted by ORCID, edited by its holder, each item public, trusted or private.

🏛️ Who runs it

  • Operator: ORCID, Inc., a Delaware nonprofit (2010), sustained by member fees.
  • Dates: announced 2009; registry 16 October 2012; 14,727,479 live accounts in August 2022.

🔢 Format

  • iD: a 16-character URL whose check digit may be X; the usual example belongs to "a fictitious professor used in examples and testing".
  • ISNI: ORCID iDs are a subset of ISNI, so one person may hold "two ISNIs".

🔗 Use

  • Required: by many publishers and funders and by NIH; DataCite and Crossref update records; public data under CC0.

🎭 Names and authorship

  • Unread: how a record holds a pseudonym or heteronym; ORCID's names page refused the census.
  • Shared iD: the archive's heteronym creators carry 0009-0000-1599-0703; "Sharks, Mary Lee" publishes on it.
  • Whose iD: the legal identity's, with alternate names (#1009); the heteronym's (#794, #928); the orthonym's, a sameAs to it "the collapse" (#1446).
  • Civil name: a heteronym is "attributable through the civil bearer without being reducible to that bearer" (#940); ORCID verifies no authorship.

📡 At the name

  • Overview: a researcher's service, accurate at its grain; several names, ISNI, holder and dates absent (Capture Registry).
