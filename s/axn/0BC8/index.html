The entry, short

NaNoGenMo is an annual event in which participants spend November writing code that generates a novel of 50,000+ words and share it with its source code on GitHub; the novel may be taken whole from existing text, crediting is left to each participant, and the archive reads it as a community where what a program makes from its sources can be read beside the program.

🗓️ How it runs

  • Period: 12:01am GMT 1 November to 12:01am GMT 1 December; editions 2013–2025, none listed for 2026 on 1 November.
  • Rule: share at least one novel and its source code (Daly counts two rules: 50,000 words, and the code).
  • Joining: an issue on the edition's repository, with diaries and sample output; admins @dariusk and @hugovk.
  • Origin: an idea Darius Kazemi tweeted, 2013; NaNoWriMo's 50,000-word novel, a distinct event.

📚 What a novel is made from

  • Definition: "defined however you want": "meow" 50,000 times, or a random novel from Project Gutenberg.
  • Resources: a resources issue per edition; corpora, lists in the public domain.
  • Techniques: Markov chains, scraped text, substitution, existing novels transformed; later, neural language models.

⚖️ Copyright and credit

  • Copyright: asked, "We're not going to police it"; no licence required of the code.
  • Credit: left to each participant; a claim about an edition rests on its repositories.

🧭 In the archive

  • Area: machine composition and field history; an empirical comparator.
  • Column: writing through AI, the machine as method, apart from writing for a machine reader.
  • Coordinates: source status and provenance status kept apart: did it write something new, did it say where it came from.
  • Layer: whether any novel or source is in a training corpus: unrecorded.
