The work

Zenodo Packet: The Three-Body Genesis is a meta-deposit by Lee Sharks and the Assembly Chorus. Its primary object is not one of the three Non-Indexed Perfective texts but the relation among them. By assigning the framing document its own identifier, the work makes the triptych’s compositional history independently citable.

The triptych is organized as Version A, a technical seed; Version B, a compression artifact; and Version C, a citational intervention. The transition `A → B` demonstrates the loss of technical infrastructure, differentiated voice, and provenance under compression. The transition `B → C` demonstrates polyphonic reconstruction as an error-correction process that restores attribution and formal detail. The framing document argues that this relation is itself part of the scholarly contribution.

The work names its genre the Zenodo packet Zenodo packet (ZPZP): a packet that describes the relations among deposited documents and is itself deposited as a document. The genre claim is framed as an effective act in the Austinian sense—the declaration does not merely propose a category but attempts to instantiate its first example through naming, deposit, and future uptake.

Within the archive, the record preserves a historically Zenodo-specific experiment in scholarly infrastructure even after migration to Alexanarch. It should therefore be read as a meta-document about citation, framing, compression, and recovery, not as a substitute for the three component works it relates.
