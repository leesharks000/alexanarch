The entry, short

Electronic Literature Lab is Washington State University Vancouver's lab and archive of born-digital literature, art and games, founded and directed by Dene Grigar, keeping obsolete computers working so works can be read on the equipment they were made for; the authorship census asks whose authorship it keeps, and under what names.

🏛️ What it is

  • Kind: one of four U.S. media archaeology labs, the only one preserving literary apps (its page); census: infrastructure.
  • Mission: archives, restores, reconstructs and produces born-digital works; "care for human expression".
  • Origin: an ELO exhibition, 2008; moved in January 10, 2011; Wikipedia: 2011, filed under 2008.

📚 Holdings and rooms

  • Counts: 61 computers, 200 works (2021-era page; census); over 80 and over 3,000 (Wikipedia); over 100 computers (own site).
  • Rooms: Reading Room; Archives Room with The NEXT's and the ELO's papers; Restoration Room; Studio.
  • ELO: the center of archiving electronic literature for it.

🔧 The work

  • Traversals: recordings on original platforms since 2013; Pathfinders, MIT Press 2017.
  • Conservation: Larsen, Disch's Amnesia, Holeton, Moulthrop, Bly, McDaid, Kolb's unfinished "Caged Text".
  • The NEXT: grown from the repository (2016–2020); managed; rebuilt for machine readers, 2021.
  • Metadata: ELMS 1.0–3.0; the NEH grant not funded.
