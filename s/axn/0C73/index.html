The work

The Word That Became Text is a two-part essay by Johannes Sigil and Rebekah Cranes, heteronyms of Lee Sharks, designated EA-LOGOS-01. Version 2.0 was deposited on 12 November 2026 and succeeds version 1.0 of 5 April 2026 (#626), which stays on the record with errata at each point corrected. Part I contests the consensus that the divergences of the Slavonic Jewish War from the Greek are a medieval translator's work. It reads the Old Russian from N. A. Meshcherskii's edition, seated as EA-CORPORA-37 (#1760). In the Essene chapter it weighs nine divergences in a ledger, each with its best medieval explanation and a defeat condition. Five agree with Hippolytus's Refutatio against the Greek. George the Monk's ninth-century chronicle, read in Rus' in Slavonic, accounts for the night office and carries none of the agreements with Hippolytus. Across the whole work the essay argues for two layers: a Christian and Rus' layer, and a Jewish, legal and anti-Roman one that Josephus's own account of writing the War twice organizes better. Its instances are the martyrs of 4 Maccabees in that book's words, the count at Jotapata and the open oracle. Part I reads the eight Jesus-and-John passages in the recension's order as a structure of the word that acts, is inscribed and is read. Part II, in a doctrinal register, reads the Slavonic hedge 'if it is fitting to call him a man' as a mirror of Catullus 51. In Catullus flesh becomes word; in the Slavonic, effective word acquires a human biography. Version 2.0 traces version 1.0's unattested Greek phrase ei exestin to Whiston's English 'if it be lawful'.
