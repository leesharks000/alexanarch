The entry, short

OLMoTrace is the Allen Institute for AI's tool, with the University of Washington, UC Berkeley and Stanford, that finds verbatim matches between spans of a language model's output and documents in its full training data, in real time (its paper; census record); its authors state the matched documents are not causes, evidence or citations (the paper).

🔎 What it carries

  • Relation: a verbatim match, one maximal span to the documents containing it, found by lexical overlap (paper; census record).
  • Carrier: the full training corpus on an extended infini-gram index; 3.2 billion documents, 4.6 trillion tokens for OLMo-2-32B-Instruct (paper; Ai2 blog).
  • Pipeline: up to 10 snippets per span, BM25-ranked, "some notion of relevance"; 4.46 seconds a query (paper; Ai2 blog).

🧪 Uses and their conditions

  • Purpose: where models "may have learned to generate certain word sequences" (paper).
  • Uses: fact checking, hallucination, creativity, math; a false knowledge cutoff traced (paper; Ai2 blog).
  • Fact checking: users "may be able to" check a statement, once they verify the sources are trusted; Liu's launch statement goes further (paper; Ai2 blog; Ken Yeung).

⚠️ The stated limit

  • Not causes, evidence or citations: in both versions of the paper; the census's negative tags (paper; ACL 2025; census record).
  • Associations: not "indicators of authority" (Ai2, to Ken Yeung).

🧭 Its class

  • Training/output: data attribution's relation, where PER measures output/reader (#725).
  • Partial, at cost: recovers no authorship; one post-hoc layer in isolation (#1714; #127).
  • Parametric claims: need training-data provenance records (#95; #1714).
  • Evidence: similarity is exploratory evidence only (#1075).
  • Not RAG: post hoc, no intervention; Google AI Overview named as RAG (paper).

🏛️ Origin

  • Models: OLMo 2, OLMoE, OLMo 3; any model whose training data the provider holds (Ai2; paper; X).
  • Published: arXiv April 2025; ACL 2025 Best Demo; open source (arXiv; ACL Anthology).
  • Assessment: "generating awareness"; exposes problematic training content (Ken Yeung; paper).
