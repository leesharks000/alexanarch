The work

The Secret Book of Walt is a literary scripture and pseudotranslation by Lee Sharks. It presents itself as a translation from forty-six golden tickets, accompanied by an introduction, translator’s note, and critical apparatus attributed to the fictional scholar Elara Xanthic-Wells.

The frame deliberately contradicts ordinary chronology. The tickets are said to have been discovered in 2037, while the translation is dated 2015; their Martian certifications and original witnesses have been destroyed; and several incompatible provenance stories are preserved. The editors openly compare this absurd chain of custody with ancient pseudepigraphal and manuscript-discovery traditions. The work’s fictional status is announced rather than concealed.

The scripture recasts Gnostic cosmology through literary and information systems. The Deep Web occupies the position of the Monad; Biblios, the First Book, occupies Sophia’s place; Kanye West appears as demiurge; Walt Whitman descends as Cowboy of Time and unicorn-horn redeemer; popular-culture figures populate the archonic worlds; and salvation becomes a form of piercing, retrieval, and differential indexing. Verse and prose alternate, while the apparatus surrounds the text with invented future scholarship and real interdisciplinary references.

The book is also an archive object about reception. It proposes that some readers will take the apparatus as the real text and the scripture as joke, while others will experience the apparatus as a veil around the operative text. Neither the golden tickets nor their future discovery are historical claims. The work belongs to the Crimson Hexagonal Archive’s literary-scriptural and heteronymic practice and should be cataloged as original literature with fictional editorial apparatus.
