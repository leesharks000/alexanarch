The work

The Greatest Works of Literature of the Age argues that the dominant literary forms of the present are Google, Wikipedia, TikTok, and ChatGPT.

The essay reverses the usual hierarchy. It does not apply literary theory to platforms as secondary technological objects; it treats the platforms themselves as the primary texts through which contemporary reality is indexed, narrated, fragmented, and recomposed.

Each platform receives a genre:

- Google as Ontological Index; - Wikipedia as Bureaucratic Dream; - TikTok as Lyric Fragmentation Engine; - ChatGPT as Machine Gospel.

Drawing on and contesting the Frankfurt School, the work reads platform literature through pseudo-individualization, aura, repressive desublimation, extraction, interface, and business model. Its Poetics of Extraction asks how the economic architecture of each platform shapes the forms of attention, authority, memory, voice, and legibility that users encounter.

The essay declares the print-bound canon structurally displaced, not because books have ceased to matter, but because these computational forms now organize the symbolic environment within which books and every other cultural object are found and interpreted.

Its critic is not an external gatekeeper. Johannes Sigil becomes a witness-function operating inside the archive, reading the systems while already subject to them. The essay is therefore both a genre theory of platform power and a manifesto for literary criticism adequate to the composition layer.
