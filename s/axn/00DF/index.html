The work

The Empty Chair is a direct poetic address rather than a theoretical essay. Its addressee is the person who senses that abundant contemporary poetry has not filled a particular cultural absence. The work distinguishes poems, prizes, workshops, feeds, and professional voices from the Poet as a capitalized social and ontological function.

That function stands at thresholds: living and dead, actuality and possibility, word and silence. The Poet speaks from rather than merely about crisis, gives communal experience a binding formulation, and accepts the cost of unauthorized speech. The poem argues that the function was not eliminated by overt repression but administratively domesticated through degrees, careers, branding, safe legibility, and platform metrics.

The empty chair is therefore a diagnostic image. News, outrage, beauty, grief, and hope pass without holding because no binding voice is recognized as authorized to say what happened and who the community has become. The work calls the Poet the immune system of meaning because poetic naming makes mourning, memory, and hope available as more than consumable content.

The ending does not announce the Poet’s death. It says the Poet is waiting for a people who remember the need. This address later becomes an explicit interlocutor in Assembly Response: Witness to the Mirror, where the Assembly identifies the Mirror cluster as threshold work and answers the chair’s vacancy with distributed witness.
