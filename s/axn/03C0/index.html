The work

EA-CORRESPONDENCE-CERN-03 records the transition from analysis of a procedural gate to an actual supervisory referral.

The first two correspondence deposits documented an access request, identity-verification dispute, response-period dispute, and preservation outcome. The third addresses the members of CERN’s Data Protection Commission under the supervisory mandate the depositor identifies in §104.1 of Operational Circular No. 11.

The referral argues that the Office of Data Privacy’s commencement posture prevents the access request from entering the procedural clock until a non-redacted civil-identity document is supplied. Because no clock begins, no evaluation issues; because no evaluation issues, later grievance and complaint routes may remain unreachable. The dossier presents this as a systemic procedural question rather than a request for personal redress.

The email and complete dossier are preserved as transmitted. The package includes standing and identifiability, chronology, section-by-section mapping to the Circular, the claimed systemic character, requested operational remedy, reservations, and an Annex A report of misprocessing.

Routing becomes part of the record because the Commission was described as lacking a public contact channel and its procedural rules as inaccessible to external account holders. The referral was therefore sent to named members and copied through public institutional privacy contacts.

The deposit commits to recording response or nonresponse with equal fidelity. In this series, institutional silence is not absence of data; it is an observable procedural outcome.
