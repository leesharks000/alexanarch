The work

The essay uses liquidation in three related senses:

- conversion of structured assets into fungible value; - elimination or dissolution; - removal of relation and provenance.

It argues that water and meaning undergo the same structural operation on different substrates.

Water - enters as a watershed and commons; - is priced as an input; - is used for cooling; - may leave the facility as evaporated loss.

Meaning - enters as an authored and situated utterance; - is abstracted into retrievable units; - loses provenance and context; - becomes reusable by the operator.

The essay proposes responses including:

- water-sensitive siting; - sustainable-cooling requirements; - closed-loop and immersion systems; - treated-wastewater use; - waste-heat integration; - efficiency standards; - pricing that reflects scarcity and community cost.

Its final ethical demand is to make extraction visible by restoring source, relation, and borne cost.
