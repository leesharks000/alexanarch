The work

Whose Face Is on the Twenty? is a visual-forensic provenance study by Rex Fraction. The work compares differently presented portrait images and asks how curatorial mediation changes what viewers can see. Its central concepts are latent feature activation and post-hoc salience: a resemblance or feature may become visually dominant only after changes in scale, framing, background, contrast, or presentation regime.

The paper separates observation from attribution. It documents the visual effect, identifies the curatorial operations that increase its salience, and asks for the missing provenance that would explain those operations. The gap is not filled automatically with an intentionalist or conspiratorial account. Instead, the study treats incomplete documentation as the object of forensic inquiry: what was selected, enlarged, suppressed, or recontextualized, by whom, under which production process, and with what recorded rationale?

Figures are essential to the argument. The work’s PDF and image surfaces should therefore remain available alongside its text. Within the later Moving Statues Made of Rubies Mint, the study becomes the hand’s Thumb or GRASP function: it holds an object steady, fixes the frame, and establishes provenance before interpretation proceeds.
