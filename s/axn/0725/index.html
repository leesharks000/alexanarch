The entry, short

Attributization An operator: the act a text performs and the encyclopedic sentence states as an attribute of an entity, so that the operator speaks with the entity's voice (#1696). The field holds no account of it.

definition Entry

  • Strings Enters by a label, a description and its strings; for the specimen, "nothing at all" and "first work".
  • Row Its row composes the operator as subject: what it does, on what domain, where performed, which entities it speaks through, and where it stops.
  • Status An operator is no author in the archive; every mention of its strings is bearing.

test Test

  • Question Does the source perform an act that the sentence states as a property of the entity? Record the conversion, the surviving relations (scope, distinctions, evidence, consequences), and whether they keep authority to revise the description.
  • Locator Flags an active verb in the source rendered as a credential noun or priority adjective ("we had nothing to say" → "first work").

specimen Specimen

  • Operation A first-person count over a stated domain ending in a total negation; the attribute is primacy of a work.
  • Loss Surviving: the attribution to Aristotle and the locus. Lost: the quantifier's exhaustiveness and its domain.
  • Revision The cited locus lets a reader holding the text reopen the sentence; the sentence as reused carries no such route.

modes Modes

  • Inversion The verdict the source hands its hearers becomes Aristotle's assertion of primacy; the article closes what the source holds open.
  • Narrowing A stated criterion becomes membership in a later taxonomy; Aristotle's consequence becomes one modern formal fallacy.

limits Limits

  • Control Where the operation survives in the sentence, no attributization.
  • Transfer The concept does not transfer outside the session except where a noun or adjective of standing replaces a verb.
  • Round 01 Yields no validation; translation fidelity recorded contested.

prediction Prediction

  • P1 A hypothesis: attributization with loss of operative authority occurs at a higher rate on entity pages than on work and method pages.
  • Falsifier No difference or a lower rate on entity pages; a null leaves the protocol intact and removes the claim that closures fall unevenly.
