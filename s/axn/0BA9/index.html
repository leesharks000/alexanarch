The entry, short

Wordcraft Writers Workshop is a 2022 collaboration of Google's PAIR and Magenta teams with thirteen professional writers, who wrote stories with Wordcraft, a LaMDA-powered editor, published under their own names; the census reads it as machine participation declared at the level of the tool and the cohort, authorship held by the named writer.

🛠️ The workshop

  • Tool: a "magic text editor" on LaMDA: ideas, rewrites, elaboration, freeform prompts, a chatbot; not public.
  • Cohort: 13 commissioned writers, eight weeks, a story each, journals, interviews, no instruction on how much to generate.
  • Makers: Andy Coenen and Ann Yuan (PAIR), Daphne Ippolito, Sehmon Burnam (Magenta); the site illustrated with Imagen.
  • Dates: the tool July 2021 or March 2022, by source; the workshop 2022; the paper 9 November 2022.

✍️ Authorship

  • Names: each story under its writer's name; initials in the paper; LaMDA credited as the model only.
  • Notice: the writers' "own creative vision"; Google endorses no story.
  • Share: five stories largely generated, three mostly written, one siloed; output "rarely used" directly, per the findings page.
  • Unpublished: which sentences the model wrote.
  • Terms: consent and pay, in the paper; "strict nondisclosure agreements", in Engadget.

📊 Findings

  • Use: brainstorming and writer's block; clichés and tired tropes; voice not kept.
  • Whole stories: "a dead end", in Douglas Eck's words, quoted two ways; writers not replaced soon.
  • Origin: three writers feared plagiarism and "reputational harm".
  • Limits: a small, biased sample; interface and model hard to separate; other models untested.
