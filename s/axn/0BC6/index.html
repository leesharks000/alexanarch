The entry, short

Internet Archive is an American non-profit digital library founded in 1996 by Brewster Kahle in San Francisco, running archive.org and the Wayback Machine, a web archive of over a trillion captures that lets a page be read back by URL and date; its holdings are recorded as no training corpus, while outlets now restrict its crawlers over AI scraping.

🌐 The web archive

  • Crawl: crawlers gather most of its data; public since 2001; its ARC format (1996) became WARC.
  • Readback: earlier versions of sites, and vanished ones; Archive-It searchable within a week.
  • Save Page Now: saves the page in view.
  • Persistence: the census reads it as making open-web transmission inspectable after the fact; it tests no training set.

🚫 Exclusion and takedown

  • Exclusion: robots.txt and owner requests leave pages unarchived; coverage policy opaque (archive).
  • Takedown: a BBC video removed under the DMCA (2023); Reddit: it respects takedowns.

📚 The library

  • Books: its own scanners, Microsoft's 300,000, Google's uploads tagged scanner:google.
  • Holdings: 47 million texts; Open Library 25 million records; Scholar 35 million articles.
  • Items: files with metadata, gathered into collections.
  • Scans: OCR uncorrected, sometimes script-blind; md5 published; rights fields often absent.

⚖️ Lending and the law

  • Lending: one copy, one patron, under controlled digital lending.
  • Judgment: since 11 August 2023, no lending of books sold as e-books; upheld 4 September 2024.
  • Exposure: legally vulnerable, with documented self-censorship (archive).

🤖 The training layer

  • Scraping: outlets fear their content is scraped through it and restrict its crawlers.
  • Common Crawl: built as a fresher alternative to the Wayback Machine; trains models.
  • Census: no training use, set or model recorded; holdings prove no model exposure.
  • Archive: open texts are in the corpora; persistence places work where the layers look.

🔗 The archive's relations

  • Custody: pre-fork records recovered from the Wayback Machine; doctrine: sovereign static plus the Internet Archive.

ℹ️ Identity
