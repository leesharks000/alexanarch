The entry, short

Digital Labour Platform Policy Tracker is the International Labour Organization's global tracker of laws, policies and decisions on platform work, launched 14 August 2025 in its Observatory on AI and Work, with a component on union–platform agreements since 7 November 2025 (ILO; LV record); judicial decisions are planned (ILO).

🗂️ What it records

  • Unit: national and regional initiatives, and agreements, by content, scope and legal status (ILO; LV record).
  • Issues: employment status, pay, automated decision-making, working time, data, safety, termination, bargaining (ILO).
  • Coverage: 33 countries; 58 laws, 15 agreements, 11 other initiatives; agreements "publicly available in Europe" (homepage).
  • Names and makers: also Policy Tracker on Digital Labour Platforms; twelve legal scholars helped (ILO; homepage; LV record).

🧭 Who supplies, directs, gains

  • Labor: workers on digital labour platforms (LV record).
  • Direction: platform companies and their algorithms; governments regulate, unions bargain (LV record).
  • Gains: not recorded as outcomes (LV record).
  • Reading: the layer of rules on who may direct labor; none on DSL or user labor drag (LV record).

⚖️ The rules and their class

  • Bargaining: law keeps "collective bargaining" while "administering labor's containment" (#239).
  • Platform-worker rights: institutional demands, at risk of capture (#148).
  • Platforms: worker bears the risk, platform takes the data (#713); gig workers' value extracted (#469).
  • Algorithms: gig time "algorithmically governed" (#433); pay read from faces (#713).
  • Pay and status: ghost work "paid badly" (#163); reclassification a cost (#950); not the archive's class as such (#1545).

🎯 Purpose and what it settles

  • Settles: who regulates which issues; which EU agreements exist (LV record).
  • Does not settle: enforcement, or workers' conditions (LV record).
