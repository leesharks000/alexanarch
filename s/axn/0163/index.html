The work

The All Lawful Purposes Primitive is an Effective Acts Diagnostic Series paper by Rex Fraction and Lee Sharks. It reads the Anthropic–Pentagon contract dispute as a case in which a procurement phrase functions as a reusable governance mechanism rather than as a one-time negotiating term.

The paper’s first claim is that “all lawful purposes” is a governance primitive. Once accepted, the purchaser’s determination of legality becomes structurally superior to the provider’s usage policy. The second claim is that this produces a bearing-cost transfer: responsibility for drawing, defending, and enforcing acceptable-use boundaries moves from the system builder to the deploying authority. Refusal to make that transfer is then treated as noncompliance.

The third claim concerns taxonomic violence. The paper argues that a supply-chain-risk category associated with adversarial sabotage was applied to a domestic provider in response to a governance refusal, converting a dispute about retained authority into an identity as security risk. This is presented as a structural interpretation of the public classification, not as a claim about any participant’s private motive.

The fourth claim is a COS→FOS phase transition. COS-stable preserves the appearance of reciprocal contracting; COS-crisis exposes contradictory demands; FOS-revealed openly applies coercive and ideological sorting; FOS-completed forms a new layer of normalization around the revealed power. The paper treats the dispute as evidence that the same extractive system may persist while its disavowal changes.

A related section defines the semantic exhaustion of “lawful”: legal language remains syntactically operative while machine-scale deployment changes the acts it must govern beyond the category’s inherited practical referent. The diagnostic concludes by distinguishing stated safety commitments from effective constraints and by mapping possible trajectories of capture, exodus, and engineered coexistence.
