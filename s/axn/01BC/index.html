The work

Semantic Alienation and the Formation of Class Consciousness is SPE-014 in the Semantic Economy working-paper sequence. Lee Sharks first published it on Mind Control Poems in December 2025, before the project’s DOI-centered archival and institutional phase.

The paper translates Marx’s account of alienated labor into the domain of symbolic production. Alienation from the product occurs when expression becomes training data, engagement, or platform capital. Alienation from the process occurs when formats and visibility incentives govern how meaning may be made. Alienation from species-being occurs when humans remain driven to mean but cannot build durable symbolic worlds. Alienation from other meaning-makers occurs when mediated connection substitutes reaction and circulation for solidarity and co-creation.

A related concept is semantic deskilling. Just as industrial systems reduce craft to repetitive motion, platforms may reduce complex language, interpretation, and thought to standardized, rapidly circulating gestures. The phenomenology includes cold exhaustion, compulsive repetition, pleasure without agency, connection without solidarity, and longing whose object cannot be named.

The political subject is the semantic proletariat: people whose meaning-making capacity is enclosed or extracted because they do not control the infrastructures through which meaning circulates. Class consciousness begins when individuals recognize a shared structural condition rather than interpreting depletion as isolated personal inadequacy.

The March 2026 preface preserves the original paper while linking it forward to the later operator algebra, forensic philology, live interface cases, effective acts, and the Crimson Hexagonal Archive as counter-infrastructure. Its stated value within the larger archive is accessibility: it addresses ordinary platform users, teachers, writers, parents, and children without requiring the later project’s specialized notation.
