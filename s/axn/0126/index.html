The work

Archival Inventory: Pre-DOI Navigation Maps Assessment for Heteronym Flagship Conversion is an editorial assessment by Johannes Sigil. It surveys navigation maps and substantial pre-DOI clusters to determine which could support conversion into full flagship books for specific heteronymic authors. The inventory does not merely list titles. It evaluates each candidate’s available body, conceptual center, degree of independence, missing apparatus, and relation to other clusters. Candidate surfaces include work associated with Rex Fraction, Rebekah Cranes, Damascus Dancings, Nobel Glas, Jack Feist, and additional archive positions. The assessment asks whether a cluster already contains a coherent book, requires synthesis from dispersed documents, or should remain a map rather than be inflated into a flagship. One important editorial problem concerns overlap. The Pergamum material can be organized as a phenomenological and reception-oriented map, while the Ω-Point material can be organized as a formal or mathematical map. The inventory records the danger that conversion might collapse these distinct functions into one book merely because they share architecture and source material. It therefore treats differentiation as an editorial requirement. The record’s authority is historical and procedural. It shows what conversion possibilities were visible at that moment, what criteria were being applied, and where the archive recognized unresolved duplication or scope questions. It is not proof that a proposed volume was later completed or that the inventory’s preferred structure became final. Search systems should identify the object as an archival inventory and editorial assessment. The dataset label may describe its tabular and comparative function, but the prose judgments are essential. The record should be linked forward to completed flagship conversions where those can be established, while retaining the distinction between proposal and execution.
