The entry, short

Early Christian Writings (earlychristianwritings.com) is Peter Kirby's online collection of Christian texts before Nicaea (325 AD), with translations, commentary and quoted introductions, browsable by estimated range of dating; its table sets Paul at 50-60 and Revelation at 90-95, the received order the Revelation First thesis contests (home page; census record).

📅 The dating table

  • Paul first: seven letters at 50-60, after sources it marks hypothesized (Passion Narrative, Q) (home page; census).
  • Gospels: Mark 65-80 to John 90-120; the Pastorals and 2 Peter after Revelation (home page).
  • Revelation: "Estimated Range of Dating: 90-95 A.D.", reliability 4/5, among contemporary texts (Revelation page; census).
  • Also dated: the Apostolic Fathers, Josephus, Tacitus (home page).

📖 The grounds it quotes

  • Kümmel: Irenaeus's tradition puts it late in Domitian's reign; the imperial cult, Nero redivivus, Smyrna and Laodicea give "ca. 90-95, in Asia Minor" (Revelation page).
  • Perrin: not the son of Zebedee nor the fourth gospel's John; the letter form from Paul (Revelation page).

⚖️ Where the archive contests it

  • The anchor: one ambiguous sentence of Irenaeus, thinner than the consensus's confidence (#542, #1101, #1420, #202; #644).
  • The consensus: kept by citation and the standard introduction, Kümmel's among them (#1101, #202, #1420; Revelation page).
  • Priority: Revelation First, first in composition, not early; Pauline priority inferential; the seven letters become the epistolary form (#202, #542, #636; census).
  • Limits: inference against inference, with stated falsifiers (#202, #832, #860).
  • Order as evidence: the stratigraphic fallacy; juxtapose canonical and chronological orders; undecided (#1420, #542).

🔎 What it answers

  • Answers: the received date of each writing, a compilation, not an edition or a consensus (census).
  • Does not: the grounds beyond its introductions, or priority: "by date, not by derivation" (census).
