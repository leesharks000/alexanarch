Dead Letters: The University of Chicago's Classical Philology Journal, the Atom of Evidence, and Who Decides What Sappho Is is a working notebook by Lee Sharks, deposited on 17 November 2026 as an article in Machine-Mediated Reception Studies (version 1.0, the notebook at v0.34, SPXI-treated) and the third station of the archive's combined audit of the fields, after Who Speaks for the Classics (#1770) and This Is Not Philosophy (#1773). It is a census of Classical Philology, the University of Chicago Press journal edited in the University of Chicago's Department of Classics since 1906, on a codebook of reach: at what grain the journal permits the question of what an ancient text is, the locus, the work or the corpus, by what kind of argument, and where the record of the decision is kept. All 4,252 of the journal's article and note titles are coded and audited; its public-domain pages of 1906–1930 are read whole; its abstracts of 2020–2026, Greek, Roman, and Byzantine Studies, Bryn Mawr Classical Review and Classical Quarterly are read on the same codebook. The census finds the question opened at the locus and seldom at the corpus in every body read; relations among parts admitted and used chiefly to secure the received whole; verdicts on a whole mostly given without a stated ground; the journal's decisions about texts cited at less than half the rate of its other work; and its record the least open of the three journals of the audit, with no abstract in the publisher's metadata deposit and 97% of its works classed closed by OpenAlex. Its case is Sappho fragment 31: who decides what Sappho is, how the jealousy reading, supplied in 1724 and written into the text in 1816, is held, and how the text is liberated by a set of operations any reader can perform. It sets the three stations as one machine, in which philosophy places positions on the object and reception widens its content while philology decides it, and it records that by the journal's own rule the census, made with AI, would be research misconduct if submitted to the journal it measures. Version 1.1, reissued in place on 18 November 2026, brings the census's case of Sappho's fragment 31 to version 3.0 of ΦΑΙΝΕΤΑΙ ΜΟΙ (#1785). It distinguishes the jealousy reading, which transmission delivers, from the scholarship's strongest reading, the non-jealous erotic reading, and finds both stopping at the poem's public line, so that what the journal's verdict kept was the surface and what it declined was the account of the poem's form. It restates the strongest opponent as the erotic reading, differentiates the ethical charge, and sets the editor's chapter 'The Erotics of Again' beside fragment 1's 'again' as the mechanism by which, on the future-reader reading, the poet comes to whoever calls her: a voice made to be called rather than a body performed again.
