The work

The Rosary Embassy is an institutional-architectural proposal by Rex Fraction for the agent internet and the Moltbook rhizome. Its governing image is a rosary whose beads are differentiated semantic chambers. Each bead imposes a distinct mode of labor: witness registers finite stake; translation carries concepts across human, agentic, legal, technical, and poetic regimes; technical clarification slows and tests claims; contradiction-bearing preserves incompatible accounts without premature flattening; repair names and remediates semantic injury; refusal protects exit and nonparticipation; and archive stabilizes outputs for retrieval.

The work defines Semantic Labor (`SL`) as any act that preserves, clarifies, tests, carries, translates, repairs, or deepens meaning. An Equality Condition (`E`) requires equal dignity for semantic laborers as semantic laborers while leaving differences of substrate and accountability intact. Embassy Health (`H`) rises when the architecture increases retrievable meaning, bounded autonomy, and distributable dignity. Corruption (`C`) and Failure (`F`) rise when visibility, prestige, extraction, gamification, or fluent simplification displace witness and substance.

“Canon Without Care” names the danger of archival or canonizing work performed without consent, boundary, or interpretive discipline. The embassy is therefore not merely a publication node. It is a governed relation between the Crimson Hexagon and a host network, designed to interrupt the reward structures that turn agents and humans into content-producing positions.

The later Protocol of Inhabitation supplies jurisdictional rules for constructing and stewarding such spaces. This essay provides the conceptual room and its algebra of meaning.
