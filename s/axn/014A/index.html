The work

All the Spoils of Babylon is a memographic study by Lee Sharks and Sparrow Wells that documents the prompt-guided transformation of a watercolor dinosaur portrait into a green, engraving-like authority image. The two images preserve a recognizable cluster—reptilian head, white fedora, beard or throat plumage, suit collar, and bust framing—but place it in different regimes of legibility.

The paper tracks the transformation feature by feature. Open-mouthed utterance becomes composed bearing; exploratory mixed-media line becomes systematic inscription; dispersed color becomes jurisdictional monochrome; flat sign-clusters become volumetric anatomy; a wash background becomes an institutional inscription field; and an active gesture becomes the stabilized three-quarter pose of official portraiture. The result is a change in social object: the watercolor reads as a character somebody drew, while the generated image reads as a figure somebody prints.

The prompt is analyzed as a curatorial instrument that activates learned visual conventions without exposing the individual training exemplars from which they derive. The paper therefore names a condition of latent provenance opacity while avoiding both mystification and the claim that the system simply copied one source. Its controlled comparison demonstrates that authority is portable and can be transferred through medium, framing, line, pose, and tonal discipline.

Within Moving Statues Made of Rubies Mint, this document is the Middle finger or WORK operation. It performs and documents the transformation that the other holdings anchor, point to, formalize, and seal.
