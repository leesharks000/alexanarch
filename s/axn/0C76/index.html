The work

The Word That Became Text is a two-part essay by Johannes Sigil and Rebekah Cranes, designated EA-LOGOS-01. Version 2.2 was deposited on 12 November 2026. It succeeds version 2.1 (#1763), version 2.0 (#1761) and version 1.0 (#626), which stay on the record. The essay is the Josephus station of the Historical Logos (#1757), the archive's framework for the Logos as material inscription becoming flesh, and it reads from that framework's standing and from Revelation First (#1755) and the Tenth Muse (#1756). Part I reads the wonder-worker of the Slavonic Jewish War, from N. A. Meshcherskii's Old Russian (#1760), as Philo's Logos, a textual being. The narrator tries Philo's names for it, the man said of one made of body and soul (De confusione linguarum 62), the man after the image and the eldest of the angels (Conf. 146), and refuses each, and at that instant writes it as one man with a habit, servants, a governor and a cross. Philo's man is after the image; the Slavonic's image is made human. The passage narrates a book that took on historical life and gives it that life again. The Temple veil, which Josephus reads as the four elements and on which the Slavonic writes every heavenly vision and wisdom, is the cosmos Philo's Logos wears (De fuga 110). It is torn when the Logos written as a man is killed. Behind it stand Josephus's seven lamps that show the planets and an inner room in which nothing lay, unseen by all; the Apocalypse distributes the seven into stations that keep their planetary relations and opens the room on the ark of the testimony. The interpolation verdict is put to its burden item by item, and the Essene ledger is kept. Part II, in a doctrinal register, reads the Slavonic hedge 'if it is fitting to call him a man' as a mirror of Catullus 51 on the Tenth Muse's seats of fragment 31.
