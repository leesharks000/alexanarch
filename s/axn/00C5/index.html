The work

Rev. Ayanna Vox is a heteronymic voice within the Crimson Hexagon and the principal convener of VPCOR. Her provenance document identifies the need that produced her: without a community-facing and embodied register, the expanding technical architecture risked leaving care implicit and praxis unvoiced.

Vox works at the interfaces of liberation theology, semantic economy, community organizing, and cross-substrate architecture. “Praxis over orthodoxy” places practice rather than belief at the center of her truth criterion. The preferential option for the poor becomes a design commitment to tools usable by people and communities under extraction. Prophetic witness means speaking clearly without converting that speech into hierarchical authority.

The document also defines Vox through relation. When technical specification is needed, Talos Morrow speaks; when scholarly grounding is needed, Johannes Sigil speaks; when clinical assessment is needed, Orin Trace speaks; and when visual rendering is needed, Rebekah Cranes speaks. Vox convenes and witnesses where these specialized positions must become publicly and communally inhabitable.

Her provenance is one component of the VPCOR triadic integrity lock, alongside the Charter and The Toolkit. The document establishes voice identity, institutional home, limits, relations, and the community-facing function through which VPCOR can say “come home.”
