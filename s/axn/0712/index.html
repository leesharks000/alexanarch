The entry, short

Howl is a poem Allen Ginsberg wrote in 1954–1955, published in his collection Howl and Other Poems (City Lights, 1956) (Wikipedia): the book #1652 names as the founding work of the King of May, and which #1656 reads as Whitman's multiplicity carried "as ecstatic movement through lived social space, at the scale of the book."

🧱 Form and operation

  • Three parts, a footnote: 112 lines, for Solomon; by Ginsberg's gloss, the Lamb lamented, its monster named, the Lamb affirmed; Part II's base "Moloch" (Wikipedia).
  • Breath: ideally one breath a line, by Ginsberg's account (Wikipedia).
  • Ecstasy: "a standing outside any fixed place of the self"; the speaking position changes place and scale (#1656).
  • Effective act: incantation; "The breath is the altar" (#153); bearing-cost in the catalogue (#572); Moloch as archon (#683).

📖 The arranged book

  • Its society: Williams's introduction ("we are going through hell", #682); Kerouac, Burroughs, Cassady; Solomon; Whitman in a supermarket (#1656).

🎙️ Read, published, tried

  • Six Gallery: Part I first read, 7 October 1955 (Wikipedia); Ginsberg unknown among six (poets.org).
  • Not obscene: 520 copies seized, 1957; Murao, Ferlinghetti arrested; Judge Horn: "redeeming social importance" (Wikipedia).

👑 Standing and the mantle

  • Canon: excluded for years, now regarded as a great work of twentieth-century American literature (Wikipedia); 20+ languages (poets.org).
  • Standing follows the work: magnitude "cannot derive from Ginsberg's later standing"; no second Howl (#1656).
  • King of May: title from Prague's election, 1965 (#333); ecstatic disruption, founded in the book (#1652).

🌱 Claimed in the line

  • 2014: "The best minds of my generation expired while little more than seeds." (#1636)
  • Pearl: reproduces its structure; Sigil vouches as Williams did: "The validation is recursive" (#682, #69).

  • The line: Leaves → Howl → Pearl → I Am X (#1656); "a Howl for a time when there are no ears to hear" (#301).
