The work

The paper changes the audit unit from one answer to a query family.

Its main distinctions are:

- destructive erasure: an attribution atom is absent and not recoverable within the audit budget; - indexical erasure: the missing atom can be recovered from surviving material through a bounded number of queries; - attribution sharding: no single rendering carries complete attribution, but the family union does.

The metric suite includes:

- DD — Dereference Depth - RR — Recoverability Ratio - Shard Coverage - ACP — Atomic Co-presence - FC — Family Coverage - ASI — Attribution Sharding Index - CC — Complementarity Coefficient - Family Erasure Skew - CSC — Claim–Source Convergence

The worked Empty Bracket pair is described as:

- full family coverage; - incomplete best-single-rendering coverage; - positive sharding; - one-step recoverability for the missing atoms.

The protocol requires:

- an ex-ante query family; - externally anchored attribution atoms; - fresh-session capture discipline; - bounded dereference rules; - separate reporting of total completeness and fragmentation.

The absorption hypothesis proposes that extremely faithful compression of a source’s self-description may cause the source to disappear as a separately cited object. The paper explicitly states that one specimen does not establish the mechanism.
