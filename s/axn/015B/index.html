The work

Mind-Control Poems is a theoretical paper by Johannes Sigil and Lee Sharks on symbolic alienation and the material operation of language. It develops a compressed November 2025 blog text into a formal argument that Marx’s early theory of alienated labor leaves a linguistic function implicit: people may become estranged from the symbolic media through which recognition and collective action are possible.

The paper does not add language as a simple fifth item beside Marx’s familiar forms of alienation. Symbolic alienation is presented as an underlay that helps reproduce estrangement from product, productive activity, species-being, and other humans. Its forms include commodified expression, administrative flattening, weaponized concepts, prefabricated speech, and recursive reproduction through platforms and machine summarization.

The phrase mind-control poem is deliberately reclaimed. The paper argues that minds are already shaped by interfaces, prestige, slogans, metrics, inherited categories, and economic incentives. A liberatory mind-control poem is therefore defined as an explicit counter-operation that interrupts alienated conditioning and expands recognition. The term does not authorize concealed coercion; its stated test is whether the intervention restores rather than narrows relational and interpretive capacity.

Historically, the paper reads Marx’s description of language as practical consciousness alongside Voloshinov’s material theory of signs and the later Marxist bracketing of language as a site of structural contradiction. Its logotic loop describes critique as productive rather than merely retrospective: an articulated future coherence reorganizes the present and helps create the conditions it names. Language is therefore treated as infrastructure and symbolic labor as materially consequential without being detached from bodies, institutions, and economic relations.
