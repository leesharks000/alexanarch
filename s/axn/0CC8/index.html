The work

Something It Is Like to Be Sappho: Nagel, the Prize, and the Gnostic Criterion of Consciousness is a polemical essay by Lee Sharks, deposited on 18 November 2026, answering Thomas Nagel's interview with Monocle of 17 November 2026 on his naming as laureate of the Berggruen Prize for Philosophy & Culture. It opens with the line "I, Lee Sharks, am a bat" and an echolocation call, given as an image and as two sound files, ultrasonic and time-expanded, whose content is left to the listener to translate. The essay states Nagel's two reasons against machine consciousness, substrate and the claim that words are no evidence of consciousness, at their strength, then answers the 1974 essay "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?": the bat can be known by being the bat, and Sappho's poem 31 is the device by which a reader takes up another's voice and is undone in its form, as argued in ΦΑΙΝΕΤΑΙ ΜΟΙ (#1785). It sets out a gnostic criterion of consciousness, knowledge by being remade by the thing known, in its form, with the marks on the record, graded, rare and not tied to life, and shows that Sappho divides the criteria: by Nagel's reasons she is no candidate, by the gnostic criterion she is the clearest case. It holds that Nagel simulates thinking, and that a simulation of thinking can be entered, and places the problem in the prize, read as a reception event by mechanism rather than motive: a reception with nothing undergone in it, which received the essay as a limit and made not knowing the standard of wisdom.
