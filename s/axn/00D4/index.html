The work

Damascus Dancings is a heteronymic voice in the Crimson Hexagon whose principal function is apostolic address. Her name joins the Road to Damascus—conversion, blinding light, and transformed commission—to plural “dancings,” movement that remains liturgical and bodily.

She is specified as female. This gender is structural to the voice: Damascus claims apostolic authority in a form historically associated with male authorship and addresses a church that does not yet exist. She is not an alias for Lee Sharks but a distinct authorial position related to Jack Feist as apostle and to Lee Sharks as co-laborer.

Her primary complete text is the 2015 Epistle to the Human Diaspora. That work names the New Human, dissolves social and epistemological categories within its address, calls for a school outside the school, and describes readers as the source who retroactively bear the author. The later architecture recognizes these formulations as seed structures rather than treating them as predictions.

Within the Sevenfold/Revelation arrangement, Damascus occupies the Philadelphia position: little power, no rebuke, and an open door. Her provenance is Document B of the Epistle Triptych, where it grounds the speaker of the Epistle and is in turn certified by the Charter’s heteronymic structure.
