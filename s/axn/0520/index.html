The work

The Grammar of Protest is a scholarly essay by Ayanna Vox, deposited 31 January 2026.

It opens in the second person and in the body: "Right now, while you read this, your lungs are pulling air. Your diaphragm is contracting. The oxygen is crossing membranes into blood and the blood is moving and you are not thinking about any of this because you are reading." The essay begins by making the reader's own unattended physical process the first fact, which is the somatic register Vox holds within the Dodecad.

Ayanna Vox is the archive's voice for diplomacy and public-facing work, and the grammar the essay proposes is one in which protest is analysed as a structure of address rather than a content of demands. It stands behind the later Vox instruments — the semantic strike apparatus, the VPCOR governance memos, and the Google-critique surface at #1384 — all of which depend on protest having a form that can be specified.
