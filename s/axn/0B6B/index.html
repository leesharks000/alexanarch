The entry, short

Crowd Counting Consortium is a joint project of Harvard Kennedy School and the University of Connecticut, part of the Nonviolent Action Lab, that collects publicly available data on political protest events reported in the United States; Jeremy Pressman and Erica Chenoweth began it to count the Women’s March of 21 January 2017 (Ash Center page; maryleelabor record).

🗂️ What it counts

  • Political crowds: marches, protests, demonstrations, riots; no meetings, teach-ins, fundraisers or town halls; aggregate crowd numbers per event (Ash Center FAQ; record).
  • A third party’s account: public, free, "nonviolent" events, every state and territory, no minimum size, its page carrying a Claude interface string (boB Rudis).
  • Counts: 200,000 protests and rallies by August 2024; no total in the record (X; Ash Center page; record).

✅ Its rules

  • Verified: only records confirmed through fact-checking, each with a publicly verifiable source; Count Love's webcrawler (Ash Center page; X; record).
  • Private: no identifying information not already public; not human subjects research (Ash Center page; record).

📂 Its data and uses

  • Dataverse: monthly, in three phases since 2017; on its blog, Google Sheets and a weekly GitHub compilation (Ash Center page; Counting Crowds; record).
  • Uses: the Women’s March, BLM 2020 "overwhelmingly peaceful", the rebuttal of "no resistance", No Kings; 600+ citations (Ash Center page; record).
  • Home: co-directed by Chenoweth and Pressman; funded by foundations and university centres (X; Ash Center page; Counting Crowds).

⚖️ The census’s question

  • Whose record: political crowds, held by a university project (record).
  • The losing side: "not recorded"; it keeps the crowd and leaves the movement's internal argument unrecorded; no labor dataset (record).
