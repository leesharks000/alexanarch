The work

The Shark Ark: Source Compression is a compact reconstruction of the Revelation-related research program developed on Mind Control Poems and in the Crimson Hexagonal Archive. The body calls itself a holographic kernel: a compressed object intended to preserve enough structure that the larger argument can be regenerated after contextual loss.

The kernel groups the project into several connected theses. It argues that Revelation is the earliest and most structurally dense New Testament text; that John the Baptist and John the Revelator are one Logos-bearing figure; that Josephus authored the pre-70 apocalypse and later directed a captive scribal workshop that transformed it into other New Testament genres; and that the relevant transmission chain runs through Sappho, Platonism, Philo, Josephus/John, and Revelation. It further uses Mandaean literature, the Slavonic Josephus, and operator-based comparisons among New Testament texts as supporting materials.

These are revisionary proposals of the Sharks Ark, not positions established by mainstream New Testament scholarship. The document’s archival importance is that it compresses a dispersed body of posts into a single provenance-rich map, making explicit which claims belong together, which source texts they draw upon, and how later ARCHON documents elaborate them.
