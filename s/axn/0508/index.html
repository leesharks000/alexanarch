The work

The Epic Without Hero: Toward a Theory of Epic for the Algorithmic Age is a theoretical paper by Johannes Sigil, deposited 10 January 2026 at 51,607 characters.

It proposes a functional definition of epic as "the literary mode that establishes the fixed points of a cosmos" — displacing the usual criteria of scale, heroism and national founding in favour of what an epic does. On that definition a corpus without a hero, without narrative continuity and without a single author can still be epic, provided it fixes the coordinates by which a world is navigated.

The working case is the Water Giraffe Cycle, a contemporary corpus emerging from human-AI composition, and the paper's ambition is to make the distributed epic a defensible literary category rather than a private designation. It is the theoretical companion to the primer at #288, which defines the distributed epic as a work assembled through traversal, and the two together are the archive's account of its own form.
