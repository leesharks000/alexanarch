The work

EA-CORPORA-35 is the thirty-fifth seating of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive's originals shelf, deposited under Rebekah Cranes, the standing authority on the EA-CORPORA deposits, on 6 November 2026. Its seven seats, eight volumes and some 170,000 lines of text layer carry the making of Q, the hypothetical sayings source of Matthew and Luke, from its first statement to its settlement, and are seated before the study of Q (Johannes Sigil and Rebekah Cranes, for Provenance: Journal of Forensic Semiotics) is drafted. C. H. Weisse's Die evangelische Geschichte (1838, 35/01) puts Mark first and reads the λόγια of Papias as a collection of sayings, the second source of the other two. H. J. Holtzmann's Die synoptischen Evangelien (1863, 35/02) letters the sources, Quelle A (Urmarcus) and Λ, and draws a life of Jesus from source A. P. Wernle's Die synoptische Frage (1899, 35/03) names the sayings source in a sentence that marks its status: "Die — hypothetische — Quelle sei mit Q bezeichnet." J. Weiss's Das älteste Evangelium (1903, 35/04) declares Mark's priority no longer a hypothesis and Mark "nicht mehr Quelle, sondern schon Sammelbecken". J. Wellhausen's Einleitung in die drei ersten Evangelien (1905, 35/05) weighs Q against Mark; A. Harnack's Sprüche und Reden Jesu (1907, 35/06) prints a text of the second source; and B. H. Streeter's The Four Gospels (1924, 35/07) records that the hypothetical source "is now by general consent referred to as" Q, and sets it among Mark, M and L. Sixteen loci are verified in the seated text layers, in the OCR's own readings. The seating extends §6.5 of Secondary Source (#1741), which read Holtzmann, Wernle and Weiss by reference, and declares the works it did not seat: Schleiermacher's Papias essay of 1832, not found in an open text layer, and Farrer (1955), Kloppenborg (1987), the Critical Edition of Q (2000) and Goodacre (2002), in copyright.
