Reading
The routing/assembly distinction is a better account of the entity effect than person-versus-thing. A name that resolves is answered by retrieval; a name that does not resolve is answered by construction, and construction is unbounded in a way retrieval is not. 'Crimson Hexagonal Archive' has an address. 'Lee Sharks' has a person, an archive, a protocol, twelve heteronyms, four domains, a poetry corpus and a PhD, and no single place that is all of them — so the system builds the object before pricing it, and prices what it built.
Analysis
If assembly rather than routing is the mechanism, two things follow that bear on the archive's own practice. First, the entity effect should be strongest for entities whose material is DISTRIBUTED and weakest for those with a single canonical surface — testable, and the archive holds both kinds. Second, and less comfortably: the archive's own strategy has been to give every entity a canonical address, which is precisely what converts assembly into routing. On this reading, successful disambiguation may LOWER the machine valuation by making the object smaller and better defined. That is not an argument against disambiguation; it is a previously unnoticed cost of it, and it should be measured rather than assumed away.
