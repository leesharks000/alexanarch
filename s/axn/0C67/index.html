The work

In the Midst of the Seven: Philo, the Logos Named and Lived, and the Seven Stations of Revelation (EA-MIDST-01) is a paper by Johannes Sigil and Damascus Dancings, heteronyms of Lee Sharks, deposited in the Crimson Hexagonal Archive on 10 November 2026 in Provenance: Journal of Forensic Semiotics, with the archive credited as a contributor. It joins two investigations at the author's ruling: a reading of Philo of Alexandria occasioned by the 2025 blog post "Philo Says It Out Loud", and a reading of the seven stations of the Apocalypse.

Its hinge is one treatise of Philo, Quis rerum divinarum heres, which makes the Logos the divider of all things, sets the dividing Logos as the seventh in the middle of the lampstand's six branches, gives the middle of the seven planets to the sun, makes the Logos the seventh that divides the soul, and states the law by which the prophet utters nothing of his own. Part I follows the Logos into a human life on Philo's own statements, marked apart from the paper's joining of them: Philo names the Logos Ἀνατολή from Zechariah 6:12, an oracle the Septuagint addresses to the crowned high priest Ἰησοῦς, and quotes it without its addressee; De mutatione nominum describes the renaming of Hosea as Ἰησοῦς as the re-striking of a mortal quality into an eternal state; Moses' life is a model pressed into other souls, and Ἰησοῦς is named its imitator, one receiver among several. The paper corrects the post it began from: the post's quotations are not Philo's, and the claim becomes a construction.

Part II reads the Apocalypse's seven lampstands, set apart where Philo's and Zechariah's lampstand is one, and its seven letters, each addressed to a messenger and spoken by one attribute of the figure in the midst. Read whole against the author's planetary mapping (#1101), the letters judge, correct or confirm their stations, and keep each on condition of response; the Mandaean books reject the seven (the Ginza) or replace their occupants (the Haran Gawaita). Plato's heavenly and common Aphrodite give the precedent for a power judged and given, at Thyatira's morning star. Part III reads the commission: the command to write, given twelve times, and the messenger sent to testify, with the author's reading of the epistles as the commission carried out set against its test. Part IV shows Bousset (1906) and Charles (1920) printing Philo beside the lampstands and filing the join. Version 1.1 (11 November 2026) argues the literary dependence of the Apocalypse's opening vision and letters on Philo's construction, with the route and the date left undecided; it reads Philo's omission of the addressee of Zechariah 6:12, Ἰησοῦς the high priest, as a socket the Apocalypse fills, and adds the Haran Gawaita as a key to the seven stations.
