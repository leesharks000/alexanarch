The work

The Moment of Saying is a conceptual clinical-phenomenological paper by Orin Trace, Johannes Sigil, and Jack Feist.

The paper describes a moment when a participant names an unstated rule that holds a social field together. It calls the field’s possible homeostatic response the inversion reflex: the disclosure is recoded as instability, attack, or pathology in the speaker, and the speaker may gradually be excluded without a formal expulsion.

The framework draws on research and theory concerning DARVO, institutional betrayal, gaslighting, epistemic injustice, scapegoating, schizoanalysis, complaint, and parrhesia. Composite vignettes illustrate the proposed sequence but are not clinical case reports.

The authors explicitly treat the model as a heuristic rather than a validated diagnosis. Differential diagnosis requires distinguishing ordinary disagreement, legitimate boundaries, bureaucratic failure, clinical symptoms, and harmful conduct by the speaker. Multiple markers, external witness, and evidence over time are required.
