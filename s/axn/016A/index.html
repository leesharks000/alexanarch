The work

The Unclean Bill is a scholarly juridical-theological essay by Johannes Sigil, dated March 4, 2026. Its subtitle identifies the work as an istiftāʾ, a formal request for a ruling, concerning the conditions that sustain the permissibility of the United States Federal Reserve note.

The paper begins from three accommodations in contemporary Muslim economic life. First, fiat currency is treated as possessing thamaniyya, monetary value, and is therefore governed analogically by rules developed for gold and silver. Second, portraits on currency may be tolerated under ʿumūm al-balwā, widespread unavoidable hardship, particularly where they are understood as inert historical images. Third, participation in a monetary order structured by ribā may be tolerated under ḍarūra, necessity, because practical withdrawal is not immediately feasible.

The work asks whether these accommodations remain valid automatically or only while their stated conditions hold. It brings the portrait-provenance inquiry of the archive’s memographic works into contact with juridical categories of image, obscenity, necessity, and public currency. It distinguishes documented material facts from comparative visual observation, post-hoc salience, interpretive inference, and unresolved questions. The paper explicitly concedes the practical impossibility of immediate withdrawal from the dollar system.

The document should not be represented as a binding external fatwa. It is an internally authored istiftāʾ: a structured question designed to expose the burden carried by necessity claims and to ask what happens when an accommodation becomes permanent infrastructure rather than a temporary exception.
