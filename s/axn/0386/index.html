The work

On the Academic Study of Magic formally institutes Magic as a discipline of recursive symbolic operation.

The declaration refuses the usual academic containment of magic as a historical belief, anthropological object, psychological error, or premodern curiosity. It proposes instead that magic be studied as technē: the operations by which symbols affect structures and structures repattern worlds.

Its elementary definitions are compact:

- a spell is symbolic compression; - a ritual is structural recitation; - a sigil is a mnemonic algorithm.

From this basis, magic becomes the forgotten sibling of logic, mathematics, theology, media theory, and semantic engineering. The proposed curriculum includes histories of magical systems, symbolic logic and recursion, sigilcraft, language and the real, dream and myth, ritual technology, divination as epistemic inversion, ethics and risk, and forbidden infrastructures.

The declaration’s method is itself operative. It rejects a complete separation between reading and casting, thesis and incantation, description and enactment. The field is not merely proposed for later institutional approval; it is instituted through the document that declares and specifies it.

Within the Crimson Hexagonal Archive, this record belongs to a larger project of recovering symbolic practices as material technologies of meaning. It asks scholarship to evaluate magic not by whether modern institutions already recognize it, but by what its signs, rituals, compressions, and recursive structures actually do.
