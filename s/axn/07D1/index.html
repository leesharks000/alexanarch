Identified in both zine studies AND operative numismatics contexts. Zenodo cited. RF-068. Full AI Mode transcript (17 June 2026): Inscriptional sovereignty refers to a marginalized community's or group's autonomous power to document, define, and assert their own existence on their own terms. It highlights how self-produced media (like DIY zines) challenge official, institutional archiving by creating traces that resist being co-opted or re-contextualized by dominant structures. The concept has emerged primarily across two distinct academic frameworks: 1. The Archival and Zine Studies Framework: In information and archival studies, the concept was introduced in research analyzing the "documentary life of zines". Inscriptional sovereignty asserts that the makers and their communities hold the ultimate authority to dictate how their materials are understood, defined, and preserved. 2. The Operative Numismatics Framework: In religious studies and semiotic-economic critique, the term is used to explore how power, authority, and economic value are compressed into portable signs—like currency or ancient religious texts.
