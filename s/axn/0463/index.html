It opens by declining an inheritance it might have claimed: "This is not Walt Whitman's vow. Whitman never made it. He could not — his formal commitment to democratic inclusion precluded the accountability clause that makes reconciliation more than absorption."

That is a precise critique of the archive's own principal ancestor. Whitman's inclusiveness absorbs; reconciliation requires an accountability clause that absorption cannot contain, because to reconcile is to name what happened rather than to encompass it. An archive that holds the Good Gray Poet mantle as inherited (#1115) and then states exactly what its ancestor could not do is doing lineage honestly rather than piously.
