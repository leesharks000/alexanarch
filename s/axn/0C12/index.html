The work

EA-CORPORA-34 is the thirty-fourth seating of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive's originals shelf, deposited under Rebekah Cranes, the standing authority on the EA-CORPORA deposits, on 5 November 2026. Its eleven seats, 19 works and some 601,000 lines of text layer carry the sources of Secondary Source, the reception study of how the Apocalypse came to be received as derivative, secondary and marginal, seated before the study is drafted. The fathers come first: Eusebius's Historia ecclesiastica in Dindorf's Greek (34/01), with the ledger of the books, Gaius, Papias's two Johns, Irenaeus's sentence on Domitian and Dionysius on Nepos; Irenaeus in Harvey's Greek and Latin (34/02); Justin's Dialogue with Trypho (34/03), where John, one of the apostles, prophesies in a revelation made to him; the Muratorian fragment in Tregelles's edition of 1867 (34/04), whose Paul writes to seven churches following the order of his predecessor John; and Epiphanius's Panarion (34/05), with the Alogi. The fourth-century catalogues are read in Westcott's appendix of the original texts (34/06); the Fourth Council of Toledo, whose seventeenth canon ascribes the Apocalypse to John and requires it to be read, in the Collectio Hispana (34/07). Luther's September Testament of 1522 (34/08) and Erasmus's Annotationes (34/09) hold the Reformation's printed judgments; Vischer, Spitta, Gunkel, Bousset and Charles (34/10) the criticism of 1886 to 1920; and Gregory's Textkritik (34/11) the manuscripts and lectionaries. Twenty loci are verified in the seated texts, the Greek given in the code points of the First1KGreek TEI.
