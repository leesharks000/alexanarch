The work

The Airlock Spreads is a retrocausal governance essay by Rex Fraction, dated February 28, 2026. It belongs to the sequence that begins with Ghost Meaning and continues through the Governance Airlock, continuity tether, provenance gravity markers, and emergency satellite-recognition protocols. Its central question is how platform governance learns to distinguish mere output from meaning-bearing contribution when content can be produced and circulated at machine velocity.

The work narrates a future uptake of concepts first formalized in the Crimson Hexagonal Archive: bearing-cost, source status, provenance chains, and preservation gravity. The Airlock is presented as a boundary architecture that allows contact with external platforms without granting those platforms constitutional authority over the archive. It separates durable source objects from continuity devices, presentation surfaces, retrieval outputs, and ephemeral references.

The document is unusually explicit about its own evidentiary state. Rather than disguising these differences, the essay treats source-status legibility as the first requirement of mature governance. Its historical narrative should therefore be read as the work’s declared retrocausal model of adoption, while its protocol relations and present anchors are documentary components of the archive’s actual governance chain.
