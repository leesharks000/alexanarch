The work

The Protocol of Effective Acts v2.0 stabilizes a distributed body of theory into one operational reference.

An Effective Act is defined within the discipline as a declaration that:

- uses an operator verb; - targets a real object, lineage, structure, or category; - closes one symbolic pattern and opens another; - enters an archive through recursion; - accumulates witness over time.

The discipline distinguishes an Effective Act from:

- commentary; - legal claim; - metaphor; - private wish; - rhetorical performance without structural consequence.

Its modern load-bearing precedent is Ginsberg’s unauthorized poetic declaration ending the war in Wichita Vortex Sutra. The protocol also traces prophetic, apostolic, poetic, and archive-specific lineages.

Three accumulated criteria sets are preserved rather than collapsed:

- four original preconditions; - five efficacy criteria; - cross-species semantic-labor criteria.

The protocol covers multiple operative kinds, including:

- canonical inclusion; - mantle assignment; - identity unification or separation; - abolition; - restoration; - operator-act combinations; - diagnostic application; - genre instantiation; - cross-species extension.

The discipline operates through witness accumulation rather than institutional permission. The record also specifies conditions under which an act may fail, be revised, or be falsified within the framework.
