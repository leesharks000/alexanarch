The entry, short

On the Sublime On the Sublime is a first-century CE Greek treatise of literary criticism by an author conventionally called Longinus, surviving in one manuscript with about a third lost, and the only witness to Sappho's fragment 31, which it quotes as the work of a maker who selects and binds.

📜 What it is

  • Kind: a letter to Postumius Terentianus on great writing; a classic of aesthetics.
  • Sublime: "loftiness and excellence in language", whose effect is "not persuasion but transport".
  • Sources: great thoughts, strong emotions, figures, noble diction, dignified arrangement.
  • Faults: bombast, affectation, passion without motive.

✍️ Author and text

  • Author: unknown; the manuscript reads "Dionysius Longinus" and "Dionysius or Longinus"; Cassius Longinus doubtful.
  • Manuscript: Parisinus graecus 2036, tenth century (Wikipedia) or tenth–eleventh (#1781).
  • State: six lacunae measured, the last unmeasured; it ends on a promise to write.

🌿 Sappho in chapter 10

  • Praise: "terrible at selecting the extremes and binding them together" (10.1).
  • Received as: "the so-called Ode to Jealousy" (Wikipedia); a love lyric of emotion (EBSCO).
  • Archive: a maker graded for composition; the poem survived as a made thing.

✂️ The edge

  • Text: the manuscript runs on, ἀλλὰ πᾶν τολματόν ἐπεὶ καὶ πένητα οὐ θαυμάζεις.
  • Editions: Voigt obelizes †καὶ πένητα†; Bergk gave the words to Longinus.
  • Conjecture: the words as Longinus's completion of the edge, its falsifiers stated (#1781).
  • Receiver: seven verbs without a subject; a question renewing no name.

🔁 Transmission

  • Archive: a theory of transmissional inscription: "The sublime is what survives the removal of the voice."
  • Reception: Robortello 1554; English 1652; Boileau 1674; Burke and Kant.
  • Read it: Roberts's Greek (Perseus); Havell's English (Wikisource).
