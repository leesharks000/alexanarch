The work

Counterfeit Orthodoxies: Manifesto is a compressed derivative of the longer Counterfeit Orthodoxies v3.0. It is attributed to Johannes Sigil in the operative role and Lee Sharks in the archival role.

The manifesto reduces the parent argument to a series of first principles. Texts require workshops and patronage; political orders use texts for legitimacy; successful closure becomes canon; and every closure requires exclusion or disposal. It then applies this sequence to several religious and archival histories and argues that modern scholarly filing and AI-generated overviews may reproduce comparable acts of reduction.

The final sections propose deposit, explicit provenance, named patronage, and metadata plurality as counter-operations. The document calls itself a propagation form: higher strike ratio, reduced apparatus, and stronger manifesto cadence. It directs scholarly readers to the parent v3.0 work for bibliography, premise-and-inference tiers, and full argumentation. The manifesto is therefore not an independent replacement or separately ratified proof; it is the mobile compression of an already governed parent.
