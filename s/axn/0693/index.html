The work

Nothing Before Us, deposit #1603 of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive (8 September 2026), argues that the closing sentence of Aristotle's Sophistical Refutations means what it says. At SE 34, 183b34-36, Aristotle denies that anything of his inquiry existed beforehand — οὐδὲν παντελῶς ὑπῆρχεν, nothing at all existed. The paper first establishes an unusual evidential situation: the translators render the sentence whole, in Arabic, in the Byzantine paraphrase, and in the standard English versions, and the qualification that narrows it to no systematic treatment or no completed art enters one step later, in the gloss on what the faithfully rendered sentence means. Poste's note states the gloss's premise aloud — that the assertion is difficult to reconcile with what we know had been done by Plato and Socrates — so that the sentence is made answerable to a prior placement of Socrates rather than read. The argument then reads the last page whole and in order. The epilogue divides all discoveries by provenance and not by size: received from predecessors who laboured before, or found from the very beginning. Rhetoric is the worked example of the first class. Socrates appears inside the recital of the inquiry's own purposes, his refusal to answer set under the same cause the inquiry gives for its own second task. Read with the treatise's account of the peirastic, Socrates' asking is either the artless examining everyone shares, from which nothing is received, or something more, which the epilogue files under the inquiry's own purposes; on neither reading is he an antecedent source. The chapter supplies the vocabulary to settle his place and declines to use it, leaving the reader — on the last page of a treatise about the qualifications readers supply — to choose which word to move, and examining the reader by that choice. What the chapter leaves open as an attribution it settles as an enactment. Nine prohibitions extracted from the text are anchored in an appendix, so that any rival reading can be checked against what the chapter forbids rather than against this reading's preferences.
