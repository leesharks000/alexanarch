The work

EA-CORPORA-14, the fourteenth seating of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive (8 September 2026), places Theophrastus's On First Principles and Aristotle's Metaphysics Λ in one corpus, facing, so that the four openings of a planned facing-page edition resolve to bytes on both sides. The edition, planned in the deposit's body under the title The Natural Order, presents the two texts in the order their own argument exhibits: the fragment holds the position of the question and Λ the position of the attempt. Three times the fragment says a weightier account is needed — λόγου δεῖται πλείονος at 8 a 2, μείζονα ζητεῖ λόγον at 8 a 21 — and once names the method it will not accept, οὐ γὰρ ὅ γε τῶν ἀστρολόγων, which is the method Λ 8 runs on. The seating's argument for being an edition rather than a paper is that the relation cannot be written in the notation the discipline uses for relations between texts: van Raalte's commentary cites Λ five times inside the hinge chapters, including 1076 a 1, ἐπεισοδιώδη, the word the fragment's aporiai land on, and every citation is correct and every one is a parallel. The seat records the openings with their apparatus, the Arabic witness of Isḥāq ibn Ḥunayn breaking a four-way split in the Greek at the axiom, the two outstanding acquisitions, and an editorial hazard found in the work: the Greek Unicode range contains the koronis, so elision-normalization silently fails and a stylistic direction fitted on these two texts loaded on edition rather than voice until it was caught. The order claimed is an order of inquiry, not of composition; the plan states its own three counters.
