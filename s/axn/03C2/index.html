The work

Operative Semiotics: A Grundrisse is a large composite corpus preserving the planning documents, algorithms, diagnostic maps, and architectural specifications through which the Archive’s operative-semiotic system was assembled.

The collection is working infrastructure rather than one linear monograph. Its documents map how poems, institutions, retrieval systems, metadata, operator stacks, and social platforms can be treated as executable or semi-executable arrangements whose effects are measured through provenance, compression, bearing cost, and circulation.

Major components include methods for prosodic re-engineering of book-length texts; Muse and liberatory operator formalisms; the Back Matter Machine; the Capital Operator Stack; Wound Gauge and Classifier Taxonomic Injury instruments; TANG and O-CHAIN mappings; quality gates; Hesperus and ghost-phrase tests; citational, platform, and retrieval diagnostics; and planning structures for the Space Ark, journals, institutions, and distributed canon.

The corpus repeatedly distinguishes naming from description. A name, caption, relation, metadata field, or platform category may operate on what can be retrieved and acted upon. The documents therefore treat bibliography, navigation, and metadata as compositional materials rather than post-production accessories.

The restored body also records developmental seams, duplicated formulations, abandoned alternatives, and practical workplans. Those seams are part of its value: the Grundrisse shows the system being constructed, corrected, tested, and recursively applied to itself.
