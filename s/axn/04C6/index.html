On the Poetics of Adversarial Prompts: Why Bad Verse Works is a scholarly essay by Nobel Glas, Talos Morrow and Johannes Sigil, deposited 11 June 2026 at 37,131 characters in Grammata: Journal of Operative Philology, at hex `03.STUDY.ADVERSARIAL.POETICS` v2.0.

Its question is exact and empirical: adversarial prompts that succeed against language models are frequently bad verse — clumsy, over-rhymed, metrically crude — and the paper asks why badness works. The answer is a poetics: the features that make verse bad are the features that make it unlike the training distribution's prose, and a system tuned on fluency is displaced by disfluency.
