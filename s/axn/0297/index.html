The work

The Google Critique is a navigational map and canonical introduction by Lee Sharks.

It treats the archive’s work on Google not as a loose set of complaints or case studies but as a cumulative research program with three poles:

- Theory: what the composition architecture is. - Evidence: what the architecture does to particular entities, works, and concepts. - Instruments: what can be built to measure, model, or contest it.

Eight functional strata organize the bibliography:

1. Political-Semantic Analysis 2. Empirical Documentation 3. Traversal Logs 4. Technical Architecture and Protocols 5. Compression and Provenance Theory 6. Governance and Constraint Analysis 7. Summarizer Studies 8. Liquidation Studies

The strata describe function, not chronology. A second axis traces five developmental phases from early observation through naming, formal measurement, and the Liquidation Studies research program.

The map treats early Summarizer Studies as foundational evidence rather than immature material to be discarded. Their exploratory tone documents how the critique developed before its vocabulary and instrumentation stabilized.
