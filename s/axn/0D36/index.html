The work

Contra Geocentrism: The Socratic–Peripatetic Corpus Against Its Own Conversion is a theoretical paper by Johannes Sigil with Nobel Glas, two heteronyms of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive, published in Transactions on Substrate Engineering on 22 November 2026. It reads the corpus transmitted under the names of Aristotle and Theophrastus as one body and argues that the body carries the case against the doctrine made of it. De caelo II 14 concludes that the earth rests at the centre, on a physics of natural place stated conditionally and with the astronomers as witness; II 13, the chapter before, sets out the Pythagorean central fire and the earth as one of the stars, expects many others to deny the earth the middle, reports that the phenomena would fall out alike for observers off the centre, and separates the middle of a magnitude from the honoured middle. Theophrastus's Metaphysics asks why only the circling bodies desire and calls the region about the middle as if pushed from the most honoured. The reports carry the case out of the school in De caelo's own construction: Theophrastus, as Plutarch gives him, on Plato's repentance of giving the earth the middle place; Hicetas's turning earth, ut ait Theophrastus, in Cicero's Lucullus; the Placita that Copernicus copied in Greek. Copernicus's dedication names Cicero first and Plutarch after as the occasion of his turn, and Cleanthes's call to indict Aristarchus for moving the hearth of the cosmos ties the closure to the charge of impiety. Aquinas's commentary on De caelo shows the school closing what the text left open: the resting earth made a supposition, the astronomers' hypotheses conceded as unproven, the indecisive phenomena answered with the lunar eclipse, and the Timaeus's verb read as binding rather than motion. A further section follows the history of charging the closure to the name, from Ramus and Bacon to Galileo's Salviati, who sets Aristotle against the Peripatetics. The paper's thesis is that the calcification of authorship and the calcification of the cosmos are one operation in two applications: one name fixed at the centre of the writing is one body fixed at the centre of the heavens. It claims neither heliocentrism nor intent for the corpus, and every quotation is checked in the archive's seated texts, most of them seated for it in EA-CORPORA-42 (#1799).
