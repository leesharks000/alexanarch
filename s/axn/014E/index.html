The work

Hymn to Demeter Chthonia is a scholarly-creative reconstruction by Johannes Sigil based on fragment 702 PMG, attributed to Lasus of Hermione. The work combines a reconstructed Greek hymn with translation, strophic organization, vocabulary notes, line-by-line commentary, cult-historical reasoning, and verification of the poem’s asigmatic constraint.

The reconstruction begins from the limited surviving evidence and asks what kind of hymn could plausibly fit the known linguistic, musical, ritual, and cultic conditions. Its choices are made visible through apparatus rather than concealed behind a seamless pseudo-antique surface. The document explains where a word or image follows attested evidence, where it represents a constrained inference, and where poetic completion is required because the source cannot determine a unique reading.

A second component offers an interpretive symbolic reading of the reconstructed hymn. That reading belongs to the archive’s operative and logotic architecture but is marked separately so that readers may evaluate the philological reconstruction without accepting every symbolic extension. This separation is essential to the work’s evidentiary integrity.

The document belongs to a broader program of constraint-driven reconstruction in which philological limits generate rather than prevent creative work. Its claim is neither that the hymn has been discovered nor that the reconstruction is the only possible completion. It offers a transparent, testable, and historically situated realization of what the lost work may have been able to do.
