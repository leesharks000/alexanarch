The entry, short

WikiWho is the algorithm and API that attribute each word (token) of a Wikipedia article to the revision, and so the editor, that introduced it, with every removal and reintroduction (MediaWiki; README; provenance census).

🔎 What it is

  • Definition: "A service that provides authorship attribution of Wikipedia articles" (MediaWiki); "provenance/changes of words (tokens)" (README); mining changes from revisioned writing platforms (wikiwho.wmcloud.org).
  • Census reading: lineage authorship and reuse; algorithm and API; records and tests; unit, a token in an article revision (provenance census record).

⚙️ How it works

  • Algorithm: parses every revision to find "who wrote and/or removed and/or reinserted which exact text at token level" (Flöck and Acosta, WWW 2014; README; MediaWiki; Wikipedia).
  • Carried: origin, deletion, reintroduction, on the re-parsed revision history; each token keeps a unique ID (census; README).
  • Accuracy: 95% on fairly revision-rich articles; the task "is not trivial"; best "as far as we can tell" (README; Meta; census, paper unchecked).
  • Limit: wikitext; other text may need new tokenization (README).

🧩 Tools and uses

  • WhoColor: annotated HTML colouring an article by original author (README; MediaWiki; Meta).
  • Who Wrote That?: powered by the WhoColor API; a Community Tech browser extension (MediaWiki; Wikipedia). The census lists both as names of WikiWho.
  • Uses: conflict, editor interactions, disputes, Gamergate, TokTrack, Contropedia (README; Meta; Wikipedia).

🏛️ Makers and hosts

  • Origin: Flöck's KIT dissertation; with Maribel Acosta at GESIS (wikiwho.wmcloud.org; Wikipedia).
  • Since August 2021: Wikimedia Cloud Services; Community Tech (infobox: Moderator Tools) and Wiki Education (MediaWiki; census). Wikipedia, on 2022 sources: shutdown planned, migration under way.
  • Access: wikiwho.wmcloud.org, 70 language APIs; "only Wikimedia will continue its support" (README; census: active, GESIS host to give way).

📅 Dates

  • Languages: four, in beta, on the Meta page of 2024, "an earlier state" (census); 70 wikis on the MediaWiki page of 2026.
  • Wikipedia: no article; a redirect to the WikiWho section of the GESIS article, its sources retrieved 2022, WhoColor still "in development".
