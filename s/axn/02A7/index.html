The work

Socially Necessary Scholarly Labor is a methodological paper by Lee Sharks on labor spent producing academic legitimacy.

The paper adapts Marx’s socially necessary labor time to scholarship. It asks how much work is required to establish a contribution and how much additional work is imposed by publishing, tenure, disciplinary style, repeated literature review, defensive citation, formatting, and institutional queues.

Its comparison between depth and coverage architectures argues that shorter, rapidly deposited scholarship may preserve the central contribution while avoiding some institutional rent. The paper does not dismiss archival depth, peer review, teaching, service, or all long-form scholarship.

The proposed five-to-ten-times labor ratio is a theoretical order-of-magnitude claim rather than a measured field average. Lee Sharks is the author; Claude, operating as TACHYON, is disclosed as composition support.
