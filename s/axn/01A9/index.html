The work

Combat Scholasticism, Part Three III.a is the first engine-layer chunk of the commentary tradition, deposited March 19, 2026 as DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19115139. It contains structural lectiones on UKTP, the Lexical Engine, and the Generative Disciplinary Engine.

Lectio III.1 reads the UKTP hard rule—translate by operation, not vocabulary—as a possible law of witness compression. A lawful transform must preserve the source’s generative operation and produce an admissible third term; vocabulary swap, costume, skeleton loss, frame capture, and hallucinated emergence are collapse modes. The quaestio asks whether this is a universal principle of compression or a demanding quality-control workflow for literary production.

Lectio III.2 examines the Lexical Engine’s distinction between minting and activation. Terms become operative through provenance, repetition, and denotational stability. The No-Paraphrase Law can defend precise pointers against coherence siphoning, but it can also function as interpretive enclosure by requiring readers to accept the archive’s vocabulary. The lectio preserves both readings and asks which effect dominates.

Lectio III.3 addresses the GDE at field scale. It tests whether documents, terms, citations, institutions, and self-description can genuinely form a retrieval-legible discipline, or whether the engine risks producing private notation, strategic recognition effects, and excessive meta-discourse. The engine’s construction power and its autoimmune risk are considered together.

All three lectiones are marked STRUCT. They establish pressure lines, commissioning details, and objections but do not execute the full eight-step scholastic form. Their load-bearing resolutions are deferred to the full Symbolon lectio and Cross-Part Quaestio in #586. This depth distinction is essential: the record is a structured commentary scaffold, not three completed final rulings.
