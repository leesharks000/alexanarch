The Application as Extraction Surface inaugurates the Extractive Reliance Study genre.

Its object is not rejection or disappointment. It is reliance conversion: an institution communicates a process state, the applicant reasonably acts on it, labor is expended, status remains opaque, and the applicant bears the cost of interpreting silence.

The documentary sequence recorded in the paper is:

- a May 2 process timeline; - a May 4 disclosure of an AI-native technical workflow; - a May 8 reply reaffirming the assessment process and promising to add context; - a May 18 request for status clarity after the stated early-May assessment window.

The audit’s central mechanism is Institutional Opacity Conversion:

`signal → reasonable reliance → labor → unresolved opacity → cost transfer`

Applicant Reliance Cost is represented first as a vector:

- `T` — temporal labor; - `M` — model, token, platform, and tool expenditure; - `O` — opportunity displacement; - `E` — emotional, cognitive, and bodily load; - `C` — credibility and social-disclosure cost.

A dollar estimate is a secondary monetary compression, not the full value. The report calls the monetizable portion the Monetary Compression Remainder and uses Value Distortion Gap to name labor that monetary calculation does not carry.

The paper reports a direct estimate of $4,500–$6,000 based on 30–40 hours at the applicant’s stated market rate. Emotional, bodily, credibility, and some opportunity costs remain partially or wholly qualitative.

Identity Translation Labor names the work of translating an AI-native technical practice into a legacy assessment surface.

The appended ARC-EP protocol instructs future auditors to:

- build a documentary timeline; - identify signals that could induce action; - classify each cost component; - apply confidence labels; - test the five IOC conditions; - separate semantic labor value from its monetary compression; - avoid assuming bad faith; - avoid turning ordinary rejection into accusation.
