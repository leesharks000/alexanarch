The entry, short

Worker Info Exchange is a London non-profit, founded in 2020, that helps gig and platform workers "turn data into power" through data access requests, investigations and data trusts (its sites; census); its Dutch foundation, Stichting WIE International, brings the Dynamic Pay Claim against Uber.

🗂️ What it does

  • Data access: subject access and data portability requests on behalf of workers: how work is allocated, paid and assessed (home page; census record).
  • Investigations: expose unfair algorithmic decision making; support litigation against automated dismissals (home page).
  • Data trusts: workers' data aggregated with unions for collective bargaining; not a union (home page; census record).
  • Route: workers' digital rights under the law; "enforcement rather than reform" (About Us; foundation).

🧭 Who directs the labor

  • Direction: platforms, through "hidden algorithms" and opaque automated management (About Us; census record).
  • What it answers: what a platform holds on a worker and how it directed them; not platform-wide rates (census record).
  • Directionality: the worker's-side record of how an algorithm directed the work (census record, the archive's reading).

⚖️ The Uber action

  • Claimant: the foundation, "in cooperation with" WIE, under WAMCA; WIE coordinates it (foundation; census record).
  • Grounds: GDPR Article 22; algorithmic transparency; data unlawfully sent to the US (foundation).

📉 Dynamic pay

  • Before and after: 25% commission, then real-time pay; nearly or over half the fare in cases (foundation).
  • Oxford–WIE audit: 1.5 million trips; real pay down, Uber's margins up; built from drivers' access requests (foundation; Safak).
  • Gains: no quantity in the census's sources; a video gives "29-50%" (census record; NOVO ORG).
  • TUC: research partner on The Human Price of Dynamic Pay, 2026 (foundation).

🔗 Its class, in the archive

  • Extraction: the platform extracts from gig workers and from the meaning that makes the relation "partnership" (#469; #19).
  • Data and risk: the worker assumed all risk, the platform captured all data (#713).
  • Portability: a non-predatory mediator requires it (#746).
  • Institutional demands: digital rights and platform-worker organizing contest law and policy, at risk of capture (#148).
  • Not the class: gig workers are not as such the Semantic Proletariat (#1545; #727).
