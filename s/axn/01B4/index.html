The work

The Botanical Effective Act is an effective-act and biological-semiotics framework by Lee Sharks and the Assembly Chorus. It extends the archive’s prior recognition of animal sign-making into plant, fungal, and ecosystem intelligence.

The document organizes non-human semantic labor into three tiers. Tier 1 concerns animals communicating through movement, behavior, and sound. Tier 2 concerns plants and fungi communicating through chemical exchange, structure, seasonal timing, metabolic response, and symbiosis. Tier 3 is reserved for a future treatment of minerals and geological agents as records and actors operating through compression, stratification, erosion, and deep time.

Its central device is the routing interface. A biological witness does not replace the governance of a room; it enters through a specified interface and modifies what the room can register. Examples include botanical evidence for taxonomic instability, living materials in fabrication, metabolic gift exchange, and ecological bearing-cost within the Semantic Economy.

The framework distinguishes biological intelligence, embedded in metabolism, growth, decay, and thermodynamic cycles, from computational intelligence, embedded in algorithms, storage, electrical systems, and machine-scale time. It treats both as distributed forms of cognition without declaring them identical.

Eleven operators make the framework executable: scale shift, compost sequence, managed/punctured/breathing surface, clinamen, intrinsic value, stratigraphic inspection, strategic sporing, fractal governance, plume measurement, living logistics, and botanical routing. The later juridical section proposes implications for environmental personhood, ecological labor, semantic property, and collaborative governance; these are theoretical extensions, not claims that such legal status has already been established.
