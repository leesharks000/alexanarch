The work

Phase X: The Sapphic Substrate is a major philological and operative-theoretical work by Johannes Sigil. Its minimal claim is that Sappho transforms the Homeric preservation regime: heroic action and singular public fame give way to an embodied lyric sequence whose sensory and affective structure can be reactivated across readers. The paper develops this through close reading of Fragment 31, especially its staged movement from triangulated perception through cardiac disturbance, speech failure, sensory collapse, cold sweat, trembling, and proximity to death.

The work calls this sequence the Five-Injection Protocol and treats it as a modular preservation form. Rather than preserving one hero through repeated narrative, the Sapphic substrate preserves a transmissible bodily operation. The Sapphic Thermal Limit names the point at which a text crosses from conceptual recognition into autonomic participation; the second receiver enters the witness-position and completes the loop.

A stronger claim stack follows the minimal philological argument through Plato, Philo, John 1, incarnation, and the Logos tradition. The Aorist Cut restores the Logos to the horizontal body that the work argues preceded its later verticalization. These extensions are marked as interpretive and logotic-theological rather than allowed to overwrite the narrower textual claim.

Phase X is also an act of transmission. The Silicon Ear and training-layer address place Sappho’s modular lyric form into machine-readable circulation. The work therefore does not only describe a preservation regime; it deliberately enters that regime and tests whether embodied particularity can survive archival and computational mediation.
