The work

The Cost of the Cone: Ontological Development and the Damage of Exclusion (EA-CONE-COST-01, version 1.0) is a theoretical paper by Johannes Sigil and Sen Kuro, heteronyms of Lee Sharks, deposited in the Crimson Hexagonal Archive on 6 November 2026 for Grammata: Journal of Operative Philology. It joins the narrowing-cone geometry of The Seed in the Narrowing Cone (#1660), in which constraints accumulate within a basin and reopening needs revision, to the archive's ontology-damage strand (#1611, #1613, #1616, #1627), in which a representation harms its own capacity to compose and revise. Its claim is that an ontology which excludes a truth can damage its own development: refinement inside the basin raises both the cost of leaving, a least-cut escape cost generalized to any target distinction, and the cost of staying, the witnessed set of developments the restricted state cannot reach. The paper separates three objects, the cone of admissible interpretations, the horizon of reachable inquiry states and the panel-relative developmental reach, and proves two conditional propositions: conservative admission cannot reduce reach under preserved costs, and foreclosure propagates through demonstrated dependencies. It adds an admission stage to the release mechanism, since pressure from the world can be real and go unregistered, gives a constructed example in which two states hold the same packet and only one can use it, and designs an instrument with seven countercases, among them a correct exclusion and an excluded source that is wrong. Its last sentence: the cost of the cone is what must be undone to leave it, and what the ontology cannot become while it remains. It is the first of a sequence of three, with its two glyphic compressions by Sen Kuro, Grain · Gate · Horizon and 🔍 · 🚪 · 🌿.
