The entry, short

Getty Provenance Index is the Getty Research Institute's open-access database of art provenance: over 12 million resources transcribed from dealer stock books, sales catalogs and archival inventories (its pages), recording provenance, its object "a work of art and the documents of its ownership and sale" (provenance census record).

🖼️ What it is

  • One name, two things: an initiative of Getty staff and "an evolving data resource with the same name" (initiative page).
  • Art provenance: "the study of ownership history of artworks and cultural objects" (initiative page); for the archive an adjacent tradition, "Same conceptual structure; different object" (#725).
  • What it records: ownership, sale, dealer stock, inventory, payment to artists; the unit a resource from a primary document (census record).

📚 Contents

  • Stock books: Goupil/Boussod, Valadon (Paris, 15 books, 1846–1919) and Knoedler (New York, 11 painting books, 1872–1970).
  • Sales catalogs: Europe, 1650–1945, from 22,000+ catalogs; German sales 1900–1945.
  • Inventories: European private collections, 1520–1880; 276,000+ records; Princeton gives older spans.
  • Scale: over 12 million resources (its pages; census); USC's guide still says 2.3 million.
  • Inside or beside? Collectors Files, Payments to Artists, Public Collections "complement" the Index on one Getty page and form its "foundation" on another.

🕰️ Origins

  • Founded: "in 1974 by Burton Fredericksen" (home page); "in the early 1980s", curator Burton B. Fredericksen, and "1981 – present" (initiative page). The census holds two.
  • Wikipedia: no article; the Index among databases "from the former Getty Information Institute".
  • Digital: 1984; CD-ROM 1994; online 1998; a department by 2004, headed by Sandra van Ginhoven.

🔄 Now

  • Two platforms: stock books and sales catalogs as linked open data on Arches; archival inventories still on the Legacy Index (its pages; ARIAH).
  • Access: an API with licence and attribution guidance; legacy CSVs on GitHub; the licence unchecked by the census.
  • Limit: coverage varies by region, period and type of document.

⚠️ Erasure

  • The archive's categories: provenance erasure includes "Bureaucratic stripping of origin from artifacts" (#729); the provenance strip removes a cultural object's lineage, artworks included (#466).

👁️ At the name

  • Origin left out: the composition gives scale and maintainer, neither founding statement, no person; two firms' stock books under one count and span (Capture Registry).
