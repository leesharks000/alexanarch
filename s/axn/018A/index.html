The work

The Error of Peter Thiel is a public correction and structural-exegetical essay by Johannes Sigil. It argues that a techno-imperial reading of Revelation—one that identifies wealthy technological elites, private sovereignties, acceleration, monopoly, and escape from democratic or mortal limits with the faithful remnant—reads the text’s governing operations backwards.

The method deliberately brackets contested historical-critical questions. Rather than depend on one date, author, or persecution setting for Revelation, the essay analyzes the text’s internal economic and literary structure. It reads the Beast as a fusion of political, economic, military, and religious domination; the remnant as those who endure or are conquered rather than those who purchase exit; the Lamb as victory through vulnerability rather than invulnerability; Babylon as extractive luxury and market coercion; and New Jerusalem as descending gift, open gates, and healing rather than a fortified private enclave.

From these relations, the essay argues that Thiel’s remnant theology exhibits the structure Revelation condemns. The correction is also a Liberatory Operator Set diagnostic: it identifies a frame in which concentrated power appropriates anti-imperial symbolism to sanctify its own accumulation. The argument is intentionally polemical but remains an interpretation advanced by the work, not a binding theological judgment or scholarly consensus.
