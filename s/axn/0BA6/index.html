The entry, short

UbuWeb is an independent online archive of avant-garde work, "the internet's largest archive of free avant-garde materials" in its own words, founded in 1996 by the poet Kenneth Goldsmith; it holds copies of out-of-print and hard-to-find works and leaves their copyrights with their holders.

🏛️ What it holds

  • Sections: Film & Video to Ethnopoetics; /ubu Editions, Papers, Outsiders; the whole Tellus cassette archive.
  • Count: none stated in the saved sources.
  • Origin: a repository for visual, concrete and sound poetry, November 1996, founded against the marginal distribution of avant-garde material; users untracked.

📜 On what terms

  • Rights: "The copyrights belong to the copyright holders, not us"; no licence, no permissions record.
  • Rule: out of print is "fair game"; in print and available to all, untouched; some work posted without permission.
  • Removal: immediate on return to print; an artist is asked to "let us know" (FAQ); the census reads removal on the artist's request.
  • Copies: poor quality by choice, many from fans' rips.
  • Authorship: editors select and post; no author page and no naming policy in the saved pages.

⚙️ How it runs

  • Means: volunteers, US$50 a month hosting, bandwidth from universities and named partners; independent.
  • Makers: editors across the United States; masthead, board and manifesto unread; hand-coded html.
  • Free: nothing for sale, no ads, no registration; mostly English.
  • Status: paused January 2024, resumed February 2025; archived by the Library of Congress; mirrored.

🔎 The archive's reading

  • Holding: copies circulate; rights stay with holders, standing with the artist.
  • Copying: conveying copies is no authorship without a distinct function.
  • Names: how it names a maker, and what a collapse of names loses, is unshown.
  • The Overview: accurate at its grain; counts on no saved page; rights, holder and artist dropped (Capture Registry); "pirate shadow library" in one reference.
  • Existence tax: falls on the independent; an adequate answer names the object at its own level.
