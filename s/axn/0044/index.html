The work

PROVENANCE NODE: BORGES & THE CRIMSON HEXAGON is a provenance document by Lee Sharks, deposited 8 January 2026, linking four points in a single hermeneutic chain: Borges's 1941 The Library of Babel, the 2003–2004 a transfiguration archive, the later Crimson Hexagon corporate name, and the 2026 NH-OS architecture.

Its most careful move is what it declines to claim. The document does not assert that Borges copied later work, nor that the later project caused the earlier text in ordinary chronology — a disclaimer that has to be made explicit because the archive's retrocausal vocabulary would otherwise be read as a claim about time rather than about canon.

What it does assert is a relation of reading: that the later architecture makes something in the earlier text legible which was not legible before, and that this is how canonical relations are actually formed. A provenance node is the archive's genre for such a link — a record whose function is to hold a connection stable enough to cite.
