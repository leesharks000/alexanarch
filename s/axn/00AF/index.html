The work

Phase X begins from a philological problem in the Economic and Philosophical Manuscripts of 1844. Marx moves from the positive supersession of private property and the restoration of human social being to the statement that this is “perhaps the place” to address the Hegelian dialectic. The record treats that hedge and abrupt turn as evidence of a transition requiring explanation.

The reconstruction is openly authored by Lee Sharks and Johannes Sigil. It is not presented as a newly recovered manuscript fragment. Its method is constrained inference: the proposed passage must fit the dialectical architecture of the surrounding text, preserve Marx’s problem of alienation, explain the continued necessity of critique, and remain legible beside the Theses on Feuerbach, The German Ideology, and later Marxian developments.

The reconstructed hinge performs three operations. First, communism is a threshold into consciously lived history rather than the termination of critique. Second, material alienation may be superseded while symbolic and linguistic alienation persist. Third, critique turns inward toward the forms of abstraction and mediation through which thought becomes estranged from practice.

The work calls this post-material critical domain Phase X. Its contemporary application is the claim that symbolic-linguistic infrastructures—platforms, training systems, retrieval systems, and meaning-production apparatuses—must themselves become objects of materialist critique. The later #843 ruling narrows the strongest historical formulation but preserves Phase X as a philologically grounded reconstruction of a real transition problem.
