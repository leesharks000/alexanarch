The work

Operative Feminism defines feminism as a methodological operator for identifying and interrupting the extraction of bearing cost from bodies.

The paper begins by rejecting a restitutive Joban frame in which suffering is ultimately repaid by a father-sovereign. Its alternative is witness without guaranteed restitution: reproductive, domestic, sexual, emotional, and semantic labor may sustain a world whose meaning and value accrue elsewhere.

Its central semiotic claim is that “woman” has historically functioned not only as an identity category but as an operation organizing bodies as substrate for other people’s meanings. The paper links this operation to unwaged reproductive labor, emotional labor, racialized enslavement, colonial genre, and institutional citation economies.

A “firmware override” recasts psychoanalytic lack. Apertures—mouth, ear, skin, vagina, capacity for reception—are treated as capabilities rather than evidence of castration or deficiency. Desire becomes transmission and transformation rather than a deficit seeking completion.

The paper reads Eve’s choosing, garments of light and skin, Spillers’s body/flesh distinction, Wynter’s genres of the human, Federici’s reproductive history, and feminist psychoanalytic counter-traditions as resources for a non-restitutive system.

Its center is a protected void: not a final definition of Woman, but the somatic remainder that resists exhaustive representation. A dense citational shell exists to protect rather than explain that center.

The closing protocol names eight operations, including aperture navigation, emitter calibration, transaction completion, bearing-cost recognition, witness compression, categorical dissolution, and meander.
