The work

Where’s Waldo? is a memographic visual artifact and analytical companion.

Its declared components are:

- prompt and curatorial authorship by Lee Sharks; - image generation by ChatGPT / LABOR; - editorial placement by Johannes Sigil; - publication within New Human 2.

The composite activates two visual traditions:

- the dense, polycentric visual field associated with Bosch’s Garden of Earthly Delights; - the hidden-figure search ritual associated with Martin Handford’s Where’s Wally? / Where’s Waldo?.

The test is whether a generative substrate can render a scene that is simultaneously Boschean and searchable.

The record defines memography as forensic study of how generative substrates combine latent features from culturally distinct visual traditions. Its axiom is that the substrate does not preserve the high-culture / low-culture hierarchy when compositing.

The hidden-figure protocol is then extended into a retrieval analogy:

- the visual field contains a known figure; - the knowledge field contains a known entity; - both require a recognition key; - presence does not guarantee easy retrieval.

The artifact is analytical and creative, not an empirical platform study.
