The entry, short

Generative AI and Jobs: A Refined Global Index of Occupational Exposure is the ILO's index of occupational exposure to generative AI, made with NASK (Working Paper 140, 20 May 2025; the paper): an exposure estimate of tasks' technical feasibility for automation that observes no performed work, its figures an upper threshold, not an impact (Labor Vector record; the paper).

🧮 How it is built

  • Scores: three LLMs, 1,640 workers in Poland and experts score tasks 0 to 1; a predictor trained on their scores scores the rest (the paper).
  • Gradients: four, by the mean and spread of task scores; Gradient 4 is high, consistent exposure (the paper; ILO brief).
  • Makers: the ILO with NASK, whose page names the ILO–NASK Index; eight authors listed, or seven (the paper; NASK).

📊 What it finds

  • Exposed: one in four workers; 3.3% of employment in Gradient 4, 4.7% of women's, 2.4% of men's (the paper).
  • Income and sex: 34% exposed in high-income countries, 11% in low-income; there 41% of women, 28% of men (the paper).
  • Occupations: clerical most exposed; digitized and media roles rising; physical tasks least (the paper; ILO brief).
  • Against 2023: mean score 0.29 against 0.30 (ILO brief).

🔄 Transformation, qualified

  • Most likely: transformation, not redundancy (the paper; ILO brief; NASK).
  • Yet: efficiencies "may mean that fewer workers are needed" (the paper).

⚠️ What it is not

  • Not impact: exposure, "not the actual impact on employment"; an upper threshold; a 2025 snapshot (the paper).
  • Its survey: Poland only, comparable within groups; all tasks weighted equally (the paper).
  • Unsettled: observed job or wage change, whether exposure causes it (Labor Vector record).

🧭 Whose labor

  • Supply, direction, gains: not recorded; it scores feasibility, not performed work (Labor Vector record).
  • Gains: in complementing expertise, cited from economists (the paper).
  • The class: semantic labor automated under concentrated ownership (#499); displaced labor relocated (#533); job loss as scenario (#521, #520).
