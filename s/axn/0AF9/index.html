The entry, short

Bossware and Employment Tech Database is Coworker.org's database of more than 550 labor-focused technology products, researched 2020 to 2021 and published 17 November 2021, that touch the labor process from hiring to productivity monitoring, each assessed against worker-rights criteria (Coworker; the report; census record); it is historical and not exhaustive (census record; the report).

🗂️ What it holds

  • Products: one row each, 635 saved; monitoring, safety, benefits, labor-market optimization (census record; the report).
  • Scope: AI, location tracking and biometrics, "often without their knowledge" (Coworker).
  • Assessment: consent, workers' access to their data, grievance, forced use; only 35% share privacy information (Coworker; the report).

⚖️ Who supplies, directs, gains

  • Supplies: the workers each product targets, low-wage and hourly workers at the report's centre (census record).
  • Directs: employers who deploy the products, through vendors (census record).
  • Gains: vendors and investors; costs shifted onto workers (census record; the report).

📕 The report

  • Little Tech: an unregulated marketplace aggregating data about workers (Negrón, 2021; CC BY-NC-ND).
  • Findings: eroding labor standards and worker power; capital-driven; questionable hiring tech (the report).

🧭 Hiring as production

  • Screening: AI interview platforms such as HireVue, in the table, capture candidates' data as institutional capital, the labor supplied free; no illegality alleged (#747).

⚠️ Limits

  • Not exhaustive: non-U.S. and emerging products, and the gig platforms, omitted (the report; census record).
  • Public documents: use, frequency and effect on wages it cannot show (the report; census record).
  • Not current: no update since 2021 (census record).
  • Not testimony: stories invited, none held (Coworker; census record).
