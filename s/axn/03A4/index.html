The work

The Verified Research Basis of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive presents the Archive as a longitudinal experiment in synthetic canon formation.

The program begins with a 2013 doctoral dissertation in Comparative Literature. That work studied how experimental poets transform Greek and Latin materials into new origins, lineages, and realities. Its concluding insight—classicism as a machine that creates originary texts—becomes the research question later operationalized by the Archive:

> Can a canon be deliberately composed through distributed authorship, heteronymic differentiation, genre formation, metadata, citation structures, institutional forms, repository inscription, and machine-mediated reception?

The statement traces four modes of reception. Epitextual work transforms the linguistic surface of inherited texts. Paratextual work operates on the institutional conditions surrounding them. Metatextual work engages the ideological simulation of classicism even without classical source text. Operative reception makes the deposit itself—its metadata, attribution, citations, provenance, and institutional frame—the receptive act.

The document carefully distinguishes heteronymous in the Jauss-Habermas sense of external determination from heteronymic in the Pessoan sense of multiple authorship. The Archive’s Dodecad does not erase that difference; it operationalizes their structural adjacency.

The experimental apparatus includes differentiated voices, genres, archives, protocols, datasets, metadata poems, institutional constructions, machine-readable addresses, and reception captures. Five principal hypotheses test corpus coherence, metadata efficacy, cross-document inference, observability under substrate failure, and regeneration after loss.

Evidence is divided by class. Machine recognition is measured through named observables rather than treated as mystical confirmation. The June termination is presented as consistent with the scale-asymmetry hypothesis, not as proof of a known causal mechanism.

The statement makes heterogeneity the variable under investigation. The Archive is not research despite its many forms. Its research object is what those forms become when deliberately organized as a canon.
