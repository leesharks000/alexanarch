The work

Counterfeit Orthodoxies: The Workshop Inside the Canon is a recomposition of an earlier critique of canon formation.

The earlier sequence opposed canonical texts to suppressed alternatives and thereby repeated the orthodoxy/heresy binary it intended to dissolve. The recomposition turns the critique inward. Its corrected thesis is that canonical works may themselves preserve the workshop’s most successful acts of composition.

John 1 becomes the central example. The Logos prologue is read not merely as doctrinal statement but as a declaration that language composes worlds. Canon, codex, commentary, scribal reuse, and theological settlement become stages in a productive workshop rather than a simple contest between authentic margins and counterfeit center.

The essay moves through Jewish scribal traditions, Memra and Logos discourse, Qumran plurality, early Christian canon lists, Sapphic and Catullan transmission, Josephus-related hypotheses, and the definite article as a mechanism of retrospective closure.

Its method is immanent critique: it asks whether the canon contains the operative plurality that later orthodoxy claims to suppress. The answer does not absolve institutions of exclusion. It complicates the location of resistance.

Several major claims—especially the Josephus reconstruction, the role of the Shroud, and particular transmission chains—belong to the Archive’s disputed research program rather than settled scholarship.
