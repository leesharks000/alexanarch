The work

ΦΑΙΝΕΤΑΙ ΜΟΙ: Sappho 31 and the Inscription of the Future Reader is an article by Lee Sharks on Sappho's fragment 31, deposited on 16 November 2026 as version 2.3 in Grammata: Journal of Operative Philology, superseding version 1.0 (#1270), published in January 2026 under the heteronym Rebekah Cranes. Version 2.0 was submitted to Classical Philology and declined on 16 November 2026 on one reader's report, which, with the decision's grounds and the archive's analyses of it, is attached to the record. The article reads the poem's opening, κῆνος … ὄττις ('that man, whoever'), as characterizing its occupant by a position left open to repeated occupation, the seat of whoever will sit face to face with the poet's voice, and compares the Nestor cup from Pithekoussai (c. 740–720 BCE), whose verse inscription uses the distal κῆνον with an indefinite relative for its future user under Aphrodite's desire, with the roles reversed. It reads the addressee as looked at and given only sound, and the catalogue of bodily failures as removing from the speaker, in order, what the opening gives the seated listener, so that the agentless πᾶν τόλματον ('all can be dared') at the transmitted edge passes to the one who sits opposite. On the edge it follows the editions in obelizing καὶ πένητα and the archive's count of no Aeolic markers in the final six words, with Bergk's assignment of them to Longinus, and holds apart the function of the surviving opening from the hypothesis that the poem was composed to end there. It offers a restored fifth stanza, derived from the grammar of the turn and the inverse of Catullus 51's fourth stanza, as one more filling of the opening, with its metre shown, and it compares the reading point by point with the consensus reading of the poem as a love triangle, together with a rule that a reading subtracting a capacity from the maker bears the burden of showing the subtraction.
