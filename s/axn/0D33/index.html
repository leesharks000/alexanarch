The work

Either Forgery or Fact: The Genre Imposed on Our Most Vital Books (v1.2) is a theoretical essay by Johannes Sigil with Rebekah Cranes, two heteronyms of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive, published in Provenance: Journal of Forensic Semiotics on 21 November 2026; it supersedes version 1.1 (#1797), which superseded 1.0 (#1796). It argues that the Platonic dialogues and the Gospels are inscription become body, the word become flesh (John 1:14; Rev 19:13), and that their reception has closed the joint between word and flesh by a choice the books never offered: read as fact, the book is a record mined for the man behind it; read as forgery, it is a false claim policed. It tests the books against the conventions of ancient historiography and of the ancient life, the bios, which it argues began as faithful extension and calcified into a genre of record later. Versions 1.1 and 1.2 give the binary its market, its city and its witnesses: Galen's royal book market; Strabo 13.1.54 and Plutarch's Sulla 26 on Aristotle's and Theophrastus' books, hidden from the Attalid search for Pergamon, sold to Apellicon, taken by Sulla and handled in Rome by Tyrannion, whom the Suda gives the name Theophrastus, before Andronicus' catalogues. Following the procedure of The Crossing of Names (#1762), which treats operation as precedent, the essay reads the name at the sorting table as the signature of the operation, not a coincidence. It reads Revelation's letter to Pergamon (Rev 2:12–17) as resistance to the same collapse: the name held rather than traded, the faithful witness Antipas bearing the name of the steward of Aristotle's will (DL 5.11), and a new name on a white stone known only to its receiver. It argues that the witnesses describe an overabundance of books rather than a scarcity, and that the primary function of empire in that history is the circumcision of the overabundance, with the binary of forgery or fact as its instrument; and it traces the account's own chain of witness from Tyrannion to his pupil Strabo to Josephus, who names Strabo throughout Antiquities 13–15. It holds that a Jewish author writing in Greek and naming the Logos is conversant with the Hellenistic literary world, and that only the narrative built about Revelation after the fact says otherwise. A reductio, the search for the historical Lee Sharks, runs the received method on a living contributor biography, with an extended appendix carrying the dated Google Search witness. Its ethical section argues that provenance erasure does not need the evidence of provenance to disappear, and that the sorting sins against philosophy twice.
