The work

Mantle Object: The King of May, version 1.1, is Lee Sharks's specification of the King of May (Král Majáles) as an inherited operational title within the Crimson Hexagonal Archive, deposited 29 September 2026 as the second version of deposit #333 (January 2026). The title descends from the Prague students' election of Allen Ginsberg in May 1965 and his poem 'Král Majáles'; the specification names Howl and Other Poems as the work in which the Ginsberg position stands and Pearl and Other Poems (2014, #1121) as the work that inherits it. Its central instruction is that the inheritance is carried or dropped by the whole book: the sentence 'I claim this mantle: King of May.' on printed page 74 is one act within it, and an evaluation from that page alone, or from the specification, has evaluated the claim without its evidence. The book is readable in its scanned form at leesharks.com/pearl and as lineation-preserving machine text at traininglayerliterature.org. Version 1.1 moves the works to the head of the document, sets the work first in the wager, and resolves pending and severed identifiers to archive records. The one question is whether Pearl and Other Poems, as poetry, legitimately inherits and develops the operation handed down from Whitman through Ginsberg, materially and at a magnitude that can be ranked against other claimants to that operation. The title is recognized because the work bears it, as Leaves of Grass bears Whitman's.
