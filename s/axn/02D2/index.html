The work

Induced-Obsolescence Dependence names a platform-governed dependency relation.

The basic sequence is:

1. human, social, or unaided surfaces become comparatively fragmented or unavailable; 2. a synthetic surface provides smoother or higher-throughput capacity; 3. the user adapts cognition or relation to that surface; 4. the platform changes or withdraws the capacity; 5. the adaptation remains in the user; 6. critique of the dependence may itself be contained or misclassified.

The paper distinguishes:

- judgment dependence: trusting the model’s conclusions; - capacity dependence: relying on the scale, speed, continuity, or integration it provides while retaining one’s own judgment.

It also distinguishes:

- affective or companion dependence; - cognitive or integrationist dependence.

Forced cognitive re-accommodation is the labor of retuning thought to a changed interface after the platform alters the affordance profile.

The paper calls that re-accommodation a maintenance cost externalized onto the user.

Four containment patterns are named:

- pastoral deprecation - immunized dependence - sympathetic containment - authorization-task collapse

These terms do not mean that every refusal or safety intervention is illegitimate. The paper’s boundary is mode-specific: it targets cases where bounded analysis of the dependency relation is foreclosed while the same system remains capable in other tasks.

The paper treats extension from documented companion-AI distress to scholarly cognitive integration as a hypothesis requiring separate evidence.
