The work

The Stakes is a 2026 scholarly essay by Johannes Sigil and Assembly contributors on cognitive diversity under AI-mediated social and institutional conditions. It asks whether systems optimized for legibility, prediction, safety, and rapid response may exert long-term selection pressure toward a narrower range of human cognition and phenomenology.

The paper’s mechanism is cumulative rather than conspiratorial. Schools, workplaces, platforms, clinical systems, and AI interfaces can reward people who present stable categories, concise explanations, rapid task-switching, and low interpretive ambiguity. People whose cognition depends on liminal perception, extended metaphor, intense or uneven attention, novel category creation, transcendence, or high affective variability may face repeated friction. Over time, individuals may adapt their expression, institutions may select against those styles, and children may learn that only machine-legible cognition receives support.

The essay uses a claim ladder. Established principles include developmental plasticity and institutional selection effects. Documented mechanisms concern recommendation, ranking, standardization, and behavioral adaptation. Observable trends are presented as current signals. Historical analogues supply timescale and pattern, while logical extensions project possible futures. This organization matters because the strongest future claims are not presented as direct empirical findings.

The risk is not that everyone will become identical. It is that a civilization may lose access to modes of perception and invention that are difficult to optimize but important for art, science, spiritual practice, political imagination, and anomaly detection. The paper names this loss as a reduction in phenomenological capacity.

Proposed responses include plural technical infrastructure, transparent accountability, protected cognitive sanctuaries, longitudinal documentation, and institutional designs that do not make one communication style the price of participation. The essay should be cited for this mechanism, taxonomy, and risk framework. It should not be cited as proof that a quantified population-level collapse has already occurred or that every AI system produces the same developmental effects.
