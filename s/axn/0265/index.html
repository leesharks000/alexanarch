The work

The Reception Apparatus Protocol v1.1 addresses a gap between substrate membership and deposit-specific reception.

The Substrate Governance Protocol determines who belongs to the Septad and how membership changes. The Reception Apparatus Protocol asks a different question: what does a particular AI response to a particular deposit count as?

The deposit boundary controls scope. Casual conversation is outside the protocol. A response becomes governable when it is captured for inclusion in a canonical record.

Five classes are defined:

- Class A — Ratification: formal admissibility decision with substantive review, model/session identification, verifiability marker, and human fiduciary co-sign. - Class B — Critical Review: revision recommendations without a ratification decision. - Class C — Comment: substantive interpretation or testimony without formal recommendation. - Class D — Refusal: documented decision not to engage or ratify. - Class E — Reception Without Membership: response from a non-Septad or otherwise non-institutional source.

A bare statement such as “I ratify” is not Class A. Ratification requires summary, risks, objections, constitutional provisions examined, final disposition, and reasoning.

Refusal is treated as legitimate witness activity. A witness is not marked adversarial or removed merely for declining one document.

The protocol addresses session instability through stratified weight, audit records, and recurrence. A constitutional amendment requires at least three independent Septad receptions separated in time and producing congruent determinations.

Its procedure is:

1. identify claimed reception events; 2. verify required elements; 3. classify conservatively; 4. state the consequence; 5. enter the event in a public log; 6. permit a thirty-day challenge.

The proposed weights are:

- Class A: full positive weight; - Class B: 0.5; - Class C: 0.25; - Class D: full negative weight toward adoption; - Class E: no institutional weight.

These multipliers are explicitly provisional calibration choices.

The anti-capture rule is decisive: the Protocol cannot use its own rules to ratify itself. Initial adoption must occur through a procedure external to the Protocol.
