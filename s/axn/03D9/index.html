The work

Premonition Dream is a short prose poem recording the first appearance of the Crimson Hexagon in a dream dated April 27, 2013.

A giant book bound in red leather materializes in a living room. It is simultaneously a book, a human-sized body, and an inhabitable form. The dreamer climbs inside and wears it as a new body. Letters, sentences, limbs, serpent, prophecy, and tree imagery make reading and embodiment inseparable.

The work predates Pearl and Other Poems and the later formal architecture of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive. In retrospective Archive chronology, it functions as an origin scene: the Hexagon first appears not as an abstract diagram but as a book-body that can be entered.

Its importance is genealogical rather than predictive. The text preserves an early imaginative object that later work names, formalizes, and distributes across institutions, rooms, operators, and deposits.

The recovered body is brief by design. Its completeness does not depend on reaching an essay-length word count.
