The entry, short

Sophistical Refutations Aristotle's treatise on apparent refutation: reasoning that seems genuine but is not, or is valid yet inappropriate to the subject, closed by a recapitulation and a sentence denying that anything of the inquiry existed before it.

definition What it treats

  • Subject "Sophistic refutations, i.e. what appear to be refutations but are really fallacies instead."
  • Refutation Reasoning involving the contradictory of the given conclusion.
  • Extended sense Includes reasoning that is valid but only appears appropriate to the thing in question.
  • Sophist's art "The semblance of wisdom without the reality."

taxonomy Kinds of fallacy

  • Two styles Dependent on language, and independent of language.
  • Six in language Ambiguity, amphiboly, combination, division of words, accent, form of expression.
  • Seven outside language Stated as seven kinds.
  • Why names Names are finite and things infinite, so one name carries several meanings.

practice Examination and sophistry

  • Examination A branch of dialectic.
  • Dialectician and sophist The sophist only appears to apply the common principles to the matter in hand.
  • Krabbe's reading "A useful treatise for sophists", read in deposit 1697 against the article's sophistry opposition.

placement Place among the treatises

  • Appendix to the Topics Held as a field interpretation.
  • Organon A grouping by the ancient commentators; the title was never Aristotle's.
  • Stylometry At the world pole on the first axis; the real text on the apparatus side after the file erratum.

ending The closing chapter

  • Aim A syllogistic capacity from endoxa, for dialectic and peirastic.
  • Socrates "Asked and did not answer"; on the archive's reading an antecedent source on neither reading.
  • No predecessors "οὐδὲν παντελῶς ὑπῆρχεν", read literally in deposit 1603.
  • Rhetoric and eristic Rhetoric grew by succession; paid teachers gave products, not the art.
  • Pioneering limit Omissions are to be forgiven and discoveries thanked.

reception Reception

  • Gloss Translators render the sentence whole; the narrowing enters in the gloss.
  • Primacy The article's lead turns the total negation into the attribute "the first work to treat".
  • Deleted particle A review series deleted οὐ at composition, reported as unperceived.
  • Faithful sentences Some article sentences stay faithful to their source.
