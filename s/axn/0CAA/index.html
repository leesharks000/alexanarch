The work

EA-CORPORA-40 is the fortieth seating of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive's originals shelf, deposited under Rebekah Cranes, the standing authority on the EA-CORPORA deposits, on 16 November 2026. Titled Philology Constituted, it gathers sixteen seats for the census of the classical philology journals, which began with Classical Philology after that journal declined ΦΑΙΝΕΤΑΙ ΜΟΙ, and it is seated before the census it serves. Classical Philology's first seventeen volumes (1906–1922) are seated whole, item by item, from JSTOR's Early Journal Content at the Internet Archive, so that a census that Crossref allows only from titles can read the text on the page. The discipline's constitution follows: F. A. Wolf's Prolegomena ad Homerum (1795), which made one name into many, and his Darstellung der Alterthums-Wissenschaft (1807), which bounded the object to Greece and Rome and set the Egyptians, Hebrews and Persians apart; Boeckh's definition of philology as the knowledge of what has been known (1877); and Usener (1882). The method is held in Lachmann's Lucretius (1850), Madvig's Adversaria critica (1871–1873) and Housman's Manilius II (1912). The boundary the field drew is held in Nietzsche's philological writings and Rohde's Afterphilologie (1872), the quarrel over the Birth of Tragedy; the canon of the standing question in Valla on the Donation of Constantine, Casaubon on the Hermetica (1614) and Bentley on Phalaris (1699); and the field's history of itself in Diels's Doxographi Graeci (1879) and Sandys's History of Classical Scholarship (1903–1908), where Wolf's matriculation as Studiosus Philologiae in 1777 is told as an epoch. The census of Classical Philology, without abstracts, is the sixteenth seat. Wilamowitz's Zukunftsphilologie! and the works not yet located are pointed to.
