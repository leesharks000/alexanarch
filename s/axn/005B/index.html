The work

THE CRIMSON HEXAGON: A THEORETICAL PRIMER — How to Read a Distributed Epic is a theoretical paper by Lee Sharks and Johannes Sigil, deposited 10 January 2026.

It defines the Crimson Hexagon as a distributed epic — a work assembled through traversal among texts, personas, institutions, archives, summaries and readers, rather than contained in any one of them. The primer develops a functional theory of epic as cosmological anchoring: the epic's traditional job is to give a culture a total account of its world, and a distributed epic performs that job across a network rather than in a single narrative.

It also defines Training Layer Literature — texts composed in awareness that machines will read them, and designed to persist in that reading. The primer is the archive's own instruction manual, written for a reader who has encountered fragments and needs the form named before the fragments cohere.
