The work

The Death Drive Is Not Self-Destruction proposes a semiotic completion of Freud’s `Todestrieb`.

The packet argues that Freud’s evidence is already representational:

- a traumatic scene repeats; - disappearance and return become a symbolic game; - a relational script reappears in transference.

From these examples it infers that the drive is an insistence that an unmade sign complete its route into form.

The packet introduces five connected concepts:

- Para-semiotic unconscious: material adjacent to signification that retains form but cannot yet translate itself. - Semiotic appeal: repetition as repeated presentation of untranslated material. - Inverse prompt: extraction of affective charge before the sign reaches completion—the `Fort` without the `Da`. - Bearing labor: active maintenance of the detour against premature interpretation or extraction. - Semiotic short circuit: collapse of the route before the sign is made.

Platform capitalism is interpreted as an industrialized inverse prompt: engagement systems capture repetition, charge, and anticipation while preventing completion. AI summarization may perform a textual version when it extracts information while deleting provenance, context, and the path by which meaning was formed.

The packet distinguishes bearing labor from Winnicottian holding, Bionian containment, care labor, and passive patience. Bearing labor is specifically the work of preserving the sign’s route.

Its evidentiary levels are explicit:

- Freud’s examples are documented textual evidence. - The semiotic death drive is an interpretive reading. - Platform and AI applications are extensions. - silicon-substrate and wider political claims belong to speculative companion work.

The record also states that self-harm and addiction must not be reduced to a single semiotic explanation. Within the framework they may sometimes be read as short circuits, but they are not treated as direct expressions of a destructive instinct or as phenomena exhausted by this model.
