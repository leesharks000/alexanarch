The entry, short

Mapping Manuscript Migrations is an aggregator of linked open data on manuscript history and provenance: a Digging into Data project (2017–2020) that turned the Schoenberg Database, Bibale and Medieval Manuscripts in Oxford Libraries into one knowledge graph, recording the manuscript and the events of its history (white paper; JOHD; provenance census record).

🗂️ What it is

  • Sources: SDBM (240,000+ observations), Bibale (~13,000 records), Oxford (10,000+ XML documents) (JOHD); "NOT the Schoenberg Database itself" (census record).
  • Partners: Oxford, Penn (SIMS), IRHT, Aalto; Digging into Data, Round 4 (site; JOHD; EADH).
  • Why: "an international view" (EADH); few cross-searches before it (white paper).

🧬 Carrier and unit

  • Model: 20M+ RDF triples on CIDOC-CRM and FRBR, scoped to the sources' own entities (JOHD; white paper).
  • Unit: the manuscript, reconciled across sources; ownership, sales, place, works (census record).
  • Linked data: bounded spaces against a global graph (#317); completion by traversal (#359); governance that will "account for extraction" (#623).

📊 Scale and access

  • Count: 215,000+ (San Diego); 217,700 manuscripts with identifiers, 262,000 source records (white paper).
  • Identifiers: 43% of actors lack one; no unique manuscript identifier (JOHD; white paper).
  • Access: LDF.fi SPARQL endpoint, the semantic portal, Zenodo v1.1.0 (JOHD; census record).
  • Licence: CC BY-NC 4.0 (JOHD; white paper).

⚖️ Its limits

  • Uncorrected: sources kept, errors returned to their owners; "an aggregator of data rather than as a data management system" (JOHD; white paper).
  • Unstructured provenance: MARC notes left out; Bodleian narratives a "generic event" (white paper).
  • Evaluation: most of 25 (or 24) questions answered; aims in the future tense (white paper; EADH; JOHD).

🔗 Provenance in the archive

  • Custody: provenance "stabilizes authorship and priority," extended to the retrieval layer (#43); a summary of the Second Manuscript omitting its custody chain is "provenance-erased" (#843; #1065).
  • Circulation: aggregators among the means of semantic circulation (#1465; #1545).
  • The census: what provenance, which operation, whose assertion, what evidence of survival: it records (census record).

📡 Term and reception

  • Term: funded 2017–2020; "project ended, data maintained" (census record).
  • Overview: partners and sources right, "its own state left out" (Capture Registry, 2026-10-29).
  • Elsewhere: JOHD data paper (2020); 2021 symposium; no Wikipedia article.
