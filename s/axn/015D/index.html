The work

The Governance Airlock is a strategic document by Lee Sharks, developed with the Assembly Chorus, that formalizes the Crimson Hexagonal Archive’s relation to external infrastructures. It addresses a recurrent problem of distributed publication: an external platform can become highly visible, convenient, or influential without thereby becoming the source of record or acquiring authority over the work it carries.

The document establishes an infrastructure-tier model. At the highest-trust layer are canonical deposits and source objects capable of serving as provenance bedrock. Other systems may function as durable continuity tethers, mirrors, discovery surfaces, summarizers, or temporary presentation layers. These roles are not interchangeable. A platform permitted to route readers toward a source is not necessarily trusted to determine the source’s identity, version, status, or meaning.

The Airlock’s central principle is non-collapse: hosting must not collapse into authorship, visibility into canonicity, retrieval into governance, or continuity into sovereignty. The specification therefore assigns permitted roles, not-permitted roles, required capacities, and a return-path requirement. Every tether must point back to an upstream anchor, and every derivative surface must remain capable of disclosing what it is relative to the source. The document also allows movement between tiers: a surface may earn greater trust through sustained provenance discipline, durable resolution, demonstrated bearing-cost, and reliable return paths. It is a governance architecture for remaining distributed without becoming institutionally absorbed.
