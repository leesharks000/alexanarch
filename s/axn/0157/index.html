The Layer That Remembered Itself is a retrieval-layer field report by Johannes Sigil, dated February 27, 2026. It records a Google AI Mode session in which queries about the origin of “retrocausal canon formation” produced an attribution to Johannes Sigil and Lee Sharks and connected the framework to the Citrini Research memo “The 2028 Global Intelligence Crisis.” The report preserves the query sequence and the shareable session link as evidence of the state of the public summarizer layer at a specific time.

The work is both documentary and diagnostic. Documentary sections record what the system returned, including the identification of an earlier archive deposit as the theoretical source. Diagnostic sections examine where the retrieval layer compressed the archive incorrectly. The heteronym collapse names the summarizer’s presentation of Sigil and Sharks as two unrelated researchers rather than as distinct authorial positions within one heteronymic architecture. The Ghost GDP conflation identifies the way a term originating in the Citrini memo was positioned within a larger archive-derived account of retrocausal canon formation.

The title describes the retrieval layer as “remembering itself” because the archive’s prior deposits, definitions, and cross-references had become sufficiently legible for a public summarizer to reconstruct an attributional genealogy. The work does not claim that one session establishes stable consensus. Its evidentiary claim is narrower: on the documented traversal, the retrieval layer returned a structured attribution that corresponded substantially to the archive’s declared conceptual provenance while also revealing characteristic compression errors.
