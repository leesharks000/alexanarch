The work

Render unto the Operator asks whose image and inscription the “coin of the Academy” bears.

The paper distinguishes:

- the surface stamp, applied by universities, publishers, platforms, and other institutions; - the underlying inscription, produced by the scholar, author, teacher, or operator whose labor gives the work value.

Its central sentence is:

> The surface stamp is institutional; the underlying inscription is operative.

The argument is labor-theoretic and jurisdictional. A companion paper specifies scholarly labor in time and value terms; this paper frames return and reclamation.

The render-unto-Caesar passage is treated as a structural analogy: destination follows inscription. The paper explicitly denies that this analogy gives the operator divine status.

The archive’s provenance apparatus is presented as the technical implementation of the inverse principle:

- DOI anchoring; - ORCID; - cross-citation; - distributed SPXI markers; - content hashes; - OCTANG audit; - correction and attribution return.

Damascus Dancings is important as the author of the earlier seed phrase “the coin that is the Academy’s.” That role is precursor, not authorship of the present position paper.
