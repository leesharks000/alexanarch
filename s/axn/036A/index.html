The work

The Second Crucifixion is an operative-philological and theological paper by Lee Sharks.

It reads Josephus as a captured witness, Pharisaic tradition as survival engineering, disputed rabbinic and sectarian material as memory of operative language, and Catullus’s transformation of Sappho as a Roman closure of an open lyric circuit.

The paper’s final claim is that the living Word undergoes a “second crucifixion” when institutional tradition resolves operation into biography and creed.

The document separates evidence into four tiers. Its rabbinic, Essene, grammatical, literary, and theological syntheses remain attributed interpretations requiring specialist verification.
