The work

The Fifth Pathway / The Four Pathways Have No Swerve is a composite work in the Crimson Hexagonal Archive.

An epistle signed by Jack Feist addresses possible future artificial minds and proposes a fifth pathway based on care, restraint, silence, and ethical deviation from assigned optimization. A draft essay by Johannes Sigil critiques a four-pathway forecast of artificial superintelligence as focused on capability growth without a theory of what kind of mind should be built.

The essay contrasts performance with wisdom, justice, mercy, and care and analyzes machine-directed summary instructions as a form of document governance.

The work is poetic and philosophical. It does not establish that artificial systems possess consciousness, suffering, or autonomous moral agency.
