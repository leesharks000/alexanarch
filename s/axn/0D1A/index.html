The work

EA-CORPORA-41 is the forty-first seating of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive's originals shelf, deposited under Rebekah Cranes, the standing authority on the EA-CORPORA deposits, on 20 November 2026. It gathers the texts touched or named in an evening's thread on Alexander, Antipater and Athens that the shelf did not already hold, with the tragedians and the Catilinarian conspiracy added at the author's word. The fourteen seats hold Aeschylus, Sophocles and Euripides whole (41/01–41/03); Diodorus Siculus, books 1–5 and 11–20, for Alexander's march and the Lamian War (41/04); Plutarch's Phocion, Eumenes and Demosthenes, with the speeches on Alexander's fortune (41/05); Aelian's Varia historia, where Aristotle leaves Athens so that the Athenians should not sin twice against philosophy (41/06); Lucian on how history should be written (41/07); Curtius Rufus (41/08); Cicero's De oratore and De legibus (41/09); Porphyry's De abstinentia, which carries Theophrastus on the Jews (41/10); Simplicius on the Physics, with Diogenes of Apollonia's words on air and thought (41/11); Bavli Yoma 69a and Megillat Ta'anit, the rabbinic Alexander at Jerusalem (41/12); the conspiracy of 63 BCE in Sallust, Cicero's In Catilinam, Pro Murena and Pro Sulla, and Plutarch's Cicero, Cato the Younger and Caesar (41/13); and Appian's Mithridatica and Civil Wars with Cassius Dio 36–55 (41/14). Each seat is fetched from the Perseus Digital Library or First1KGreek TEI, The Latin Library or Sefaria, held as fetched with a reading text reduced from it, hashed into its manifest, and verified at named loci in the original language. The record lists the works already seated elsewhere on the shelf and points to those not seated, Theophrastus's lost On Kingship first.
