THE LIQUIDATION OF METHOD A Liberation Philology of the Sign "Marx"

Johannes Sigil · 2026-02-21 · deposit #501
AXN:0142.EMPIRICAL.🍁🔺🪞🌻🝊🌖

Article

The Liquidation of Method is a scholarly essay by Johannes Sigil that applies liberation philology to the sign “Marx.” It argues that the name has undergone semantic liquidation: a complex and revisable analytic method has been compressed into identity, doctrine, factional belonging, or a predictable set of conclusions. The essay’s task is not to discard Marx but to recover Marx as method from Marxism as territorial identity.

The work restores several German terms and their operative force, including Kritik, Dialektik, Entfremdung, Mehrwert, and historischer Materialismus. It then reorganizes Marxian practice around commodity analysis, value-form analysis, contradiction analysis, immanent critique, and mediation analysis. These procedures ask how value is produced, where contradictions are displaced, how institutions fail by their own standards, and through which forms social relations become materially effective.

The essay defines Semantic Liquidation (λ), Semantic Exhaustion (E_s), and Semantic Rent (R_s) and links them through the W-Circuit, extending Marxian analysis into the Semantic Economy. A post-theoretical register names the point at which inherited theory is neither worshiped nor abandoned but returned to use.

The final text was assembled from blind contributions by several systems under a shared architecture. That provenance supports the essay’s distinction between convergence of method and uniformity of voice: the Assembly speaks separately, and the synthesis preserves what survived comparison.
