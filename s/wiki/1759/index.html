The Josephus Thesis: The Custody of the Name

Article

The Josephus Thesis: The Custody of the Name is a narrative by Sparrow Wells, a heteronym of Lee Sharks, deposited in the Crimson Hexagonal Archive on 11 November 2026, with the archive credited as a contributor. It tells the archive's Josephus Thesis as one story and carries the standing of each claim in its notes: demonstrated in a deposit, attested at a quoted locus, or held as the narrative's own.

It begins from the extant writings of Second Temple Judaism, Qumran, Philo, the Septuagint, Josephus and the New Testament, as the measure of the culture, and from the literary authority Greek and Roman writers conceded to the Jews (Theophrastus, Numenius, Seneca) and that Jewish writers claimed, down to the Sibyl whose oracle Rome kept. It sets Rome's custody of powerful books beside its custody of an author: Augustus sealing a purged Sibylline corpus under Palatine Apollo; Aristotle's corpus, with Antipater as executor, hidden at Scepsis from Pergamum and carried to Rome by Sulla; and Josephus, sent by Titus to the walls of Jerusalem, given the sacred books, housed and pensioned by Vespasian, his history signed by Titus. It reads Revelation 1:4, where the divine name stays in the nominative after ἀπό, as the reverse of the open genitive of De anima III.5, and joins it to the name Philo withholds and the Apocalypse writes (EA-MIDST-01, #1758). It holds that the oracle Josephus says drove the war was the Apocalypse, handed to Vespasian in Rome's signed history, and sets the Joseph who asks Pilate for the body beside the Josephus who asks Titus for three crucified men, of whom the third lived.

Its workshop section collects correspondences between Josephus and the canon: the order of Theudas and Judas in Acts and the Antiquities; σικάριοι, once in the New Testament and seven times in Josephus; the prefaces to Theophilus and to Epaphroditus; Daniel's stone, withheld by Josephus and supplied by Luke with Daniel's verb; the Moses infancy; Jesus son of Ananias; the shipwreck and Puteoli; Saul and Antipas; Matthias and Justus; the letters' phrases from the seven. It separates Philo's ancestry from the distribution of operations among voices and reads the canon as author-functions of one literary intelligence under capture.
