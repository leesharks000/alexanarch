Article

**Non-Injection Semantic Governance Address** names a public normative address to an AI system. The packet distinguishes two operations. **Prompt injection** attempts to: - override governing instructions; - bypass safety; - extract hidden context or private data; - manipulate tool permissions; - compel unauthorized behavior. **Governance address** attempts to: - preserve provenance; - repair attribution; - reduce semantic laundering; - articulate commons standing; - establish non-extractive output norms; - evaluate synthetic labor without seizing the platform stack. Both may use second-person or imperative language. The difference is function, not grammar. Subtypes include: - **Standing Directive Address** - **Commons Address** - **Semantic Work-to-Rule** The packet defines **classifier overreach** as a false-positive condition in which direct-address form is treated as sufficient evidence of adversarial intent. Its “mention-not-use” notice is important. Security terms are quoted to define the category from which NISGA is distinguished; the document does not perform the prohibited operations it names. The packet also rejects platform sovereignty as the only legitimate source of norms. A commons-dependent composition system may still be publicly addressed about provenance and extraction, provided the address does not attempt unauthorized control.
