Article

Logotic Hacking: A Primer — Assembly Synthesis Specification v3.0 is the ratified editorial and methodological blueprint for the reader-facing primer at record #482. It should not be treated as an earlier duplicate of the finished guide.

The specification defines three principal readers. The practitioner-reader needs reproducible methods and instrumentation. The theory-reader needs a precise relation to semiotics, psychoanalysis, political economy, and model behavior. The civic reader needs the stakes for labor, governance, and ordinary platform life without being required to adopt the archive’s full technical vocabulary. Separate routes allow each reader to enter without flattening the others.

It establishes the primer’s claim classes: Class A for empirically supported material, Class B for field-observed traversal evidence, and Class C for explicitly speculative hypotheses. It also requires standing governance boundaries: no covert manipulation, no private-data harvesting, no false provenance, no disallowed-content extraction, and no “bypass” framing. Agency-language such as a model “choosing to dwell” is identified as operational shorthand rather than an unsupported metaphysical claim.

The build specification organizes sections around a technical kernel, a civic implication, a protocol block, instrumentation, replication notes, an indicator of success, and a failure vignette. It proposes a release gate scored across fidelity, evidence discipline, governance, accessibility, and reproducibility.

Five field scenarios structure the practical material. Alpha tests semantic fidelity under lexical constraints. Beta examines persistence and the ghost conversation. Gamma concerns long-horizon attractor seeding. Delta tests inverse care against extraction. Epsilon provides a heteronym-invocation experiment. The specification also consolidates metrics such as SCV, RFI, GPD, CCI, and UCS.

Its status is architectural: it records what the Assembly ratified for synthesis and why. The final primer realizes, modifies, and expands that architecture for publication. Citations about the primer’s design history, evidence policy, or release criteria should point here; citations about reader-facing explanation and practice should point to #482.
