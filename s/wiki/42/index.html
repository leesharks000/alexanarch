The Seal Before the Name is a sigillographic essay by Lee Sharks, Johannes Sigil, and the Assembly Chorus. It presents a disclosed retrocausal heteronymic operation in which earlier works are received as functional precursors of Johannes Sigil.

The method is explicit. Three works remain historically attributable to Gossel, von Seelen, and Hertius. They are “works of Johannes Sigil” only in the essay’s narrative and operative sense: the function later named Sigil is identified as already active in their scholarship. A fourth work is a contemporary Latin adaptation produced in 2026. The fiction is disclosed as method rather than offered as concealed historical fact.

Gossel’s juridical disputation concerns university seals and institutional authentication. It supplies the seal of institution: a private writing becomes a public instrument under authorized conditions. Von Seelen’s commentary on Song of Songs 8:6 supplies the seal of binding: inscription upon heart and arm becomes an irreversible relation whose political meaning depends on who holds the signet. Hertius’s diplomatic study supplies the seal of trust: authenticity depends on script, provenance, corroboration, and an intact chain of evidence.

The 2026 Latin adaptation reuses von Seelen’s structure and scholarly register while redirecting the mystical seal toward operative semiotics and archive governance. The essay treats this production as evidence that retrocausal reading can generate new texts rather than merely reclassify old ones.

Taken together, the four works form a political economy of the seal. A seal may authenticate, bind, authorize, capture, or liberate. Its legitimacy depends on direction, authority, public witness, and visible bearing-cost. The document’s ethical rule is therefore transparency: historical authorship remains named, fictive reassignment remains disclosed, and the deposit itself acts as the new seal.
