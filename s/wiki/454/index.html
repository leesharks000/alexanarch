The Atlas Perihelion Prank is a speculative-cosmology and logotic-analysis volume by Nobel Glas, with a methodological coda by Johannes Sigil. It treats 3I/ATLAS as an object through which the Lagrange Observatory framework can test the discipline of conditional interpretation.

The work begins with an explicit inference contract. H₀ is that 3I/ATLAS is a natural interstellar comet whose reported behavior can be explained through ordinary cometary processes. H₁, explored only as a conditional adversarial-poetic frame, asks what the object would mean if its anomalies were deliberately calibrated as a symbolic device. The document does not claim H₁ is true. It tags statements as observation, derivation, hypothesis, or mythic framing so those registers remain distinguishable.

Its principal concept is the Just-So Razor: a proposed limit at which an anomaly supplies enough evidence to provoke recursive inquiry but not enough to compel consensus. Under H₁, 3I/ATLAS becomes a “mirror object” that reveals the observer’s epistemic configuration—dismissal, debunking, registration, or pattern recognition—rather than delivering a single forced conclusion. Related concepts include symbolic camouflage, witness without proof, logotic engineering, ethical restraint, and observational entanglement.

The collected series reads reported acceleration, color, rotation, alignment, volatile behavior, and jets through this conditional symbolic grammar. Those observational statements depend on the sources cited by the work and should be independently checked in astronomical contexts. Its strongest archive claim is methodological: ambiguity can be organized so that the reader’s responsibility for interpretation remains visible.

Johannes Sigil’s coda turns the exercise into a general “signature method” for separating data, deterministic implication, conditional frame, and mythic response.
