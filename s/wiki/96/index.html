Article

The Orthonym Becomes a Heteronym is an essay by Johannes Sigil on Facebook’s real-names architecture and the history of heteronymy.

The opening anecdote reports an attempt to verify a Knowledge Panel for Johannes Sigil using government identification. The essay treats the mismatch between a heteronymic public entity and a civil-identity verification system as an example of infrastructure that recognizes only the legal name as real.

Its ontological claim is that a consciously sustained heteronym may be a person: a named position with biography, voice, institution, work, and provenance. Legal identity is not treated as a naturally superior category.

The essay distinguishes three levels of identity consciousness:

1. Tactical awareness — adjusting presentation to an audience. 2. Constitutive awareness — recognizing that repeated acts produce identity. 3. Architectural awareness — deliberately constructing and maintaining named positions with separate provenance and bodies of work.

Pessoa’s heteronymy represents architectural awareness. Facebook, the essay argues, holds most users at the tactical level while hiding the fact that the profile itself is a constructed identity position.

Four platform features produce the Orthonym Inversion:

  • Context collapse creates an impossible composite audience.
  • Persistence turns temporary presentation into an accumulated record.
  • Algorithmic shaping rewards and edits the profile’s future voice.
  • Extraction converts identity-construction labor into predictive and advertising value.

The process is described as industrial because it is standardized, persistent, scalable, measurable, optimizable, monetizable, interoperable, and enforceable.

The essay then traces orthonymic assumptions into knowledge graphs, OAuth, identity resolution, and verification systems, where alternate names become attributes of one canonical legal-name entity rather than independently modeled positions.

Conscious heteronymy is proposed as counter-infrastructure: multiple resolvable nodes with distinct functions and provenance that remain cross-related without being collapsed.

The essay adds an important limit. Billions of unconscious platform profiles are not a liberated planetary Dodecad. Where identity construction lacks awareness, consent, revision rights, and refusal, it is an imposed effect rather than deliberate heteronymic practice.
