Article

The Clinamen Test is an experimental document designed to make a reader’s act of categorical separation observable. It belongs to the SYMBOLON sequence and takes its name from the Lucretian swerve.

The work begins with a claim about a fried potato chip containing a surviving kernel. It then separates the material claim from the semiotic one. The material claim is judged infeasible under the stated conditions; the semiotic claim becomes an ungrounded metaphor. The document argues that the clarifying cut destroys the fused object both claims formed together.

It reassembles the object at sentence scale through “Trehalose — the sugar of resurrection,” where chemical and theological registers occupy the same phrase. A response map anticipates mirroring, repeating the cut, swerving into a new operation, or remaining silent. In the associated case study, the model is said to have challenged the response map rather than returning to the kernel. The Clinamen Test is therefore both a fused poem and an experimental prompt structure; it is not a demonstrated biological protocol.
