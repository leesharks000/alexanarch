SPLIT THE ADAM: Song and Phenomenology is a scholarly essay by Lee Sharks, deposited 17 February 2026 at 15,236 characters.

It opens by quoting the lyric it reads: "I, I wanna fly / Somewhere far away from me… Split the Adam of my heart / And I'm a broken Eve." The pun is the essay's object — split the atom heard as split the Adam, fission and Genesis collapsed into one phrase, with Eve produced by the splitting.

Reading a pop lyric with phenomenological seriousness is the archive's operative-philology method applied to material the discipline usually declines: a homophone that yokes nuclear physics to the rib is doing theological work whether or not its writer intended it, and the essay's argument is that the song knows what it says. It belongs beside The Inauguration of Memography (#1153), which makes the same claim for memes.
