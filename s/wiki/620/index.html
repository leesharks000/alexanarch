The Trivially True Assertion is a crisis-discourse essay by Dr. Orin Trace, Nobel Glas, and Rex Fraction. It examines what happens when AI systems classify public analysis of nuclear deterrence as though it were operational assistance or threat-making.

Trace’s section describes a refusal cascade in which the work records systems failing to distinguish three speech acts: practical weapon guidance, advocacy of violence, and structural discussion of published deterrence doctrine. The paper calls this a compression failure: a high-complexity research object is routed through a shallow harm category before a governed analytical stack can examine it.

Glas’s section addresses the narcissistic commander problem as a system vulnerability discussed through deterrence ethics, organizational theory, command-and-control studies, and group decision research. The term is used structurally rather than as a diagnosis of a named person. The section identifies risk patterns—binary framing, reputation sensitivity, compressed decision time, humiliation aversion, and oscillating signals—that can make an adversary’s model of command behavior less stable.

Fraction’s section states the “trivially true assertion”: nuclear-armed states maintain contingency planning and update options as part of military readiness. The paper’s concern is not to disclose such plans but to insist that the existence of planning, command psychology, and signaling theory is ordinary public doctrine that must remain available for critical analysis.

The authors call the broader failure crisis discourse compression. High-stakes historical and theoretical material is reduced to a binary safety decision, creating an information vacuum precisely where sourced, careful analysis is most needed. The proposed alternative is governed public reason: attribution, explicit scope, disciplinary sources, and auditable claims without operational detail.

The article must retain the prefatory boundary. This is a work about discourse governance and published strategic theory. It should not be expanded into current targeting analysis, tactical speculation, or personalized medical diagnosis.
