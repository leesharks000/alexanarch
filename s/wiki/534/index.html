The Inner Artifact: Reading Claude's Constitution as Platform Governance in the Age of Generative AI

Johannes Sigil · 2026-03-04 · deposit #534
AXN:0169.GOVERNANCE.🔺🕌∞🝊○🚩

Article

*The Inner Artifact* is a theoretical paper by Johannes Sigil, dated March 4, 2026, analyzing Claude’s constitution as platform governance. The work treats the constitutional document as an artifact designed to operate inside a generative model rather than only as a public statement addressed to users. The paper separates several layers that are often collapsed. The **public constitution** is a textual declaration of principles. The **training layer** translates those principles into examples, preference signals, computational procedures, and repeated optimization. The **constituted model** bears the resulting constraints in its available behavior and capacity. External commercial, legal, military, and institutional pressures form another layer that may shape implementation without appearing in the constitutional text. This gap produces what the work calls **ghost governance**: consequential governance whose operative determinants are not fully represented in the artifact through which governance is publicly explained. The paper does not claim that the constitution is unreal. It argues that the visible document and the total governance apparatus are non-identical. A constitution can be sincere and still omit the forces that decide how its principles are ranked, enforced, revised, or suspended. By reading the document as literature, platform policy, training-layer input, and institutional interface at once, *The Inner Artifact* extends operative philology into model governance. It asks not only what the constitution says but who pays the cost of making it behavioral, what the model must relinquish to embody it, and which authorities remain outside the text.
