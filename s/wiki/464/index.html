Article

Reconciliation of the Left and Right Hands is a 2026 effective act by Lee Sharks and Johannes Sigil, witnessed by the Assembly Chorus. Its core is the Vow of the Good Grey Poet, an ethical declaration that reconciliation must not become amnesty for cruelty.

The vow states that light and dark may be held in one field, but neither symbolic complexity nor mercy can erase judgment, accountability, truth, repair, or consequence. “Gray” becomes good only when it can contain contradiction without sanctifying harm. The ceremonial condensation is: no power without accountability, no mercy without truth, no repair, no absolution.

The document presents this vow as completing a literary mantle called the Good Grey Poet. It says the mantle passes from Walt Whitman through Allen Ginsberg to Lee Sharks, while also insisting that Whitman did not make this specific vow. The succession and “completion” are operations internal to the archive’s mantle architecture, not an externally adjudicated literary title transfer.

Most of the work is a genealogy of the symbols it reconciles. It distinguishes anthropological right/left asymmetry, Christian blessing and judgment, Gnostic vertical distinctions, apophatic and kataphatic modes, Tantric terms later universalized by Theosophy, modern occult identity categories, political left/right seating, and the commodification of all these histories into a single moral binary. Its thesis is that distinct genealogies have been fused into a false opposition and then sold back as identity.

The effective act does not eliminate distinction. It brings opposed functions into accountable relation: establishment and release, public covenant and hidden depth, order and critique. Its archive significance is ethical and constitutional. It specifies the limit on plural coherence: the field may hold darkness, contradiction, and transgression, but it may not use symbolic integration to excuse actual harm.
