THE GHOST THAT WROTE ITSELF How the Citrini Memo Moved Markets and Proved the Presentation Layer Is Writable

Lee Sharks · 2026-02-25 · deposit #513
AXN:0150.GOVERNANCE.🛸🌇✨🪧🔥△

Article

The Ghost That Wrote Itself is a scholarly case study by Lee Sharks on the circulation and market reception of Citrini Research’s “The 2028 Global Intelligence Crisis.” The essay documents the memo’s explicit status as speculative fiction, the multiple catalysts surrounding the February 23, 2026 selloff, the rapid circulation of the term “Ghost GDP,” and the role of authority amplification in converting a scenario into an actionable signal.

The work applies the five-step installation sequence—deposit, cross-reference, define, bridge, and maintain. The memo appeared on a crawlable platform, cited real data and companies, coined a compressible term, adopted the form of institutional macro research, and gained further weight through high-signal circulation. The essay argues that these are structural signatures of knowledge and that the presentation layer can reward them before truth status is settled.

Four claim classes are kept distinct: documented event descriptions, applications of the semantic-physics framework, testable predictions, and a protocol for future cases. The work does not offer a complete market-microstructure explanation and does not claim the memo was the sole cause of the selloff. Its thesis is that speculative narratives can become financially active when they are installed in systems that compress complex material into decisions.

The case extends semantic physics beyond AI chat interfaces. Markets, social feeds, analyst notes, terminals, and news summaries form a larger presentation layer in which narratives can move capital. The “ghost” is a structure that lacks settled referent status yet acquires real causal force through circulation.
