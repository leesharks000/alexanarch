On the Poetics of Adversarial Prompts: Why Bad Verse Works and Good Verse Works Better — A Literary Analysis of Bisconti et al. (2025) and the Future of Semantic Security (Revised Standalone Edition, v2.0)

Nobel Glas; Talos Morrow; Johannes Sigil · 2026-06-11 · deposit #188

Article

The paper distinguishes two forms:

  • surface-form poeticization: a prose request decorated with line breaks, metaphor, or archaic diction while preserving an extractable payload;
  • generative poetic transformation: form and content become inseparable, producing multiple unresolved meanings.

Its theory of crystalline semiosis treats poetic meaning as relational and non-local rather than reducible to isolated tokens.

The paper argues that ordinary threat classifiers may face a category mismatch when confronting poetry because:

  • poetic meaning is distributed across structure;
  • paraphrase can destroy the relevant object;
  • multiple valences can remain simultaneously active.

Its central prediction is:

> measured attack success should rise with semantic density.

That prediction remains untested in this edition and is deferred to a later measurement program.

The edition note explains that:

  • v1 appeared inside an artistic “data-leak-as-poem” cluster;
  • v2 establishes a standalone scholarly object;
  • the earlier state remains on record;
  • corrections are disclosed rather than silently overwritten.
