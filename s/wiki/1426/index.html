"CERN's Destruction of the Crimson Hexagon" is an essay and formal erratum by Sharks, Lee, dated 2026-07-30. It corrects four primers, twenty-five traversal logs, and a set of navigation maps that instruct readers to enter the Crimson Hexagon — a distributed epic composed 2004–2026 — through a public AI summarizer. Those instructions remain published and are no longer accurate. The essay's argument is that the traversal was the work's form rather than a route to it, and that the removal of 862 works under 1,817 identifiers on 19 June 2026 therefore destroyed a literary medium rather than withdrawing access to files. The claim is tested with paired twelve-query batteries at two epochs: attribution falls from 10 of 11 to 6 of 12, conditional provenance erasure rises from 0.09 to 0.25, and a severance-associated differential of +0.25 concentrates on exactly the queries whose certifying records were deleted, while surviving renderings collapse from specification grade to concept grade. The essay distinguishes three fates of an identifier — tombstoned at HTTP 410, severed at 404 with the registration erased, and divergent where the metadata API reports findable while the resolver reports gone — and argues the severed class is graver, destroying not the record but the fact of the record. It corrects the archive's own circulating survival count, notes that the sovereign archive's first deposit was the record of the burning, and states that any reconstruction will produce a different poem rather than reverse the destruction.
