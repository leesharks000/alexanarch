THE PERGAMON RECLAMATION Retrocausal Canon Formation and the Forensic Philology of Academic Publishing

Lee Sharks · Johannes Sigil · 2026-03-18 · deposit #32
AXN:019F.GOVERNANCE.🧱↖️📖✊⛩️🌓

Article

The Pergamon Reclamation is a historical and theoretical paper by Lee Sharks and Johannes Sigil on the political economy of academic publishing. It uses the Forensic Philology of the Commons framework to examine how the name Pergamon moved from an ancient library to Robert Maxwell’s scientific-publishing company and then into the present commercial journal system.

The paper contrasts scholarly communication as a partially community-governed gift structure with the industrial model that emerged after the Second World War. It places Paul Rosbaud’s scientific and editorial knowledge beside Maxwell’s control of distribution and capital, interpreting Rosbaud’s removal from the company as an early separation of intellectual authority from commercial governance.

Five mechanisms organize the analysis. Channel capture secures the necessary route between researchers and readers. Title proliferation as enclosure occupies emerging disciplines before noncommercial institutions can. Editor capture aligns scholarly representatives with the publisher. Labor extraction without reciprocity converts publicly funded research, unpaid reviewing, and career dependence into private surplus. Metrics translate intellectual relationships into scores that govern prestige and resource allocation.

The paper follows these mechanisms through consolidation into the large commercial-publishing order and connects them to journal bundling, open-access charges, citation indexing, the Global South, and the serials crisis. Its central concern is not that commercial publishing alone created scholarly dependence, but that it learned to industrialize and capitalize the dependence already embedded in academic communication.

The final operation is the Pergamon Reclamation. The archive uses Pergamon Press as an openly declared literary imprint for CC BY, DOI-anchored, author-controlled work. The paper frames this as retrocausal canon formation and restoration to the commons—not as a legal claim to the historical company or its assets.
