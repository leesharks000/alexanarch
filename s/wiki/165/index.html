The Pergamon Counter-Archive: Antipas, the White Stone, and the Non-Fungible Name in Revelation 2:12–17

Article

The Pergamon Counter-Archive continues an operative-numismatic sequence.

The prior framework distinguishes two inscriptional regimes:

  • the beast’s public, coercive, calculable token system;
  • the white stone’s private, receiver-completed name.

The Pergamon letter is read as an earlier local articulation of that conflict.

The paper’s three main additions are:

1. A full counter-economy: the passage’s speech, food, token, name, and participation structures form multiple paired inversions rather than simple bookends. 2. The Antipas constellation: the name Antipas is read alongside Antipater, Pergamon’s library history, and the concealment of Aristotelian books at Scepsis to model preservation under institutional pressure. 3. The Revelator as executor: the authorial position is modeled as an epitropos carrying a testament through hostile territory rather than as a modern expressive author.

The transmission dialectic is:

  • concealment may preserve while damaging;
  • institutionalization may transmit while recomposing.

The paper also proposes that Revelation’s seven-sealed scroll has a structural relation to Roman testamentary practice.

The body includes a tendentiousness discipline intended to separate stronger textual and historical claims from more speculative structural correspondences.
