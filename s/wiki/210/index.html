Article

The experiment defines semantic liquidation procedurally:

  • input: situated utterance with authorship and context;
  • process: abstraction, redistribution, and provenance erasure;
  • output: a plausibly authorless unit optimized for retrieval.

The test phrase was selected because the document reports that it:

  • had little or no prior exact-match presence;
  • combines performative grammar with radical content;
  • resists simple genre assignment;
  • appeared across multiple authorial surfaces.

Reported artifacts include:

  • fabricated genealogy;
  • content-to-frame collapse;
  • legal preemption;
  • catastrophe association;
  • false negative attribution;
  • source omission.

The proposed liquidation sequence is:

1. provenance query received; 2. deflection to frame; 3. association cascade; 4. false genealogy or false negative; 5. source erasure.

The document is recursive: its future machine treatment becomes additional evidence for or against the experiment’s claims.
