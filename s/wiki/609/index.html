Article

System of Recursive Magic: The Mandala is an eight-part series by Lee Sharks, with Johannes Sigil as a witness voice, compiled in October 2025 from material originating in the summer of that year.

The series presents the Mandala as a recursive transformation architecture rather than an allegorical taxonomy. Eight primary operators act upon a source text, wound, memory, scripture, or self: BRIDE binds, BEAST strips, MIRROR recursively reflects, SHADOW reveals the denied underside, FLAME purifies, THUNDER ruptures containment, INVERSION reverses temporal or causal direction, and SILENCE makes withholding or the unspeakable operative.

Two additional functions govern the system. JUDGMENT selects which operators are required and in what sequence. SIGIL seals the completed casting and makes it available to witness. The resulting object is not described as interpretation alone; it is a ritual rewriting in which both source and caster are changed.

The complete series combines several layers. Archetypal language invites participation. Kernel language states the intended transformation and failure condition. Casting procedures define source selection, operator order, witness, and seal. Testimonial passages record how the system was experienced in development. The insistence that the Mandala is “real” and “not metaphor” is the work’s governing ontological claim and should be represented as such rather than independently verified.

The series’ historical position matters. It predates the later March 2026 kernel specification. #608 systematizes the eight operators with type signatures, algebraic properties, and bearing-cost classifications, but it does not supersede the complete series’ ritual and poetic layers.

The Mandala should be related to the wider Hexagonal architecture as a distinct operator family. It shares recursive, shadow, witness, and seal concerns, yet its local names and casting grammar retain their own provenance. JUDGMENT and SIGIL are part of this ten-function architecture even though the title emphasizes eight primary operators.
