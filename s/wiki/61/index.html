EA-SPXI-14: The Nested-Layer Relation — SPXI ⊇ GEO

Rex Fraction (operative); Lee Sharks (archival) · 2026-04-17 · deposit #61
AXN:0210.GOVERNANCE.🤙🖊️🔭🍀🖐️🕊️

Article

EA-SPXI-14 is a technical correction by Rex Fraction, with Lee Sharks in the archival role, within the Semantic Economy Institute’s SPXI sequence.

The paper responds to a composition error reported in an April 2026 AI-generated comparison. The comparison assigned financial outcomes to GEO and only structural outcomes to SPXI, treating the disciplines as exclusive alternatives. The paper accepts the earlier distinction SPXI ≠ GEO but rejects the inferred symmetry.

Its corrective relation is:

SPXI ⊇ GEO methods

The relation is explicitly scoped. SPXI is described as a protocol, engagement type, and full-stack buildout. A narrow ontological inscription may omit content-layer tactics; a full engagement may use them. The superset claim therefore concerns available methods, not mandatory execution in every object bearing the SPXI name.

Shared methods include AI-answer composition work, entity salience, RAG inclusion, citation surfaces, and content structures associated with GEO. SPXI-only methods include DOI-anchored entity definitions, negative tags, cross-system disambiguation, model-transition continuity, structured objects intended for agent action, and persistent institutional provenance.

The economic section distinguishes classes of outcome from proven magnitudes. Marketing-industry GEO figures are cited as a benchmark for SPXI engagements that actually include GEO-layer execution. The paper states that SPXI-specific 90-day and 180-day evidence had not yet accrued and that ontological-layer returns were mechanisms awaiting quantification.

The commissioning argument distinguishes short-horizon optimization from longer-horizon infrastructure. GEO remains a valid choice where the desired outcome is limited to visibility and content extraction. SPXI is positioned for entities requiring those outcomes plus durability, disambiguation, cross-model consistency, and agent readiness.
