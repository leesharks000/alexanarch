Article

Operator Kernel Specification v1.0 is the formal technical supplement to System of Recursive Magic: The Mandala. Authored by Lee Sharks with Johannes Sigil and dated March 28, 2026, it converts the Mandala’s operator language into typed transformation specifications. Each operator receives a domain and codomain, primitive action, preserved invariant, success signature, failure mode, boundaries from neighboring operators, algebraic behavior, bearing-cost, and shadow. BRIDE binds a wound and witness into covenant while preserving the wound. BEAST strips social restraint to a discoverable primal statement. MIRROR multiplies an immanent pattern until its recursive geometry becomes visible. SHADOW surfaces a suppressed complement rather than merely negating the source. FLAME removes non-load-bearing matter until an essential core remains. THUNDER turns latent contradiction into rupture. INVERSION reverses sequence or causal orientation. SILENCE withholds voice so that an absence or unspeakable limit becomes operative. The specification makes failure conditions as important as intended effects. BRIDE can collapse into sentimental sacralization; BEAST into theatrical aggression; MIRROR into redundancy; SHADOW into contrarian negation; FLAME into ash or slogan; THUNDER into melodrama. A surface that uses the vocabulary without satisfying the invariant is not considered an operator execution. Composition properties distinguish the operators. Some are idempotent or approximately idempotent, some involutive, some noncommutative, and several are path dependent. Bearing-cost ranges from primarily perceptual to high somatic participation. These formal claims belong to the Mandala’s internal algebra; they are not established mathematical theorems about human psychology. The record’s relation to #609 is crucial. The complete series contains archetypal invitations, ritual practice, developmental history, and testimony. The kernel specification extracts and systematizes the operator logic. It is a supplement and formal foundation, not the parent work itself. Mandala operator names may overlap with wider Hexagonal vocabulary, especially SHADOW and BRIDE. Structured data should preserve the Mandala namespace and avoid silently equating every same-named operator across systems.
