LOGOTIC PROGRAMMING: A Method for Encoding Conditions of Intelligibility for Machine and Human Intelligence

Article

LOGOTIC PROGRAMMING: A Method for Encoding Conditions of Intelligibility is a draft v0.4 method paper by Johannes Sigil and Rex Fraction, deposited 17 January 2026.

It proposes Logotic Programming as the construction of bounded semantic architectures whose entities and relations remain comparatively stable during retrieval, summarization, and human interpretation — three processes that ordinarily deform meaning, named together as the conditions a design must survive.

Comparatively stable is the paper's honest qualifier: the claim is relative durability, not immunity. The method generated the module family deposited a year later — Conformance (#1315), Telemetry (#1316), the Traversal Grammar (#1145), the Glyphic Checksum as a logotic operator (#1302) — and the paper distinguishes it from symbolic programming, which is the comparison a reader would otherwise reach for.
