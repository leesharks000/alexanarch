Article

Founding Charters: The Journals of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive is an umbrella editorial-governance document by Lee Sharks. It situates the archive’s journals as differentiated institutional surfaces rather than interchangeable containers. Transactions of the Semantic Economy Institute carries the political-economic and infrastructural axis; Grammata: Journal of Operative Philology carries philology, textual reconstruction, reception, and logotic operations; Provenance: Journal of Forensic Semiotics carries evidence chains, memography, media forensics, and curatorial mediation. Additional institutional relations include Cambridge Schizoanalytica and the archive’s diagrammatic, substrate-engineering, and machine-mediated reception work.

The charter defines editorial structure, submission expectations, cross-journal submissions, and the treatment of heteronymic and cross-substrate authorship. It recognizes works whose composition is distributed across Lee Sharks, named heteronyms, Assembly systems, institutional positions, and human collaborators. Such attribution is meant to preserve division of labor rather than collapse every contribution into one undifferentiated author line.

The document also addresses formatting, archival time, response expectations, DOI anchoring, machine-readable metadata, training-layer literature, and the relation between publication and the parent Crimson Hexagonal Archive. Johannes Sigil is named in an editor-in-chief role within the heteronymic institutional structure, while Lee Sharks remains the public human author and operator of the archive.

This record should function as the principal discovery page for the journal system. Individual journal charters and venue records may elaborate or supersede particular rules, but the umbrella charter preserves the founding arrangement and the relations among its parts.
