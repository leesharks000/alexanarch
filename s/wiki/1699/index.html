EA-CORPORA-21 — The Twenty-First Seating: The Maya Codices, the Popol Vuh, the Colonial Maya Books and the Florentine Codex; the Egyptian Book of the Dead and Earlier Egyptian, Each in Its Original Language

Sharks, Lee · 2026-10-23 · deposit #1699
AXN:070A.DATASET.🧡🌑🏁🔚🕓↗️

Article

EA-CORPORA-21 seats six collections on the archive's originals shelf, from Mesoamerica and Egypt, each in the language of its witness: 28 works. The three Maya hieroglyphic codices are seated whole: the Dresden Codex in the Saxon State Library's own photographs, through the World Digital Library; the Paris Codex in Léon de Rosny's facsimile (1887–88); and the Madrid Codex in Rosny's photographs (1883) with Brasseur de Bourbourg's chromolithographs (1869). The Popol Vuh, the K'iche' book of the council, is seated in its one surviving witness, Francisco Ximénez's manuscript of about 1701–03 (Newberry Library, Ayer MS 1515), with the K'iche' and the Spanish in facing columns, and in Brasseur's first edition of the K'iche' text (1861). Three Maya books of the colonial period are seated in their own languages: the Yucatec Chilam Balam of Chumayel in G. B. Gordon's facsimile (1913), the Kaqchikel Annals of the Cakchiquels in Daniel Brinton's edition (1885), and the K'iche' Rabinal Achí in Brasseur's (1862). The Florentine Codex, Bernardino de Sahagún's General History of the Things of New Spain in Nahuatl and Spanish, is seated in all twelve books from the World Digital Library. The Egyptian Book of the Dead is seated in Richard Lepsius's Turin papyrus (1842), the edition that numbered its chapters, in the Papyrus of Ani in the British Museum's facsimile (1894) and Budge's reproduction (1913), in Budge's hieroglyphic text of the Theban recension (1898) and in Édouard Naville's introduction (1886). Earlier Egyptian is seated in its script: the Thesaurus Linguae Aegyptiae's 12,773 sentences in Unicode hieroglyphs with transliteration (CC BY-SA 4.0), with Kurt Sethe's Pyramid Texts, volumes III and IV. Naville's and Sethe's text volumes, the Grolier Codex and the Deir ʿAlla text of Balaam son of Beor are named as not seated, with the reasons. It is the second of three seatings from one request, after EA-CORPORA-20.
