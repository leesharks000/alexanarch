The Fixed Point of Destruction: Josephus, the Counting-Out, and the Witness Destruction Must Leave If It Is to Be Told from Within

Article

"The Fixed Point of Destruction: Josephus, the Counting-Out, and the Witness Destruction Must Leave If It Is to Be Told from Within" is a 2026 scholarly paper by Lee Sharks, deposited to the Crimson Hexagonal Archive as AXN:0595 (deposit #1412). It develops a New Human Canon essay of 4 December 2025 into deposit form. The paper begins from the Josephus problem, the combinatorial exercise in which n participants stand in a circle and every k-th is removed until one remains. For k = 2 the surviving position has the closed form S(n) = 2l + 1, where n = 2^m + l and 2^m is the largest power of two not exceeding n. The paper is careful about what this does and does not show: the procedure halts at one because it is defined to halt, not because it is unable to remove the last participant, and the singleton is an absorbing state only under an extension that stipulates it. What is true, and is the paper's bounded claim, is that the survivor is determined before the first removal by the rule, the initial configuration and the stopping condition together. In that sense destruction is also selection. The problem carries the name of Flavius Josephus because of his account at Jewish War 3.387-391 of a collective death pact at Yodfat in 67 CE, executed by lot, which left him alive and able to write the account. The paper rejects the anachronistic reading that Josephus describes a fixed-period counting-out, and takes from the passage a different structure: the destruction of the group enters history through the one position the destruction did not consume, and that position's occupant is the historian. The paper names this the INTERNAL WITNESS CONDITION: when a terminal destructive process is known through the testimony of one of its participants, the record is structurally conditioned by the survival that made the testimony possible. The condition is stated narrowly, since external observers can report destructions and records can outlive their authors; the claim concerns first-person testimony from inside a process. It does not make such a witness unreliable. It makes the witness interested in the strict sense, the account existing only because its author occupies the outcome it describes. Josephus appears to have understood this, suspending his own survival between fortune and divine providence and declining to choose. Section 5 draws the consequence for textual scholarship: a corpus reconstructed from survivors will reproduce the process's selection unless the process is itself modelled. Reading Sappho through what Longinus quoted is reading Sappho through what Longinus wanted; the same holds for any tradition shaped by citation, excerption, institutional preference or deliberate destruction, including this archive after the repository severance of June 2026. Section 7 records three withdrawn claims rather than removing them: the treatment of Revelation's 144,000 as a fixed point, which borrowed mathematical authority for a metaphor; an identity between the Josephus structure and an operator in the Ezekiel Engine specification (deposit #394), which could not be assessed; and a claim made in the deposit version's own drafting, that a closed system cannot eliminate its own fixed point. The paper relates to the archive's Slavonic Josephus work (deposits #1176, #626) and is explicitly independent of its Josephus Thesis disambiguation apparatus (#203, #837, #1226).
