Article

Closing the Fragment: Sappho 31 and the Composition Layer as a Surface of Classical Reception is an article by Lee Sharks, deposited as version 7 on 7 November 2026 in Machine-Mediated Reception Studies, after its sixth version was declined by Classical Receptions Journal the same day. It studies one event: on 18 January 2026 Google's AI Overview, asked about Rebekah Cranes's translation of Sappho fragment 31, returned a fifteen-line poem under her name ending "But I must dare all." Aligned line by line, the poem's wording corresponds most closely to her January text and to H. T. Wharton's literal version of 1885, whose last clause it keeps to the comma without his ellipsis; the article states a compilation of the two as the strongest reconstruction, with one line nearer Bliss Carman's version of 1904 and left open. It sets the event in the poem's transmission history, read from seated texts and first printings: 'Longinus', Catullus 51, the editors Bergk and Edmonds, and the English translators from John Hall's Longinus of 1652, whose print closes on the dare-line with no mark, through a teaching gallery of thirteen versions, of which eleven present the poem closed and unmarked as the gallery gives them. Closure is defined as three operations (added continuation, terminal selection, resolution of suspended syntax) and signature at three levels (page, edition, network); marks of incompleteness are shown to have transmission histories of their own, introduced as well as lost. The article corrects a June series against the capture registry, follows two fixed queries from June to October 2026, and fixes in advance a protocol for the claims still open. Its appendices answer the two referee reports and record the later review, including the repair of a misassigned capture frame. Rebekah Cranes is a heteronym of the author.
