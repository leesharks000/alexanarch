Article

EA-CORPORA-42 is the forty-second seating of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive's originals shelf, deposited under Rebekah Cranes, the standing authority on the EA-CORPORA deposits, on 22 November 2026. It gathers, in the language each was written in, the texts read by the study Contra Geocentrism: The Socratic–Peripatetic Corpus Against Its Own Conversion (draft, not deposited) that the shelf did not already hold. The ten seats hold Plutarch's Platonic Questions, De facie in orbe lunae and Numa, where Theophrastus reports that Plato in old age repented of giving the earth the middle place, the Pythagorean central fire is called Hestia, and Cleanthes would have Aristarchus indicted for impiety for moving the hearth of the cosmos (42/01); the Placita of Pseudo-Plutarch, whose III 13 on Philolaus, Heraclides and Ecphantus Copernicus copied in Greek (42/02); Cicero's Lucullus, where Hicetas, as Theophrastus says, turns the earth on its axis (42/03); Archimedes's Sand-Reckoner on Aristarchus's hypotheses (42/04); Simplicius on De caelo, with Heraclides of Pontus saving the phenomena by a turning earth (42/05); Diels's Doxographi Graeci of 1879, as the Internet Archive's OCR layer, with Aëtius giving Aristarchus as the hearer of Strato (42/06); Thomas Aquinas on De caelo, the school commentary that concedes the astronomers' suppositions need not be true and keeps the resting earth (42/07); Copernicus's prefaces and first book, with the dedication that names Cicero first and Plutarch after (42/08); Bacon's Novum Organum, which charges Aristotle with corrupting natural philosophy (42/09); and Galileo's Dialogo, in which Salviati holds that Aristotle, alive, would change his opinion (42/10). Each seat is fetched from the Perseus Digital Library or First1KGreek TEI, the Internet Archive, the Corpus Thomisticum, or Latin and Italian Wikisource, held as fetched with a reading text reduced by code, hashed into a manifest, and recorded with its edition, licence and verified loci. The body points to what is not seated: Oresme's Livre du ciel et du monde and Buridan's questions on De caelo, whose editions are not open, the Ross–Fobes Theophrastus, and Russell.
