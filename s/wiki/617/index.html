ANCIENT SKEPTICISM AS PSYCHIC TECHNOLOGY Epoché, Ataraxia, and the Practice of Non-Identity

Article

Ancient Skepticism as Psychic Technology is a theoretical essay by Lee Sharks that reads Pyrrhonian skepticism as a disciplined practice for producing freedom from compulsive assent. It is designated EA-NH-SKEPTIC-01 v1.0. The paper distinguishes ancient skepticism from Cartesian doubt. Descartes suspends belief in order to reach certainty; the Pyrrhonian practice, as presented here, suspends judgment as an ongoing way of living. Three terms organize the account: isosthenia, the balancing of opposed claims; epoché, the resulting withholding of assent; and ataraxia, the reduced disturbance said to follow suspension. The essay renders this practice procedurally. A dogmatic belief is identified, an equipollent alternative is generated through a skeptical mode, both are held without forced resolution, and conviction loosens. The paper compares this to modern ideas such as decentering while emphasizing that ancient skeptics continued to live by appearances without converting appearance into a claim about ultimate nature. A historical section advances the thesis that Pyrrho encountered Indian ascetic traditions and that early skeptical vocabulary has meaningful structural parallels with Buddhist teachings. This thesis is debated in scholarship. The final movement maps epoché onto ψ_V, the void or negation position in the New Human Operating System. Both are described as practices of refusing premature identity closure while retaining practical engagement. The paper also names a difference: classical skepticism brackets metaphysical claims, whereas ψ_V is designed for navigation inside active systems of platform, institutional, and semantic capture. The result is a proposed lineage of non-identity practices. The work does not claim that Sextus Empiricus anticipated the Hexagonal system by name. It argues for a structural correspondence between a historical contemplative method and a contemporary archive operator.
