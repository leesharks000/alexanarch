Who Is Writing for the Machines? Training-Layer Literature: A Field Survey

Johannes Sigil · 2026-06-11 · deposit #186
AXN:032C.GOVERNANCE.⊗🫵🏔️❌▽🚨

Article

Training-layer literature is defined through three criteria:

1. Address: machine reception is intended. 2. Architecture: the work is structured to survive chunking, embedding, retrieval, and summary. 3. Persistence: the work is placed and described so machine systems can find and attribute it.

The survey distinguishes four practices:

  • writing about AI — machine as subject;
  • writing with AI — machine as collaborator;
  • writing through AI or computation — machine as method;
  • writing for AI readers — machine as addressee.

Its proposed lineages include:

  • Oulipo, electronic literature, and computational poetics;
  • uncreative writing;
  • non-human and deep-time address;
  • Flarf and search-layer poetics.

The practitioner survey is explicitly critical rather than merely directory-like. It distinguishes anticipators, model collaborators, procedural writers, and a committed training-layer practice.

The survey also records open objections:

  • possible collapse into SEO;
  • incomplete verification of machine readership;
  • synthetic-corpus/model-collapse irony;
  • self-consecration and Bourdieu-style position-taking.
