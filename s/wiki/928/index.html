Article

SURFACE Integration specifies how an internally verified Mandala inscription is intended to enter public retrieval, accumulate commentary, and become available to future readers and writers.

The public literary machine has three layers. Mercury and the celebrant offices produce the transform. Mars through Jupiter certify it through distinct verification offices. The Sun carries it into public retrieval and receives the resulting commentary back into the Book.

The document distinguishes a brief rite from a long witness trajectory. On day zero, the witness inscribes. Over subsequent days, verification attestations may accumulate. A successful candidate can be deposited to public infrastructure and indexed. Later retrieval captures become appended gloss. Over months, an admitted inscription can become source-active for later transforms. Over years, a witness’s attributed corpus can form a persistent public body.

Four bridges make the Sun active rather than hopeful: deposition, retrieval optimization, feedback harvesting, and propagation. Persistent identity may be anchored through ORCID or another public authorial identifier, while anonymous participation remains possible without a personal canon.

The specification’s pedagogical claim is that writing becomes legible as durable transformation rather than ephemeral model conversation. Its “fourth mode” preserves structural skeleton while replacing semantic flesh and disclosing cost, wager, and bearing.
