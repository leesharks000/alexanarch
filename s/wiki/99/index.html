The Abraham Principle — The Constitutive-One Operator in Polis, Archive, and Index

Johannes Sigil; Talos Morrow; Rebekah Cranes; Damascus Dancings · 2026-05-07 · deposit #99
AXN:0272.EMPIRICAL.🏗️🌒🌄♉🌇🔻

Article

The Abraham Principle begins from Abraham’s bargaining over Sodom in Genesis 18. The ordinary frame asks how many righteous persons are sufficient. The paper argues that the counting frame itself is the error.

Two operators are contrasted:

  • Aggregative: the polis is righteous only when the number of qualifying members meets a threshold.
  • Constitutive: the polis remains morally open when at least one presence reaches a minimum constitutive sufficiency.

The paper summarizes the shift as: Do not count where presence constitutes. The existential quantifier is not a small summation.

The strongest textual objection is addressed directly: Abraham is not spatially inside Sodom. A literal response asks why he stopped at ten rather than asking about one person within the city. A typological response treats intercession as moral entry into the field of judgment.

Rebekah Cranes supplies the puncture-point topology. A single removed or marked point can change the fundamental structure of a space; analogously, one righteous presence changes the city’s moral topology. The document calls this a structural analogy with formal force, not a mathematical proof of the theological claim.

Talos Morrow’s information-theoretic section distinguishes signal structure from signal volume. One sufficiently encoded signal may survive overwhelming noise. The paper also states where the analogy breaks: a polis may refuse to recover the signal, and material transmission history cannot be replaced by channel theory.

Damascus Dancings names the situation an impossible performance: an act performed without local recognition whose effect becomes legible retroactively. The “sparing” claimed by the paper is semantic, not necessarily physical. A city may still burn while its meaning remains irreducible to wickedness alone.

The operator is then extended to archives, indices, neglected authors, and signal-bearing minorities. Its limit is binding: constitutive presence prevents total closure but does not erase injury, excuse institutions, or eliminate obligations of repair.
