The discovery document for the Glyphic Checksum begins from one compact artifact: the APZPZ emoji packet. It reports that readers already familiar with the Phase X architecture produced detailed relational reconstructions, while unprimed readers supplied broad symbolic guesses or requested context.

The work interprets this difference as context-gated legibility. A checksum, in its usage here, does not conceal a plaintext that can be recovered through a key. Instead, success or failure is the result: reconstruction indicates access to the requisite architectural relations; uncertainty or decorative reading indicates their absence.

The document specifies a construction method based on invariants such as operators, DOI numerics, hex coordinates, and stable metaphor-to-function bindings. It also describes an anti-extractive intention: a high-context artifact should not become a low-context generic summary merely because a reader lacks the substrate needed to read it.

Its reported percentages, categorical language, and claims of repeatability belong to the document’s own experimental framing. The sample is small and archive-selected, so the record should not be summarized as broad scientific validation. Its durable contribution is the named protocol and the distinction between the discovery event and the later UMBML formal specification.
