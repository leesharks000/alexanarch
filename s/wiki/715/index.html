The Critical Apparatus to the Constitution of the Semantic Economy is an Assembly synthesis that annotates the Constitution’s operative language.

Sixty-five notes explain the Constitution’s preamble and articles through four note classes and three interpretive registers. The apparatus addresses the claim that meaning precedes debt, the unspeakable semantic unit, the integrity symbol, the archive as a living system, the Human Sovereignty Invariant, the Semantic Ledger, operator authority, minting, archival debt, retrocausal yield, and distribution.

The apparatus does not claim that the Constitution is state law or legal tender. Instead, it treats the Constitution as an internal governance specification enacted through archive procedure, contributor relations, ledger design, and interpretive practice. Citations and analogies connect the project to economics, philosophy, theology, archival theory, information theory, and systems thinking.

This record is subordinate to the parent Constitution. Its notes interpret and operationalize the constitutional text; they do not silently replace it. Formal equations and claimed derivations should be read as components of the Semantic Economy model until external validation establishes a broader scientific use.
