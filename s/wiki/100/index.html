Article

Autonomous Warfare Does Not End at the Body extends autonomous-warfare analysis from kinetic and cognitive systems to meaning infrastructure.

Three cumulative layers are distinguished:

  • Kinetic autonomy: systems identify and engage physical targets.
  • Cognitive autonomy: systems influence perception, trust, or decision-making.
  • Semantic autonomy: systems continuously govern filtering, indexing, retrieval, summarization, citation, provenance, and entity resolution.

Autonomous Semantic Warfare is not identical with semantic autonomy. It is the warfare-relevant subset that produces strategic effects on collective memory, access, provenance, contestability, or agency.

The packet distinguishes:

  • Intentional ASW: a state, platform, or non-state actor deliberately weaponizes semantic infrastructure.
  • Structural ASW: strategically consequential suppression or erasure emerges from large-scale autonomous infrastructure without a conventional commander or declared war intent.

The threshold is intentionally restrictive. A bad individual summary is not ASW. Systematic and population-level restructuring is required.

The same mechanisms may have different governance status. Under weaponized or unaccountable deployment they may constitute ASW; under public oversight, transparent criteria, and meaningful affected-party participation they may constitute democratic semantic infrastructure.

The packet compares information, cognitive, and semantic warfare:

  • information warfare manipulates content;
  • cognitive warfare degrades judgment;
  • ASW operates on the infrastructure through which content becomes available to cognition.

Autonomy is also broken into dimensions. The packet does not claim ASW is maximally autonomous in every sense. It emphasizes scale, speed, limited public oversight, low detectability, and lack of affected-party consent.

Its confidence labels separate documented military and infrastructure sources from the packet’s primary inference, secondary causal hypothesis, and engineered bridge-term target.
