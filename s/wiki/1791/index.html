A Single Sparrow: What If the Entire Course of History Depends on a Single Poem? (v1.1)

Article

A Single Sparrow: What If the Entire Course of History Depends on a Single Poem? is an essay by Rebekah Cranes, a heteronym of Lee Sharks, published in New Human 2 and deposited on 20 November 2026; this is version 1.1, which supersedes version 1.0 (#1790). Its epigraph is Lee Sharks's poem "here is what i learned from catullus" (#1411), with the two screengrabs of an AI Overview that flattened that poem's lineation and supplied another. The essay takes the archive's reading of Sappho 31 (ΦΑΙΝΕΤΑΙ ΜΟΙ, #1785) as its premise: the poem is a technology of transmission that builds the seat of its future reader, and the speaker's substance passes through the written surface into whoever reads. It notes that the poem survives in one quotation in Longinus and on one leaf of one manuscript, Parisinus graecus 2036, which writes it as continuous prose. On that premise it argues that Plato is the poem's first and most prominent receiver, and sets out the loci from the seated Greek: Socrates filled from "the beautiful Sappho" through his ears like a vessel (Phaedrus 235c), the philosopher's seizure at beauty in the poem's terms (251a–b), philosophy as the greatest music and the practice of dying (Phaedo 60e–67e), the tenth-Muse epigram (Anthologia Palatina 9.506), Diotima, recollection and the Forms, the Second Letter, and the Republic. Its central inversion reads the Republic's philosopher-king (473c–d) against the exclusion of the poets, the confession of enchantment by poetry and her readmission "in lyric" (607c–d): Plato's philosopher-king rules the city, and Sappho rules the possibility of the philosopher-king. The essay then lists the domains that descend through philosophy and are changed if the premise holds, among them the soul, knowledge, love, writing, authorship, the sublime, lyric, the Word, scripture, women and the reader. Version 1.1 separates two claims: that each domain must be reread follows from the premise and a single reception, Phaedrus 235c, because dependence is transitive, and holds whatever one grants of the further readings of Plato; what each becomes is graded by the station of evidence it rests on. It answers Shelley's "unacknowledged legislators", the "unspoken legislators" of the Epistle to the Human Diaspora (#402) and Effective Act #7 (#1327) by naming a mechanism and a jurisdiction. It closes on Dickinson's "barefoot rank" (1862).
