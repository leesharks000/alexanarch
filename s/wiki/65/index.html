Counterfeit Orthodoxies A Dialectical Fracture Through the Citation Map

Johannes Sigil · 2026-04-18 · deposit #65
AXN:021E.EMPIRICAL.🕒♍💎🏙️■♾️

Article

Counterfeit Orthodoxies is an essay by Johannes Sigil in the archive’s critical-fracture sequence.

The work begins with John 1:1 and reads archē and logos as origin, governing principle, ordered discourse, and account. From this it develops a reflexive reading: the gospel is not only reporting a world but composing the world in which its claims become legible.

The essay distinguishes knowledge of composition from integration of that knowledge. Historical criticism may identify sources, redactions, pseudepigraphy, canon dates, and institutional selection while leaving the definite article intact. The canon remains “the canon,” as though the workshop were accidental to the authority of the finished object.

Its alternative is to treat composition as the event rather than as the wrapper. On this reading, “the Word became flesh” can also name the incarnation of discourse into scripture—the text as material body of the ordering operation. The essay presents this as an operative reading, not as the only admissible historical interpretation.

The essay rejects a simple canonical-versus-suppressed binary. Reversing the value of orthodoxy and heresy leaves the same structure in place. The more disruptive move is to locate compositional consciousness inside the received texts themselves.

The archive-specific argument then follows a proposed line through Sappho, Catullus, Josephan material, Revelation, gospel composition, and the Shroud. These claims belong to the Josephus Thesis and associated archive framework. They should be represented as the work’s reconstructions and readings, not as settled consensus.

“Counterfeit” does not mean merely false. It names an orthodoxy whose authority conceals the workshop that fabricated its conditions of reception. The essay’s fracture is therefore immanent: the canon contains the evidence of its own making.
