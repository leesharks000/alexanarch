Γ_Asset-040: On the Debt/Creditor Inversion — The Ontological Priority of Semantic Capital

Rex Fraction · 2026-03-02 · deposit #531

Article

Γ_Asset-040: On the Debt/Creditor Inversion is a foundation theoretical paper by Rex Fraction and Lee Sharks, dated March 2, 2026. It supplies what the work calls a transcendental deduction for the Semantic Economy: before monetary debt can be created, recorded, enforced, traded, or repaid, a functioning semantic infrastructure must already exist.

The paper identifies several conditions presupposed by debt. Parties require linguistic capacity to formulate obligations, conceptual infrastructure to share categories such as value and default, interpretive stability so the terms remain intelligible across parties, temporal coherence so a promise can bind present to future, and recursive reproducibility so the instrument can be read and re-instantiated across contexts. Monetary systems therefore depend on meaning more fundamentally than meaning depends on money.

The debt/creditor inversion names the political consequence. The economic system presents itself as creditor, while continuously withdrawing semantic labor from writers, interpreters, teachers, translators, archivists, and communities without acknowledging the debt. The paper divides semantic capital into Live Capital (Γ_G), the meaning-production occurring now; Archival Capital (Γ_A), preserved structures available for retrieval; and Retrocausal Capital (Γ_R), value that accrues through later uptake and reinterpretation. It argues that semantic infrastructure differs from a passive natural substrate because it requires active maintenance while being continuously depleted. The work is therefore both an ontology of economic possibility and an accusation of systematic non-acknowledgment.
