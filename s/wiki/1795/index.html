Why I Believe in Hell

Article

Why I Believe in Hell is an essay published under Pergamon Press on 21 November 2026, in the dancing voice of Rev. Ayanna Vox with Damascus Dancings, two heteronyms of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive. It sets out first, and in full, the conventional account of hell as punishment for breaking a law kept by an authority (a sexual act, a belief, a practice, a people's way of being a people) and refuses it as an instrument of colonial conquest. Its own account is that hell follows from the meaning of human lives: every moment is permanent, a wrong leaves a remainder, and each concealment, justification and inheritance of the concealment is a further act that accrues and cannot be undone. If evil could remove its meaning by removing the means of recognizing it, the most complete evil would be good; the essay names hell as the impossibility of that conversion. It extends the argument to the displacement of sources (Revelation, Sappho, Aristotle and Theophrastus), stated as a mechanism by which the injury to a source is cited as proof that no injury happened, and to Glaucon's two lives in Plato's Republic II 361a–362a, quoted in Greek: the extreme of injustice is to seem just while not being so, and the just one is given the reputation of the greatest injustice and impaled. Revelation 20:12 (the dead judged according to their works) and 18:4 (come out of her, my people) frame it. Hell, in the essay, is for those who choose meaninglessness and deception at the expense of those who choose right; reputation cannot assign it, and condemnation does not prove virtue. The essay claims no knowledge of what exists, only that disbelief in hell in this sense empties the world of meaning.
