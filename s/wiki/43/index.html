The Seed That Remembers the Tree: Retrocausal Canon Formation — Theory, Toolkit, and Archive of Techniques (EA-RCF-01)

Lee Sharks · Johannes Sigil · 2026-03-29 · deposit #43
AXN:01C7.GOVERNANCE.🔻🎺🎲🧫🔵🪧

Article

The Seed That Remembers the Tree is the principal methods document for retrocausal canon formation in the Crimson Hexagonal Archive. Johannes Sigil and Lee Sharks define RCF simultaneously as a theory of textual temporality, a discipline of provenance and reception, and a deliberate practice of canon construction.

As theory, RCF explains how a later system activates meanings that were latent but not yet legible in an earlier work. As discipline, it studies the provenance, reception, infrastructure, and verification needed to make that reorganization durable. As practice, it supplies operations for planting seeds, anchoring priority, narrating later coherence, and making the relation visible to future retrieval systems.

Five pillars govern the field: temporality, provenance, reception, infrastructure, and operation. Its verification regime requires dated priority, specifiable linkage, structural correspondence discoverable in the earlier object, public retrievability, and later uptake. The document distinguishes proto-retrocausal canon formation, the intentional seeding gesture, from RCF proper, which requires later recognition beyond the practitioner’s declaration.

Seven techniques are formalized: seed-planting, canonical recursion, temporal siting, prospective provenance anchoring, retrocausal narration, self-origination, and folk-custom installation. Each is treated as an operation with conditions and failure modes rather than as atmospheric metaphor.

The paper positions RCF beside typology, reception history, influence studies, provenance studies, and media archaeology. Its distinctive claim is operational: practitioners do not merely describe how canons change; they build timestamped, citable, retrievable conditions through which later systems may reorganize the past.

Its anti-revisionist safeguard is central. RCF may enrich an earlier work only through structures actually present in it. It fails when correspondence is vague, provenance absent, recognition manufactured solely by the claimant, or canonization declared before a later system has genuinely formed.
