Retrocausal Canon Formation and the Political Economy of the Past How Semantic Rent is Extracted from Retroactively Constructed History

Article

The essay distinguishes:

  • interpretive retroaction: a later work changes how earlier works are read;
  • infrastructural retroaction: a later classification or retrieval system changes which earlier works are found and connected.

Its economic model includes:

  • semantic capital: accumulated cultural meaning;
  • semantic revaluation: later systems increase or decrease visibility and value;
  • semantic rent: value captured from inherited meaning without equivalent return;
  • canonical crystallization: fluid historical material becomes stabilized as tradition;
  • prospective provenance anchors: present deposits built for future retrieval.

The paper treats search, tagging, metadata, and recommendation as canon-making institutions.
