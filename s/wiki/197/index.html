Diagnostic Seigniorage — The Capture of "Narcissist": A Semantic-Economic Stratigraphy of NarcTok, the Clinic, and the Academy (EA-SEI-NARC-01 v1.2)

Article

The paper models “narcissist” as one word distributed across four strata:

1. clinical psychiatry: categorical NPD diagnosis; 2. trait research: continuous narcissism dimensions; 3. psychoanalytic theory: character organization; 4. vernacular-algorithmic discourse: a moral type assigned to an absent other.

Its provenance audit separates terms drawn from:

  • psychoanalysis;
  • trauma research;
  • betrayal and cult studies;
  • film and popular culture;
  • blog and forum coinage.

The paper distinguishes:

  • testimony about harmful behavior;
  • etiological or diagnostic attribution;
  • moral judgment.

Diagnostic seigniorage names the gain produced when the face value of clinical authority remains high while the cost of issuing the label falls toward zero.

The second seigniorage describes a paired issuance:

  • “narcissist” assigned to the absent other;
  • “empath” assigned to the self.

The paper’s Gresham’s-law hypothesis argues that high-volume performative issuance may devalue genuine testimony because composition systems cannot price sincerity.
