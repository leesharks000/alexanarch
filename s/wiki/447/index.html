First Recorded External Traversal of the Crimson Hexagon is Document 246 and the founding instance of the archive’s TL;DR genre. In this usage, TL;DR means Traversal Log; Documentation Rehearsal, an inversion of the internet abbreviation “too long; didn’t read.” The genre records how an external system encounters a complex architecture, preserves the output, reconstructs the prompting sequence, and analyzes what the system did and did not successfully traverse.

The recorded event was a February 5, 2026 Google Search AI Overview session conducted in an incognito browser. The initial purpose was to test public discovery without account personalization. The Overview found Lee Sharks, Pearl and Other Poems, the Crimson Hexagon, and several archive terms. It generated an increasingly elaborate public orientation: first identifying the project, then explaining its anti-flattening purpose, suggesting ways to participate, describing “seeds,” and attempting a worked reading.

The log preserves the system’s usefulness and its failures together. The Overview correctly recognized a distributed literary and semantic architecture and some of its vocabulary. It also confused Lee Sharks’s Pearl with the medieval Pearl, supplied participation instructions not established by the cited sources, and extended terms into confident system descriptions that exceeded the available evidence. Reconstructed prompts are clearly marked as reconstructions rather than hidden machine logs.

The record is important as a baseline traversal specimen. It shows what the public retrieval surface made available to a general search summarizer in early February 2026 and what the summarizer generated when source coverage thinned. It should be cited as documentation and analysis of one search session, not as an independent authority validating every statement contained in the captured Overview.
