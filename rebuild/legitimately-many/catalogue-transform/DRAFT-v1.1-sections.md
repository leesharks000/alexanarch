## replaces 1.4.2

1.4.2. *The catalogues file one making under two names, and say where the other half is.* The Theophrastan list shares twelve titles letter for letter with the Aristotelian, runs the alphabet twice before an unordered tail, carries nine internal doublets, and once files a work under a disjunction of the two names, Ὑπομνημάτων Ἀριστοτελικῶν ἢ Θεοφραστείων (V; #1581; locus table L09). Its first two entries are the titles of Aristotle's extant Analytics. The opening of Aristotle's catalogue, the lost dialogues, is a list of Plato's subject-titles with the names removed, seventeen of twenty-six entries, at seven times the base rate of the rest of the list, and two of its entries say what they are, *extracts from Plato's Laws* and *extracts from the Republic*; its residue is the Socratic examination-dialogues, the one function with no catalogue (#1590). What each catalogue files as lost under its own name stands extant under the other name, generated by a transform the catalogue's own form supplies (§4.5.4–4.5.5).

## replaces 4.2.5

4.2.5. *J5, reciprocal filing across the catalogues.* At each line the works catalogued and lost under one name are, at the level of the title, the works extant under the other. Aristotle's lost dialogues are Plato's extant dialogues as a treatise-catalogue files them, by Thrasyllus's subject-titles with the persons removed, seventeen of twenty-six at seven times the base rate (#1590 §2, §5); Plato's lost technical teaching, the divisions and *On the Good*, survives under Aristotle, the divisions handed over in Diogenes only as Διῄρει δέ, φησὶν Ἀριστοτέλης … κατὰ τὸν Ἀριστοτέλην and the title Περὶ τἀγαθοῦ filed in Aristotle's catalogue (#1590 §3). At the second line the same configuration holds: six of Aristotle's extant physical works that his own catalogue lacks stand in Theophrastus's, the eight-book Physics among them, and Theophrastus's extant plants, weather signs and stones stand, lost, in Aristotle's (§4.5.5). The Socratic examination is the function with no catalogue, and it is the residue of the transform (#1590 §2c).

## replaces 4.5.4

4.5.4. *The lost dialogues, and the hypothesis they carry.* The innocent reading first: a young member of the Academy wrote dialogues on the school's subjects, some under titles the master had used, and they were lost with the rest of the exoteric works; the master taught a technical doctrine he did not publish, and Aristotle reported it. Two independent losses, one on each side.

Read at the lines, the two losses are one filing seen from its two sides. Aristotle's list in Diogenes carries, in one run, Περὶ πολιτικοῦ α′ β′, Σοφιστὴς α′, Μενέξενος α′, Ἐρωτικὸς α′, Συμπόσιον α′ (V.22; L99): the Statesman's subject, and the Sophist, the Menexenus and the Symposium by name, filed as Aristotle's dialogues, none extant. The rest of the opening follows a rule that can be written down (#1590 §2c): Thrasyllus's second title, the one from the πρᾶγμα, kept and re-lexicalized in the treatise-corpus's vocabulary (Φαίδων ἢ περὶ ψυχῆς → Περὶ ψυχῆς; Πολιτεία ἢ περὶ δικαίου → Περὶ δικαιοσύνης, with Τὰ ἐκ τῆς πολιτείας beside it); the person-name dropped; a kind-name kept; the multi-book Platonic works yielding the only multi-book Aristotelian dialogues. In the other direction Plato's divisions survive whole in Diogenes, thirty pages of the analytical Plato, introduced and closed as Aristotle's report, and Aristotle's catalogue carries Διαιρέσεις and Περὶ τἀγαθοῦ in three books.

The study states the hypothesis this configuration carries, as a hypothesis: **the reciprocal filing.** The dialogues catalogued under Aristotle and lost are the dialogues extant under Plato's name, as the treatise-catalogue files them; the technical discourses credited to Plato and lost are extant under Aristotle's name, as his report and his treatises. What appears in each catalogue as the loss of one author's works is their survival under the other name. A tradition committed to two authors needs the dramatic Aristotle and the analytical Plato to have existed, and can find them only across the partition; the catalogue keeps each ghost's title, so that the mode is credited to the name, and withholds its text, so that the mode does not cross. The loss of works whose titles are the existing works of the other name is the configuration that leaves the author boundary unpunctured, and it is the configuration a single corpus organized as two would leave.

What the hypothesis claims and what it does not. It claims the titles, the rule that generates them, the rate at which the rule fits (seventeen of twenty-six in the opening against one in twenty in the rest of the period's titles) and the two entries that name their source. It does not claim that any lost title is identical in its text with the extant work; for most of the titles there is no text, and identity is unascertainable in principle, so the datum is the shape of the match (#1590 §5). Where fragments exist the test is a collation of the fragments ascribed to the Aristotelian dialogue against the Platonic dialogue of the same subject-title (Rose; Ross), which this study names and does not run.

## new 4.5.5

4.5.5. *The same filing at the second line, by the line's own transform.* The innocent reading first: a pupil wrote on the master's subjects under the master's titles, epitomized the master's books, kept the school's library, and his catalogue records his versions; the master wrote a little on plants and weather signs, and those books were lost. The line's records read the two catalogues at the lines and the extant pairs at the passages, and what they find is one transform under two names (#1581; #1590 §2b; #1605 §§3, 13).

(a) *The titles.* Twelve titles stand letter for letter in both lists: Περὶ βασιλείας, Περὶ ζῴων, Περὶ κινήσεως, Περὶ λέξεως, Περὶ μουσικῆς, Περὶ πλούτου, Περὶ φιλίας, Περὶ φύσεως, Περὶ ἑκουσίου, Προτρεπτικός, Τὰ πρὸ τῶν τόπων, Ἐρωτικός (#1581 §3.4). Five of the Aristotelian dialogue titles that Plato does not account for have their twin under Theophrastus, the Προτρεπτικός twice (#1590 §2b). Each list names the other within a title: Ὑπομνημάτων Ἀριστοτελικῶν ἢ Θεοφραστείων, Περὶ ἡδονῆς ὡς Ἀριστοτέλης and Ἐπιτομῶν Ἀριστοτέλους περὶ ζῴων under Theophrastus (#1581 §3.5), Πολιτικῆς ἀκροάσεως ὡς ἡ Θεοφράστου under Aristotle (V.22–27).

(b) *The transform.* The line's named-function model writes the Theophrastan operation down: extract, classify, register, reopen, *from formed object back to examinable constituents* (#1605 §13.1), and measures it at the extant pairs with controls inside each name: the Characters against Aristotle's own sketches of ἤθη in Rhetoric II, the same genre under two names, differing on exactly the inventory's axes (γάρ 37 against 259 per 10,000; cause-demand 0.74 against 3.02; the type-marker 16.4 against 9.0); the Metaphysics fragment against Λ, the sealed account reopened as named aporiai (ἀπορ-/ζητ- 7.12 against 2.37); De sensibus against the treatises' first books, the survey that the treatise performs as an opening standing alone as a work (#1605 §§3.2–3.3, 13.4). The catalogue under Theophrastus's name carries that operation as titles, applied to the works extant under Aristotle's: Ἀναλυτικῶν ἐπιτομή, Περὶ φυσικῶν ἐπιτομῆς, Ἐπιτομῶν Ἀριστοτέλους περὶ ζῴων ϛ′, Φυσικῶν δοξῶν ις′ with its own epitome, Προβλημάτων συναγωγῆς, Τὰ πρὸ τῶν τόπων, the title that names the position (#1605 §13.5). The "lost" Theophrastan works are, at the title, the extant Aristotelian works under the Theophrastan transform: epitomized, surveyed, put as problems, set before.

(c) *The configuration.* Read for the extant works title by title (catalogue-transform/RESULTS.md §2, post hoc): six physical works extant under Aristotle and absent from his own catalogue stand in Theophrastus's, Περὶ οὐρανοῦ, Περὶ γενέσεως, Μεταρσιολογικῶν, Περὶ αἰσθήσεων, Περὶ ὕπνου καὶ ἐνυπνίων and Περὶ γήρως, with Φυσικῶν α′–η′, the Physics in its eight books, where Aristotle's own list gives Φυσικὸν α′; the list opens with the two Analytics. In the other direction Aristotle's list carries Περὶ φυτῶν α′ β′, Σημεῖα χειμώνων α′ and Περὶ τῆς λίθου α′, whose extant candidates stand under Theophrastus, and the Περὶ φυτῶν and Ἀνέμων θέσεις transmitted under Aristotle's name are filed spurious. The rate test that separated the dialogue opening from the period (#1590 §5) does not separate this line: by the strict first-stem rule Theophrastus's list meets Aristotle's extant titles at 14 per cent, within the 5–13 per cent of Diogenes' other catalogues and level with Aristotle's own (plan frozen, sha256 87f81394…). The stems of Aristotle's extant titles are the period's subject-words; at this line the coincidence is in whole titles, their pairing, a book count and the transform's own names, which a stem rule does not see.

On the reciprocal-filing hypothesis the two lines are one operation: under each name the "lost" works are the extant works of the other name, generated by the transform of the line, the treatise-catalogue's subject-title at the first and the Theophrastan operation at the second, and their loss keeps the boundary from being tested. The study claims the titles, the transforms and the configuration; it does not claim the identity of any catalogued title with any extant text.

## replaces 8.5

8.5. **The losses keep the boundary from being tested.** At both lines the works filed as lost under one name stand extant under the other: the dialogues filed under Aristotle with Platonic titles survive in the list only (L99) and their candidates stand under Plato; the divisions credited to Plato survive as Aristotle's report; the plant, weather-sign and stone books catalogued under Aristotle stand under Theophrastus, and six of Aristotle's extant physical works stand in Theophrastus's catalogue and not in his own (§4.5.4–4.5.5). The pages of Nicolaus's Compendium that carried the ground of the ascription are lost (§3.4a); the testimony of Hermippus survives in excerpts, and its strongest claim for the break between persons is left unassigned in Diogenes (§4.6). Where the text is lost, the filing stands by default, and the doubt the custody story raises is admitted and closed in one paragraph (Hegel, p. 309; L93).
