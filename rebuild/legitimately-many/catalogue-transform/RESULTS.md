Under the strict rule the coincidence does not separate from the period's base rate at this line. Theophrastus's catalogue meets Aristotle's extant works at 14.3 per cent, inside the controls' 4.6–13.3 per cent and level with Aristotle's own catalogue (15.3). Aristotle's catalogue meets Theophrastus's extant works at 2.1 per cent, inside the controls' 0–4.0. The reason is visible in the matched titles: the stems of Aristotle's extant titles are the period's subject-words (φυσικ-, πολιτ-, ψυχ-, ῥητορ-), so Xenocrates' Φυσικῆς ἀκροάσεως and Chrysippus's Περὶ τῆς ἀναλύσεως τῶν συλλογισμῶν match as readily as any Theophrastan title. The stem rule that separated Plato's subject-titles from the period (#1590 §5) cannot see this line, because here the coincidence lies in whole titles, their pairing and their book counts.

## 3. The transform at this line, written down (post hoc)

From an extant title under one name to a catalogued title under the other: (a) the title kept, in the catalogue's genitive (Ἀναλυτικῶν, Τοπικῶν, Φυσικῶν, Περὶ οὐρανοῦ, Περὶ ποιητικῆς); (b) a synonym substituted (μετεωρο- → μεταρσιο-; ἔλεγχοι → σοφίσματα); (c) a paired title reduced to one member (Περὶ γενέσεως καὶ φθορᾶς → Περὶ γενέσεως; Περὶ νεότητος καὶ γήρως → Περὶ γήρως) or two titles joined into one (Περὶ ὕπνου καὶ ἐγρηγόρσεως + Περὶ ἐνυπνίων → Περὶ ὕπνου καὶ ἐνυπνίων); (d) the book count changed, except the Physics, whose eight books stand under Theophrastus. In the other direction the extant Theophrastan title loses its specification (Περὶ σημείων ὑδάτων καὶ πνευμάτων καὶ χειμώνων καὶ εὐδιῶν → Σημεῖα χειμώνων; Περὶ λίθων → Περὶ τῆς λίθου; the two plant works → Περὶ φυτῶν β′). The residue under Aristotle's extant works: the Metaphysics, absent from both lists; the Categories and De interpretatione, carried by Aristotle's own list only.

## 4. What this establishes, and what it does not

It establishes, at the lines, that for this seam the coincidence lies in whole titles and their configuration: six extant Aristotelian treatises absent from his own catalogue stand in Theophrastus's, the eight-book Physics among them, and three extant Theophrastan works stand, lost, in Aristotle's. It does not establish that any catalogued title is the extant work; for lost titles there is no text to collate (#1590 §5). The strict-rule rate test, frozen, does not separate this line from the period, and that result stands beside the hand reading.

## Deviations

The hand reading of §2 and the transform of §3 are post hoc, outside the plan, labelled as such. The catalogue runs were located by a scan for title-lines; Strato's catalogue (V.59–60) is set as prose in the seated text and was not among the runs, so it is not a control.
