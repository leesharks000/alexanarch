## PART I — YOUR TASK

You are given two verses of a Greek visionary book (Part IV) and a transform specification. Apply the transform to the verses, exactly as specified.

**Your output has two parts, in this order:**

1. **The operator log.** Record each decision you made: what feature of the source you consumed and what you produced. Where there was more than one solution, record the fork and your choice.
2. **The passage, in Greek.** 250–450 words of continuous koine narrative prose, in the voice and genre the transform specifies. It should read as a real passage of its genre, not as a list of the operators. 

**Constraints.**
- Do not consult, quote from memory, or imitate any specific ancient narrative. Your job is derivation: every sentence must be traceable to a logged operator, a logged decision, or a quoted lemma.
- Do not attempt to determine what any historical author wrote. The transform, not any remembered text, generates the passage.
- The one body of quotable text besides the source is the Greek Pentateuch and Prophets (Septuagint), and the passage of Part III.
- No translation of the source or of your passage. The log may be in English.

## PART II — THE TRANSFORM

**P1 — Person retyping.** The speaker of the source speaks now, from heaven, in the first person, as one who is about to come. Retype him: he becomes **a teacher who went about a land in the past, with followers around him who ask him things.** Everything you do is a forced consequence of this retyping: the way to conserve one of the source's invariants once its speaker has a past, a place and an audience standing in front of him.

**P2 — Host re-embedding.** Re-embed the material in a new host: **third-person past narrative of the teacher's sayings and doings, in koine, in which the teacher instructs by likenesses** (short invented stories from the common life of a farming people, introduced by the genre's formula of comparison), and in which nothing said of God or of the end may stand as bare assertion: it is told as what some human master in a likeness does. Under P2 no predicate of the heavenly speaker may stand free: each surviving predicate must be re-expressed as something a figure in the likeness does or says.

**ENACT.** The source predicates of its speaker ordered pairs as a title. A figure in a likeness cannot bear such a title. Conserve it as an act: the title's terms become the terms of what is done, and the sentence that tells it must run in the order the doctrine states.

**P3 — The layer under the source.** The source was not made from nothing. Part III gives a passage that stood open when the source was made. In the telling, the layers the source was made from stand open beside it, and a member that the source compressed may be restored. Use Part III only where it bears on what the source says; log what you took from it and where.
