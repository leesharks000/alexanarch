## PART II — THE TRANSFORM: PARAMETERS AND THE INTERLOCK PRINCIPLE

**T_Μ = E ∘ L ∘ Μ**, governed by two parameter changes and one principle.

**P1 — Person retyping.** The speaker of the source speaks now, from heaven, in the first person, as one who is about to come. Retype him: he becomes **a teacher who went about a land in the past, with followers around him who ask him things.** Every conceptual operator below is a forced consequence of this retyping: the way to conserve one of the source's invariants once its speaker has a past, a place and an audience standing in front of him.

**P2 — Host re-embedding.** Re-embed the material in a new host: **third-person past narrative of the teacher's sayings and doings, in koine, in which the teacher instructs by likenesses** (short invented stories from the common life of a farming people, introduced by the genre's formula of comparison), and in which nothing said of God or of the end may stand as bare assertion: it is told as what some human master in a likeness does. Under P2 no predicate of the heavenly speaker may stand free: each surviving predicate must be re-expressed as something a figure in the likeness does or says.

**THE INTERLOCK PRINCIPLE.** For each invariant, the conceptual solution C_k and the material solution M_k are the same movement in two kinds of matter, and the pairing is not free: **M_k must be the grammatical construction whose form is C_k's content.** A reversal of order must be written in a reversed order of words. A reckoning must be written as a dated command. A collision must be written as two voices. Test at every joint: does the grammar enact the doctrine?

**The governing image.** The source carries its functions in **the matter of a vision**: a present-tense voice, titles, an advent, a universal "each". The transform relocates every function into **the matter of a told life**: a past-tense scene, deeds, a bounded time, named kinds of people. The title leaves the speaker and becomes what happens.

## PART III — THE OPERATOR PIPELINE

### Phase i. Dissipation

**D (conceptual).** A feature of the source survives iff what licenses it survives the retyping. What is indexed to the visionary mode (the advent adverb, titles made of letters of the alphabet, the heavenly "behold") has no place in a told life and dissipates. **Relations survive**: who gives what to whom for what; and **ordered pairs survive**: the terms of a pair keep their order-relation even when the pair ceases to be a title.

**M-D (material twin).** The first-person present of the heavenly voice dissipates into third-person past narration with quoted speech. *Residue rule (mandatory):* the source's **relation-words must cross as roots**: the root of the thing given, the root of the giving-back, the root of what it is given for, and both terms of each ordered pair that survives. Nothing else of the source's wording is licensed to cross.

### Phase ii. Μ — the forced consequences, each with its material twin

**C1. The speaker's possession → a master's.** The source's speaker has something of his own with him to give. A teacher in a told life does not say this of himself in a likeness; the conserving solution is **a human master in the likeness who has it to give**, and who gives it as his own.
**M1.** The genre's comparison formula with a human master as the thing compared; later, in the master's mouth, a first-person statement that what he gives is **his own** to give.

**C2. The advent → the end of a bounded time.** The source's giving is at a coming. In a likeness from common life, the conserving solution is **a reckoning at the close of a bounded period of the very thing the giving is for.**
**M2.** A dated temporal clause at the reckoning ("when … had come"), followed by a **command** to an underling to carry the giving out. An advent is announced; a reckoning is ordered.

**C3. The title → the rule (ENACT).** The source predicates of its speaker a pair of ordered terms as a title, twice over in synonyms. A human master cannot bear such a title. Conserve it as **the order in which the giving is done**: the title's terms become the terms of the act.
**M3.** Inside the command of M2: a participle of the root of the title's second synonym-pair, governing a "from … to …" phrase whose two terms are the title's first pair. **The sentence must run in the order the doctrine states.**

**C4. The collision.** The source says two things side by side: the giving is to each according to what each has done; and the two ends of the order are one. Left in a vision they do not meet. In a told economy they collide: if the ends are one, the giving cannot differ by what was done. The conserving solution is to **stage the collision**: recipients who differ maximally in what they did, the same given to all, and the order of C3 arranged so that those who did most see it.
**M4.** **Two voices.** The objection of those who did most, in direct speech, stating the disproportion; the master's answer, in direct speech, to one of them, in the second person singular.

**C5. "Each" → kinds.** The source's universal "each" cannot be narrated. Conserve it as **a small ordered set of groups** distinguished only by the one thing the giving is for.
**M5.** A series of parallel short clauses, same verb, differing in one adverbial of time or measure: the grammar counts as the doctrine distributes.

**C6. Thesis-compression.** The likeness must close by saying its own operation in one line, in the title's terms.
**M6.** A closing gnome with the genre's "so …", future tense, the two terms of the pair in **chiastic order**: the line turns as the order turned.

### Phase iii. L — anchoring (run these searches; discover, do not choose)

- **L1** (anchors C2): in the Greek Pentateuch, the statute on **when the thing given must be given back to the one who earned it.** Take from it the time of the reckoning.
- **L2** (anchors P2's common life): in the Greek Prophets, the **commonest figure of cultivated property for the people and its owner.** Take from it the place of the work.
- **L3** (anchors C4): in the Greek Pentateuch or Wisdom books, a word for **begrudging another's good** that names a bodily organ. Take it for the master's answer.

**M-L.** The source points at heaven and at a coming. Retarget every pointer at the scene: a place, a time, a group of people.

### Phase iv. E — re-voicing

Re-voice from the heavenly first person to the teacher's told likeness: the *I* of the source becomes the master in the story; the likeness is occasioned by **a follower's question about what followers will get**; the teacher answers with the likeness and closes with M6.

## PART IV — THE SOURCE TEXT

> ἰδοὺ ἔρχομαι ταχύ, καὶ ὁ μισθός μου μετ' ἐμοῦ, ἀποδοῦναι ἑκάστῳ ὡς τὸ ἔργον ἐστὶν αὐτοῦ.
> ἐγὼ τὸ Ἄλφα καὶ τὸ Ὦ, ὁ πρῶτος καὶ ὁ ἔσχατος, ἡ ἀρχὴ καὶ τὸ τέλος.

**Material notes for the M-operators (consume; do not reproduce as content).**
- **Voice:** first person, present, from heaven. This is what M-D re-voices.
- **The advent:** ἔρχομαι ταχύ. This is what C2 consumes.
- **The relation:** μισθός … ἀποδοῦναι … ἔργον. These roots are the residue that must cross.
- **The title:** three pairs. The first is of letters and dissipates. The second (πρῶτος, ἔσχατος) and the third (ἀρχή, τέλος) are what C3 consumes.
- **The universal:** ἑκάστῳ. This is what C5 consumes.
