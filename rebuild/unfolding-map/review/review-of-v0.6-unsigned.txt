The document does something rare: it computes its own limits and then argues past them honestly. The cost run (2D.5) shows that edge-level measures can't decide direction; the author's answer (2E) doesn't pretend they can, it moves the question to the whole graph. That's the right move, and it's the one that's not yet built.

## What's strongest

**The null pairs (2D.4) are the best test in the file.** Revelation at 29 links per 100 verses, Acts at 29.3, James at 31.5, and the floor at 6–13 for the Pauline letters. The instrument finds known relatives and puts Revelation among them. It doesn't find relations everywhere. That's a real control.

**The conceded cases (2E.4) are the second strongest.** Seven derivations where the received chronology already puts the direction: 2 Peter, Jude, the Pastorals. The operators that work there are the same operators that work in the disputed Gospels. That's the finding the document should lead with more than it does: the mechanism is invariant across the disputed and undisputed cases. The one thing the reverse account needs is a second mechanism for the disputed cases. It hasn't been shown.

**The delta census (2D.3)** is clean even with its limit stated: Matthew's additions to Mark lean toward Revelation's vocabulary (21.1%) and toward no other book, but 88.8% of those stems are in the Septuagint, so both could be drawing on the same prophetic Greek. The document says so. The limit is in the reading, not the data.

## What's not yet strong

**The four laws (2C.3–2C.6) are the most rhetorically prominent thing in the file and the least tested.** 2C.8 says it plainly: "They were found by reading the lists, so they are hypotheses with a test attached, and the test is the blind run." The blind run (2D.6) is designed and not run. Until it's run, the laws are descriptions of what was found, not predictions of what comes next. A reader who doesn't get to 2C.8 will think they're confirmed.

**The seven within seven (section 4) is one datum.** The κράτος doxology dividing the letters (4.6) is a real finding, and the Muratorian order is a real thing. But one formula and one seat is not a structure. The section is honest about this ("left open, at the author's word"), but it's the part of the file where the gap between what's found and what's hoped is widest.

## The one thing that matters most

**The graph-level measure (2E.3) is the decisive one and it's not built.** The cost run prices edges; the direction argument prices the *set* of sources a writer must hold. The one-book-against-many-books asymmetry is the argument, and it's an argument until it's a number. The control that's closest to bearing on it is the one the document already names: writings of the same decades outside the canon. If Revelation's links stop at the canon's edge, that's evidence for the forward account. If they don't, it's evidence against. Seating the Didache, 1 Clement, and Hermas and running the link instrument on them is a day's work and would be worth more than any further refinement of the operator set.

## Two specific things

**The two-verse Septuagint model (2D.5, owed) matters more than it looks.** The received account of Rev 1:7 and Matt 24:30 is exactly a two-verse model: Dan 7:13 joined to Zech 12:10. If a two-verse Septuagint model is cheaper than the forward transform for that seed, that's a problem for the forward account that the current one-verse model can't show. If it's not, it's evidence. That one test needs to run.

**The operator distribution in 2E.4 is the file's quietest result.** PARTITION and REORDER dominate both the Gospel derivations (68%, 41%) and the late-letter derivations (86%, 29%); the Gospels add the narrative operators (REVERSE-DOER, UNDO, TRANSFER-ATTRIBUTE, NEGATE) and the doxologies have no use for them. That's what you'd expect if the mechanism is one thing applied in different genres. The reverse account needs to explain why the same operators work in both directions but the doxologies don't need the narrative ones. It's easier to say the mechanism is one and the genres differ.

## What the file should say about itself

The document is a working file, and it's honest about that throughout. But a reader coming to it cold will see the engine's 40-for-40 fit (2B.3) before they see 2B.5's disclaimer that the derivations were written with the targets in view. The order in the file favors the rhetorical claim over the methodological one. The method is the stronger thing; the file should put it first.
