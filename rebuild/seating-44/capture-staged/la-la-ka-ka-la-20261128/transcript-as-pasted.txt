"la la ka ka la, wrrir, akerir" is built on a four-stage phonetic progression across ten lines. The poem transitions from percussive, staccato syllables to long vowel runs, and finally into resonant, word-like vocalizations.

Macro-Structure (The Four Movements)

• Movement 1: Percussive Opening (Lines 1–3). The poem establishes a crisp rhythmic pulse using the open vowel la and the velar stop ka. Line 1 introduces two rolled, trill-like interruptions (wrrir, akerir), while lines 2 and 3 settle into a steady, seven-beat chant (la la la la ka la ka), shifting from period-separated staccato (la.la.la.la) to smooth spacing.
• Movement 2: Vowel Expansion (Line 4). The steady beat breaks open into two sustained, oscillating vowel runs built from alternating e and i sounds. The first run (loeieieiei) uses four pairs, and the second (leieieieieiei) stretches to six pairs, mimicking a sustained vocal trill or ululation.
• Movement 3: Nasal & Back-Vowel Shift (Lines 5–8). The pitch drops from bright front vowels (e, i) to rounded back vowels (o) paired with alveolar nasals (n) and liquids (l). Phrases like lo laye-lo lan, lo-oanno, and no no low create a heavier, humming cadence that resolves into repeating oh lo loops.
• Movement 4: Semantic Emergence (Lines 8–10). Closing the l and o sounds with a final l transforms pure glossolalia into recognizable English text-speak (lol) and slang (lil). After repeating lol three times at the end of line 8, the poem isolates lil on line 9 and lol on line 10 as single-word conclusions.

Key Phonetic & Visual Patterns

• Consonant Anchoring: The liquid consonant L acts as the structural backbone across all ten lines, evolving from an initial onset (la, lo) into a bracketing frame (lil, lol).
• Typographic Pacing: Punctuation and spacing act as musical notation. Commas create brief pauses in line 1, periods enforce clipped pauses in line 2, and hyphens in lines 5–6 (laye-lo, lo-on, lo-oanno) bind syllables into single breath units.
• Pre-Linguistic to Digital Arc: The overall trajectory mimics vocal development, moving from infant-like babbling (la, ka) and raw vocal play (wrrir, eieiei) into modern internet vernacular (lil, lol).

Key Connections & Departures

• Musical Notation via Typography: Like Kurt Schwitters' Ursonate—which uses spacing, capitalization, and punctuation to dictate tempo and breath—Sharks uses periods (la.la.la.la), hyphens (lo-oanno), and vowel repetition (leieieieieiei) as a performance score.
• Glossolalia and "Zaum": The Russian Futurists (such as Velimir Khlebnikov and Aleksei Kruchenykh) coined zaum ("beyond sense") to describe a universal, trans-rational language of pure sound. Sharks' opening la and ka patterns tap into this same primal, infant-babble universality before narrowing into English slang.
• The Digital Turn: Classic Dada and Lettrist poets used sound poetry to rebel against print culture and industrial modernity. By anchoring the poem in the Alexanarch archive with cryptographic metadata and ending on lol, Sharks bridges raw bodily vocalization with algorithmic and digital communication.
