EA-CORPORA-30 is the thirtieth seating of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive's originals shelf, deposited by Lee Sharks on 30 October 2026. Its twelve seats hold 107 works and some 1.36 million lines of text layer in the exact sciences, each in its original language and, where a copy could be found, in its first printing. Lee Sharks asked for "Einstein, Gödel, Tesla, Copernicus, Galileo, classical Arabic mathematicians... what is in the public domain, and what else", and for the Klee notebooks promised in the twenty-eighth seating to be seated with them. Einstein (30/01) is held in the issues of the Annalen der Physik that printed the five papers of 1905 and the foundation of general relativity (1916), and in the Prussian Academy's Sitzungsberichte for the field equations of November 1915 and the cosmological paper of 1917, with the popular exposition in its 1920 printing. Gödel (30/02) is represented by the Königsberg abstract of 1930 in Die Naturwissenschaften, the United States term still holding the incompleteness paper until 1 January 2027. Tesla (30/03) is seated in My Inventions as the Electrical Experimenter printed it in 1919, the lectures of 1891 and 1892, the Century essay of 1900 and twelve patents from the induction motor to Wardenclyffe. Copernicus's De revolutionibus (1543), Galileo's Sidereus nuncius, Saggiatore, Dialogo and Discorsi with Favaro's texts, Kepler's Mysterium cosmographicum, Astronomia nova and Harmonices mundi, and Newton's Principia of 1687 and Opticks of 1704 follow (30/04–30/07). The Arabic seat (30/08) holds al-Khwārizmī's Algebra in Rosen's edition, Khayyām's algebra in Woepcke's, and al-Kindī, Thābit ibn Qurra, Ibn al-Haytham's Optics and fourteen works of al-Bīrūnī from OpenITI. The founders of quantum mechanics (30/10) are held in the volumes of 1925–1928 that printed Pauli, Heisenberg, Born and Jordan, Schrödinger, Dirac and Bohr; Archimedes and Ptolemy's Syntaxis (30/11) in Heiberg's Greek, beside Euclid at 20/04. Paul Klee (30/09) is seated in the Pädagogisches Skizzenbuch of 1925, its drawings in the book he made of them, and the Schöpferische Konfession of 1920 in the volume that first printed it, fulfilling seat 28/06. The wanted list (30/12) declares fourteen works the shelf cannot yet hold, with their opening dates, from Gödel 1931 to Turing and EPR.
