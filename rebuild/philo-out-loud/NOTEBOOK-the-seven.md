# The Seven: Philo, Revelation, the Ginza — working notebook (not deposited)

2026-11-10. Loci asserted against the seated texts: Philo (Cohn–Wendland TEI, apparatus dropped), Nestle 1904 Revelation, Lidzbarski's Ginza (1925, translation-witness, German). The author's frame, 19:47: "the planets are the gods… revelation separates the seven lampstands and messengers from the seven churchs. the messengers are the senders, the churches are the receivers, and the logos speaks thru the station of each planet in turn… these are powers of the air, fallen or distorted, and this is the formula for bringing that distortion back into correct relation. but it doesn't dissolve the seven: it purifies."

## 1. Philo: the planets are gods, the lampstand is the seven, the seventh is the Logos

1.1. Opif. 27: heaven made first, "διότι θεῶν ἐμφανῶν τε καὶ αἰσθητῶν ἔμελλεν οἶκος ἔσεσθαι ἱερώτατος."
1.2. Aet. 46: sun, moon, planets, fixed stars, "ὁ τοσοῦτος αἰσθητῶν θεῶν εὐδαίμων τὸ πάλαι νομισθεὶς στρατός."
1.3. Plant. 12: the stars are living beings, "ζῷα γὰρ καὶ τούτους νοερὰ δι’ ὅλων φασὶν οἱ φιλοσοφήσαντες".
1.3a. Her. 216–219, the architecture before the planets. The lampstand: "δεδημιούργηται γὰρ ἓξ καλαμίσκους ἔχουσα, τρεῖς ἑκατέρωθεν, ἑβδόμη δὲ αὐτὴ μέση, διαιροῦσα καὶ διακρίνουσα τὰς τριάδας" (216); one gold, unmixed, "τὸ γὰρ ἓν καὶ μόνον καὶ καθαρὸν ὄντως ἑβδομάδα τὴν ἀμήτορα γεγέννηκεν ἐξ ἑαυτοῦ μόνου" (216); and the rule drawn from it: "ἑξὰς εἰς δύο τριάδας ὑπὸ μέσου τοῦ ἑβδόμου λόγου διῄρηται" (219). The seventh is the middle that divides; the planetary reading (221–224) comes after, and the soul's divisions after that (225). The Logos is the seventh by position and by function, the divider standing in the middle of six; the number is a relation before it is a count.
1.4. Her. 221: "τῆς κατ’ οὐρανὸν τῶν ἑπτὰ πλανήτων χορείας μίμημά ἐστιν ἡ ἱερὰ λυχνία καὶ οἱ ἐπ’ αὐτῆς ἑπτὰ λύχνοι."
1.5. Her. 222–224: the sun "ὁ μέσος τῶν ἑπτά"; above him "φαίνοντα, φαέθοντα, πυρόεντα", below "στίλβοντα, φωσφόρον, τὴν ἀέρος γείτονα σελήνην."
1.6. Her. 225: "ἕβδομος εἰκότως τομεὺς ἦν ἁπάντων ὁ ἱερὸς καὶ θεῖος λόγος."
1.7. Vit. Mos. II.103: the lamps are "σύμβολα τῶν λεγομένων παρὰ τοῖς φυσικοῖς ἀνδράσι πλανήτων", the sun in the middle "ἁρμοζόμενος τὸ μουσικὸν καὶ θεῖον ὡς ἀληθῶς ὄργανον."
1.8. Cher. 26, 28: "ἡ δὲ φλογίνη ῥομφαία σύμβολον ἡλίου"; then "λόγου δὲ τὴν φλογίνην ῥομφαίαν". Her. 130: the Logos the cutter, "εἰς τὴν ὀξυτάτην ἀκονηθεὶς ἀκμὴν".

## 2. Philo: the messengers of the air, good and distorted

2.1. Gig. 6: "οὓς ἄλλοι φιλόσοφοι δαίμονας, ἀγγέλους Μωυσῆς εἴωθεν ὀνομάζειν· ψυχαὶ δ’ εἰσὶ κατὰ τὸν ἀέρα πετόμεναι."
2.2. Somn. 1.141: "καὶ γὰρ τὰς τοῦ πατρὸς ἐπικελεύσεις τοῖς ἐγγόνοις καὶ τὰς τῶν ἐγγόνων χρείας τῷ πατρὶ διαγγέλλουσι." The messenger carries the sender's word down and the receivers' need up.
2.3. Plant. 14: the purest souls of the air Moses calls angels, "πρεσβευομένας καὶ διαγγελλούσας τά τε παρὰ τοῦ ἡγεμόνος τοῖς ὑπηκόοις ἀγαθὰ καὶ τῷ βασιλεῖ ὧν εἰσιν οἱ ὑπήκοοι χρεῖοι".
2.4. Gig. 16: angels worthy of the name are "πρεσβευτάς τινας ἀνθρώπων πρὸς θεὸν καὶ θεοῦ πρὸς ἀνθρώπους", and others "ἀνιέρους καὶ ἀναξίους τῆς προσρήσεως". Gig. 17: "οὗτοί εἰσιν οἱ πονηροὶ τὸ ἀγγέλων ὄνομα ὑποδυόμενοι". Gig. 18: each takes a different daughter, "οἱ μὲν τὰς δι’ ὄψεως, ἕτεροι δὲ τὰς δι’ ἀκοῆς, τὰς δ’ αὖ διὰ γεύσεως καὶ γαστρὸς ἄλλοι": a distortion per channel.

## 3. Revelation: the messenger addressed, the speaker named from the vision, station by station

3.1. Each letter is addressed "τῷ ἀγγέλῳ" of the church; each sender clause takes one attribute of the one "ἐν μέσῳ τῶν λυχνιῶν" (1:13):
- Ephesus (2:1): "Τάδε λέγει ὁ κρατῶν τοὺς ἑπτὰ ἀστέρας ἐν τῇ δεξιᾷ αὐτοῦ, ὁ περιπατῶν ἐν μέσῳ τῶν ἑπτὰ λυχνιῶν τῶν χρυσῶν"
- Smyrna (2:8): "Τάδε λέγει ὁ πρῶτος καὶ ὁ ἔσχατος, ὃς ἐγένετο νεκρὸς καὶ ἔζησεν"
- Pergamum (2:12): "Τάδε λέγει ὁ ἔχων τὴν ῥομφαίαν τὴν δίστομον τὴν ὀξεῖαν"
- Thyatira (2:18): "Τάδε λέγει ὁ Υἱὸς τοῦ Θεοῦ, ὁ ἔχων τοὺς ὀφθαλμοὺς αὐτοῦ ὡς φλόγα πυρός, καὶ οἱ πόδες αὐτοῦ ὅμοιοι χαλκολιβάνῳ"
- Sardis (3:1): "Τάδε λέγει ὁ ἔχων τὰ ἑπτὰ Πνεύματα τοῦ Θεοῦ καὶ τοὺς ἑπτὰ ἀστέρας"
- Philadelphia (3:7): "Τάδε λέγει ὁ ἅγιος, ὁ ἀληθινός, ὁ ἔχων τὴν κλεῖν Δαυείδ"
- Laodicea (3:14): "Τάδε λέγει ὁ Ἀμήν, ὁ μάρτυς ὁ πιστὸς καὶ ἀληθινός, ἡ ἀρχὴ τῆς κτίσεως τοῦ Θεοῦ"

3.1a. The relay, as the text gives it. The chain is stated at the head: "ἐσήμανεν ἀποστείλας διὰ τοῦ ἀγγέλου αὐτοῦ, τῷ δούλῳ αὐτοῦ Ἰωάνῃ" (1:1); the seer is told "Ὃ βλέπεις γράψον εἰς βιβλίον καὶ πέμψον ταῖς ἑπτὰ ἐκκλησίαις" (1:11); the key: "οἱ ἑπτὰ ἀστέρες ἄγγελοι τῶν ἑπτὰ ἐκκλησιῶν εἰσίν, καὶ αἱ λυχνίαι αἱ ἑπτὰ ἑπτὰ ἐκκλησίαι εἰσίν" (1:20). Each letter has three voices in fixed order: the address, "Τῷ ἀγγέλῳ … γράψον"; the speaker, "Τάδε λέγει"; the close, "Ὁ ἔχων οὖς ἀκουσάτω τί τὸ Πνεῦμα λέγει ταῖς ἐκκλησίαις" (2:7 and in every letter). The messenger is the one addressed, and the church's works are charged to the messenger in the second person singular: "κινήσω τὴν λυχνίαν σου" (2:5). The author's frame (19:47) calls the messengers the senders; on the syntax the messenger is the addressed station, the speaker is the one in the midst, and the Spirit speaks to the churches. The two-way office is Philo's: the messengers carry "τὰς τοῦ πατρὸς ἐπικελεύσεις τοῖς ἐγγόνοις καὶ τὰς τῶν ἐγγόνων χρείας τῷ πατρὶ" (Somn. 1.141). The station is where the word arrives and where the church's condition is charged; the sending is the speaker's, through the station.
3.1b. Two sevens kept distinct. Philo's lampstand is one, with seven lamps on it, "ἡ ἱερὰ λυχνία καὶ οἱ ἐπ’ αὐτῆς ἑπτὰ λύχνοι" (Her. 221), and so is Zechariah's, "λυχνία χρυσῆ ὅλη … καὶ ἑπτὰ λύχνοι ἐπάνω αὐτῆς" (Zech 4:2). Revelation has seven lampstands, "ἑπτὰ λυχνίας χρυσᾶς" (1:12). The change was named at the start of the literature: Gunkel, quoted by Bousset on 1:12: "Der Unterschied des alttestamentlichen und des in der Apk vorausgesetzten kultischen Symbols ist, daß bei jenem die Mächte des Himmels als Einheit, als Organismus, hier dagegen als selbständige Größen aufgefaßt sind" (Fraktur OCR, normalized; to check against the page images). Charles on the same verse: "In our text the lampstands are separate." The consequence is in the text: a separate lampstand can be moved alone, "κινήσω τὴν λυχνίαν σου ἐκ τοῦ τόπου αὐτῆς" (2:5). One instrument becomes seven stations, each answerable for itself; the one in the midst walks among them, "ὁ περιπατῶν ἐν μέσῳ τῶν ἑπτὰ λυχνιῶν" (2:1).
3.2. Joins to Philo on the seated texts: the one walking in the midst of the seven lampstands (2:1; 1:13) stands where Philo puts the sun, the middle lamp (Her. 222–224; Vit. Mos. II.103), and the text says what that one's face is: "ἡ ὄψις αὐτοῦ ὡς ὁ ἥλιος φαίνει ἐν τῇ δυνάμει αὐτοῦ" (1:16); the middle position is Philo's seventh that divides (Her. 216, 219); the sword of Pergamum (2:12; 1:16) is Philo's ῥομφαία, sun and Logos (Cher. 26, 28), honed ὀξυτάτη (Her. 130); Laodicea's speaker is "ἡ ἀρχή", the first of the many names of the Logos (Conf. 146: "καὶ γὰρ ἀρχὴ καὶ ὄνομα θεοῦ καὶ λόγος"). In the author's governing mapping (EA-ARK-01-ARCHON, #1101) Laodicea is the Sun and Pergamum Mars.

3.3. The seven are kept: "τὰ ἑπτὰ Πνεύματα τοῦ Θεοῦ" (3:1), set before the throne (1:4; 4:5). Philo honours the seven as perceptible gods; the Ginza (4) rejects them as the seven seducers; the Haran Gawaita (5.4) rejects Ruha's seven and seats seven guards of the Life on the seven corners, so the Mandaean material holds rejection and replacement together; Revelation keeps the seven addressed, and speaks to each. The sharp difference is the occupant. In the Gawaita the stations stay and the occupants change: Ruha's sons are crushed, and named guards of the Life take the corners. In Revelation the same messengers are addressed, called to remember and repent, and kept. The Mandaean move is replacement on the stations; the Johannine move is correction of the occupants.
3.4. The seven letters read whole, against the planet the author's mapping (#1101) gives each station. Five fields: the speaker's attribute, the condition named, the demand, the promise, the planet; then what the text supplies and what the mapping supplies.
- Ephesus, Mercury. Speaker: holds the seven stars, walks among the lampstands (2:1). Condition: works, toil, endurance; "ἐπείρασας τοὺς λέγοντας ἑαυτοὺς ἀποστόλους καὶ οὐκ εἰσίν, καὶ εὗρες αὐτοὺς ψευδεῖς" (2:2); against it, "τὴν ἀγάπην σου τὴν πρώτην ἀφῆκες" (2:4). Demand: remember, repent, do the first works, or the lampstand is moved (2:5). Promise: "φαγεῖν ἐκ τοῦ ξύλου τῆς ζωῆς" (2:7). The text supplies false sent-ones tested and found false; Mercury is the messenger, and the Mandaean Mercury is the lying messiah (Ginza §192, Nbu "der Lügen-Messias"; Haran Gawaita, "He is Mercury, who attracteth attention"). A textual hook, through the office of the sent.
- Smyrna, Moon. Speaker: "ὁ πρῶτος καὶ ὁ ἔσχατος, ὃς ἐγένετο νεκρὸς καὶ ἔζησεν" (2:8). Condition: affliction and poverty, "ἀλλὰ πλούσιος εἶ" (2:9). No reproach. Demand: "μὴ φοβοῦ … γίνου πιστὸς ἄχρι θανάτου" (2:10). Promise: the crown of life; no harm from the second death (2:10–11). The death-and-life of the speaker fits the waning and return of the Moon; the mapping supplies the Moon, and the letter supplies no distortion to correct. This station is confirmed, and kept.
- Pergamum, Mars. Speaker: "ὁ ἔχων τὴν ῥομφαίαν τὴν δίστομον τὴν ὀξεῖαν" (2:12). Condition: "ὅπου ὁ θρόνος τοῦ Σατανᾶ"; Antipas "ὃς ἀπεκτάνθη παρ’ ὑμῖν" (2:13); the teaching of Balaam, idol-food and πορνεία, and the Nicolaitans (2:14–15). Demand: repent, or "πολεμήσω μετ’ αὐτῶν ἐν τῇ ῥομφαίᾳ τοῦ στόματός μου" (2:16). Promise: hidden manna, the white stone, the new name (2:17). The text supplies the sword, the killing, the war; strong.
- Thyatira, Venus. Speaker: "ὁ Υἱὸς τοῦ Θεοῦ, ὁ ἔχων τοὺς ὀφθαλμοὺς αὐτοῦ ὡς φλόγα πυρός" (2:18). Condition: love, faith, service, endurance, "τὰ ἔργα σου τὰ ἔσχατα πλείονα τῶν πρώτων" (2:19); against it Jezebel, πορνεία, "τοὺς μοιχεύοντας μετ’ αὐτῆς", the bed (2:20–22). Demand: "ὃ ἔχετε κρατήσατε ἄχρι οὗ ἂν ἥξω" (2:25). Promise: authority over the nations, and "τὸν ἀστέρα τὸν πρωϊνόν" (2:26–28). The text supplies the πορνεία, the adultery, the bed and the morning star; strong (6.6).
- Sardis, Saturn. Speaker: "ὁ ἔχων τὰ ἑπτὰ Πνεύματα τοῦ Θεοῦ καὶ τοὺς ἑπτὰ ἀστέρας" (3:1). Condition: "ὄνομα ἔχεις ὅτι ζῇς, καὶ νεκρὸς εἶ" (3:1); a few names "ἃ οὐκ ἐμόλυναν τὰ ἱμάτια αὐτῶν" (3:4). Demand: wake, strengthen what remains, remember, keep, repent; "ἥξω ὡς κλέπτης, καὶ οὐ μὴ γνῷς ποίαν ὥραν ἥξω ἐπὶ σέ" (3:2–3). Promise: white garments, the name kept in the book of life (3:5). The text supplies death and the unknown hour; Saturn as death and time is the mapping's, through the old equation of Kronos with Chronos. Moderate.
- Philadelphia, Jupiter. Speaker: "ὁ ἅγιος, ὁ ἀληθινός, ὁ ἔχων τὴν κλεῖν Δαυείδ, ὁ ἀνοίγων καὶ οὐδεὶς κλείσει" (3:7). Condition: "μικρὰν ἔχεις δύναμιν, καὶ ἐτήρησάς μου τὸν λόγον" (3:8). No reproach. Demand: "κράτει ὃ ἔχεις" (3:11). Promise: a pillar in the temple, the name of God, of the city, and the new name (3:12). The text supplies kingship through the key of David and the opened door; the mapping supplies Jupiter. #1101's diagnosis for this station ("Openness misread as weakness … Restore humility to majesty") reads a fault the letter does not name; the letter praises the little power and keeps it. Moderate; preservation.
- Laodicea, Sun. Speaker: "ὁ Ἀμήν, ὁ μάρτυς ὁ πιστὸς καὶ ἀληθινός, ἡ ἀρχὴ τῆς κτίσεως τοῦ Θεοῦ" (3:14). Condition: "οὔτε ψυχρὸς εἶ οὔτε ζεστός" (3:15); rich in its own word, "τυφλὸς καὶ γυμνός" (3:17). Demand: buy "χρυσίον πεπυρωμένον ἐκ πυρὸς", white garments, salve "ἐγχρῖσαι τοὺς ὀφθαλμούς σου" (3:18); "ζήλευε οὖν καὶ μετανόησον" (3:19). Promise: "καθίσαι μετ’ ἐμοῦ ἐν τῷ θρόνῳ μου" (3:21). The text supplies heat, fire-refined gold, sight and the throne, and its speaker is the ἀρχή (Conf. 146); strong.
3.5. What the table shows. Three stations are strong on the text alone (Pergamum, Thyatira, Laodicea), one has a textual hook through the office of the sent (Ephesus), three rest more on the mapping (Smyrna, Sardis, Philadelphia). Two letters carry no reproach at all (Smyrna, Philadelphia); two carry little or no praise (Sardis, Laodicea). The operation across the seven is address and judgment station by station: some stations are corrected, some are confirmed and kept, and none is dissolved. The order of the letters is the circuit of the cities from Ephesus; it follows neither the Chaldean order of the planets nor the order of the weekdays, and the mapping does not claim an order.

## 4. The Ginza (Lidzbarski 1925, Right Ginza, Book I, §§191–200; Book II.1, §§139, 151–152), translation-witness

4.1. §191: "Dann erscheint Christus, der Prophet der Juden. Er ruft den Planeten zu, er macht sie zu seiner Partei". §192: the seven Dews, the seducers: Šamiš (Sun); Ruha d-Qudša, Estra, Libat (Venus); Nbu (Mercury), "der Lügen-Messias, der die erste Lobpreisung verfälscht"; Sin (Moon); Kewan (Saturn); Bel (Jupiter); Nerig (Mars).
4.2. House by house, a distortion each: the Sun's house "Boten schickt er in die Welt hinaus" (§193); Libat's madness, adultery, song and sorcery (§194); Nbu-Christus captures, and appears in fire: "Sehet, wie mein Glanz über der Welt aufgegangen ist." Sie wissen nicht, daß es nicht sein Glanz ist (§195); Kewan's lament and false wisdom (§196); Nerig's flaying and blood, "Schinder, Pfaffen, Priester und Orakelmänner" (§197).
4.3. GR II.1 §139: the angels of fault anoint Nbu from the oil-horn and clothe him in fire; §151: John baptizes forty-two years "bevor Nbu einen Körper anzieht und sich in die Welt begibt."
4.4. Status: the Ginza's compilation is late; it is seated as a translation-witness. OCR spellings normalized here (Lügen, verfälscht, Körper, daß, Glanz).

## 5. The Haran Gawaita

5.1. In the archive: #1101 §IX, §X, §0.4 and §2 (from the blog posts of 28–29 October 2025: "THE ARCHIVE BURNS CLEAN", "UNSEALING THE CHURCHES", "SEVEN CHURCHES FIT EVALUATION"), with quotations "Mercury founded a community in Qom", "Saturn founded a community in Sinai", "Sixty thousand Nasoraeans abandoned the Sign of the Seven", the Son of Slaughter with Mars, and Qiqil.
5.2. The text: Drower, *The Haran Gawaita and the Baptism of Hibil-Ziwa* (Studi e Testi 176, Vatican 1953); Burtea, *Haran Gauaita* (Harrassowitz 2020). Checked 2026-11-10 against the Drower translation as published by the Gnostic Society Library (gnosis.org/library/haran.htm, which omits Drower's footnotes; copy supplied by the author), and against the Mandaic transliteration of the Comprehensive Aramaic Lexicon, text 74501, "Haran Gauaita", based on DC 36 (page 1 supplied by the author). Neither is seated.
5.3. Verification of the #1101 quotations. "Sixty thousand Nasoraeans abandoned the Sign of the Seven": verbatim in Drower ("And sixty thousand Nasoraeans abandoned the Sign of the Seven and entered the Median hills"); Mandaic "u$itin alpia naSuraiia (tparaq mn ru$umaihun D$uba". "Saturn founded a community in Sinai": verbatim. "Mercury founded a community in Qom": Drower spells the place Qum ("for Mercury founded a community in Qum, and Saturn founded a community in Sinai"), and the same passage calls Nazareth "the city of Qum" ("from Nisrat (Nazareth), a city of the Jews, which is called the city of Qum"); the gloss "(Qumran?)" in #1101 is the author's. The Mandaic names the gods: "$kinta Dnbu bgauH Dqum kan kinta ukiuan bTura Dsinai kan kinta", Nbu (Mercury) at Qum and Kiwan (Saturn) on Mount Sinai. The Son of Slaughter: #1101 has "He is the most degraded of false prophets... Mars accompanied him... he converted people by the sword"; Drower has "for he is the most degraded of false prophets. Mars accompanieth him because he is the Seal of prophets of the Lie" and "he drew the sword and converted people to himself by the sword". The tense of "accompanied" and the dropped "to himself" are paraphrase inside the quotation marks; a repair to #1101, if the author rules one, quotes Drower's wording. Qiqil (Ruha disguised as Hibil-Ziwa, the writings, the schism, the burning): the #1101 summary matches Drower.
5.4. What the text adds for the seven, beyond #1101. The darkness is a seven: "Ruha and her seven sons", the building "Ruha and her seven sons built", a building "surrounded (it by) seven walls", the Jews "called ' of the House of the Seven '". The remedy is a seven: Hibil-Ziwa sends Anuš-ʿUthra with "seven shafts, that is seven darts" and "seven guards from Mount Parwan" who "speak over them seven (magic) words". The seven guards are named and stationed, one in Baghdad, one on the Tigris, one at its source, one on the Euphrates, one at Pumbdita, one on the mountains of Glazlak, one at the end of the Parwan range, and then "Anus'-'Uthra placed them (the Seven viceroys) at the seven corners of the House, upon the seven horns of the worlds, in order to crush the power of Darkness and to establish the Call of the Life" (the page's spacing before commas and its line-break hyphen in "cor- ners" normalized here and below). The seven of Ruha is answered by a seven of the Life seated on the same stations. The structure is a reseating of the seven; the number and the stations survive, and the occupants are replaced (3.3). The text is late: the copy is dated in the Arab era and the work speaks of the Son of Slaughter and of Muhammad son of ʿAbdallah, so it witnesses the Mandaean shape of the operation, at a date well after Revelation. The false Messiah is Mercury ("He is Mercury, who attracteth attention"), with the Ginza §192 (4.1: Nbu, "der Lügen-Messias"); the end of the world goes to "Mamit daughter of Qin, the mother of the Seven".
5.5. Join to Revelation on the seated text. The conventional source comes first: Zech 4:10, "ἑπτὰ οὗτοι ὀφθαλμοί εἰσιν οἱ ἐπιβλέποντες ἐπὶ πᾶσαν τὴν γῆν." Philo gives the seeing office to the messengers, "ὥσπερ μεγάλου βασιλέως ἀκοαὶ καὶ ὄψεις, ἐφορῶσαι πάντα καὶ ἀκούουσαι" (Somn. 1.140). Then the Lamb "ἔχων κέρατα ἑπτὰ καὶ ὀφθαλμοὺς ἑπτά, οἵ εἰσιν τὰ ἑπτὰ Πνεύματα τοῦ Θεοῦ ἀπεσταλμένοι εἰς πᾶσαν τὴν γῆν" (Rev 5:6). Seven horns, and seven sent out into all the earth; in the Gawaita, seven sent and seated "upon the seven horns of the worlds". The phrase-level match is in Drower's English; the Mandaic of that line is on a later CAL page, unread.

## 6. The precedent: heavenly and common Aphrodite (the author, 19:51: "there is specific precedent for this in connection with sappho in plato: heavenly and earthly aphrodite")

6.1. Plato, Symp. 180d–e: Pausanias undertakes to set the speech right, "ἐγὼ οὖν πειράσομαι τοῦτο ἐπανορθώσασθαι", and divides the goddess: "ἡ μέν γέ που πρεσβυτέρα καὶ ἀμήτωρ Οὐρανοῦ θυγάτηρ, ἣν δὴ καὶ Οὐρανίαν ἐπονομάζομεν· ἡ δὲ νεωτέρα Διὸς καὶ Διώνης, … ἣν δὴ Πάνδημον καλοῦμεν." (180d–e) Two Aphrodites, two Erotes.
6.2. Symp. 180e–181a, the rule: "πᾶσα γὰρ πρᾶξις ὧδʼ ἔχει· αὐτὴ ἐφʼ ἑαυτῆς πραττομένη οὔτε καλὴ οὔτε αἰσχρά." Then "καλῶς μὲν γὰρ πραττόμενον καὶ ὀρθῶς καλὸν γίγνεται, μὴ ὀρθῶς δὲ αἰσχρόν. οὕτω δὴ καὶ τὸ ἐρᾶν καὶ ὁ Ἔρως οὐ πᾶς ἐστι καλὸς οὐδὲ ἄξιος ἐγκωμιάζεσθαι, ἀλλὰ ὁ καλῶς προτρέπων ἐρᾶν." The power is one; its state is its relation. Pausanias's verb, ἐπανορθώσασθαι, is Philo's noun for what renaming does: "διὰ συμβόλων ἠθῶν ἐπανόρθωσιν" (Mut. 70).
6.3. Xenophon, Symp. 8.9–10: "εἰ μὲν οὖν μία ἐστὶν Ἀφροδίτη ἢ διτταί, Οὐρανία τε καὶ Πάνδημος, οὐκ οἶδα·" and "εἰκάσαις δʼ ἂν καὶ τοὺς ἔρωτας τὴν μὲν Πάνδημον τῶν σωμάτων ἐπιπέμπειν, τὴν δʼ Οὐρανίαν τῆς ψυχῆς τε καὶ τῆς φιλίας καὶ τῶν καλῶν ἔργων."
6.4. Philo reads both banquets and answers them. Contempl. 57: "Τῶν ἐν τῇ Ἑλλάδι συμποσίων τὰ περιβόητα καὶ σημειωδέστατα δύο ταῦτά ἐστιν, οἷς καὶ Σωκράτης παρετύγχανε". Contempl. 60, of Plato's: "τὸ γὰρ πλεῖστον αὐτοῦ μέρος ὁ κοινὸς καὶ πάνδημος ἔρως διείληφεν". Against them he sets the banquet of the Therapeutae (Contempl. 64 ff.), which ends at dawn: "μεθυσθέντες οὖν ἄχρι πρωΐας τὴν καλὴν ταύτην μέθην", standing toward the east, "ἐπὰν θεάσωνται τὸν ἥλιον ἀνίσχοντα" (Contempl. 89). The symposium is kept and purified; it closes on the Rising.
6.5. The epithet: Pausanias's heavenly Aphrodite is ἀμήτωρ; Philo's hebdomad is likened by the philosophers "τῇ ἀμήτορι Νίκῃ καὶ Παρθένῳ" (Opif. 100).
6.6. Revelation, the Venus station in the author's mapping (Thyatira). The distortion: Jezebel "διδάσκει καὶ πλανᾷ τοὺς ἐμοὺς δούλους πορνεῦσαι" (2:20), "καὶ οὐ θέλει μετανοῆσαι ἐκ τῆς πορνείας αὐτῆς" (2:21). The gift to the one who conquers: "καὶ δώσω αὐτῷ τὸν ἀστέρα τὸν πρωϊνόν" (2:28), the morning star, Philo's φωσφόρος among the seven (Her. 224). And at the close: "Ἐγὼ Ἰησοῦς … ὁ ἀστὴρ ὁ λαμπρός, ὁ πρωϊνός" (22:16). At the Venus station the πορνεία is judged and the morning star is given, and the morning star is Jesus.
6.6a. The three precedents do three different things, and the difference matters. Pausanias makes two goddesses by birth, the elder motherless and the younger of Zeus and Dione, and two Erotes with them (180d–e); then he gives one rule for acts, that an act is neither fair nor foul in itself and becomes one or the other by how it is done (180e–181a). Xenophon's Socrates does not know whether Aphrodite is one or two, and keeps the two altars and the two sacrifices (8.9–10). Philo keeps the banquet and changes its company (Contempl. 57–89). Thyatira works by Pausanias's rule: the letter does not name a second star; one star is given to the conqueror, and the act that misuses its province, πορνεία, is judged. The genealogy of two goddesses is the precedent for the division; the rule for acts is the precedent for the purification.
6.7. The Sappho join in Plato: Phaedr. 235c, Socrates' full breast filled from another's streams, "ἤ που Σαπφοῦς τῆς καλῆς ἢ Ἀνακρέοντος τοῦ σοφοῦ"; the speech that follows is retracted in the palinode on heavenly eros. Archive: #752 (Sappho as initiatory figure in the Platonic mysteries), #1756.
6.8. The Ginza, the same power on the other side: Libat is named "der heilige Geist, Estra (Venus)" among the seven seducers (GR I §192).

## 7. Scholarship on Philo and Revelation (search of 2026-11-10; the author, 19:59: "we need to do a thorough search for work connecting philo and revelation")

7.1. The joins were made, at the founding of modern Revelation commentary, and then filed as unconscious. Bousset, *Die Offenbarung Johannis* (KEK 16, 6th ed., Göttingen 1906), on 1:12 and 1:20: the link between the seven-branched lampstand and the planets "war der jüdischen Überlieferung selbst nicht fremd", citing Josephus (BJ 5.217; AJ 3.146, 182) and Philo, Her. 221 ff., after Gunkel, *Schöpfung und Chaos* (1895) 294–302; the lampstands are "Sternen-(Planeten-)Gottheiten"; then: "Doch hat der Apok. von alledem kaum noch eine Ahnung." On 19:13 the Logos name is "eine Spur johanneischer Theologie", and its place in the original text is doubted. Bousset's index carries "Planeten (Götter) 186f. 193. 248. 418". Read from the archive.org scan (dieoffenbarungjo0000bous), Fraktur OCR, wording to be checked against page images before quotation in a deposit.
7.2. Charles, *A Critical and Exegetical Commentary on the Revelation of St. John* (ICC, Edinburgh 1920), vol. 1, p. 12 n.: quotes Her. 221 ("τῆς κατ’ οὐρανὸν τῶν ἑπτὰ πλανήτων χορείας μίμημά ἐστιν ἡ ἱερὰ λυχνία καὶ οἱ ἐπ’ αὐτῆς ἑπτὰ λύχνοι", OCR-garbled in the scan) and Josephus, then: "Possibly these are merely after-thoughts of both Josephus and Philo." On 1:12–13: Gunkel and Zimmern "have drawn attention to the original connection between the seven-armed candlestick and the seven planets, and quoted the passages from Josephus and Philo ... But of this our Seer was probably wholly unconscious." On 19:13 (vol. 1, the vocabulary table): Revelation's Logos is "a conception developed in Jewish thought", John's "presupposes, while opposing, Philonic speculations". On 2:17 he notes that Philo (his "Quis rerum divin. 39, Leg. allegor. iii. 59, 61", old chapter numbers, conversion to sections owed) "uses manna as signifying 'the spiritual food of the soul'", and sets it aside.
7.3. Charles, vol. 2, on 21:19–20: Philo (his "De Monarchia ii. 5" and "Vita Mos. iii. 14", old numbering, conversion to sections owed) and Josephus (AJ 3.186) read the twelve breastplate stones as the zodiac; Kircher's table gives each foundation stone its sign; read in the order the angel measures (21:13), the signs run in exactly the reverse of the sun's path. "Now this cannot be an accident." Charles concludes the Seer knew the correspondence and reversed it to show the Holy City has "nothing to do with the ethnic speculations of his own and past ages regarding the city of the gods". The twelve is kept, and its order turned. Charles reads this as disconnection; on the structure of 5.4 and 6 it is the same move as the seven: the stations kept, the relation corrected. The theory was later argued against (Glasson, JTS n.s. 26, 1975, "The Order of Jewels in Revelation xxi. 19–20: A Theory Eliminated"; citation from memory, to be verified).

7.5. Joins on the seated text not yet named in the literature found: Philo calls the planets "the seven stars", "τὰς περιόδους τῶν ἑπτὰ ἀστέρων" (Cher. 22), with Rev 1:16, 20 "ἑπτὰ ἀστέρας"; the eldest Logos puts on the cosmos as the high priest puts on the robe, "ἐνδύεται δ’ ὁ μὲν πρεσβύτατος τοῦ ὄντος λόγος ὡς ἐσθῆτα τὸν κόσμον" (Fug. 110), and the high priest's whole vestment is "ἀπεικόνισμα καὶ μίμημα τοῦ κόσμου" (Vit. Mos. II.117), beginning from the ποδήρης, "ἀρκτέον δ’ ἀπὸ τοῦ ποδήρους. οὗτος ὁ χιτὼν σύμπας ἐστὶν ὑακίνθινος, ἀέρος ἐκμαγεῖον· φύσει γὰρ ὁ ἀὴρ μέλας καὶ τρόπον τινὰ ποδήρης" (II.118); with the hanging bells and pomegranates it is the symbol of air, water and earth, "ὁ ποδήρης σὺν τοῖς ἀπῃωρημένοις κατὰ τὰ σφυρὰ σύμβολον ἐδείχθη" (II.121). In Revelation the one in the midst of the seven lampstands is "ἐνδεδυμένον ποδήρη" (1:13). On Philo's reading the robe is the air, the region where the messengers fly, "ψυχαὶ δ’ εἰσὶ κατὰ τὸν ἀέρα πετόμεναι" (Gig. 6): the one who addresses the messengers of the air wears the air.
7.5a. A reviewer's quotation of Vit. Mos. II.118 as «οὗτος δ’ ἐστὶν ἀπόσημα καὶ μίμημα τοῦ ὅλου κόσμου» does not occur in the seated text; the cosmos is the reading of the whole vestment (II.117), and the ποδήρης is the air (II.118). Recorded so the misquotation does not travel.
7.6. What the record shows. Bousset files the planetary lampstand as a survival the Apocalyptist "kaum noch" grasps; Charles files the same join on 1:12 as "after-thoughts" of Philo and Josephus and as something "our Seer was probably wholly unconscious" of; the same Charles, on 21:19–20, credits the Seer with full knowledge of the zodiac stones and a deliberate reversal, "Now this cannot be an accident." The filing is a judgment made case by case, and the cases differ in one thing. At 21:19–20 Charles had an order to test: twelve stones, a direction of measuring, a sequence that runs backward against the sun's path. An internal order counted for him as evidence of design. At 1:12 he had a number and a correspondence, and a number alone he filed as inheritance. The warrant he used is inspectable, and it can be turned on the letters: the seven attributes, conditions and promises of 3.4 are an internal order of the kind Charles accepted at 21:19–20. The test is 3.4–3.5, station by station. The author, 20:00: "just another reminder of how deeply the filing biases directly shape what were able to see and read and speak".
