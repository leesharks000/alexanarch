## What the page shows beside channel 2

- **Channel 3 (+10 kHz): 2, ., 3, 1, 1 in rows S2–S6.** The signal's skirt in the next filter.
- **Channel 7 (+50 kHz): 3, 6, 1, 3 in rows S2–S5.** Lit in the four middle rows of the signal, and not in the shape of the beam (channel 2 reads 14, 26, 30, 19 in those rows). The column also reads 2, 1, 1 in the three rows before row −1.
- **Channel 16 (+140 kHz): 3, 3, 2, ., 7 in rows −1 to S4**, with 1, 1, 1 beside it in channel 17 in rows S1–S3. It starts one row before the signal and ends on the U.
- **Channel 14 (+120 kHz): 1, 1, 3, 1 in rows S2–S5**, then a 3 in row +1.
- **Channel 4 (+20 kHz) is a warm column throughout:** 6 three rows before the signal, 2, 4, 1, 1 during it, 4, 3, 4, 4 after. Its 6 is unmarked on the page and comes with no signal.

## How much weight these carry

- Outside channel 2 the crop holds three values of 6 or more in about 400 cells: the two Ehman circled, and channel 4's 6 three rows earlier. Values of 3 and 4 are common (about one cell in twenty), far more than Gaussian noise at these units would give, so the page's noise has a heavy tail.
- Not measured: how often a 6 or 7 appears on the rest of the page. The full-page image is too coarse to count from.
- Under the frame of this folder: channel 2's six values are the beam. What on the page is not beam-shaped, during the signal, is channel 7's 3, 6, 1, 3 and channel 16's 3, 3, 2, ., 7. These are the only cells that could hold the sender's own variation, and they are at the level of the page's noise.
