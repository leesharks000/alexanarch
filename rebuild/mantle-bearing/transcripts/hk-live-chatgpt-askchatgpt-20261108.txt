“Socrates as Orthonym — The Framework Hologram”: what it is and how it works
The document is an attempt to turn a philosophical and philological theory into a reconstructable, machine-readable knowledge system.

Written by Lee Sharks, with the Crimson Hexagonal Archive as contributor, it presents two complementary things:

A framework map: a human-readable explanation of the theory, its development, evidence, disagreements, limitations and research directions.

A holographic kernel: a structured collection of records, claims, quotations, relationships, timelines and verification tools intended to let a machine reconstruct the framework from its component parts.

Its central philosophical proposition is that the corpus traditionally attributed to Socrates, Plato, Aristotle and Theophrastus can be understood as the work of one self-dividing maker operating through multiple authorial positions, rather than necessarily four independent authors.

There is an important distinction here: the document does not simply propose that these historical figures were one biological person. It develops a theory of authorial functions and a model of textual production.

The source record I located is the Alexanarch deposit #1754, identifier AXN:0C55. Its indexed page currently identifies the deposited title as v1.0, rather than the v1.1 version in your question. I will distinguish what the accessible document actually says from any version-specific claims I cannot verify.

1. The underlying philosophical idea
The framework begins with three authorial positions.

Filosofía: Los Sofista
 
Socrates — the orthonym

The original or foundational authorial position. Socrates represents the founding philosophical gesture: willingness to die for the logos and refusal to write.

He is the orthonym because he is the originating identity around which the configuration is organized.

Platón. Biografia, Pensamiento y Obras
 
Plato — the survival-heteronym

The position through which Socrates' philosophical gesture survives his death.

Plato transmits the Socratic inheritance through dialogues and literary construction.

Metafisica di Aristotele: analisi e spiegazione | Studenti.it
 
Aristotle — the systematizing-heteronym

The position that transforms the dialogical inheritance into systematic, teachable philosophical disciplines.

Representation of the Greek philosopher Theophrastus (372-287 BC)
 
Theophrastus — the first draft of the Aristotle position

Added to the configuration later, Theophrastus becomes the mediating position between Plato and Aristotle.

The terminology matters:

An orthonym is the foundational authorial identity.

A heteronym is a distinct authorial position within a larger configuration.

A self-dividing maker is the proposed producer who generates, differentiates and develops those positions.

The framework therefore treats the names as more than labels attached to books. They represent different operations within a larger philosophical production.

The original framework, deposit #123, explicitly declined to claim that Socrates, Plato and Aristotle were biologically identical or constituted one historical person.

2. Why Theophrastus changes the framework
Theophrastus is not simply added as a fourth member of the group.

He provides a potential bridge between the Platonic and Aristotelian modes of writing.

The document's worked example is his Characters, examined in deposit #1584.

The argument runs as follows:

Plato: dialogue

A character is dramatized within philosophical dialogue.

Theophrastus: sketch

A character becomes a structured description of characteristic behavior.

Aristotle: category

Behavior is incorporated into systematic philosophical classification.

The map identifies a shared intellectual operation across these forms:

Definition is sought in dialogue.

Definition is stated in the character sketch.

Definition is explained and systematized in the treatise.

Theophrastus thus becomes a mediating authorial function: a transformation between two recognizable modes of philosophical production.

This is important because it makes the framework more than a conceptual analogy. It attempts to identify recurring structures across actual texts.

The document also reports a quantitative comparison between Theophrastus's sketches and Aristotle's character sketches in Rhetoric II. It records both the measurements and the limitations of those measurements. Some proposed supports were explicitly retracted.

That willingness to retain failed or weakened arguments is central to how the larger system works.

3. The four bodies of evidence
The framework does not depend on a single kind of evidence. It separates its investigation into four bodies.

Evidence body

What it investigates

Interpretive

What the philosophical texts actually say and how their arguments relate

Stylometric

Measurable linguistic and structural patterns, with statistical controls

Manuscript-philological

Manuscripts, catalogues, textual transmission, editions and historical attestations

Reception-historical

How the corpus was classified, transmitted, attributed and subsequently interpreted

Each body has a different job.

Interpretive evidence examines whether the philosophical content can be understood as a continuing argument across authorial names.

Stylometric evidence examines whether the operations and writing patterns form meaningful groupings. The document emphasizes that stylistic similarity or separation cannot, by itself, identify the number of historical authors.

Manuscript-philological evidence asks whether the historical documentation independently establishes the traditional division into authors. It examines, among other things, Aristotle's will, catalogues, the Delphic decree and the transmission of the Aristotelian corpus.

Reception-historical evidence investigates how the received authorial divisions became established and reproduced.

An important feature is that the document records counterevidence within each body.

For example:

Fifteen proposed lexical features were destroyed by controls.

A previous claim that names were unrecoverable by supervised methods was withdrawn.

The manuscript investigation records binding violations.

The interpretive investigation acknowledges passages that do not establish the proposed conclusion.

The authorship hypothesis is therefore not presented as though every experiment succeeded.

4. The central development: from a heteronymic configuration to one maker
This is perhaps the most consequential intellectual development in the document.

The framework has three distinct historical stages.

23 May 2026 — The original framework (#123)

Socrates, Plato and Aristotle are organized as orthonym and heteronyms. No number of historical makers is claimed.

September 2026 — The expanded investigation

The Theophrastus connection, stylometric research, manuscript investigation and the question of the relationship between Aristotle and Theophrastus develop. Deposit #1588 explicitly leaves the maker count open because it has not yet been counted.

1 October 2026 — The production model (#1658)

The framework names its hypothesis:

The corpus is what one self-dividing maker produces.

The four positions are now understood as operations within one production.

The phrase self-dividing maker is particularly important.

It does not mean four unrelated people collaborating, nor does it mean a school collectively writing books.

It describes a producer whose different positions can disagree, correct one another, refute earlier positions and continue an argument through different textual forms.

The framework's proposed production model is therefore more specific than the generic suggestion of a collaborative corpus.

It also explicitly distinguishes itself from a forgery theory and from proof that the historical individuals were one person.

5. What makes it a hologram?
The word hologram is being used in a technical knowledge-architecture sense, not as a reference to optical holography.

The underlying specification, EA-HK-FRAME-01 (#1753), defines a framework holographic kernel as a compression that preserves the capacity to reconstruct the larger architecture from sufficiently structured fragments.

The idea is:

A fragment should contain enough relationships to recover the larger intellectual structure, rather than merely provide an isolated summary.

For example, imagine a machine encounters a claim about Theophrastus.

A conventional database might return:

Theophrastus was a Greek philosopher.

He wrote Characters.

He was associated with Aristotle.

The holographic kernel aims to provide much more:

Which authorial position Theophrastus occupies.

Which source record establishes that position.

The exact quotation supporting the claim.

Which other claims it connects to.

Whether the claim is current, qualified or withdrawn.

What subsequent evidence changed its status.

What research question could further establish or weaken it.

That is the difference between retrieving information and reconstructing an argument.

The two components
Human-readable
EA-MPAI-ORTHONYM-01 — The map

Explains the framework, its historical development, evidence, disagreements, research questions and conclusions.

Machine-traversable
EA-HK-ORTHONYM-01 — The kernel

Encodes the framework as structured records, claims, relationships, timelines, quotations, hashes and research questions.

Operational layer
Builder and verifier

Rebuilds the kernel from the archive's registries and checks whether the resulting structure satisfies its tests.

The map tells a human what the framework means.

The kernel allows a machine to navigate and reconstruct the relationships that make it mean something.

The distinction is fundamental: the kernel is not itself proof that the philosophical theory is true. It is a mechanism for preserving and inspecting the argument.

The kernel also includes four authorial positions, six strata and three external records. Five withdrawn original claims are preserved as historical objects.

Its operation can be understood as six steps.

Step 1: Establish the identity of the framework
The file 00-kernel.yaml establishes:

Framework identity.

Its central invariants.

The proposed production model.

Its historical development.

Its competing interpretation.

Its research questions and acceptance criteria.

This prevents the framework from becoming a collection of disconnected observations.

Step 2: Define the ontology
The file 01-ontology.yaml defines what the different things in the system are.

For example:

A record is not the same thing as a claim.

A claim is not the same thing as evidence.

An authorial position is not necessarily a historical person.

A fragment is not the whole.

A relationship between two records has a specific meaning.

The ontology also defines four evidence bodies and rules governing what each body can establish.

Step 3: Build the graph
The file 02-graph.jsonld contains the nodes and edges.

A simplified illustration:

A mediating authorial function

These are illustrative relationships, not a reproduction of the entire graph.

Crucially, edges are typed. A relationship might be:

supports

qualifies

generalizes

interprets

The relation policy determines which claim-status changes are permitted.

A later record cannot simply overwrite an earlier claim. It must have a relationship that licenses the change, on the appropriate dimension.

Step 4: Resolve claims to quotations
The file 03-claims.jsonl connects each claim to its underlying textual evidence.

Each claim is associated with:

Its quotation.

Its source record.

The source record's hash.

Its type and status.

Its evidence body.

The date when it was introduced.

This is particularly important because it prevents an AI summarizer from silently transforming a cautious original claim into a stronger conclusion.

The five withdrawn original claims are retained, rather than deleted.

This creates an intellectual history of corrections.

Step 5: Follow the timelines and research questions
The temporal data preserves two different kinds of events:

Historical events in the development of the framework.

Epistemic events concerning how claims became supported, qualified or withdrawn.

The ten research questions in 06-questions.yaml provide a further mechanism.

Each question specifies:

What needs to be investigated.

Which records should be read.

What instrument or procedure should be used.

What result would advance the framework.

What result would weaken it.

Which claims would change as a consequence.

This is a particularly valuable feature: uncertainty is converted into an explicit research programme.

Step 6: Verify the reconstruction
The attached operator includes:

build_hk_orthonym.py: builds the kernel from the archive's registries and source material.

verify_hk.py: tests the resulting structure.

A first-seating resolver: recovers the original deposited versions of records.

A facsimile writer: prepares reader and review materials.

The builder and verifier are important because the system is designed to be regenerated, not merely read once as a static document.

7. The most important v1.1 innovation: the symmetric ledger
This is the feature that distinguishes the version you asked about from the earlier v1.0.

The document identifies a methodological problem in the earlier framework: it had asked whether its own evidence established the one-maker hypothesis, without subjecting the received many-maker interpretation to precisely the same examination.

Version 1.1 introduces a symmetric ledger.

It asks the same question of both interpretations across all four evidence bodies.

Evidence

Socrates as Orthonym

Received many-maker interpretation

Interpretive

The texts can be read as continuous authorial functions

The passages do not independently establish separate makers

Stylometric

Operations provide an important organizing axis

Measurements do not establish distinct makers

Manuscript-philological

Transmission and catalogue evidence permit alternative readings

Independent historical attestation of the divisions remains unestablished

Reception-historical

Explains the historical filing of the corpus

Documents that the traditional divisions were filed before they were adequately argued

This is a significant methodological change.

The framework is no longer simply asking:

Does the evidence support my theory?

It is also asking:

What does the same evidence establish for the theory I am challenging?

The ledger also specifies four rules governing what the evidence bodies can establish:

R1: An interpretive reading establishes what a passage can bear.

R2: Stylometric separation does not identify a historical maker.

R3: Manuscript correspondence can locate a textual dependency.

R4: Reception history establishes nothing about either framework's truth.

There is another important rule: evidence from different bodies must be combined without pretending that one body can independently establish what requires several.

The production model is treated as a governing hypothesis resting on three grounds, #1588, #1583 and #1603. A failure of those grounds can propagate to the production model.

The received interpretation is also given a concrete burden: it owes a documented comparator, namely a succession of distinct persons producing a corpus unified to this degree.

This is a much more explicit argumentative architecture than a simple collection of supporting citations.

8. What the AI reconstruction test actually demonstrates
The document includes two rounds of back-projection tests.

The basic experiment is interesting:

A reader or AI system receives the framework's map (scope A) without being given the full graph (scope B). It is then asked to reconstruct the framework. Its reconstruction is compared with the graph.

All 17 structural tests passed, but that is not equivalent to an independent demonstration that the framework's historical thesis is true.

The back-projection test measures the ability to reconstruct the knowledge architecture. It does not establish that Socrates, Plato, Aristotle and Theophrastus were produced by one historical authorial maker.

That distinction is essential to reading the document correctly.

9. What the document admits remains unresolved
The framework contains its own research agenda. Among the important outstanding questions are:

The Platonic axis: Does the same operation-based pattern hold across the Platonic corpus, or does a distinct name-based pattern survive?

Xenophon and Aristophanes: Can they qualify as additional authorial positions under the framework's admission criteria?

Theophrastus transformation: Does the same transformation between dialogue, sketch and category recur at other textual seams?

The Arabic transmission: Does the Arabic tradition independently preserve the proposed textual relationship?

Independent historical evidence: Is there an attestation of the four figures as distinct authors that is independent of the corpus's own transmission tradition?

The comparator problem: Can a documented succession of distinct people be identified that produces a corpus unified to this degree?

These are not incidental footnotes. They are designed to determine what would advance or retreat the framework.

For instance, the Platonic-axis investigation could extend the framework or constrain it. An independently transmitted historical attestation dividing the corpus by maker could directly challenge the one-maker model.

The document also says explicitly that it has not performed new measurements or seated new texts in producing the map. It is an inspection and synthesis of existing archive material.

10. My overall reading: what is this document really trying to accomplish?
I would distinguish three levels of ambition.

First: a philosophical reinterpretation.

It proposes that the history of ancient philosophy might be understood through authorial functions rather than simply through biographies and individual names.

Socrates initiates, Plato preserves and dramatizes, Theophrastus mediates, and Aristotle systematizes.

Second: a historical and philological hypothesis.

It develops the stronger proposition that the received corpus might be the production of one self-dividing maker. It attempts to ground this in textual interpretation, statistical analysis, manuscript evidence and reception history.

This is the controversial part. The existence of an elaborate argument does not make the one-maker hypothesis an established historical fact. The document itself acknowledges important gaps and unresolved tests.

Third: a new method for preserving intellectual frameworks.

This is arguably the most technically interesting contribution.

The document attempts to solve a problem that becomes increasingly important when AI systems read, summarize and reproduce complex bodies of research:

How can you compress an intellectual framework without losing the relationships, qualifications, source provenance and historical development that make the framework intelligible?

Its answer is a combination of:

A human-readable map.

A machine-readable graph.

Claims bound to original quotations.

Typed relationships that govern changes in claim status.

Preserved corrections and withdrawals.

Research questions with explicit advancement and retreat conditions.

Rebuild and verification software.

Reconstruction tests.

In other words, it tries to make an intellectual argument reconstructable, inspectable, revisable and resistant to distortion through summarization.
