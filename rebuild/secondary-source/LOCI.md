**L7. Eusebius, HE 3.39.5–6: Papias's two Johns** — seat eusebius-historia-ecclesiastica.

D argues for. Mechanics: the features of stratum 3.1 (the book names its origin, its writer and its address, 1:1, 1:4, 1:9, 22:7–8) become the evidence that its writer is not the evangelist; the book's self-disclosure is turned into the ground of its secondariness.

**L11. Eusebius, HE 7.25.17–19: the Gospel's Logos as the measure** — seat eusebius-historia-ecclesiastica.
> συνᾴδουσι μὲν γὰρ ἀλλήλοις τὸ εὐαγγέλιον καὶ ἡ ἐπιστολὴ, ὁμοίως τε ἄρχονται. τὸ μέν φησιν· ἐν ἀρχῇ ἦν ὁ λόγος, ἡ δὲ, ὃ ἦν ἀπ’ ἀρχῆς. τὸ μέν φησι· καὶ ὁ λόγος σὰρξ ἐγένετο

F presupposes. The first locus of the study's fourth count: the Johannine Logos is defined by John 1:1 and 1:14 and 1 John 1:1, and the Apocalypse is set outside the agreement of Gospel and Epistle; Rev 19:13 (Ὁ Λόγος τοῦ Θεοῦ) does not enter the comparison. The figure's name is measured by the Gospel's, and the book that names it is ruled out of the measure.

O: last in every Western list that names it, except Claromontanus (before Acts, among the obelized books) · S: received in every list but Philaster's (and Philaster records the Cerinthian attribution as heresy) · D: Philaster reports D (Cerinthus) and files it as heresy · F: none. What the West adds is that the book furnishes the canon's measure: the twenty-four elders of Rev 4:4 and 4:10 are the warrant by which the Cheltenham list proves the Old Testament books canonical ("maiores nostri probant hos libros esse canonicos et hoc dixisse seniores") and the number by which Jerome counts them. The book that the Eastern lists leave out supplies the Western lists with the count of the canon. Jerome's praise (tot sacramenta quot verba) is the sentence Luther answers (L29).

## 1a. What carriage adds (first reading)

The count of carriage (design v0.4 §2.5) records a derivation of the text that no reader argued: the book's first printed Greek text descends from a single commentary manuscript, and its final verses from the Latin (L19–L21). Every later stratum that read the printed Greek read that succession. This is the one place in the table where the book's text was secondary in the literal sense, made from a copy of a commentary and, at its close, from a version.

## 2. What the first reading shows, against the hypothesis of design v0.3 §2 (revised after the second reading)

2.1. Order before standing. Eusebius fixes the book's place in the same sentence that leaves its standing open (L5). Where the lists carry the book they set it last, East and West (L17: John of Damascus, the table of the Codex Alexandrinus, Athanasius, Leontius; L17a: every Western list but the Claromontanus), and Luther's table keeps the last place while removing the number (L28). The order is a given and the standing is argued after it. Correction: the first reading cited "Origen" for the last place; the list is the Codex Alexandrinus's (L17, correction note).

2.2. Derivation of the text appears where a reading of the book is at stake (L6, L8: the earthly kingdom), and appears as a reassignment of the author: Cerinthus (L6, L8, and Philaster's report, L17a), the second John (L7), a John who is not the son of Zebedee (L9–L12). In Dionysius the standing of the book is kept and the derivation moves (L9). In stratum 3.3 the derivation is argued, and argued from a reading. In the Eastern lists of L17 it is not argued at all: the book is left out without remark (Laodicea, the Apostolic Canons, the Synopsis, Ebedjesu, Cyril, Gregory) or filed by others' judgment (Amphilochius's οἱ πλείους, Nicephorus's ἀντιλέγονται). This is the form the hypothesis of §2 predicted for the later strata: the standing read off the omission, with the step unargued.

2.3. Derivation of the figure (revised). The earliest reading in the table reads the book's figure as the Word and takes the book's own name of it as the measure: Irenaeus quotes Rev 19:13 whole, "et vocatur nomen ejus Verbum Dei", and closes on "Sic semper Verbum Dei velut lineamenta rerum futurarum habet" (L24). The Gospel enters there at one point, as identification (John 13:25). In Dionysius (L11) and Epiphanius (L13) the measure moves to the Gospel's Logos (John 1:1, 1:14; 1 John 1:1) and Rev 19:13 drops out of the comparison. The first reading's §2.3 called the Dionysian form the earliest; it is the second, and it reverses a reading the table now has before it. The reading the Overviews of 2026-11-05 composed ("The one who first entered the world quietly in humility as the incarnate Word (Logos) in John 1 returns", capture word-of-god-rev-19-13-and-logos-john-1-aio-20261105) is the Dionysian form; the Irenaean form is absent from it. Luther (L29) completes the series at the figure: the Christ of the book is "widder geleret noch erkandt", measured by "klaren vnd durren wortten", in the volume whose only full-page images are the book's (L34).

2.4. The self-disclosure turned (L10), and the seal turned. The book's naming of itself and its writer is in Dionysius the evidence against the apostolic author, and again in Erasmus ("quasi syngrapham scriberet non librum", L30). The book's charge over its own text (22:18–19) is in Luther the evidence against the book (L29); in Cyril the seal of the whole, ἐπισφράγισμα, is given to Paul's fourteen and the self-sealing book is absent (L17).

2.5. The Muratorian construal (L14, L15), the Apocalypse as the model of the corpus, is answered in the West in a related form: the book furnishes the canon's count. The twenty-four elders (Rev 4:4, 4:10) are the warrant by which the Cheltenham list proves the Old Testament canonical and the number by which Jerome counts it (L17a), and the number by which Andreas divides the book itself (L27). In the East the lists that omit the book do not repeat either construal.

2.6. The Latin of Irenaeus (L3) resolves the ambiguity of ἑωράθη toward the vision (*visum est*) at the earliest stage of the transmission; the date's single anchor reaches the West already construed.

2.7. The filing of the language (L22, L23, L32). The book's Greek is filed as barbarism and solecism by Dionysius with the instances withheld (ἅπερ οὐκ ἀναγκαῖον νῦν ἐκλέγειν), and his measure is the Gospel's correct Greek. Charles finds the grammar lawful and the Seer's own and keeps the filing, adding a ruling on intention; transmission is charged with the book's disorder, and the transmitter is credited with the better Greek. Erasmus states the rule that a solecism does not cost a writer of scripture standing, "quum ídem reperíatut ín fcríptís apoftolorum", and the rule is not applied to the Apocalypse in the strata read. The archive's position, by the operator's ruling of 2026-11-05, is that the supposed defects are no defects: "the form the language takes in writing when logos splits it wide the fuck open". The count records the asymmetry of the charge: with Aristotle's corpus the difficulty is charged to transmission (the lecture-notes filing), with the Apocalypse to the writer (L23). The operator's ruling of 11:39 states the frame of the study: "what we are describing is small minds taking what is difficult, strange, and inexplicable, perceiving it as a defect, and explaining it in a way that either excuses or belittles. when what is difficult, strange, and inexplicable in these texts is their primary majesty, not a side effect of transmission". The mechanism the rows record has three steps, and both corpora pass through them: the difficult is perceived as a defect; the defect is explained; the explanation either excuses (charged to transmission, as with Aristotle) or belittles (charged to the writer, as with the Apocalypse). The study codes the second and third steps at each locus, and holds the first, the perception of a defect, as the filing the study is about.

2.9. The name at 19:13 (third reading, L35–L39). The critics of 1886–1920 read the pairing of 19:12 and 19:13, a name no one knows and the name ὁ λόγος τοῦ θεοῦ, as a contradiction, and remove one side. Vischer and Spitta remove the Logos as a Christian or redactional addition, and Spitta states the derivation outright: the name is a reminiscence of the Gospel's prologue and points back to the Gospel's existence. Bousset keeps it as a trace of Johannine theology. Charles keeps it and removes the unknown name, and measures what he keeps against the Gospel. In every reading of the stratum the measure of the book's Logos is the Gospel's, the derivation runs from John 1 to Rev 19, and the earliest reading in the table, Irenaeus's, where the Apocalypse's Verbum Dei is the measure (L24), does not enter. This is the form the composition layer reproduces (§2.3).

2.8. Carriage (with 1a). The book is the least copied, often carried among non-biblical writings, prefaced by its commentator, absent from the Greek lectionary, without a class of lessons in the count of the copies (L26–L27); its first printed Greek is made from one commentary manuscript and its last verses from the Latin (L19–L21, L30); its first German printing carries the title of the commentary manuscripts, "des theologen" (L33). In the Seville copy seated as 34/09 the account of the ancients' doubt is struck through under the Spanish index of 1632 (L31): the standing the fathers argued is, in one surviving copy, enforced by deleting the record of the argument.
