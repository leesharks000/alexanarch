## 1a. What carriage adds (first reading)

The count of carriage (design v0.4 §2.5) records a derivation of the text that no reader argued: the book's first printed Greek text descends from a single commentary manuscript, and its final verses from the Latin (L19–L21). Every later stratum that read the printed Greek read that succession. This is the one place in the table where the book's text was secondary in the literal sense, made from a copy of a commentary and, at its close, from a version.

## 2. What the first reading shows, against the hypothesis of design v0.3 §2

2.1. Order before standing. Eusebius fixes the book's place in the same sentence that leaves its standing open (L5). The lists that carry it set it last (L17, Origen). The order is a given and the standing is argued after it.

2.2. Derivation of the text appears where a reading of the book is at stake (L6, L8: the earthly kingdom), and appears as a reassignment of the author: Cerinthus (L6, L8), the second John (L7), a John who is not the son of Zebedee (L9–L12). In Dionysius the standing of the book is kept and the derivation moves (L9). The hypothesis of §2 (derivation read off order and standing) needs one refinement here: in stratum 3.3 the derivation is argued, and argued from a reading; it is the later strata that the table has yet to read for derivation read off order without the step argued.

2.3. Derivation of the figure appears first in Dionysius (L11) and Epiphanius (L13), and in both the measure of the Logos is the Gospel's (John 1:1, 1:14; 1 John 1:1). Rev 19:13 does not enter either comparison. This is the earliest form of the reading the Overviews of 2026-11-05 composed: "The one who first entered the world quietly in humility as the incarnate Word (Logos) in John 1 returns" (capture word-of-god-rev-19-13-and-logos-john-1-aio-20261105).

2.4. The self-disclosure turned (L10). The book's naming of itself and its writer, which design §0.1 sets as its claim to primacy, is in Dionysius the evidence against its apostolic author.

2.5. The Muratorian construal (L14, L15) is the opposite of 2.3: there the Apocalypse is the model and Paul follows. In the strata read so far nothing answers it; Eusebius, Dionysius and Epiphanius do not cite the fragment, and the catalogues of L17 do not repeat its order. Whether any later list or father repeats or answers it is the open question of the next reading.

2.6. The Latin of Irenaeus (L3) resolves the ambiguity of ἑωράθη toward the vision (*visum est*) at the earliest stage of the transmission; the date's single anchor reaches the West already construed.
