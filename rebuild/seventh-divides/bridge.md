
# Part II. In the midst of the seven: the stations

The first three parts have three tasks. Part I establishes how the divine pattern is named, impressed, embodied and transmitted through a human bearer. Part II examines how a related architecture differentiates seven stations, judges them, corrects them, confirms them, or, in the Mandaean books, replaces their occupants. Part III tests whether the architecture also authorizes further writing. The join between Parts I and II is in Philo's own treatise. The Logos that names and re-strikes in De mutatione is, in Quis rerum divinarum heres, the divider of all things (Her. 130), the seventh in the middle of the lampstand (Her. 215–219), whose place among the planets is held by the sun (Her. 222–224), the seventh that divides the soul (Her. 225), and the voice that sounds through the displaced prophet (Her. 259–265). Philo's word for the place is the middle: the seventh branch "αὐτὴ μέση" (Her. 216), the sun "ὁ μέσος τῶν ἑπτά" (Her. 222); the Apocalypse's is the same, "ἐν μέσῳ τῶν λυχνιῶν" (1:13), "ἐν μέσῳ τῶν ἑπτὰ λυχνιῶν" (2:1). Part I follows the Logos into one life. Part II follows it into the midst of seven stations.
