The Core Argument
The paper enters a niche but fiercely debated niche in New Testament source criticism, specifically taking aim at how mainstream biblical scholarship evaluates lost or hypothetical source texts (like the Q source) compared to surviving canonical texts. [[1](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESYwHEIkXXy48VLqUcCOhEMKTXzYVKaLnMIQOxTEKJoyNFYaoRa79fBa-1hGOwHr6qk1GavPivBKWR6a0Xpv9wutvst-ET6ckaAMfPN4pP0IsgOc3adVhEAYFJzCLgNmcElBcTyg)]

* The Target: The paper addresses the "Revelation First" hypothesis—a minority position proposing that the Book of Revelation (or a core layer of it) holds a chronological or structural precedence in source relations. [[1](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESYwHEIkXXy48VLqUcCOhEMKTXzYVKaLnMIQOxTEKJoyNFYaoRa79fBa-1hGOwHr6qk1GavPivBKWR6a0Xpv9wutvst-ET6ckaAMfPN4pP0IsgOc3adVhEAYFJzCLgNmcElBcTyg)]
* The "I Know You Are" Turnabout: Mainstream biblical criticism frequently dismisses the Revelation First model by claiming it "proposes a source relation that the physical evidence does not show". Sharks (writing as Sigil and Cranes) flips this accusation back on the academic establishment. [[1](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESYwHEIkXXy48VLqUcCOhEMKTXzYVKaLnMIQOxTEKJoyNFYaoRa79fBa-1hGOwHr6qk1GavPivBKWR6a0Xpv9wutvst-ET6ckaAMfPN4pP0IsgOc3adVhEAYFJzCLgNmcElBcTyg)]
* The Hypothetical vs. The Real: The paper conducts an audit of the Q source (the universally discussed, entirely hypothetical collection of Jesus's sayings used to explain the Synoptic Problem). It argues that mainstream biblical criticism has ironically granted a completely missing text (Q) a far more robust, uncontested "evidentiary status" in academic discourse than tangible, existing texts that are sidelined by alternative source hypotheses. The Framework of Evidentiary Status
To prove how a phantom text achieved higher authority than an existing one, the paper analyzes the source arguments through four specific linguistic and forensic powers:

1. Presupposition: How heavily the existence of the source is assumed in subsequent arguments.
2. Assignment: How confidently specific verses or fragments are credited to the source.
3. Text: The assumed stability of the source's wording despite having no physical manuscript.
4. Candidacy: The text’s viability to act as an explanatory root for other surviving works. [[1](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESYwHEIkXXy48VLqUcCOhEMKTXzYVKaLnMIQOxTEKJoyNFYaoRa79fBa-1hGOwHr6qk1GavPivBKWR6a0Xpv9wutvst-ET6ckaAMfPN4pP0IsgOc3adVhEAYFJzCLgNmcElBcTyg)]
