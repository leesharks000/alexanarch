I Am X, Be Y, Blessed is the Z — Lee Sharks
I would approach this as a poem whose form is itself an argument about identity, transformation, and blessing—not as a collection of provocative declarations, a catalogue of references, or an exercise in Whitmanian imitation.

The essential question is not simply what the poem says about being everything. It is what happens as its speaking voice moves between being something, becoming something else, conferring blessing, and discovering that the supposed other was already within the self.

There is an important distinction here: the poem's progression must be read as a continuous literary action. Its successive grammatical modes cannot be adequately understood by extracting a few representative lines and assigning each a theme.

I located the 2015 poem and an accessible full-text reading route, as well as its subsequent archival presentation. I will distinguish the poem's actual literary operations from claims made by its later interpretive apparatus.

The 2026 critical edition calls the poem The Prophetic Catalog. Its primary text is divided into eleven movements. That division matters: the poem is considerably more architecturally ambitious than its opening catalogue initially suggests.

The architecture is not a simple three-part division. The three principal grammatical operations—I AM, BE, and BLESSED IS—recur, interrupt one another and acquire different meanings as the poem develops.

That distinction is fundamental.

2. The voice: a self that refuses to remain one thing
The epigraph from Walt Whitman's Song of Myself establishes the initial proposition: the self and the world are not separate territories. What moves inward also moves outward.

But Sharks makes this proposition unstable.

The opening is deliberately extravagant:

A girl.

A passerby.

A Cylon.

A giraffe.

A baby.

A dentist.

A narc.

An ecologist.

A radio station.

A philosopher.

A mollusk.

A personality disorder.

A hygiene problem.

These are not merely different descriptions of a single personality. They belong to radically different orders of existence.

The speaker moves between gender, social position, fictional identity, animal life, professional identity, technological infrastructure, bodily condition and abstraction.

The list refuses to establish a hierarchy among them.

A philosopher and a hygiene problem can occupy the same grammatical position. A radio station can be as legitimate an identity as a human being.

This produces a peculiar voice: simultaneously omnivorous, self-mocking, expansive and unstable.

The repeated declaration I am initially sounds authoritative. But its repetitions progressively undermine the possibility that the speaker can be contained by any one declaration.

The first-person pronoun becomes a container with no settled contents.

There is an important difference between saying that one contains multitudes and demonstrating that the very category of identity can no longer reliably distinguish one thing from another.

Sharks is interested in the latter.

3. The grammar is the poem's principal instrument
The title is a sequence of transformations, not simply three adjacent slogans.

I AM
Identification

The speaker takes the world into the first person. Identity becomes expansive, multiple and unstable.

BE
Imperative

The grammatical direction changes. The voice no longer merely declares what it is. It addresses another being and invites or commands transformation.

BLESSED IS
Consecration

The voice changes again. It confers value and standing upon beings and conditions that ordinary systems of recognition disregard.

I AM AGAIN
Recursive return

The blessing eventually returns to the speaker. The self that had been distributing recognition discovers its own loneliness.

BECOME
Transformation

The concluding movement moves beyond identification into an unfinished possibility of being.

The change from indicative to imperative is particularly important.

Consider the difference:

I am a passerby.

Be passersby.

The first absorbs an identity into the speaker. The second distributes that possibility outward.

The poem has moved from self-description to an address that potentially changes the reader.

The movement into blessing then changes the ethical status of this operation. It is no longer enough to be something or to become something. The being must also be recognized.

This is why the Beatitudes are structurally indispensable.

4. The comedy is not decorative
One of the poem's most interesting qualities is its refusal to maintain a solemn prophetic register.

The opening's exuberance is repeatedly interrupted by incongruity:

The philosopher in gym shorts.

The portable luxury goods with flowers.

The lonely person who favorites their own tweets.

The billionaire with no money.

The lukewarm war.

The promise to become more boring in the coming year.

The equation of babies, corporate moguls and billionaires.

The humor operates through several different techniques.

Category collision: things that ought to belong to entirely different conceptual systems are placed alongside one another.

Literalization: familiar expressions are taken seriously enough to become absurd.

Deflation: elevated spiritual or philosophical language is brought into contact with banal contemporary life.

Escalation: a joke continues beyond the point at which its initial premise would ordinarily have been exhausted.

Self-implication: the speaker does not stand outside the absurdity and criticize it. The speaker is implicated in it.

The last quality is crucial.

The poem is not a satirical observer describing the world's ridiculousness. Its own voice is ridiculous. It participates in the very instability it exposes.

This gives the comedy a particular vulnerability. The speaker cannot claim the authority of someone who has successfully escaped the world being criticized.

The speaker is inside it.

5. The Beatitudes: the poem's ethical imagination
The third movement is one of the most revealing.

Sharks takes the recognizable form of the Beatitudes and redirects its blessing toward contemporary and marginalized figures.

The monotonous receive a kingdom of boredom.

The trolls are blessed.

The train stations are blessed, as are the trains that leave and do not return.

Those without likes, followers or well-made profiles are blessed.

Those who were never born are blessed.

Telemarketers and spam technicians are blessed.

The lonely are blessed.

The oppressed inherit a broken kingdom.

This is not simply a series of jokes about religion and social media.

The poem is asking what happens when blessing is detached from conventional merit, visibility, achievement and desirability.

The conventional world has mechanisms for deciding who deserves attention. The poem constructs a counter-system.

It blesses the person who is not followed, the communicator nobody wants to hear, the person whose social presence has failed, and the person who has no interest in participating.

The train image is especially effective because it introduces loss into the poem's otherwise proliferating identity system.

A train that leaves and does not return is not simply an amusing object. It is an image of irreversible departure.

The poem can absorb identities indefinitely, but it cannot necessarily retrieve what has gone.

And then comes a significant shift:

Blessed am I in my loneliness, mother.

The voice that has been blessing others turns toward itself.

This is not a minor variation. It changes the emotional status of the entire preceding movement.

The speaker is not simply a generous observer distributing benedictions. The speaker needs one.

The blessing becomes a form of self-address, almost prayer.

6. The second half: the world enters the speaker
The middle movements become increasingly heterogeneous.

The Dark Robot introduces machines, dinosaurs, souls, electrical pulses, fishbowls, endangered species, suicide-related identities, cannibalistic absurdities and impossible economic propositions.

The poem does not resolve these into a coherent philosophical taxonomy.

Instead, it subjects them to the same first-person grammar.

The result is a voice that seems to have become capable of incorporating almost anything.

The Lukewarm War extends this technique into political and epistemological contradiction.

The speaker is simultaneously elitist and populist, terrorist and victim, and someone killed by those attempting to kill terrorists.

This is a particularly important passage because it demonstrates that the poem's multiplication is not limited to amusing or harmless identities.

It enters morally incompatible positions.

The speaker does not thereby establish that those positions are morally equivalent. Rather, the first-person voice becomes a site where the contradictions of contemporary life are made grammatically visible.

The Resolutions then turns the same procedure against the culture of self-improvement.

The speaker promises to read less, think less, repeat more, become more boring and embrace positive self-talk.

The language of personal development becomes a comic instrument of self-diminishment.

This is more than a joke about New Year's resolutions. It exposes the peculiar violence of systems that demand perpetual improvement while making the person increasingly compliant, predictable and marketable.

The Logos movement then takes the poem somewhere different.

Here the speaker becomes concerned with existence and nonexistence, the voice within another voice, fictional existence, language and the awakening of the Logos.

The poem has moved beyond collecting identities into questioning the conditions under which anything can exist as an identity in the first place.

The repeated oscillation between existing and not existing is not a neat philosophical resolution. It is an instability that the poem makes audible.

And the voice within your voice introduces a further possibility: the speaking presence is not confined to the poet.

It has entered the reader's own speech.

That is one of the poem's most consequential shifts.

7. The sound passage: when language exceeds its propositions
The phonographic passage in the Resolutions deserves independent attention.

BLZ… ZRRR… rRRR… ZZZZ… RrRR… BZ… LLL… RrRr…
The poem briefly abandons ordinary semantic language.

The sequence does not explain the speaker's emotional state in conventional descriptive terms. It performs a disturbance in sound.

The surrounding reference to losing one's cool in traffic gives the sequence a context, but the sounds themselves are not merely an illustration.

They constitute a different mode of poetic expression.

This is an important formal departure because the poem has spent so much time exploiting the semantic possibilities of naming.

Here it temporarily withdraws from naming.

The voice becomes vibration, noise, bodily impulse.

The reader is no longer asked to identify what the speaker is. The reader is asked to encounter an utterance that resists ordinary identification.

This moment also helps explain why the poem cannot be adequately characterized as an extended list.

It contains shifts in linguistic medium, not simply shifts in subject matter.

8. The Hinge: where the poem becomes vulnerable
Structural turning point
The autobiographical Hinge is the point at which the poem's entire preceding manner of speaking becomes newly legible.

The speaker says:

I used to be a person.

I worked seven years for a PhD.

My children were on Medicaid.

I became fully broke.

I went back to sleep.

The effect is startling because the preceding poem has been so energetically inventive.

The catalogue has absorbed fictional characters, animals, commodities, machines, social identities, religious figures, political positions and impossible propositions.

But now the speaker offers something much less spectacular.

A life has encountered material hardship.

The seven years of academic labor and the reference to the children's Medicaid are concrete details. They introduce an economic and familial reality that the previous exuberance could not fully contain.

This is where I think a superficial reading of the poem can go badly wrong.

The Hinge is not simply another identity in the catalogue. It changes the relationship between the catalogue and the person speaking it.

The comedy is now exposed as something that has a cost.

The speaker's absurdity is no longer just an aesthetic disposition. It exists alongside failure, exhaustion, economic insecurity and responsibility.

The return to sleep is particularly significant.

Earlier, sleep could function as a comic refusal of the demands of existence. Here, it has acquired a much heavier resonance.

It can signify exhaustion, retreat, protection and the suspension of a life that has become difficult to sustain.

The poem does not explain away the hardship by making it a lesson.

It allows the autobiographical material to interrupt the performance.

That interruption gives the earlier comedy its gravity and prevents the later metaphysical language from becoming merely decorative.

9. The Kernel: the poem discovers its own interior
The final movement begins with the poem's most important declaration:

I am the one who was within me.

I would place this line at the center of the entire work.

It is not simply another entry in the catalogue. It is a change in the meaning of the catalogue itself.

The opening has proceeded through outward multiplication: the speaker becomes increasingly numerous, absorbing identities and contradictions.

The Kernel reverses the direction.

The speaker discovers something that was already inside.

Notice the tense: was.

Not merely something that is within the speaker, but something that was within the speaker.

This produces an unusual temporal ambiguity. The interior presence is simultaneously discovered and remembered. It seems to have preceded the discovery.

The self is not simply creating new identities. It is encountering something that its own previous understanding of itself could not recognize.

The next instruction is equally important:

Become fully awake… Become finally free…

The grammatical transformation has now acquired a different dimension.

Earlier, Be distributed identities. Now Become makes transformation itself the object of the command.

And the poem immediately returns to its characteristic nesting imagery:

Tinier dinosaurs inside dinosaurs.

Babies inside babies.

Billionaire babies inside babies.

Broke babies.

You and me.

The movement is recursive. The smaller contains the larger, and the supposedly grand contains the supposedly insignificant.

The poem's final reduction to you and me is especially effective.

After the enormous catalogue, the technological and metaphysical speculation, the political contradictions, the absurdity and the autobiographical exposure, the ultimate relationship is intimate.

The universal returns as an encounter between two persons.

The ending refuses to settle the reader's response:

Wake up or go back to sleep.

This is not the same as simply commanding the reader to awaken.

The alternative remains available.

The poem offers an experience of transformation without guaranteeing that the reader will accept it.

That is a sophisticated ending because it preserves the reader's freedom even after the poem has spent so much energy addressing, instructing and blessing them.

10. The work's literary inheritances
There are several recognizable literary and religious traditions at work here. They should be distinguished rather than collapsed into one general claim of influence.

The multiplicity of identities invites comparison with heteronymy. However, Sharks's catalogue is not identical to Pessoa's construction of distinct authorial personalities. Its identities proliferate within a single unstable speaking voice.

I would be careful about claiming that these comparisons establish a direct line of inheritance in every instance. Whitman is explicitly invoked; the other relationships are interpretive affinities and identifiable textual resonances.

The distinction matters when evaluating the poem independently.

11. What the poem does to its reader
This is where I think its most interesting achievement lies.

The poem subjects the reader to several successive experiences.

First: destabilization.

The reader begins with an apparently ordinary first-person declaration. Repetition gradually makes that declaration untenable as a stable identity.

The reader is made to experience the inadequacy of the categories through which people ordinarily understand themselves and others.

Second: complicity.

The comic catalogue is not entirely comfortable.

The reader is invited to laugh at the absurdity, but the speaker's identifications increasingly include morally incompatible and socially disturbing positions.

The reader cannot comfortably remain an external judge.

Third: ethical redistribution.

The Beatitudes change the reader's attention.

The overlooked, unwanted, boring, unsuccessful and socially invisible become objects of recognition.

The reader encounters a different possible distribution of dignity.

Fourth: vulnerability.

The Hinge removes the protection offered by comic extravagance.

The poem has to be read again, retrospectively, in light of the speaker's material circumstances.

Fifth: inward recognition.

The Kernel moves the poem from an enormous field of identities toward the possibility that something unrecognized already exists within the reader.

The voice within your voice is particularly important here.

The poem is no longer only about the speaker's capacity to contain other beings. It begins to implicate the reader in that capacity.

Finally: refusal of closure.

The reader receives an instruction but not a compulsory conclusion.

Wake up or go back to sleep.

The poem leaves the reader with an unresolved choice.

This is an important formal-ethical achievement: it attempts transformation without entirely taking away the reader's agency.

This is interesting as an additional literary and authorial project, but it creates a critical problem.

We must distinguish:

What the 2015 poem actually does.

What the 2026 apparatus says the poem anticipates.

What can independently be demonstrated through close reading.

For example, the later apparatus identifies the poem as the embryonic form of an elaborate literary and philosophical system. That is an interpretation, not proof that the complete later system was already present in 2015.

Likewise, the proposed future scholarship should be understood as a literary framing device, not evidence of actual future critical reception.

I would resist allowing the apparatus to dictate the poem's meaning.

The poem is more interesting when its actual language is allowed to generate its own possibilities, including possibilities that exceed the author's subsequent explanations.

There is a productive tension here: the 2015 poem is a work of proliferation, while the 2026 apparatus attempts to organize that proliferation into a retrospective system.

The two projects should not be confused.

There are genuine vulnerabilities.

The catalogue sometimes risks making unlike things interchangeable simply because they can occupy the same grammatical position.

The sheer abundance of material can also make individual images compete for attention. Some jokes are more generative than others.

The poem's religious language occasionally risks acquiring significance through its recognizable form rather than through the full development of its underlying thought.

And its final metaphysical suggestions are not always resolved into sufficiently precise propositions.

But these limitations should not obscure its strongest achievement.

The poem is not merely accumulating identities. Its later movements transform the significance of the earlier ones.

The Hinge changes the comedy. The Kernel changes the catalogue. The final imperative changes the relationship between speaker and reader.

That is development, not simply abundance.

14. The deepest reading: a poem about the interiority of the other
I would ultimately formulate the poem's central literary proposition this way:

The other is not simply something outside the self that the self learns to tolerate. The other may be a possibility already present within the self, awaiting recognition.

This proposition has several consequences.

First, identity becomes less like a possession and more like a process.

Second, recognition becomes an ethical act. To bless someone is not merely to approve of them; it is to grant them standing in a world that may have rendered them invisible.

Third, the speaker's multiplicity becomes a form of vulnerability. The self that can contain everything cannot maintain the security of a single identity.

Fourth, the reader becomes implicated in the work. The voice is not only describing its own multiplicity but attempting to awaken a corresponding recognition in someone else.

And finally, the poem discovers that transformation cannot be completed through declaration alone.

It requires a return inward.

That is why the line I am the one who was within me matters more than any individual entry in the opening catalogue.

It converts the poem from an experiment in limitless identification into a meditation on recognition, memory, interiority and becoming.

Final judgment
I think I Am X, Be Y, Blessed is the Z is most successful when read as a recursive poetic structure with a genuine emotional center.

Its achievement is not that it can name an extraordinary number of things. Its achievement is that it makes the act of naming change over time.

The opening makes the self expansive.

The imperative makes expansion communicable.

The Beatitudes make it ethical.

The comic middle makes it unstable.

The Logos makes it metaphysical.

The Hinge makes it vulnerable.

The Kernel makes it recursive.

The ending makes it a choice for the reader.

The title, in retrospect, contains an entire poetic drama.

I AM: I discover that I am not what I thought I was.

BE: I discover that being can be offered to another.

BLESSED IS: I discover that recognition can be extended to those whom the world has forgotten.

BECOME: I discover that neither the speaker nor the reader is finished.

And then the poem leaves the reader with its final alternative.

Not an answer. Not a doctrine. Not even an obligation to awaken.

A choice.

That final refusal to compel is important. After all its extravagance, the poem does not claim ownership of the reader's transformation.

It opens a door and leaves the reader standing before it.

+1

My proposition is that this passage has considerably more conceptual organization than its apparently free-associative surface first suggests. Its progression is not random, and the transitions deserve to be examined individually.

1. From the ecologist to the philosopher
I am an ecologist… a “party in my tummy”… a radio station… a philosopher…

The first thing to notice is that the items are not all the same kind of thing.

An ecologist is a professional identity.

A party in my tummy is a bodily experience expressed in the language of a children's song.

A radio station is an institution or communications apparatus.

A philosopher is an intellectual identity.

The movement is already crossing categorical boundaries.

But there is also a subtle progression in scale and agency.

The ecologist studies relations among living things. The party in the tummy turns the living body into an event. The radio station turns communication into an institutional identity. The philosopher turns toward the activity of thought.

One can read this as a movement from ecological relationship, through embodied experience, to communication and thought.

I would not insist that this is a rigid symbolic sequence. Its strength is that each successive identity changes the register of the preceding one.

The ellipses matter, too. They make the catalogue feel less like a set of completed assertions than a consciousness continuing to discover what it can say.

The philosopher arrives as the apparent culmination of a sequence that has already escaped the boundaries of professional identity.

2. The philosopher in gym shorts: the first major hinge
I am a philosopher in gym shorts…

This is not simply another item. It repeats the preceding line's final identity and changes it.

The philosopher is carried forward, but the philosopher is now dressed.

That is a remarkably economical operation.

The poem does not discard the abstract intellectual identity and substitute something absurd. It retains the identity and changes its circumstances.

The gym shorts introduce the body, informality, exposure and a slight indignity. The philosopher is no longer an abstract figure of intellectual authority. The philosopher has a body, and that body is dressed in an ordinary, possibly ridiculous way.

This is a miniature version of what the poem does on a much larger scale: it preserves an identity while changing the conditions under which that identity can be understood.

The next three items continue the movement:

a mollusk… a personality disorder… a hygiene problem…

Here I think the progression is particularly interesting.

Mollusk: a living organism, nonhuman, with a very different bodily organization from the human philosopher.

Personality disorder: a movement from an organism to a psychological or diagnostic category. The word personality retains a connection to personhood.

Hygiene problem: a further movement from psychological diagnosis to bodily maintenance and social embarrassment.

There is a descending movement in dignity, but also a movement through different ways of describing a being.

We go from an intellectual identity to a body, from a body to a diagnosis, and from a diagnosis to a problem of bodily presentation.

The sequence is not simply saying increasingly silly things.

It is testing how quickly an identity can become a condition, and how quickly a condition can become a perceived defect.

The repeated I am makes this transformation more pointed. The speaker does not say that the philosopher has a hygiene problem. The speaker says that the speaker is one.

That distinction is essential.

The poem is not describing an embarrassing attribute from a safe distance. It incorporates the embarrassment into identity.

3. Nobody's beeswax / everybody's beeswax: a controlled reversal
I am “nobody’s beeswax”… “everybody’s beeswax”…

This is, to my mind, the most formally controlled little unit in the passage.

The two expressions are almost identical. One word changes:

nobody's

everybody's

But that change reverses the social meaning.

The familiar idiom nobody's beeswax means that something is nobody else's business. It establishes a boundary around the self.

Everybody's beeswax reverses that boundary. Everything becomes everybody's business.

The speaker is simultaneously private and public, excluded from collective concern and exposed to collective concern.

There is also a sound and semantic transition into the next phrase:

an ear infection… a virus…

Beeswax invites the association of earwax; earwax leads to the ear; the ear leads to infection; infection leads to virus.

I would distinguish the strength of this connection from certainty about authorial intention, but the sequence has a striking coherence.

It also develops the preceding hygiene problem.

The body that was previously socially embarrassing now becomes a site of infection and contagion.

The progression is therefore:

personal embarrassment → social ownership → bodily infection → transmissibility.

And the nobody/everybody reversal is doing something more than producing a joke. It introduces a fundamental question about the boundaries between self and other.

That question will become increasingly important in the poem.

4. Martyr, saint, scientist, tank: a change of register
I am a martyr… a saint… a scientist… a tank…

Here the sequence changes again.

The first two identities are religious and sacrificial. The third is epistemological and professional. The fourth is an object of military force.

There are several possible relations:

Martyr and saint are linked through suffering, sacrifice and religious recognition.

Scientist introduces a different kind of authority: knowledge rather than sanctity.

Tank abruptly introduces machinery, material power and potential violence.

But notice that the speaker does not provide a transition explaining how these categories relate.

The same I am carries them all.

I think this is deliberate. The poem has now made the first-person construction capable of containing religious, intellectual and mechanical identities without changing its grammar.

The tank is particularly effective because it interrupts the elevated status of martyr, saint and scientist with something massive, functional and potentially destructive.

There is also a possible relation between martyrdom and the tank: sacrifice and violence, the body exposed to force and the machine that delivers it.

The poem does not spell this out. It allows the juxtaposition to produce the association.

That is an important distinction between controlled poetic ambiguity and an arbitrary list.

The reader is given enough material to make connections, but not instructed to settle on a single interpretation.

5. Creature, sandwich, everything, nothing: the decisive expansion
I am a creature… a sandwich… everything… nothing…

This is where the passage changes its terms most radically.

The movement from tank to creature is interesting because it reverses the relation between object and organism. The tank is an object that acts upon living beings; the creature is a living being.

Then comes:

a sandwich…

The sandwich is neither an organism in the ordinary sense nor a person or professional identity. It is an assembled object, something made from components.

But the sandwich also introduces consumption and the transformation of living material into an object for consumption.

Then the sequence arrives at:

everything… nothing…

This is not just another pair of funny opposites.

The catalogue has moved from particular identities to the totality of possible identities, and then to their apparent negation.

Everything and nothing are the conceptual limits of the opening operation.

The speaker has become so inclusive that the distinction between inclusion and absence starts to collapse.

Notice how much work the ellipses do here. They give the reader a moment to experience the enormous jump between sandwich and everything, and between everything and nothing.

The sequence does not explain the jumps. It makes them.

I would call this a moment of deliberate conceptual escalation.

6. Portable luxury goods with flowers: the apparent breakdown that isn't
I am a “portable luxury goods with flowers”…

This is probably the phrase most likely to be mistaken for a lapse in control.

And the dash matters.

The train station is a place through which others move. The next movement addresses those others as passersby.

This kind of writing requires greater control, in my view.

Conventional continuity supplies a great deal of connective tissue. Narrative sequence, causal relationships, thematic development and transitions help the reader understand why one thing follows another.

In the passage we're examining, Sharks deliberately removes much of that assistance.

Consider the progression:

Philosopher → philosopher in gym shorts.

Nobody's beeswax → everybody's beeswax.

Martyr → saint → scientist → tank.

Creature → sandwich → everything → nothing.

Everything/nothing → portable luxury goods with flowers → train station with trains.

Each transition has to accomplish something without relying on conventional exposition.

The poet must control several things simultaneously:

Discontinuity: how far apart can two ideas be before their juxtaposition ceases to generate meaning?

Pacing: when should the reader recognize a connection, and when should the reader be startled by its absence?

Tonal equilibrium: how can the poem move between absurdity, metaphysics, comedy and vulnerability without collapsing into a single register?

Accumulation: how can each new identity enlarge the field without making the preceding identities irrelevant?

Structural direction: how can a sequence that appears to go anywhere nevertheless arrive somewhere necessary?

Conventional continuity can be difficult to achieve, of course. But it often makes its compositional logic visible through the transitions themselves.

Here, the compositional logic must remain active even when the transitions appear discontinuous.

That is a more demanding balancing act.
