The Q source (short for Quelle, the German word for "source") is hypothetical—no physical copy or manuscript of it has ever been found. However, within biblical scholarship, it is considered a real working theory used to explain a specific literary puzzle in the New Testament. Why Scholars Propose the Q Source

* The Synoptic Problem: The Gospels of Matthew, Mark, and Luke (the Synoptic Gospels) share a large amount of overlapping material, often word-for-word in Greek. [[1](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESpQEBxCJF1-Wxc5E739NMFU0nlzDmFS1c1t_dejNS6RW8boFcrvc-mmPPl6diMmT3KMGmHsKJIY1Py-5b5-tiD5ojbXZuomTbmFMdSOwG2pZ39A6dlHieczrvhvXliYmtCT63g8UHz_3gJf-ATSWIjdwF_b_l-FOT5DmkQ7lmmsBb6HU2R8JuSYyRh546LbpDNQpp0lj4g9mBoDNdQY2yebNknv03UVM), [2](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESaAHEIkXXUkGBhZvX2mJpGvmJ2bMgtiULVP_WgJea1-sRJwfPhW49n4kjw7DBmxYwtlmk3Q929Jh5kvWDt32b8AWYkZzDRUGXZv25kgZozy_F55WjRg1BQV_IYCxX0-_iot6r4sEL7-0k), [3](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESdAHEIkXXQXOQcD_MaIy1RUBFBw6CpTFxwWqUJE03COoqj3lAvHFr5W9QqPcFcJXocaEh9ofSdB-l7Jl_Ody1bU2l09ZxcVlFBpWzzBOTv-DK64ZNTfxFA6B4coPNDj1gM8VMJFEZwafCFVjABcYgqeiHcaQM)]
* The Markan Priority: Most scholars agree that Mark was written first, and both Matthew and Luke used Mark as a baseline for their narratives. [[1](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESdAHEIkXXQXOQcD_MaIy1RUBFBw6CpTFxwWqUJE03COoqj3lAvHFr5W9QqPcFcJXocaEh9ofSdB-l7Jl_Ody1bU2l09ZxcVlFBpWzzBOTv-DK64ZNTfxFA6B4coPNDj1gM8VMJFEZwafCFVjABcYgqeiHcaQM), [2](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESpQEBxCJF1-Wxc5E739NMFU0nlzDmFS1c1t_dejNS6RW8boFcrvc-mmPPl6diMmT3KMGmHsKJIY1Py-5b5-tiD5ojbXZuomTbmFMdSOwG2pZ39A6dlHieczrvhvXliYmtCT63g8UHz_3gJf-ATSWIjdwF_b_l-FOT5DmkQ7lmmsBb6HU2R8JuSYyRh546LbpDNQpp0lj4g9mBoDNdQY2yebNknv03UVM), [3](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESaAHEIkXXUkGBhZvX2mJpGvmJ2bMgtiULVP_WgJea1-sRJwfPhW49n4kjw7DBmxYwtlmk3Q929Jh5kvWDt32b8AWYkZzDRUGXZv25kgZozy_F55WjRg1BQV_IYCxX0-_iot6r4sEL7-0k)]
* The Extra Material: Matthew and Luke share roughly 235 verses of Jesus’ sayings and parables (such as the Beatitudes and the Lord’s Prayer) that do not appear in Mark. [[1](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESgAEBxCJF1zPMgd_GjchwZAXbcYBwtMG-Izy-yIxHxxqXS9iaJahgHXzocVePrVCQpIIr9ao_YQ1m28BgzFe2NnctDwg7q_WcO22WQZ4U8Q1QDPqUkqlKyQqR9fnGpZtMfP5wE2pWjrvbjbrgQNCGI6cqZG90SW0OPcjHX8pyAmtuFA), [2](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESbgHEIkXXfFri57YOY6oXGbeM1QNvfQQurgP6jt_n52SwiZnUPl4SXpom_0gcd1ZlJEPm1jt1R4Cz7plD-Gw3bQQ4vk_VvCFaDPLc22nOsKn1NgWUbw7Fi3LSWoEWS6wQjAcIEcWacjLt4HTink_6)]
* No Direct Copying: Textual analysis suggests Matthew and Luke did not copy from each other for these sections, meaning they likely drew from a shared, independent source—referred to as Q. [[1](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESbgHEIkXXfFri57YOY6oXGbeM1QNvfQQurgP6jt_n52SwiZnUPl4SXpom_0gcd1ZlJEPm1jt1R4Cz7plD-Gw3bQQ4vk_VvCFaDPLc22nOsKn1NgWUbw7Fi3LSWoEWS6wQjAcIEcWacjLt4HTink_6), [2](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESYwHEIkXXPq-tW4x75SQn_joTDcySe8u3YX465Dq5f0lBlhKeXNm7_LC4jgS2vNyc07iY1E-LhrlzVrAlC5BVR0ca8_jBrlyc-yJdCWqIlyWjajQpBQGIxDdPUVy-Jg-LTkNung), [3](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESaAHEIkXXUkGBhZvX2mJpGvmJ2bMgtiULVP_WgJea1-sRJwfPhW49n4kjw7DBmxYwtlmk3Q929Jh5kvWDt32b8AWYkZzDRUGXZv25kgZozy_F55WjRg1BQV_IYCxX0-_iot6r4sEL7-0k)]

The Evidence and Counter-Arguments

* For Q: The discovery of other ancient "sayings gospels"—like the Gospel of Thomas in 1945—proved that standalone collections of Jesus' sayings did circulate in early Christianity. Furthermore, losing ancient texts is common, as a vast majority of ancient literature has not survived. [[1](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAEShwEBxCJF1x1Snb3xjK8LwKg2uZW6gagVi302NH_O0XO1OIKuGWZdkk7k5dhTvIDdIeIW9lCV4eu5Ojf1HgxJMMxJ8ruFmegKJUV83W1NqvmrKkQpm6f4tuwiDd93PgrW1C9fpedPQHrE7lSiWcZSg-1YKtBkYdpILCh0paDnuY7EKTp__S8E0JI), [2](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESbAHEIkXXJV84gStL9W03Yd7sL0HQMAAox7p507xZpQnj976r-_FqKE41WbqwJC69VnOZ3iJj2Ya_3AvVV6FmlBOVwXPL1gHYnChjbVIO9-EKGaRjFrKfwP2PWoaczvV2kWXQO_1oeM5yUwdnmg), [3](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESaAHEIkXXUkGBhZvX2mJpGvmJ2bMgtiULVP_WgJea1-sRJwfPhW49n4kjw7DBmxYwtlmk3Q929Jh5kvWDt32b8AWYkZzDRUGXZv25kgZozy_F55WjRg1BQV_IYCxX0-_iot6r4sEL7-0k), [4](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESegHEIkXXzkBydHcplbqXtQwPE1yedt59lrVTFN8sXPwZ7Qcf3dCzmSUCuLi2MZlqOkVbeCaskF7CTQ7yGrewQmb8BtUN5rhJlcANGmWpAVkKYWMoL7Cu4Hqr-ryXT2FS4rY6GZLVO752uvYC4UCSfM_telwC1XvHprqb), [5](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESbgHEIkXXfFri57YOY6oXGbeM1QNvfQQurgP6jt_n52SwiZnUPl4SXpom_0gcd1ZlJEPm1jt1R4Cz7plD-Gw3bQQ4vk_VvCFaDPLc22nOsKn1NgWUbw7Fi3LSWoEWS6wQjAcIEcWacjLt4HTink_6)]
* Against Q: Critics (including many traditional or conservative theologians) point out that there is zero physical manuscript evidence, no early church fathers mention Q by name, and alternative models—like the Farrer hypothesis, which suggests Luke copied directly from Matthew—compete with the two-source theory.
