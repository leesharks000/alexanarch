The Farrer hypothesis is a proposed solution to the [synoptic problem](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESbAHEIkXXmTGPkfvDlHEO1eoiKqEno3AV_JhHgXiSe6w7u8aurltvB7t3v2d_Ib5GWoqoEpL_LQ3xoOX66bMaoNIJP5fhPR9OM_2MDs-qAd09n2BwZZUq3ZVphE8oUYJP-FG9T8AZKoXx0JsvTg) in biblical studies that eliminates the need for the hypothetical "Q" document. [[1](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESbAHEIkXXmTGPkfvDlHEO1eoiKqEno3AV_JhHgXiSe6w7u8aurltvB7t3v2d_Ib5GWoqoEpL_LQ3xoOX66bMaoNIJP5fhPR9OM_2MDs-qAd09n2BwZZUq3ZVphE8oUYJP-FG9T8AZKoXx0JsvTg)]
Core Literary Sequence
The theory proposes the following order of composition:

1. Gospel of Mark was written first.
2. Gospel of Matthew was written second, using Mark as a primary source.
3. Gospel of Luke was written third, using both Mark and Matthew as sources. [[1](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESbAHEIkXXmTGPkfvDlHEO1eoiKqEno3AV_JhHgXiSe6w7u8aurltvB7t3v2d_Ib5GWoqoEpL_LQ3xoOX66bMaoNIJP5fhPR9OM_2MDs-qAd09n2BwZZUq3ZVphE8oUYJP-FG9T8AZKoXx0JsvTg)]

Key Details and Origin

* Originator: Proposed in 1955 by English biblical scholar Austin Farrer in his essay "On Dispensing with Q". [[1](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESbAHEIkXXmTGPkfvDlHEO1eoiKqEno3AV_JhHgXiSe6w7u8aurltvB7t3v2d_Ib5GWoqoEpL_LQ3xoOX66bMaoNIJP5fhPR9OM_2MDs-qAd09n2BwZZUq3ZVphE8oUYJP-FG9T8AZKoXx0JsvTg)]
* Proponents: Developed and championed by later scholars including Michael Goulder and Mark Goodacre. [[1](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESbAHEIkXXmTGPkfvDlHEO1eoiKqEno3AV_JhHgXiSe6w7u8aurltvB7t3v2d_Ib5GWoqoEpL_LQ3xoOX66bMaoNIJP5fhPR9OM_2MDs-qAd09n2BwZZUq3ZVphE8oUYJP-FG9T8AZKoXx0JsvTg)]
* Alternative Names: Also known as the Farrer–Goulder hypothesis or the Farrer–Goulder–Goodacre hypothesis. [[1](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESbAHEIkXXmTGPkfvDlHEO1eoiKqEno3AV_JhHgXiSe6w7u8aurltvB7t3v2d_Ib5GWoqoEpL_LQ3xoOX66bMaoNIJP5fhPR9OM_2MDs-qAd09n2BwZZUq3ZVphE8oUYJP-FG9T8AZKoXx0JsvTg)]
* Major Contrast: It stands as a direct alternative to the dominant Two-Source Hypothesis (which posits that Matthew and Luke independently used Mark and a lost collection of sayings known as Q). [[1](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESbQHEIkXX4fjlksd-QbcEG91A9W9ZTgbuSf-qaUW6QhTpHUbOleX8-96DA-fODc2S-HQSJk1KoZ92_Cw-JZCGQwV9srj2M48cAP0cXR5d7TC2aHvLFHGJXtsf5bNNBGHkkIti-ZjJbedWMdGrMNs), [2](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESigEBxCJF1zcUui1rDNS_qyEHw69R6w2wU4fnZS5ovSsuK-Sg0NovhEUoeWNdCTuPEBNk_5ua6GpRq4Ou6Y2nqmEHGQLkpMXY5tgT1QeLgO09JfRVwFV08hOby1YPtyioCuX26LDa-1f4RhJx14Sg7axw2RkgkJKd0LgzttwyMqMcZTUeH-U60hyinXY)]

Arguments For and Against

* Arguments For: Advocates point out that it simplifies literary history by relying only on existing, canonical gospels rather than postulating a lost document (Q). It also provides a straightforward explanation for the "double tradition" (material shared by Matthew and Luke but absent in Mark), suggesting Luke simply integrated and adapted Matthew's material alongside Mark. [[1](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESbAHEIkXXmTGPkfvDlHEO1eoiKqEno3AV_JhHgXiSe6w7u8aurltvB7t3v2d_Ib5GWoqoEpL_LQ3xoOX66bMaoNIJP5fhPR9OM_2MDs-qAd09n2BwZZUq3ZVphE8oUYJP-FG9T8AZKoXx0JsvTg), [2](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESbQHEIkXX4fjlksd-QbcEG91A9W9ZTgbuSf-qaUW6QhTpHUbOleX8-96DA-fODc2S-HQSJk1KoZ92_Cw-JZCGQwV9srj2M48cAP0cXR5d7TC2aHvLFHGJXtsf5bNNBGHkkIti-ZjJbedWMdGrMNs)]
* Arguments Against: Critics argue that the theory requires Luke to systematically dismantle, rearrange, and recontextualize Matthew's well-organized discourses (such as breaking up the Sermon on the Mount) in ways that seem complex or counterintuitive for an ancient author. As discussed on [Reddit's AcademicBiblical community](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESigEBxCJF1zcUui1rDNS_qyEHw69R6w2wU4fnZS5ovSsuK-Sg0NovhEUoeWNdCTuPEBNk_5ua6GpRq4Ou6Y2nqmEHGQLkpMXY5tgT1QeLgO09JfRVwFV08hOby1YPtyioCuX26LDa-1f4RhJx14Sg7axw2RkgkJKd0LgzttwyMqMcZTUeH-U60hyinXY), it remains a notable minority view rather than the majority consensus in mainstream New Testament scholarship.
