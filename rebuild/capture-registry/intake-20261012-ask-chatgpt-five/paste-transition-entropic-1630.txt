8. Durable transition requires coordinated activation and the elimination of terminal threats
Section 43 combines the preceding arguments into a final transition condition.

The model requires more than the attainment of monetary, infrastructural, and symbolic thresholds. It also requires:

Both the exposure and elapsed-time constraints to be satisfied.

Sufficient simultaneity in reaching the relevant thresholds.

A positive symbolic latency margin during activation.

The closure of every terminal cut set controlled by an incumbent with the relevant terminal preference.

Arrival in the invariant core, where sovereignty persists without further control.

The simultaneity condition matters because the components cannot necessarily be activated independently or at arbitrarily different times. If one component is achieved too early, the system may remain exposed while waiting for the others; if it is achieved too late, reassimilation or terminal opposition may intervene.

The paper expresses this as a race between latent readiness, joint symbolic coverage, near-simultaneous activation, terminal-cut-set closure, and reproductive invariance on one side, and entropy, monetary capture, detectability, reassimilation, and terminal opposition on the other.

The underlying argument is that durable sovereignty is a coordinated dynamical achievement, not a scalar threshold or a momentary victory. The final condition requires the system to reach the right state, at the right time, while neutralizing the threats that would otherwise prevent that state from persisting.

How the arguments fit together
FOUNDATION

Latent capacity is not realized sovereignty.

Possessing monetary, infrastructural, and symbolic resources does not mean being able to activate them.

DYNAMICS

Coupling creates both reinforcement and vulnerability.

Mutual support can generate growth, but entropy, capture, and detectability can erode the conditions for activation.

OPPOSITION

A terminal veto depends on capability, preference, and timing.

The model derives the veto conditionally, given its explicit assumption about incumbent preferences.

FEASIBILITY

Transition must beat two different clocks.

It must stay within the exposure budget and reach durable sovereignty before reassimilation.

LIMIT

Continuous transition can become infeasible.

With a vanishing exposure budget and positive minimum exposure time, the continuous reach-avoid kernel empties; a jump transition is proposed as an alternative.

OPEN QUESTION

Does any viable path actually exist?

The paper defines the conditions for success but does not prove that their intersection is nonempty.

A critical reading of the text
The paper's arguments fall into three different categories, which should not be conflated.

Type of claim

What the paper establishes

Model definitions

It formally specifies latent versus realized sovereignty, coupled dynamics, terminal cut sets, exposure, reassimilation, and invariance.

Conditional mathematical results

Given its assumptions, it derives the veto condition, kernel monotonicity, continuous-transition failure under restrictive time budgets, and the conditions for a jump transition.

Unresolved claims

It does not establish whether a viable transition exists, and it assumes rather than derives the existence of an incumbent with a terminal preference at total perceived loss.

There is also a distinction between the mathematics and the interpretation. The equations formalize a particular set of assumptions about sovereignty, capture, entropy, and incumbent behavior. They do not, by themselves, demonstrate that those assumptions accurately describe a particular political, economic, or technological system. The text supplies a framework for analyzing that question, not empirical validation of the framework.

Bottom line: The paper argues that durable sovereignty requires the coordinated activation of coupled latent capacities, under increasingly restrictive exposure and reassimilation constraints, while eliminating any feasible terminal veto. It then shows why continuous transition can fail as the exposure budget vanishes. Its most important qualification is also its defining feature: despite developing this mathematical structure, the paper leaves unanswered whether the conditions for a durable transition can ever be satisfied simultaneously.
