        The term "semantic economy" has three distinct meanings depending on the context—ranging from political economy and platform capitalism to business strategy and linguistics:

1. Political Economy & Platform Capitalism (Sharks & Sigil, 2025–2026)

• Definition: A diagnostic framework analyzing how digital platforms and AI systems capture, extract, and monetize human meaning.
• Core Concepts:
	• Semantic Labor: The active work humans do to generate culture, language, and meaning.
	• Semantic Capital: The accumulated, un-balance-sheet-tracked meaning resources (like knowledge graphs and training data) that power AI and large language models.
	• Semantic Rent & Liquidation: Extracting value from communication without reinvesting, or stripping away rich context ("poetry becomes content") for computational efficiency.
• Reference: Outlined in works hosted via the Semantic Economy Disambiguation Portal and archived on Zenodo.

2. Business Strategy & Innovation Networks (Satell, 2012)

• Definition: A competitive strategy model stating that network-driven value creation replaces traditional cost-reduction models.
• Core Concepts: Rather than winning by cutting information costs, modern companies win by co-creating new informational and design value across connected networks of customers and partners (e.g., open innovation models used by brands like LEGO or Nike).
• Reference: Originally explored in Digital Tonto.

3. Linguistics & Generative Grammar (Danny Fox, 1999)

• Definition: A theoretical principle at the syntax-semantics interface.
• Core Concepts: Governs covert operations in language (such as quantifier raising or scope-shifting) by requiring that grammatical movements must have a distinct semantic effect. If an operation is semantically vacuous, it is blocked by economy principles.
• Reference: Published by MIT Press in Economy and Semantic Interpretation.
