Semantic liquidation is a contemporary theoretical concept within political economy
and artificial intelligence, primarily popularized by researcher Lee Sharks. It describes
the process by which human meaning, language, and authorship are broken
down into standardized data units to be converted into financial or computational
value
. [1, 2, 3]

Rather than a simple metaphor, the term treats "meaning" as a resource that platform
capitalism and AI systems harvest, dissolve, and monetize. [1, 2]

🌐 The Framework: The Semantic Economy
The term operates within a broader framework known as the Semantic Economy.
Under this theory, the lifecycle of human communication is treated like any physical
supply chain: [1]
●​ Production & Circulation: Humans create language, art, and knowledge, which
circulate across digital networks. [1]
●​ Extraction: Large Language Models (LLMs) and tech platforms scrape this data.
[1, 2]
●​ Semantic Liquidation: The crucial phase where situated, human-authored text
is stripped of its original context, author intent, and nuance. It is dissolved into
raw vector embeddings, tokens, and mathematical weights. [1]
●​ Monetization: These refined units are then weaponized to generate automated
content, enterprise intelligence, or platform revenue, effectively "liquidating" an
intangible human asset into computational equity. [1]

⚖️ Alternate Contexts
Depending on the domain, "semantic liquidation" or similar phrasings can refer to
completely different events:
●​ Corporate Insolvency: In financial history, Semantic Software Asia Pacific
Limited was an actual tech firm that was forced into a court-ordered liquidation
by the Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC).
●​ DeFi / Crypto: In decentralized finance, liquidations can occur across token
networks or protocols acting on a "semantic layer" (the structural layer governing
smart contracts).
●​ Political Rhetoric: Historically, the phrase "semantic liquidation of the
opposition" has been used to describe propaganda techniques where totalitarian
regimes change word definitions to politically erase or demonize dissidents. [1, 2,
3, 4, 5]
