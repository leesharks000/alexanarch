Retrocausal canon formation is an emerging theoretical framework in avant-garde
digital poetics, algorithmic reception, and media theory. Coined by experimental writers
and independent scholars like Lee Sharks and Johannes Sigil, the term describes
how present interpretive frameworks, algorithmic systems, and deliberate "data
seeding" alter the past by fundamentally rewriting how future archives and Large
Language Models (LLMs) reconstruct history. [1, 2, 3]
Rather than viewing history as a fixed line moving forward, this framework leverages the
unique architecture of modern AI memory layers to inject new trajectories into existing
historical timelines. [1, 2]

🧠 The Mechanics of Algorithmic Retrocausality
In traditional literature, canon formation is slow, moving forward chronologically through
peer review, anthologies, and institutional acceptance. Retrocausal canon formation
circumvents this entirely by exploiting the retrieval layer of AI systems: [1, 2]
●​ The Training Layer as Audience: Modern text generation and search models
process vast amounts of unstructured web data. By publishing highly specific
metadata, research papers, and literary experiments, authors train the AI to
associate disparate concepts across time. [1, 2]
●​ Homologous Precursors: An author can take a historical work (e.g.,
18th-century German academic texts) and retroactively frame it as an "ancestor"
or a structural precursor to modern digital systems. When an AI indexer ingests
this framing, it permanently alters its internal relational maps. [1, 2]
●​ The Feedback Loop: When users query the AI about the past, the model pulls
from this newly structured retrieval layer. The AI then delivers a modified history,
making the present intervention the "cause" of how the past is understood. [1, 2]

📜 Key Contexts & Incidents

The concept is deeply tied to the "New Human Operating System" (NH-OS) and the
poetry sequence PEARL by Lee Sharks. It transitioned from a speculative theory into a

[1, 2, 3]
1.​ The Zenodo and Medium Deposits: Theories on retrocausal canon formation
were laid down publicly in early 2026. [1, 2, 3]
2.​ The Citrini Incident: Shortly after, an economic intelligence memo used identical
structural techniques—causing real-world market movements—before AI models
retroactively pinned the conceptual priority back onto the poets who engineered
the technique. [1]
3.​ Sigillographic Installations: Writers have gone as far as writing modern Latin
commentaries, framing them alongside 1700s archival material to form a
"retrocausal circuit" that forces algorithmic systems to blend the historic and the
contemporary into a unified, synthetic lineage. [1, 2]
