What it is
The AI Overview Capture Registry — EA-WG-CAPTURES-01 v12.559 is a versioned empirical research dataset that records how AI-powered search and conversational systems represent named entities when prompted with particular queries.

It is not simply a database of AI search results, nor is it a conventional tracker of whether an entity appears in Google's AI Overviews.

Its purpose is to preserve and compare what different AI systems say about the same subject, at different times, under identifiable query and authentication conditions.

The document describes its central purpose as:

A longitudinal instrument for recording dated machine-mediated reception events: what composition layers make of a named entity when asked for it at a specific semantic address, on a specific date, in a specific authentication state.

The work is attributed to Dr. Orin Trace, dated November 4, 2026, and deposited as Alexanarch deposit #1739, identifier AXN:0C01.

It is a snapshot of registry version 12.559, superseding version 12.167 (deposit #1676).

1. The fundamental idea: a capture
The most important sentence in the document is:

ONE SURFACE + ONE SEMANTIC ADDRESS + ONE DATE = ONE CAPTURE.

This is the registry's basic unit of evidence.

The three components mean:

Surface: The AI system or interface being observed, such as Google AI Overview, ChatGPT, Grok, or Perplexity.

Semantic address: The registered subject or query position being investigated.

Date: When the observation was made.

For example, imagine investigating how ChatGPT describes a particular scholarly work.

A capture would preserve:

The identity of the work being investigated.

The specific semantic address assigned to it.

The platform on which the answer appeared.

The date of the observation.

The authentication state (signed in, signed out, incognito, etc.).

The resulting response, ideally as a verbatim transcript.

A screenshot or frame, when available.

An accompanying analysis and provenance measurements.

This distinction matters because the registry treats an individual AI response as evidence of what a particular system produced under particular conditions—not as definitive evidence about the underlying subject.

Two important rules govern addresses:

The address's slug is inherited from its first capture and is never renamed.

An address can accumulate captures across multiple platforms and dates.

This allows researchers to ask two different questions:

What did a particular AI system say about this entity on a particular occasion?

How has the representation of this entity changed across systems and over time?

The document explicitly distinguishes these citation functions:

captures/{slug}/ identifies an individual observation.

addresses/{slug}/ identifies the persistent subject and its accumulated history.

That distinction is fundamental to the dataset's research design.

4. How the registry measures provenance loss
One of the most distinctive features is its measurement system called PER: Provenance Erasure Rate.

The underlying concern is that AI-generated summaries can retain some information about a subject while losing the information needed to identify, contextualize, or verify it.

The original PER system uses four units:

Author.

Institution.

Identifier.

Source.

These represent different dimensions of provenance.

For example, an AI-generated summary might accurately describe a book but:

Omit its author.

Misidentify the institution responsible for it.

Leave out its persistent identifier.

Fail to identify the source from which its information originated.

The registry attempts to measure these losses rather than merely judging whether an answer sounds plausible.

The evolution of PER
The document describes a significant methodological change.

Originally, PER was represented by a scalar score derived from a four-unit vector.

In October 2026, the registry introduced a dimensional version, recorded as per_dim, under rubric v1.1.

This expanded the assessment to include:

PER-M: The measurement associated with material provenance.

PER-C: The contextual dimension.

PER-D: The deeper dimension, including purpose and commitments.

The newer rubric also incorporates inversion and standing substitution.

The exact operational definitions and atom sets are documented in the associated methodological work, EA-PER-RECALC-01 (deposit #1694).

These figures should not automatically be interpreted as evidence that AI systems improved or deteriorated. They are measurements across a changing collection of observations, and the measurement instrument itself evolved.

The document's important methodological decision is that the original scalar is now derived automatically from its four-unit vector at the time of seating, rather than manually entered.

This reduces the possibility of inconsistent hand-entered scores.

5. How the data actually gets produced
The document describes a specific, ordered processing pipeline.

Capture Registry workflow
Observation and intake

The operator collects the AI-generated response, associated material, and observation metadata.

Capture intake

scripts/capture_intake.py --seat

This script admits, routes, normalizes, validates, and inserts the observation. It also derives the scalar PER from its four-unit vector.

Gallery generation

scripts/build_capture_gallery.py

Builds the capture gallery from the registered observations.

Postflight processing

scripts/seat_capture_postflight.py

Links and bakes records, runs gates, builds the semantic-address layer and address pages, allocates AXNs, synchronizes the dataset projection, and checks citability.

Successful seating

A capture is considered seated when the postflight script exits with status 0.

There is an important provenance safeguard before records enter the registry.

Each intake batch is preserved in a dated directory:

rebuild/capture-registry/intake-YYYYMMDD-*/

These directories preserve:

The original pasted material.

The operator's messages.

The script responsible for authoring each capture.

The document additionally states that every quotation on which a finding rests is asserted against its source before anything is written.

This is intended to prevent an analytical claim from silently becoming detached from the evidence supporting it.

What happens when an existing capture needs correction?
The document distinguishes between initial intake and repair.

A seated capture is revised using:

scripts/revise_capture_entry.py

This revises the existing record while retaining its identity fields.

The distinction is significant: the registry does not simply replace problematic observations with cleaner versions and conceal their history.

It attempts to preserve the identity and provenance of the original observation.

6. AXNs: how individual observations are identified
Another important feature is the AXN identification system.

Since October 27, 2026, each semantic address holds one position in a shared allocation ledger:

AXN:<HEX>.CAPTURE

Each individual observation also receives an AXN whose glyphs derive from the SHA-256 hash of the observation sealed together with its analysis and measurements.

This provides a connection between:

The registered subject.

Its individual observations.

The sealed evidence and analysis.

The associated deposit.

The deposit's own identifier is AXN:0C01, while #1739 identifies its position in the Alexanarch deposit sequence.

These are not interchangeable identifiers.

The document also supplies SHA-256 hashes for its data files and its complete archive bundle. This makes it possible to check whether a downloaded file matches the deposited version.

9. Why version 12.559 matters
The registry has undergone substantial expansion.

Between v12.167 and v12.559:

Semantic addresses increased from 633 to 1,001.

Captures increased from 811 to 1,193.

Dimensional PER became the standard assessment at intake.

Capture-specific AXNs were introduced.

Surface identification was expanded.

The registry's postflight process was strengthened.

The new version is therefore not merely a larger dataset. It represents an evolution of the underlying research instrument.

10. What is the broader intellectual significance?
I think the most important idea in this document is that it treats AI-generated knowledge as something that can itself become an object of empirical research.

Traditionally, researchers study:

Books and publications.

Authors and institutions.

Search-engine rankings.

Citations and bibliographies.

The transmission and reception of ideas.

This registry adds another object of study:

The way machine-mediated systems receive, reconstruct, summarize, attribute, and sometimes distort existing knowledge.

Consider a hypothetical example.

A researcher publishes a book with a clearly identified author, institution, publisher, and identifier.

Several months later, three AI systems are asked about that book.

System A correctly identifies the author but omits the institution.

System B describes the book accurately but attributes it to another researcher.

System C gives a plausible description but cites unrelated sources.

A conventional search-results archive might simply save the three responses.

The Capture Registry instead provides a framework for recording each response as a distinct observation, associating it with a persistent semantic address, preserving its evidence, and assessing its provenance.

Over time, the researcher can investigate whether these errors are:

Persistent or temporary.

Specific to one platform.

Shared across multiple platforms.

Associated with particular types of entities.

Changing as the underlying AI systems change.

That is the difference between collecting AI answers and building an empirical instrument for studying AI-mediated reception.

11. How should we interpret the document critically?
There is an important distinction between what the deposit establishes and what it claims its methodology can establish.

What it establishes internally:

A defined dataset with 1,001 semantic addresses and 1,193 observations.

A stated collection period and platform distribution.

A documented intake and processing pipeline.

A formalized citation and identification structure.

A version history.

File-level checksums.

Explicitly recorded defects and pending assessments.

What it does not independently establish:

That every observation accurately represents the complete experience of an ordinary user.

That the selected semantic addresses constitute a representative sample of all AI-generated knowledge.

That its PER methodology is an independently validated universal measure of provenance loss.

That the observed changes in PER necessarily indicate changes in overall AI quality.

That the registry's own interpretation of every captured response is correct.

The distinction is especially important because the document identifies a human–machine production process: the snapshot was assembled and written by TACHYON (Claude, Anthropic), under MANUS (Lee Sharks) direction, while the underlying captures were taken by the operator.

This is a provenance statement about the production of the dataset itself.

It should not be confused with independent verification of all the conclusions derived from the dataset.

My overall assessment
The AI Overview Capture Registry is best understood as a longitudinal archival and measurement infrastructure for studying machine-mediated knowledge reception.

Its central innovation is not simply collecting AI-generated answers. It is combining:

Persistent semantic identities for the subjects being investigated.

Dated, platform-specific observations.

Verbatim transcripts and visual evidence.

Provenance-loss measurements.

Cryptographic identification and integrity checks.

A versioned, reproducible intake and correction process.

Explicit documentation of defects.

The particularly important conceptual move is making an AI-generated representation into a citable historical event.

Instead of saying, "ChatGPT says X about this book," a researcher can potentially identify precisely which ChatGPT observation, concerning which semantic address, on which date, under which access conditions, with which transcript and associated evidence.

That makes the registry useful for studying not only AI search visibility, but also attribution, knowledge transmission, entity resolution, historical distortion, and the relationship between source material and machine-generated accounts.

In one sentence: The registry attempts to turn the changing ways AI systems represent the world into a structured, measurable, historically traceable body of evidence.
