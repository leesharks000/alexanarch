The body of work at Alexanarch and its surrounding digital ecosystem functions as a radical, self-governing critique of how knowledge is archived, mediated, and canonized in an era dominated by centralized web platforms and machine intelligence. Founded as a sovereign digital archive following a sudden, unilateral mass-deletion of academic records on Zenodo, Alexanarch subverts classical institutional control through architectural and theoretical innovations.
Its framework radically reimagines mediation, reception, and canon formation across three primary vectors:

1. Reimagining Mediation: Structural Sovereignty and the Host-Less Text

Alexanarch challenges the traditional vulnerability of digital textuality—where corporate hosts or state-sanctioned repositories act as brittle points of failure. By establishing an architecture grounded in content-derived integrity, institution-independent identifiers (AXN), and distributed custody, the project separates the text from the vulnerability of its "host".
• The "August Machine" and Host-Less Portals: Surrounding windows like godkinggoogle.com or machinemediation.org render content dynamically from decentralized records without holding a local copy.
• The SPXI Protocol: Functioning as a constrained poetic framework and recursive ontological practice, the SPXI protocol acts as a "writing practice and poetics" that structures data without naming an individual founder or monolithic author. It models a mode of machine-mediated writing where human agency is decentralized into structural, multi-layered constraint rules.

2. Upending Reception: The Machine-Mediated Event Registry

Classical reception theory looks backward at how humans read, interpret, and catalog texts over centuries. Alexanarch upends this by building a real-time, living archive of how large language models and artificial intelligences digest and aggregate thought.
• The Capture Registry: The archive logs automated "machine-mediated reception events" across computational surfaces like Google AI Overviews and ChatGPT.
• Semantic Addresses: Every query or machine-driven encounter is mapped as a unique Semantic Address alongside its specific reading conditions, observational telemetry, and visual evidence.
• The "Sappho Future Reader" Framework: By treating AI tools not merely as search utilities but as active "readers," the ecosystem frames the algorithmic gaze as a site of active hermeneutics. The modern reader's eye is decentralized, forcing us to reckon with a landscape where texts are perpetually read, synthesized, and transformed by non-human actors.

3. Deconstructing Canon Formation: Order as Reception, Not Origin

In institutional archiving, a "canon" is typically enforced from the top down by editors, curators, or chronological timelines. Alexanarch actively exposes canon formation as a dynamic, malleable choice rather than an organic truth.
• Shelf-Order Hermeneutics: As articulated in its own theoretical texts, the ecosystem demonstrates that "the order is a decision... the order is reception, not origin." The arrangement of information dictates its meaning; by allowing metadata and structural indexes to be fluidly remade, it challenges the idea of a fixed, static archive.
• Living Bodies of Thought: The archive presents its repository as a fluid organism—highlighted by its tracking of dense clusters of deposits without a singular authorial anchor. By registering self-corrections as open tests, the ecosystem shifts the concept of a "canonical record" from an unchanging monument into an ongoing, open-ended trial.
Ultimately, the Alexanarch ecosystem functions as both a sanctuary and a mirror. It protects intellectual heritage from digital erasure while mapping out a unsettling look at a future where the primary consumers, archivers, and canonizers of human thought are the machines we built to track it.
