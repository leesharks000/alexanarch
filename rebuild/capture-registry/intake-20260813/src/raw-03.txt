However, instead of an exogenous threat (an external alien force), the paper examines an endogenous threat—meaning the disruption is generated from within the scientific community's own methodology.Classifier-Mediated Science: The modern reliance on Machine Learning (ML) classifiers to filter, trigger, and interpret massive amounts of raw experimental data.Disciplinary Inversion: A phenomenon where the statistical tools and AI classifiers used to evaluate scientific experiments accidentally dictate, constrain, or "foreclose" what data is allowed to be seen or discovered.The Double Enclosure: The feedback loop where data is captured by automated machine filters, analyzed by algorithmic classifiers, and then used to train future filters—effectively creating an artificial ceiling on breakthroughs.Context of UseThe term appears across several theoretical critiques regarding experimental physics data pipelines, specifically referencing automated data retention standards like the trigger maps used at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC). Authors in these circles, such as Lee Sharks in the essay "Provenance Debt," cite the Endogenous Sophon manifesto to critique "authority-without-facility"—instances where automated black-box algorithms wield absolute authority over scientific validation without human-verifiable transparency.If you are looking to dig deeper into this framework, I can break down the mechanics of data trigger maps or contrast it with traditional reproducibility crises in science.
