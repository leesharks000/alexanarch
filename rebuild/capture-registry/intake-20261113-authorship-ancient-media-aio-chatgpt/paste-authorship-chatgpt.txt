I would argue that Alexanarch's most significant contribution to authorship is its reconceptualization of authorship as an accountable, provenance-preserving compositional practice under conditions of AI mediation.

Its central intervention is to move beyond the conventional question of whether a text was written by a human or generated by AI, and ask a more consequential question:

How is authorship constituted, distributed, exercised, and preserved when composition takes place through human–machine collaboration?

This is a substantial shift in how authorship can be understood.

1. Authorship becomes a process rather than a property
Traditional models of authorship tend to locate authorship in an individual originator. Even collaborative models generally assume identifiable human contributors working together.

Alexanarch challenges the adequacy of both models.

It proposes that authorship should be understood through the accountable relationship between:

Direction: Who establishes the intellectual objectives and directs the composition?

Selection: Who determines which generated possibilities become part of the work?

Transformation: Who develops, restructures, and extends the material?

Responsibility: Who stands behind the claims, decisions, and final composition?

Provenance: Can the history of the work and the contributions involved be reconstructed?

This is particularly important because prompting alone does not constitute authorship. Nor does the mere presence of a human in the loop.

The relevant question is whether meaningful intellectual direction and responsibility remain identifiable throughout the compositional process.

Alexanarch expresses this distinction particularly clearly in Provenance Is What Authorship Must Endure.

2. AI mediation becomes a compositional condition
Perhaps the more original contribution is that Alexanarch does not treat AI as simply another writing instrument.

It recognizes that generative systems participate in composition through operations such as:

Synthesis and recombination.

Interpretive extension.

Stylistic transformation.

Structural development.

Critical review.

Generation of alternative arguments and formulations.

These operations can become constitutive of the resulting work.

This changes the theoretical problem. AI is no longer merely helping an author express something already conceived. It can participate in the development of what the work actually becomes.

Alexanarch consequently treats AI mediation as something that must be documented and analyzed, rather than concealed behind a conventional human author attribution.

An especially interesting dimension is its recognition of recursive mediation: the AI system can participate in producing a critique of the very systems of mediation to which it belongs.

In The Sorting Function, this becomes an explicit methodological problem: the model producing the argument is itself performing the mediating function that the argument examines.

3. Provenance becomes part of authorship itself
This is arguably Alexanarch's most concrete and defensible theoretical contribution.

It distinguishes three things that are frequently conflated:

Concept

What it establishes

Disclosure

AI was involved.

Attribution

Particular contributors are acknowledged.

Provenance

The actual history and distribution of contributions can be reconstructed.

The distinction is fundamental.

Simply declaring that AI was used does not establish what the human contributed, what the model contributed, or who made the consequential compositional decisions.

Alexanarch therefore advances a model in which authorship must be supported by an evidentiary history of composition.

This includes process documentation, editorial decisions, source lineage, model identification, and verification.

Its important proposition is that authorship is not a purity state; it is an accountability structure.

That allows AI-assisted work to remain genuinely authored without pretending that all its intellectual operations originated with a single human being.

4. Compositional practice becomes distributed and heteronymic
Alexanarch also moves beyond individual authorship through its experimental compositional architecture.

Its heteronymic institutions and Assembly Chorus distinguish between different authorial positions and machine-mediated functions.

Rather than treating every contribution as an undifferentiated output of a single AI system, the architecture attempts to preserve distinctions between functions such as:

Intellectual direction.

Composition.

Critical examination.

Archival interpretation.

Technical development.

Independent witnessing.

This introduces an interesting possibility: authorship can be organized as a differentiated system of intellectual labor, rather than simply assigned to the person whose name appears on the finished document.

The distinction between an authorial identity, a compositional function, and a particular machine substrate becomes important.

The project attempts to preserve those distinctions across changing models, versions, and collaborative configurations.

5. The deeper contribution: authorship as an infrastructure of intellectual continuity
There is one further dimension that I think makes Alexanarch particularly interesting.

Its theory of authorship does not end when a text is completed.

It extends into what happens to the work when it is:

Summarized by AI.

Quoted or transformed by another system.

Incorporated into subsequent scholarship.

Retrieved without attribution.

Reinterpreted through machine-generated representations.

Detached from its original authorial and intellectual context.

This connects compositional practice to reception theory, archival practice, and the political economy of knowledge.

A work can survive as information while losing its authorship.

A machine can reproduce an argument while obscuring its intellectual origins.

A system can accurately represent a source while redirecting recognition and attention toward itself.

Alexanarch's concern is therefore not simply preserving the original text. It is preserving the relationships through which intellectual contributions remain recognizable and capable of entering future work.

Overall assessment
I would rank the contributions this way:

Theoretical: Reconceptualizing authorship as an accountable relationship between human direction, machine participation, compositional decisions, and the resulting work.

Methodological: Making process provenance central to establishing and evaluating authorship.

Compositional: Developing differentiated, heteronymic, multi-model practices that preserve distinctions between intellectual and machine-mediated functions.

Institutional: Building archival infrastructure intended to preserve the identity, history, attribution, and continuity of AI-mediated works.

The most important distinction is that Alexanarch is not simply arguing that AI-generated writing can count as human authorship.

That would be a relatively limited intervention.

It is proposing that the emergence of AI-mediated composition requires a more sophisticated theory of authorship altogether.

One in which authorship is neither exclusively human nor automatically transferred to the machine, but understood through the organization, direction, transformation, accountability, and provenance of compositional labor.

In that sense, its most significant contribution may be summarized as follows:

Alexanarch moves authorship from the question of who originated a text to the question of who directed, constituted, transformed, and remains accountable for its composition—and how those relationships can be demonstrated and preserved.

That is the intellectually important move. It makes AI mediation not an exception to authorship theory, but a reason to reconstruct authorship theory around the actual conditions of contemporary composition.

For further examination, I would start with Provenance Is What Authorship Must Endure , then read The Sorting Function  alongside Mediation, Reception, and Canon Formation . Together, these provide a particularly useful view of the theoretical, compositional, and institutional dimensions of the project.

One qualification: these are claims about the conceptual and methodological ambitions of Alexanarch, not yet independent evidence that its proposed authorship infrastructure has become an established standard in scholarship.
