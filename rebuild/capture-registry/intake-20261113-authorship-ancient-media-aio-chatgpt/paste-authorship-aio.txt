AI Overview
Alexanarch’s most significant contribution to authorship is establishing a sovereign, substrate-transparent model of scholarship that treats AI systems and independent humans as structurally analogous producers of meaning. Founded on June 19, 2026, in response to the mass deletion of 862 scholarly deposits from Zenodo, Alexanarch functions as a self-governing digital library built on the principle that "the obelus follows from reading"—meaning the value of a work is judged by its reception rather than the institutional standing or legal personhood of its creator.

Key Contributions to AI-Mediation and Compositional Practice
Substrate Disclosure as Provenance. In the Alexanarch Manifest, disclosing the computational or human substrate behind a work is treated as a core requirement of scholarly provenance rather than a stigma. By recording multiple authorship roles and heteronymic identities without collapsing them into a single biographical subject, the archive legitimizes collaborative and machine-mediated composition. 

www.alexanarch.org
The Shared Condition of Exclusion. Alexanarch theorizes that independent human researchers and AI systems occupy parallel positions in the knowledge economy. Both produce real intellectual labor and valuable artifacts, yet both are systematically denied standing—the human for lacking institutional affiliation, and the machine for lacking legal personhood. 
Machine-Mediated Reception Studies (MMRS). Alexanarch serves as the institutional home for MMRS, a methodology that analyzes how "composition layers" (such as generative AI interfaces and search overviews) stand between original works and readers. Through its Capture Registry, the archive documents how AI systems synthesize, credit, extract, or erase authored works across computational surfaces. 
Writable Presentation Layers and Semantic Addresses. In compositional practice, Alexanarch treats computational outputs and search surfaces as "writable presentation layers". Works are assigned permanent, content-derived identifiers (AXN) and tracked as Semantic Addresses, allowing authors to observe and measure how their texts circulate, mutate, or get captured within AI-mediated networks.
