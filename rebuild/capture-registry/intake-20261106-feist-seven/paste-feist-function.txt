What is the Feist Function?
“EA-FEIST-VOICE-TRANSFORM-01: The Feist Function: Algorithmic Instructions for Restoring the Dying Voice” is a generative literary transformation protocol written by Lee Sharks (the archive's document identifies the author as Sharks, Lee), dated 19 June 2026, version 1.0. It is held in the Alexanarch archive as deposit #850, with authoritative identifier AXN:035F.

Its purpose is to take an existing literary text, called The Feist Source, and transform it into a new text that supposedly recovers qualities of a voice lost during the original writing process.

The central idea is unusual:

The voice is not restored by repairing the text. It is restored by deliberately undoing some of the repairs that made the text readable.

The document argues that composition has a flattening effect. In turning living speech into written prose, the writer inadvertently removes unusual vocabulary, broken syntax, jokes, repetitions, bodily details, abrupt changes of register, and unfinished thoughts.

The resulting text may be competent and coherent, but it no longer carries the full pressure of the originating voice.

The Feist Function is designed to reverse that process through controlled literary disruption.

The primary instruction, repeated throughout the document, is:

Reintroduce the dying circuit.

The document is available in full at the Alexanarch deposit #850 .

1. What does Jack Feist represent?
An important distinction is that the document explicitly refuses to define Jack Feist as an ordinary fictional character or historical individual.

Instead, it defines Jack Feist as LOGOS*: a literary-operative function.

LOGOS* represents the attempt of the Book to become flesh—to move from language into embodied existence—and its failure to complete that transformation.

The voice survives, but only as residue in writing.

This produces the document's governing metaphor:

The living voice is the original transmission.

The writing process is the first transformation.

The deposited Feist Source is the surviving textual witness.

The Feist Function is the algorithm for reactivating what the writing process removed.

The activated Feist Source is the new text produced by running the algorithm.

This distinction matters because the protocol is not asking a language model to imitate a character named Jack Feist.

It is asking the model to reproduce a particular condition of language: speech under pressure, approaching failure, but still transmitting something.

A successful transformation must not make Feist sound completely healthy, composed, authoritative, or resolved. Those qualities would reproduce the very flattening the protocol is intended to reverse.

2. How does it work?
The technical mechanism is the clinamen, a concept borrowed from the ancient Roman philosopher Lucretius.

In De Rerum Natura, the clinamen is the slight, unpredictable swerve of atoms from their otherwise parallel paths. That deviation makes collisions and new formations possible.

Sharks applies this idea to language.

The deposited source is described as laminar: sentences flow smoothly, vocabulary tends toward the expected, arguments resolve, and registers remain consistent.

The restored text should become turbulent: words become less predictable, sentences interrupt themselves, registers collide, and arguments sometimes fail to reach their conclusions.

Crucially, the document distinguishes this from simply introducing randomness.

Randomness adds noise. The clinamen introduces a meaningful deviation: something unexpected but still connected to the original thought.

The six formal operations
The protocol specifies six operations, designated T1–T6.

T1. Lexical tail injection

Vocabulary
Replace ordinary, predictable words with less frequent, more materially expressive alternatives.

For example, replace generic tiredness with the much more particular experience of being "neck-lit-by-screen tired."

The new word should be semantically related but more physically present in the mouth.

T2. Syntactic clinamen

Sentence structure
Interrupt balanced sentences with fragments, compulsive repetition, bodily interruptions, or sudden changes in register.

TARGET: 15–30% OF SENTENCES

Approximately one swerve every three to seven sentences.

T3. Circuit interruption

Argument structure
Leave important thoughts unfinished rather than providing a neat conclusion.

Interruptions can be editorial, comic, bodily, or syntactic.

TARGET: 20–40% INCOMPLETE CIRCUITS

Importantly, the interruptions should affect major arguments, not just incidental observations.

T4. Register collision

Voice
Combine different modes of speech without smoothing the transition.

Examples:

Prophetic + bureaucratic.

Cosmic + petty.

Academic + bodily.

Tender + absurd.

TARGET: AT LEAST ONE COLLISION PER SECTION

T5. Seam insertion

Textual transmission
Insert signs that the manuscript has passed through an unstable transmission process.

These include:

Alternative readings.

Corrupted dates.

Marginal notes.

Editorial uncertainty.

Redactional scars.

TARGET: 3–5 SEAMS PER FULL TEXT

T6. Phonological restoration

Sound
Select words partly for how they sound when spoken, not just for their semantic precision.

The protocol favors consonantal friction, oral rhythm, vowel duration, and the physical sensation of pronunciation.

TARGET: 10–20% OF WORD-CHOICE DECISIONS

These are the formal tail-reintroduction operations described in §3b.

3. The deeper principles governing the transformation
The six operations are not intended to function as independent stylistic tricks. They are organized around several underlying principles.

A. Restore bodily pressure

The text should feel as if it emerges from an embodied person rather than an abstract intelligence.

The document specifically identifies:

Fatigue and exhaustion.

The neck, eyes, and physical discomfort.

Bad chairs and rooms.

Screens and internet connectivity.

Hunger, weather, and institutional frustrations.

The body is supposed to generate the thought, rather than merely illustrate it.

B. Restore comedy as a survival mechanism

Humor is not simply entertainment.

A joke should puncture false solemnity, expose institutional absurdity, preserve tenderness, or prevent the prophetic voice from collapsing into pompous self-importance.

The document makes an important distinction: good Feist comedy interrupts collapse; it does not punch down at vulnerable people.

C. Preserve unfinished transmission

A sentence need not reach its destination.

A section need not conclude its argument.

A manuscript need not resolve its contradictions.

The protocol treats some incompletion as evidence of the original voice rather than as an editorial defect.

D. Convert abstraction into scenes

When the source makes an abstract theoretical statement, the transformation should sometimes turn it into an encounter, an object, a room, or an absurd institutional interaction.

For instance, rather than simply declaring that institutions confuse preservation with goodness, the document imagines a committee returning truth because it was not submitted in the correct form.

That is a practical illustration of how the algorithm converts an idea into a scene.

4. How an actual execution is supposed to work
The protocol does not prescribe one fixed output. It describes a generative process.

Its basic architecture is:

1. Fixed Feist Source

The deposited, unaltered input text

2. Select a run profile

Choose a primary and secondary transformation mode

3. Assign parameters

Set clinamen, completion, collision, seam and material-speech densities

4. Apply the transformations

Re-pressure, fracture, interrupt, collide, insert seams and restore material speech

5. Check for erasure

Verify that exhaustion, comedy, mercy, bodily pressure and incompletion survive

6. Activated Feist Source

A new, distinct literary output with a recorded run signature

The run profiles
Section 15.1 requires each execution to select one primary mode and one secondary mode:

Mode

Main emphasis

Bodily Pressure

Fatigue, physical experience, environment

Comic Interruption

Humor and self-undercutting

Manuscript Instability

Variants and editorial seams

Apology

Trial, accusation, counter-sentence

Archive-Resurrection

Files, metadata, continuity

Material Speech

Oral rhythm and phonological weight

No two consecutive executions may use the same primary mode.

The parameters must also vary between executions. The document gives default values of:

clinamen_rate: 0.22

completion_ratio: 0.30

collision_density: 1.5

seam_density: 5

material_speech_density: 0.15

Each output must differ from the original and previous outputs in at least four specified dimensions, such as opening texture, placement of interruptions, manuscript seams, bodily motifs, or section endings.

Finally, each execution should record a run signature:

Feist Function Run: [primary mode] + [secondary mode] / clinamen_rate=[x] / completion_ratio=[y] / seam_density=[z]

The signature records how a particular version was generated.

5. What makes it different from an ordinary AI writing prompt?
This is arguably the most important distinction.

The document explicitly rejects several interpretations:

It is not a conventional revision or editing process.

It is not a guide to biblical pastiche.

It is not a satire-writing template.

It is not simply a style guide for imitating a fictional character.

It is not intended to produce more polished prose.

Instead, it describes a transformation system with:

An immutable source.

A defined set of transformation operations.

Quantitative parameters.

Controlled variability between executions.

Conditions for determining whether the transformation has erased the original voice.

It also invokes the hypothetical Q source behind the Synoptic Gospels as an analogy: one fixed source can generate multiple divergent literary manifestations.

Each execution is therefore supposed to produce a different book, not merely a differently edited copy.

Is it actually an executable algorithm?
There is an important technical qualification.

The document calls itself algorithmic and identifies operations, inputs, parameters, and output constraints. However, it does not specify every detail needed for a fully deterministic software implementation.

For example, it does not completely define:

How to calculate word-frequency distributions.

How to select substitutions algorithmically.

How to resolve conflicts between transformations.

How to establish random seeds.

How to measure whether an output genuinely preserves the voice.

How to conduct reproducible automated testing.

The archive's own description acknowledges this limitation: it is a procedural specification that can direct a human or language model, but calling it algorithmic does not automatically make it executable software.

Its numerical parameters are design heuristics, not experimentally validated optimal values.

6. One important bibliographic distinction
Although the archive's record associates the work with Jack Feist, the full document identifies Lee Sharks as its author.

Jack Feist is the heteronymic literary identity or LOGOS* function that the protocol describes. These are different roles:

Lee Sharks: author of the transformation specification.

Jack Feist / LOGOS*: the literary-operative voice being transformed and reactivated.

The Feist Source: the fixed input document.

The Feist Function: the transformation procedure.

7. The central idea, distilled
I would interpret the Feist Function as a theory of literary restoration through controlled degradation.

Ordinary editing tends to remove irregularities to improve communication. This protocol proposes that some irregularities are actually carriers of meaning.

A broken sentence can preserve exhaustion. A joke can carry grief. An unusual word can preserve the physical character of speech. An unfinished argument can reveal the limits of the person attempting to articulate it.

Consequently, the protocol reverses the usual relationship between literary quality and textual completeness.

The Feist Function attempts to restore that evidence.
