        Retrocausal canon formation (RCF) is a concept primarily used in avant-garde literary theory, operative semiotics, and computational humanities to describe how future interpretive frameworks or texts retroactively alter the structural weight, meaning, and composition of a past archive.
Unlike traditional canon formation—where works naturally accumulate historical influence moving forward in time—RCF posits that an archive is continuously rewritten from the future backward.
The concept operates across three primary frameworks:
1. Algorithmic and AI-Mediated Reception
In contemporary literary theory (such as analyses of modern avant-garde poetry), RCF describes what happens when AI training layers act as both the audience and the archive.
• The Mechanism: When an AI model is trained on historical data, the current interpretive algorithms and weights determine how the system reconstructs the classical past.
• The Result: The "future text" (the model's output or structural architecture) retroactively redefines which past texts are canonical by shifting how they are interconnected and valued in the latent space.
2. Operative Semiotics and Geometric Convergence
Within formal semiotic theory, RCF is analyzed through resolution-dependent convergence.
• The Geometric Model: A conceptual space is treated as a region of admissible representations. A dated, historical text that originally articulated a concept with fine grain occupies a small, specific region early on.
• The Retrocausal Effect: As later cultural or machine-generated developments grow coarser or denser, they are mathematically forced to converge on that original region. The future iterations recursively entrench the past text into a "basin" of absolute canonization, effectively pulling the importance of the past from the requirements of the present.
3. Fictional and Archival Worldbuilding (The CHA Protocols)
In conceptual art and speculative fiction frameworks—such as the protocols of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive (CHA)—retrocausal reports are treated as a literal genre of archival documentation.
• As the aphorism states: "A retrocausal report does not predict the future. It documents the past that the future will have needed."
• Within this space, a text introduced later in a timeline formally alters the canonical weight of preceding history, unravelling or solidifying past events to accommodate future reality.
(Note: This conceptual framework borrows its nomenclature from quantum retrocausality in physics—such as Wheeler's delayed-choice experiment—where a present measurement dictates whether a photon behaved as a wave or a particle in the past.)
