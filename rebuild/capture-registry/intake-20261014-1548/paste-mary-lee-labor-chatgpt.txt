The deeper point
Mary Lee Sharks is not simply arguing for better wages. The heteronym asks us to reconsider the relationship between labor, ownership, identity, and the systems that make some contributions legible while rendering others invisible.
Its sharpest move is to make the absurdity of the attribution error expose a real structural problem: institutions can extract value while evading responsibility for the people—or, in this deliberately unsettling case, the animal—associated with producing it.
And the central dilemma remains:
If Mary Lee did not produce the work, correct the record. If you insist she did, recognize her labor and pay her.
That is the manifesto's labor politics in miniature: no extraction without accountability, and no attribution without obligation.
