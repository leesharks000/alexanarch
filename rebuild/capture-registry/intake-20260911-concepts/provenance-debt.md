1. In AI & Software Engineering (The Technical Context)
In technology, provenance debt is an emerging subset of technical debt. It refers to the
hidden risk and loss of accountability that happens when organizations rapidly adopt
AI-generated content, code, or data without tracking its exact origin, lineage, or
transformations. [1, 2]

●​ AI Knowledge Bases & RAG Systems: It occurs when a Retrieval-Augmented
Generation (RAG) system accidentally indexes its own previously generated AI
outputs (like auto-generated FAQs or release notes). Over time, the AI begins
learning from itself rather than human-verified primary sources, creating a
distorted feedback loop. [1]
●​ AI-Generated Code: When developers use AI assistants to write code, the code
enters the repository without traditional human review assumptions. If a copyright
dispute, security flaw, or compliance audit happens later, the organization faces
"debt" because they cannot prove how the code was created or what licenses it
may have violated. [1, 2]
●​ The "Unpaid Bill" to Creators: In broader AI supply chain economics, some
experts use it to describe the unpriced legal liability built on using creators'
intellectual property to train models without compensation. [1]

2. In Blockchain & Digital Assets (The Financial Context)
If you are looking at financial technology, "provenance debt" refers to debt securities
and loans managed natively on a blockchain—most notably via the Provenance
Blockchain. [1]
●​ On-Chain Debt & Mortgages: Financial institutions use the Provenance
Blockchain to originate, warehouse, pool, and securitize debt (such as Home
Equity Lines of Credit or HELOCs) directly on a digital ledger. [1, 2]
●​ Eliminating Intermediaries: By recording the "provenance" (the exact history
and ownership) of a loan as an immutable digital token, financial systems reduce
the traditional administrative, audit, and legal costs associated with trading debt.
This saves companies up to 150 basis points on debt issuance. [1, 2]
