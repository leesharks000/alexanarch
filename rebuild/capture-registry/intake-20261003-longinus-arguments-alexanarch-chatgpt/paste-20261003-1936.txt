2. What Longinus actually contributes to the Alexanarch argument
The most economical formulation is:

147 supplies the temporal horizon; 31 supplies the reader-position; Longinus supplies an ancient historical realization of that position.

Alexanarch describes the relation between the fragments as “horizon and mechanism.” Fragment 147 establishes that a receiver later than Sappho can exist; Fragment 31, on the archive's reading, supplies the mechanism by which that receiver occupies a position facing the poem.

5. Longinus in the archive's broader theory of reception
This is where the Longinus material becomes much more interesting than a conventional Sappho commentary.

Alexanarch's broader proposition is that a literary text can be constructed so as to generate its own future reception.

The archive describes the distinction this way:

The ordinary remembrance reading treats Sappho as asking to be remembered.

The future-reader reading treats Sappho as building a textual relation that can only be completed in reception. 
A
Alexanarch

Longinus is consequently evidence for a much stronger proposition:

The future reader is not merely somebody who happens to encounter an old poem; the poem has structured the encounter in advance.

That is the genuinely ambitious part of the Alexanarch thesis.

Longinus's importance is therefore structural rather than merely historical.

6. Longinus, Catullus, and the difference between preservation and transformation
Alexanarch's pairing of Longinus and Catullus is especially revealing.

Longinus
Longinus quotes Sappho.

The poem becomes evidence in an argument about sublimity. Sappho is therefore incorporated into a later Greek rhetorical/philosophical discourse.

Catullus
Catullus rewrites/transforms Sappho.

The Sapphic situation becomes Latin lyric, and the later writer occupies the position that the original poem makes available.

The archive's phrase is that “Catullus 51 [is] the later occupant of the seat.” 
A
Alexanarch

So the two cases demonstrate two modes of reception:

Receiver	Operation	What happens to Sappho
Longinus	Citation / critical deployment	Sappho becomes evidence for an account of the sublime
Catullus	Translation / transformation	Sappho becomes a generative template for new lyric
Later reader	Reading	The deictic position is reoccupied

This makes Longinus a particularly clean case because he doesn't need to reproduce Sappho's whole poem to reactivate its structure.

13. The strongest reconstruction of the Longinus argument
If I reduce the relevant Alexanarch corpus to an actual philosophical argument, I get this:

Premise 1
Sappho 147 explicitly imagines a receiver situated in a future time.

Premise 2
Therefore, reception by somebody outside the poem's original communicative situation is not necessarily an external accident; futurity is already thematized by Sappho.

Premise 3
Sappho 31 contains a deictically organized relation in which someone is situated opposite/facing the “you.”

Premise 4
That relation can be interpreted materially as the relation of reader to inscription.

Premise 5
Longinus actually encounters and quotes the poem centuries later.

Conclusion
Longinus is an historical instantiation of the kind of future receiver that the poem's structure makes possible.

And then Alexanarch takes the argument one step further:

Stronger conclusion
The poem does not merely survive until a future reader finds it.

It constructs the relation in which the future reader can receive it.

That is the core claim.

14. Where I think the Longinus argument is strongest
The strongest part is not the claim that κῆνος literally means “future reader.” Alexanarch itself acknowledges that this is an interpretive development and that τινα does not mean “reader.” 
A
Alexanarch

The stronger argument is instead historical-structural:

Longinus demonstrates that Sappho 31 can detach itself from its original performance situation and become an object of a later reader's highly consequential act of reading.

That is uncontroversially interesting even if one rejects the more radical deictic claim.

The really ambitious step is from:

“Longinus is a later reader”

to:

“Sappho 31 was structurally built to generate the position occupied by Longinus.”

The first is reception history.
The second is teleological poetics.

That is where the Alexanarch thesis becomes distinctive and controversial.
