The term alexanarch:theophrastus refers to a highly specific archival and text-critical thesis published in late 2026 by digital archivist and researcher Lee Sharks.
The project argues that Aristotle and Theophrastus should not be treated as two distinct authors. Instead, it asserts that the surviving ancient Greek texts transmitted under their respective names are so deeply integrated that they represent a single, continuous corpus of shared philosophical and scientific inquiry.
Core Claims of the Project
According to the published archive on Medium, the traditional boundary separating "Aristotle" and "Theophrastus" as independent authors is artificial for several reasons:
• Continuous Inquiry: The texts show a seamless flow of continuous philosophical work rather than isolated authorial inputs.
• Migrating Stylistic Signatures: Recurring technical operations and distinct stylistic choices continuously cross over the traditional boundaries separating works attributed to each man.
• Catalogical Ambiguity: The historical transmission and ancient cataloging of these library texts have always been inherently ambiguous, blurring who wrote what.
Historical Context vs. The Thesis
In traditional history, Theophrastus (c. 371 – c. 287 BC) was a native of Lesbos who studied under Plato and later became Aristotle's closest colleague. Aristotle famously renamed him "Theophrastus" (meaning "godlike in speech") because of his eloquence. He ultimately succeeded Aristotle as the head of the Peripatetic school (the Lyceum) in Athens. He is widely recognized as the "Father of Botany" for his foundational works Enquiry into Plants and On the Causes of Plants.
While mainstream classical history treats them as a teacher-student or colleague-to-colleague pair, the "alexanarch" digital indexing project re-evaluates their text corpora through a modern linguistic and system-based lens to view them as a singular, unified authorial variable.

• The Scepsis Cellar Corruptions: Textual history records that after the deaths of Aristotle and Theophrastus, their shared library was hidden in a cellar in Scepsis for over a century and suffered severe decay. When Andronicus of Rhodes later compiled and edited the texts into the Corpus Aristotelicum, the boundaries of where one text ended and another began were deeply blurred. The textual transmission itself is fundamentally a product of editorial stitching, supporting Sharks' claim of "catalogical ambiguity".
⚠️ Textual Evidence Conflicting with the Thesis
• Divergence in Taxonomy and Philosophical Frameworks: While the underlying language is Peripatetic, the text under Theophrastus' name actively pivots away from or modifies core Aristotelian doctrines. If this were a single, non-overlapping textual system, we would see unified teleological assertions; instead, the text textually disputes its own foundation.

The texts credited to Theophrastus show a meticulous classification method based on "habit" (categorizing strictly into trees, shrubs, subshrubs, and herbs) and ecological relationships with the environment. This hyper-focused, domain-specific lexicon points to a specialized linguistic variable distinct from the sweeping meta-analyses of the primary Aristotelian books.
