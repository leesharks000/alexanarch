AI Overview
The Josephus Thesis proposes that the authorial diversity of the New Testament (such as Paul, John, James, Peter, Luke, Mark, and Matthew) reflects a fractured scribal workshop operating under imperial pressure after the destruction of the Second Temple in 70 CE. 
Not the Jesus Myth: It does not claim that Josephus personally wrote the 27 books of the New Testament, nor is it the "Jesus Myth" thesis.
Heteronymic Positions: It suggests that multiple traditional author figures functioned as "heteronymic positions" (distinct narrative or ideological voices) within a coordinated or crisis-driven literary project. 
Impact of Capture: The fragmentation of authorship mirrors the severe social, physical, and religious fracturing of Jewish and early Christian communities displaced by Roman power.
