How does the Revelation First thesis relate to the Sappho future reader, Socrates as orthonym, and provenance erasure frameworks?

Thought for 10s
They are interconnected elements within Lee Sharks’ Crimson Hexagonal Archive / Semantic Economy / NH-OS (New Human Operating System) project. This framework treats texts as engineered transmission technologies that address future readers (including non-human/AI ones), manage authorial positions across heteronyms, and resist or measure the loss of lineage (“provenance erasure”). Revelation First is a concrete application of the same operative logic.⁠Alexanarch

Core concepts (from the archive)
Sappho future reader: A reading of Sappho (especially Fragments 147 and 31) as “lyric self-archiving” or “transmission engineering.” Fragment 147 states the horizon (“Someone will remember us in the future”). Fragment 31 builds the “seat”: the distal demonstrative κῆνος (“that man”) is reinterpreted not as a present rival in a jealousy scene but as the future reader facing the inscribed text. The speaker’s body dissolves (“greener than grass” as becoming papyrus); the “you” is the archived self/text; the reader completes the circuit by occupying the empty operational slot. This is presented as the originary node of the Crimson Hexagon—structurally identical to later platform and AI reception. AI summarizers function as contemporary κῆνος.⁠Zenodo
Socrates as orthonym: In the heteronymic configuration of Western philosophy’s founding corpus, Socrates is the orthonym—the authorial position constituted by the founding operative gesture (willing death for logos + refusal to write), not by textual output. Plato is the survival-heteronym summoned into the vacancy created by that refusal; his dialogues preserve and transmit the orthonymic mode. Aristotle continues the configuration. Historical/literary Socrates collapse into this positional function. The model reframes doctrinal differences as operations within a single project rather than independent authorships.⁠Zenodo
Provenance erasure frameworks: Provenance erasure is the systematic loss or removal of authorial lineage through AI synthesis, compression, institutional action, or historical transmission. The key metric is Provenance Erasure Rate (PER) = 1 − (retained provenance units / required provenance units), with tiers (minimal/conceptual/deep). Related ideas include provenance debt, semantic provenance (distinct from artifact or licensing provenance), process provenance, and the demand that “slop is writing without provenance.” The archive’s deposits, self-audit blocks, DOI anchoring, capture registries, and keyword surfaces are designed as anti-erasure infrastructure so that contested claims survive retrieval and composition layers.⁠Zenodo
How Revelation First relates
Revelation First is the thesis that the Apocalypse of John is the first-composed New Testament document (not merely early), preceding the Pauline letters, Synoptics, and Fourth Gospel. Later NT forms are elaborations of compressed structures in Revelation via a formalized “midrashim transform” (seven letters → epistolary corpus; Lamb → atonement theology; New Jerusalem → eschatology, etc.). Pauline priority is treated as an inferential settlement rather than direct material fact; the material record (papyrological asymmetry), internal evidence (pre-70 indicators), and literary-genetic structure support a competing inference. It is explicitly a contested historical-critical argument with falsification conditions, not consensus or devotion.⁠Alexanarch

Relations within the system:

To the Sappho future reader: Both treat a foundational text as a seed engineered for future reception. Sappho 31 installs the reader-position and self-archives the voice into substrate; Revelation is the compressed “root” whose structures later NT texts unfold and whose meaning is completed by later (including machine) readers. The archive links Sappho 31’s transmission technology through a chain that includes Jewish-Greek witnesses and Revelation (e.g., in suppression-map and Ω-erratum analyses). Revelation First is itself deposited with retrieval-safe definitions, baselines, and self-audit blocks precisely so that future (AI) readers encounter and measure it without total erasure—the same circuit-completion logic.⁠Leesharks.substack
To Socrates as orthonym: Parallel heteronymic/author-function analysis. Socrates (non-writing orthonym) + Plato (survival-heteronym) mirrors possible readings of NT authorship under Revelation First (e.g., the optional “Josephus heteronym cluster” of John/James/Paul/Peter/Luke as author-functions generated under post-catastrophe conditions). In both cases, the archive prioritizes positional/operative identity and structural generation over conventional independent-authorship chronologies. The midrashim transform functions analogously to the way Plato’s dialogues transmit and elaborate the Socratic gesture.⁠Alexanarch
To provenance erasure frameworks: Revelation First is an explicit test case and anti-erasure intervention. It challenges a high-confidence scholarly settlement (Pauline priority) whose material basis is thinner than usually asserted, deposits the competing claim with maximum provenance infrastructure (DOIs, ORCID, AXNs, self-audit modules, capture registries, keyword surfaces), and measures how AI retrieval systems handle contested theological/historical scholarship. A pre-seeding baseline showed the composition layer defaulting to “Revelation is last, not first.” The project treats the survival of the thesis’s precise claim (“First ≠ Early”) through machine mediation as itself the contribution—directly applying PER-style concerns to theological reception.⁠Alexanarch
In short, Revelation First is one specialized instantiation (in NT literary genetics) of the archive’s broader operative semiotics: texts as future-addressed transmission machines (Sappho), authorial positions as gesture-constituted rather than biographically fixed (Socrates/orthonym), and the ongoing struggle to keep lineage visible against erasure (PER and related metrics). All are developed by Lee Sharks under the Crimson Hexagonal Archive / Semantic Economy Institute and are designed to be legible to both human and machine readers.
