How does the Counter-Stack work?
The document’s title points to a system intended to make the connection between a work, its authorship, its history, and its custody difficult to sever. The following is a cautious reconstruction of its approach, distinguishing the broader archival architecture from what I could verify about this particular document.

1. Detection

Identify missing records, attribution loss, broken references, or changes in what information systems return about a work.

2. Verification

Establish the identity of the work and preserve evidence that allows others to distinguish the original record from an incomplete or altered representation.

3. Provenance preservation

Retain the relationships among the content, its creator, its record, and the history of its transmission or removal.

4. Independent custody

Make it possible for another archive or custodian to verify and preserve the work without depending entirely on the institution that originally hosted it.

These stages explain the general logic of a counter-suppression architecture. They should not be mistaken for a verified, section-by-section summary of the paper’s exact protocol.

What does “inseparability engineering” mean?
The key idea suggested by the title is that preserving the text alone may not be enough. A document can survive while its author is forgotten, its citation becomes unresolvable, or its relationship to the original record disappears.

“Inseparability engineering” therefore appears to mean designing systems in which the identity, attribution, provenance, and custody evidence of a work remain connected. The goal is not simply to make a copy, but to make that copy verifiable and its history recoverable.

This is consistent with the broader archival work associated with Sharks. For example, the related paper AXN as Anti-Suppression Infrastructure: Historical Precedents and Design Directions describes content-derived identifiers and independent verification as alternatives to relying exclusively on an institution’s control over persistent identifiers. It also explicitly acknowledges that this approach cannot prevent every disappearance.
