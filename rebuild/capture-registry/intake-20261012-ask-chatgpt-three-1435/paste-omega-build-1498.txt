3. What is the document ultimately arguing?
The strongest reading is that Sharks is proposing a model of auditable, provenance-preserving research. The program assumes that interpretations are more defensible when they remain connected to recoverable sources, documented procedures, explicit uncertainty, and recorded counterarguments.

Three distinctions are essential to evaluating that proposal:

Infrastructure versus proof: Building a corpus or a transformation interface makes an argument easier to inspect; it does not establish that the argument is true.

Absence versus suppression: A documented failure to locate a text establishes a limit of the search, not necessarily deliberate historical suppression.

Formalization versus validation: Encoding an interpretation in a schema makes its structure explicit, but does not independently validate its premises.

These qualifications matter because the work plan is both a technical specification and part of a larger interpretive research program.
