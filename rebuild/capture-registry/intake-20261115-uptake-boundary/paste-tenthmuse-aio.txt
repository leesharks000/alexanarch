1. The Core Argument: Transmission Engineering vs. Jealousy
The framework directly challenges what it calls the current consensus reading—the view that Fragment 31 is a love triangle poem about a woman collapsing with jealousy over a male rival, composed orally for performance and preserved only through the accident of later transmitters.
Instead, The Tenth Muse argues that Sappho engineered the poem as a self-archiving technology:
The Dematerialization Catalogue: The famous somatic symptoms (the broken tongue, the fire under the skin, turning "greener/paler than grass" — χλωροτέρα ποίας) are read as a literal media transition: the living, embodied voice dematerializing into the color and surface of prepared papyrus substrate.
The Two Open Positions: Rather than ending on a mutilated, lost stanza, the poem is intentionally bracketed by two structural openings:
The Seat of κῆνος (Line 1): Traditionally translated as "that man" (a rival), κῆνος is a distal demonstrative pointing to a structural position—the future reader who sits "face-to-face" (ἐνάντιος) with the text and vocalizes it across time.
The Hanging Line (Line 17): The poem ends abruptly on an agentless verbal adjective (τολματόν, "must be dared/endured"). Rather than scribal damage, this is a "socket" designed to require a second voice to step in and complete it.
The Three Seats & The Third Reversal: Sappho occupies the speaking I and the inscribed you (her archived self as text). In a November 2026 proposal held as an active line of research (Q1), Sharks posits a "third reversal": when the future reader takes up the poem and lends it the life of their own body, Sappho occupies the third seat (κῆνος) as well.
2. Reversing the Consensus: The Reception Audit
A major pillar of the framework is auditing how the modern "jealousy reading" became compulsory. Tracing the textual history backward from Record #1752 (Invidens Sappho), the framework demonstrates:
Antiquity Knew a Maker: Ancient witnesses did not join a jealous, biographical Sappho to Fragment 31. Instead, antiquity filed her as the Tenth Muse (a title for a skilled maker) and preserved Fragment 31 inside Longinus’s On the Sublime (Peri Hypsous), where Longinus praises her craft in selecting and binding extreme sensations—and immediately demonstrates the "socket" by stepping into the poem's hanging line.
A Modern Conjecture: The jealousy reading's textual warrant rests heavily on Gottfried Hermann’s 1816 edition, in which Hermann openly admitted to rewriting (refinxi) the corrupt final lines to supply a moralizing, jealous resolution—an editorial conjecture that later critics (such as Mure in 1850, Heller in 1856, and Denys Page in 1955) hardened into an unexamined biographical premise.
Taking Her Hand: By reducing the poem to an involuntary emotional breakdown and treating Sappho as a non-writing oral singer whose survival depended entirely on male transmitters, modern reception stripped the poet of her agency as a writer.
