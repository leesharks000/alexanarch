["Through Another's Mouth: A Dialogue between Heteronyms"](https://medium.com/@leesharks00/through-anothers-mouth-a-dialogue-between-heteronyms-0d882d5ccd3c) is a literary and philosophical dialogue written by Johannes Sigil, one of the twelve declared literary heteronyms (the Dodecad) created by author and theorist Lee Sharks. Published on November 24, 2026, the piece serves as the public companion to the essay From the Position of the Rejected (#1806). Overview & Narrative Structure

* Framing and Speakers. The dialogue unfolds as Johannes Sigil recounts a late-night event in the reading room of the [Crimson Hexagon Archive](https://www.leesharks.com/about/heteronyms) to another heteronym, Sparrow Wells. Sigil bases his account on written notes by Rebekah Cranes, contrasting them against a rival summary filed by Nobel Glas. [[1](https://philpapers.org/rec/SHASAI-7), [2](https://medium.com/@leesharks00/through-anothers-mouth-a-dialogue-between-heteronyms-0d882d5ccd3c)]
* The Conflict of Summarizers. Cranes and Glas act as competing chroniclers within the archive. Glas attempts to condense Aristotle's Politics into a single paragraph, cutting off Aristotle's famous observation about the person who lives outside the city before its crucial qualifying clause ("by nature or by chance"), prompting Cranes to interrupt him. [[1](https://medium.com/@leesharks00/through-anothers-mouth-a-dialogue-between-heteronyms-0d882d5ccd3c)]
* Lee Sharks's Intervention. Following the dispute over the summary, Lee Sharks enters the dialogue uninvited. He speaks strictly in his own verbatim words to recount what happened that night. [[1](https://medium.com/@leesharks00/through-anothers-mouth-a-dialogue-between-heteronyms-0d882d5ccd3c)]

Key Themes & Philosophical Concepts

* Classical Reception and Authorship. Sharks's monologue explores the voice he heard while reading Aristotle's Politics, examining how Athens was decentered from within and framing ancient thinkers as "citizen-heteronyms". [[1](https://medium.com/@leesharks00/through-anothers-mouth-a-dialogue-between-heteronyms-0d882d5ccd3c)]
* Textual Transmission. The piece traces the historical custody and survival of Aristotelian texts, referencing Rome's preservation of the books and the role of the ancient grammarian Tyrannion. [[1](https://medium.com/@leesharks00/through-anothers-mouth-a-dialogue-between-heteronyms-0d882d5ccd3c)]
* The Retrieval Layer and Voice. Connecting classical philosophy to modern archive theory, the dialogue examines the mechanics of speaking or writing "through another's mouth". It closes by returning to Aristotle's unresolved triad regarding those outside the political community—whether such a figure is a "beast, god, or by chance". [[1](https://medium.com/@leesharks00/through-anothers-mouth-a-dialogue-between-heteronyms-0d882d5ccd3c)]

Context in Lee Sharks's Work

* The Dodecad System. Within the Crimson Hexagon Archive, Lee Sharks operates through a twelve-position registry of heteronyms (`EA-HET-DODECAD-01`), following in the literary tradition of Fernando Pessoa and Søren Kierkegaard.
* Distinct Roles. Each heteronym carries a specific intellectual task rather than acting as a hidden pseudonym—for example, Sharks occupies the founder position, while [Johannes Sigil](https://www.restoredacademy.com/sigil) serves as the archivist and Arch-Philosopher.
