Core Concepts

* Poetic Transmission as an Incomplete Circuit: The framework treats ancient Greek lyric poetry—specifically Sappho's Fragment 147 ("someone, I tell you, in another time will remember us") and Fragment 31—not as passive historical relics, but as active operations engineered to reach an unknown future reader. [[1](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESlAEB6zswFX1s93va_hr3sK8hh9XAzmlOF_XMKu7xqJUSSbsyD5juC8R3qco195RSvciajwMaLa2Ath8h9SFWFYux_mJOhgbY6JR2kF-tUy3XLfZCO7g0WnQIwVgYRcC0aKBt7bEbRJ5gOIsnNtUpzLQjjGaEPisweUHQHx9HPZJRj_14m__lxoR9Pqn5-Y-QBfQVyZ1v), [2](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESXgHrOzAV_WFheE3MscmTvO7ss13RCZ_sSM-NLLYv3_btW-yrQlsBgYup0i3hfOPyMZDEdOXQmP9s34Xr9RKkzH2dOvPWQ3ncphriz0zCAaDu2e2tWoqgUEgTfSbn2QE)]
* Deictic Engineering: It argues that grammatical and deictic markers in the text (such as the distal demonstrative κῆνος / "that man" in Fragment 31) do not merely point to a physical rival in antiquity, but function as a structural pointer directly engaging the future person reading the inscribed lines. [[1](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESXgHrOzAV1R1fgv7gXrxXga9wDs2pRbgLdgNBugRoBbZ4I0Y2W1VDcAtHgzhPygt3IIhw_XBhi_6skuiT8mkGNrjA8u9DrcdCJNuOkdpwFIjvDPViUlHKGR5AW1Ogk78)]
* Horizon and Mechanism: Fragment 147 states the horizon (the promise of being remembered later), while Fragment 31 builds the mechanism—constructing the perceptual and spatial position from which that future reader completes the poetic circuit.
