The Muratorian Fragment notes that the apostle Paul wrote letters to seven churches, intentionally following the pattern or order of his "predecessor" John, who wrote to seven churches in the Book of Revelation.
YouTube·C. J. Cornthwaite +1
Key Points:

* The Seven Churches Pattern: The fragment states that Paul, "following the rule/order of his predecessor John," wrote by name to seven specific churches to symbolize a universal, catholic (global) reach to the church as a whole, mirroring John writing to seven churches in the Apocalypse.
YouTube·Rev Dr Paul Blackham +1
* Paul's List in the Fragment: The fragment enumerates Paul's letters to these seven churches (Corinthians, Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians, Galatians, Thessalonians, and Romans) as well as his personal/pastoral epistles (Philemon, Titus, and two to Timothy).
* Chronological/Canonical Logic: Rather than chronological or size-based ordering found later, the 2nd-century writer connects Paul’s epistolary corpus to John’s prophetic letters in Revelation to emphasize a symbolic completion of the number seven.

In his 1522 Preface to Revelation, Martin Luther expressed deep skepticism toward the Book of Revelation, placing it among the disputed antilegomena at the back of his New Testament. He famously doubted its apostolic or prophetic origins, complained that it failed to clearly teach Christ, and struggled with its cryptic and overwhelming imagery, admitting that his own spirit could not accommodate itself to the text. Luther's view softened in later editions, such as his 1530 and 1534 revisions, where he reinterpreted the book more favorably as a symbolic depiction of the church's spiritual struggle.
