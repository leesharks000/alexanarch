1. John 1:1–14 — The Incarnate Logos
Eternal Deity and Creation: "In the beginning was the Word (Logos), and the Word was with God, and the Word was God". John introduces Logos to show that the Son is eternal, distinct in personhood ("with God"), and fully divine ("was God"), through whom all things were created.
The Incarnation: "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us" (John 1:14). The abstract or cosmic concept of divine speech and wisdom took on human nature in Jesus of Nazareth, revealing the unseen God.

2. Revelation 19:13 — The Victorious Logos
The Title: In a vision of Christ’s return as a victorious warrior-king, John writes: "He is clothed in a robe dipped in blood, and his name is called The Word of God (ho Logos tou Theou)".
The Culmination: This bookends the Johannine writings. The one who first entered the world quietly in humility as the incarnate Word (Logos) in John 1 returns to execute final divine justice and speak the ultimate word against evil. 
3. The Thread Connecting Both Passages
Same Author/Language: Both writings are attributed to the Apostle John and utilize the Greek term logos to designate Jesus. 
Creation to Consummation: John 1 presents the Logos at creation and the incarnation; Revelation 19 presents the same Logos at the climax of redemptive history.
