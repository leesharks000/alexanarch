AI Overview
In the Book of Revelation, the Logos (rendered in English as "the Word of God") is not merely an abstract literary or rhetorical figure of speech, but a personified divine figure identified as Jesus Christ. 

Wikipedia
 +1
How the Logos Appears in Revelation
The Title: In Revelation 19:13, the rider on the white horse is explicitly described: "He is clothed with a robe dipped in blood, and His name is called The Word of God [ho logos tou theou]".
Identity: This figure is the same divine, preexistent person introduced in the Gospel of John (John 1:1) and the First Epistle of John (1 John 1:1), who becomes incarnate. 

Britannica
 +1
Role in the Text: Far from being a dry philosophical concept or literary device, the Logos in Revelation acts as a conquering divine warrior, king, and judge at the Second Coming (wearing many crowns and titled "King of Kings, and Lord of Lords"). 

Wikipedia
Literary vs. Theological Context
Apocalyptic Symbolism: The Book of Revelation is written in a highly symbolic and apocalyptic literary style, meaning the white horse, the robe dipped in blood, and the crowns are vision-driven imagery rather than literal wardrobe choices. 

EBSCO
 +1
Personification: Within that symbolic literature, the Logos functions as a real, acting protagonist/person—the divine person of Christ—rather than an impersonal literary tool used for rational persuasion (the classical rhetorical definition of logos).
