In his 1522 Preface to Revelation, Martin Luther expressed deep skepticism toward the Book of Revelation, placing it among the disputed antilegomena at the back of his New Testament. He famously doubted its apostolic or prophetic origins, complained that it failed to clearly teach Christ, and struggled with its cryptic and overwhelming imagery, admitting that his own spirit could not accommodate itself to the text. Luther's view softened in later editions, such as his 1530 and 1534 revisions, where he reinterpreted the book more favorably as a symbolic depiction of the church's spiritual struggle.
