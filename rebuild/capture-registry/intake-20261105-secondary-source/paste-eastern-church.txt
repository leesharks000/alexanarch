In the Eastern Orthodox Church, the Book of Revelation is considered part of the biblical canon, but it is not included in the official lectionary (the fixed cycle of daily and weekly liturgical readings used during church services).

·r/OrthodoxChristianity
 +1
The reasons for its exclusion from public liturgical reading include:
Fear of Misinterpretation and Sensationalism: Early and medieval church leaders recognized that Revelation's vivid, apocalyptic symbolism was frequently misused to fuel wild speculations about the end of the world, leading to confusion and false teachings among the faithful. 
Association with Chiliasm: In the early East, the book was heavily tied to chiliasm (the belief in a literal, earthly 1,000-year reign of Christ), which the Eastern Church viewed with theological caution and skepticism. 

Ancient Faith Blogs
Difficulty for Public Worship: The liturgical services of the Orthodox Church are designed for prayer, formation, and spiritual clarity. The cryptic, surreal, and catastrophic imagery of Revelation was deemed less suitable for congregational reading compared to the Gospels, Epistles, and Old Testament texts.
Timing of Canonization Relative to the Lectionary: Some historical and liturgical analyses note that the lectionary cycle was largely fixed and solidified in the Christian East before Revelation achieved universal acceptance and integration into the Eastern canon. 
Though it is never read aloud during the Divine Liturgy, the Eastern Church does not reject the book; instead, its profound and cosmic symbolism heavily influences Orthodox iconography, hymnography, and the way the Church mystical-symbolically approaches the liturgy itself.
