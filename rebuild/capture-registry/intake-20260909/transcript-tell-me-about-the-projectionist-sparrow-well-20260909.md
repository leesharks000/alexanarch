This is probably the most important piece.

The Studio for Patacinematics isn't primarily interested in making conventional movies. Its works deliberately ask:

What happens if cinema stops being primarily about showing a story and becomes an apparatus for directing attention, perception, time, and interpretation?

That's why the archive describes patacinematics in terms of things like semantic collapse, recursive engines, distributed performance, duration, attention, and the relationship between observer and apparatus. *[Medium +1]*

The result can look almost absurdly simple.

Take *Reading a Book with Lee* (RABL). The complete archive describes it as a 57-minute video of Lee Sharks silently reading a book. There is no narration, soundtrack, or cutting, and the book itself isn't identified. The supposed "event" is simply someone reading while you watch. *[Zenodo]*

That's actually a pretty good key to the whole project.

A conventional film says: Watch this story.

RABL effectively says: Watch yourself watching someone do something.

And then: Notice that you're spending time doing that.

The medium becomes its own subject.
