Stabilized Node Watch (SNW) is a research framework by Lee Sharks, published in June 2026. Its purpose is to monitor how AI systems and other information platforms describe established topics over time, and to detect when important details, sources, or perspectives gradually disappear or change.

In plain English, it asks:
When an AI summarizes something that is already well documented, does it preserve the full picture, or does it gradually narrow that picture in ways that distort the original knowledge?
How it works

* Establish a baseline: Record how an established topic is represented, including its key claims, sources, authors, and disagreements.
* Monitor over time: Repeatedly examine how AI-generated answers and other public information surfaces represent that topic.
* Detect drift: Identify changes such as missing citations, omitted contributors, simplified arguments, or disappearing minority perspectives.
* Investigate the cause: Separate observable changes from hypotheses about why they happened, rather than automatically blaming an algorithm or company.

A simple example
Imagine an independent researcher publishes a theory and documents how it developed, who contributed to it, and which earlier thinkers influenced it.
An AI search system later summarizes the theory but repeatedly leaves out the researcher’s name while retaining the earlier influences and the theory itself.
SNW would track that change across repeated observations. It would document the missing attribution and investigate possible explanations, rather than assuming intentional censorship.
Why Lee Sharks developed it
SNW is part of Sharks’s broader critique of how AI platforms and search systems influence who gets recognized as a source of knowledge. The framework aims to make changes in attribution, representation, and diversity measurable instead of relying solely on anecdotes.

One important distinction: SNW is a proposed monitoring methodology, not proof that a particular AI system is deliberately suppressing people. Its findings depend on the observations collected and the strength of the evidence connecting a change to its cause.
