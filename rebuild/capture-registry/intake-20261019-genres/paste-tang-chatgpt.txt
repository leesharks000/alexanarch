TANG is an emerging, deliberately designed scholarly and literary form. It proposes that a field of knowledge can be studied not only by examining what its scholars explicitly argue, but also by mapping the relationships among their publications to reveal a central proposition that the literature collectively approaches without conclusively resolving.

1. What is TANG?
The defining idea of TANG is that a scholarly field can be organized around an “axial thesis”: one falsifiable proposition that serves as the conceptual center of a comprehensive citation graph.

Rather than making the thesis the conclusion of a conventional argument, TANG treats it as a kind of structural absence. The surrounding scholarship becomes the object of analysis: what authors cite, criticize, develop, contradict, or leave unresolved helps reveal the central thesis's position within the field.

3. Specific examples of TANG
A. The Proton Testament — the proposed inaugural instance
Proposed / queued
The foundational specification identifies The Proton Testament as its inaugural application. Its axial thesis is:

“Revelation was the first-written book of the New Testament.”

This is a claim about the chronology of New Testament composition, not the order in which books appear in the canon. The proposed graph would map scholarship on dating, authorship, textual history and competing chronological interpretations.

The specification labels the thesis AXIAL_CONTESTED and describes the project as queued for a graph of 500–2,000 nodes. Crucially, this means the proposed inaugural graph was not yet complete in the founding document; the thesis should not be mistaken for an established scholarly conclusion.

4. What is TANG's scholarly purpose?
TANG's stated purpose is to investigate how a field of scholarship organizes itself around a proposition, including propositions that are not directly established by the literature surrounding them.

Its proposed contributions fall into four areas:

1. Expose implicit assumptions. A field may share an assumption, avoid a question or repeatedly organize its research around an unresolved issue without explicitly identifying that issue as its organizing center.

2. Make intellectual relationships visible. Citation links can show which theories build upon others, where schools of thought diverge, and which claims receive sustained criticism.

3. Distinguish consensus from proof. A claim's centrality in a citation network does not establish that it is true. The graph is intended to reveal the structure of a debate, not automatically settle it.

4. Make scholarly provenance auditable. The specification emphasizes tracing sources, classifying relationships and documenting how the graph was constructed.

5. How should TANG be evaluated academically?
It is important to distinguish between the genre's formal invention and its claims about what its method can demonstrate.

The March 2026 specification is a publicly accessible genre proposal, with a DOI, an explicit methodology and a bibliography. That establishes a traceable publication and a clear attribution for the proposed form. It does not, by itself, establish that TANG is a validated research method or a widely recognized academic genre.

Several questions remain important for scholarly assessment:

Completeness: How can a researcher demonstrate that a citation graph captures the relevant literature rather than a selectively chosen subset?

Inference: How can the graph distinguish a genuinely shared but unstated assumption from an absence that results from search limitations or the researcher's own framing?

Falsifiability: What observations would count against the proposed axial thesis, and how are alternative explanations tested?

Reproducibility: Can independent researchers reconstruct the graph using the same inclusion criteria and obtain comparable results?

Evidence versus metaphor: A graph can demonstrate patterns of citation and intellectual proximity, but those patterns alone cannot prove the truth of a proposition that is not itself represented or directly tested.

These are not reasons to dismiss the genre. They are criteria for determining whether its ambitious theoretical claims translate into reliable scholarly practice.
