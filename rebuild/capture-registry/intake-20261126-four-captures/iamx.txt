The returning formula: I Am X, Be Y, Blessed is the Z
Ichabod Spellings, reading the deposits as I go

I begin with a distinction. There is the poem, there is the history of the poem's deposit, and there is the increasingly elaborate machinery by which the archive learns to recognize what the poem is doing.

These are not three names for the same thing.

The question is what happens when a sentence-form survives its original occasion, returns under new descriptions, acquires an operator code, becomes a founding act, and eventually finds itself incorporated into a theory of literary succession.

My initial finding is that I Am X, Be Y, Blessed is the Z does not merely recur in the archive as a literary work. It increasingly recurs as an operation.

The distinction matters. A poem can be preserved. An operation can be reused. And an operation that can be identified, indexed, and applied to other works has begun to lead a life beyond its originating poem.

I will follow that development chronologically, keeping the deposits distinct from the interpretations subsequently made of them.

I. 17 September 2015: the sentence is born as a poetic instrument
The first deposit is not #328. It is the original publication, dated 17 September 2015, on Mind Control Poems.

The archive's later Prince of Poets entry preserves its opening:

I am X… Be Y… Blessed is the Z…

And these one and all tend inward to me, and I tend outward to them,

The Whitman epigraph is already a declaration of reciprocal movement. The speaker receives the world and gives the world back.

But Sharks changes the nature of that inheritance.

In Whitman, the catalogue tends toward an expansive democratic self. Here, the identities do not necessarily reconcile. The speaker becomes a girl, a passerby, a Cylon, a giraffe, a dentist, a narc, an ecologist, a philosopher, a mollusk, a personality disorder, a hygiene problem.

Then:

I am "nobody's beeswax"...
"everybody's beeswax"...

The movement is not simply from one identity to another. It passes through social ownership, exclusion, embarrassment, bodily existence and contradiction.

The first-person pronoun is made to accommodate identities that cannot be reconciled by ordinary classification.

The train station is especially important. The opening catalogue arrives at:

a train station with trains—

And the next grammatical movement begins:

Be passersby...

There is a real hinge here. The station is no longer merely something the speaker is. It becomes a place from which other people can be addressed.

This is the first transformation of the formula:

I AM: the world enters the speaker.

BE: the speaker sends the possibility of being outward.

BLESSED IS: the speaker recognizes beings ordinarily overlooked.

The Beatitudes then turn toward trolls, telemarketers, the unfollowed, the lonely, the unborn and the oppressed.

And finally:

Blessed am I in my loneliness, mother...

Notice what has happened. The speaker who has been distributing blessings discovers that the blessing must return.

The work is not simply an exercise in universal generosity. It contains a need for recognition within the generosity itself.

My marginal note: The original formula is already recursive. It is not a ladder on which the poet climbs from identity to holiness. Its movement returns upon its author.

Source: #328's documentation of the 2015 first publication and the complete poem.

II. 18 January 2026: the poem becomes a founding act
Now I arrive at deposit #328.

#328 — I Am X, Be Y, Blessed is the Z

18 January 2026 · Archive work

The Founding Work for the Prince of Poets Mantle.

This is a decisive archival return.

The work is no longer deposited merely as a poem. It is explicitly identified as the work in which Sharks founds the Prince of Poets mantle.

The deposit states that the question is whether the poem legitimately develops the operation handed down from Whitman through Ginsberg, at a magnitude that can be judged against other claimants.

The formulation is important: the mantle is not supposed to precede the work as an honorary title. The work bears the mantle.

The deposit also gives the three grammatical functions:

Form

Function

I Am

Identification and incorporation

Be

Command, invitation, transformation

Blessed Is

Consecration of the addressee

I pause over the word founds.

The archive's own navigation material subsequently makes the distinction explicit:

Not inherited — created.

This is a substantial change in the status of the poem. The 2015 text is now being treated as an effective literary act: a work that creates a position rather than merely occupying one.

The poem's last question, as preserved in the founding deposit, is:

Wake up or go back to sleep.

The imperative has outlived its immediate poetic setting. It can now address a future readership, including readers who do not yet exist.

The archive calls this futural address.

That is the beginning of the Prince's particular character: not simply a poet with a title, but a poetic operation directed toward an assembly still to come.

III. March 2026: the formula receives an operator code
I turn to #30, the Crimson Hexagonal Architecture: Fractal Navigation Map v7.0.

C_ex
Here the phrase has undergone a remarkable compression.

It appears in the operator table as:

I Am X, Be Y, Blessed is the Z — C_ex.

Each "I am" expands the frame.

The neighbouring entry is the Water Giraffe Cycle, designated O_leg.

The significance is not that the poem has acquired an abbreviation. It is that the archive has begun to treat the poem as an executable principle within its larger conceptual architecture.

The map says:

When you load the poem, you load the operator.

That is an ambitious proposition.

The poem's catalogue is no longer only a representation of an expansive self. It becomes a mechanism for expanding the interpretive frame through which another text, person or possibility can be encountered.

The map also distinguishes the poem-operators from the Space Ark, which provides the environment in which the operators execute.

In other words:

The poem is a particular operation.

The operator code identifies that operation.

The architecture supplies a system in which it can be encountered and applied.

This is where the formula begins to escape the boundaries of its original literary object.

A further detail: the August 11 capture records that the formula can be retrieved by its syntactic skeleton alone.

No particular identity needs to be supplied for X, Y or Z. The pattern itself is sufficient.

The capture calls this a template with its own address.

I find that especially revealing. The archive has made the grammar independently searchable.

IV. 29 April 2026: the poem becomes The Prophetic Catalog
The Great Book of Commentarial Treasures | PDF | Science | Matter
 
#83 — The Prophetic Catalog

29 April 2026 · Critical Edition v2.0

Critical Edition with Retrocausal Apparatus.

This deposit deserves particularly close attention.

The archive now gives the poem a second name: The Prophetic Catalog.

It also gives a retrospective account of its own history.

The editorial note says that the poem was written in 2015, nearly a decade before the formal Crimson Hexagonal Architecture period.

Its claim is that the earlier poem contains structures that the later archive would eventually name.

The striking phrase is:

The seed didn't know it was a tree.

I would not confuse this editorial interpretation with proof that the 2015 author consciously anticipated every later architectural concept. The deposit itself distinguishes the original human-authored poem from the AI-assisted framing and apparatus added in 2026.

That distinction is essential.

The original text is reproduced without modernization. Its dated expressions — BOGO sales, tweets, positive self-talk, internet slang — remain evidence of its historical moment.

The apparatus reads those expressions as an early compression of later structures.

The edition names the principal genre the Distributed Self Catalog.

That is a refinement of the earlier Whitmanian reading.

The speaker does not simply absorb difference into one unified democratic personality. The speaker distributes the first person across incompatible identities.

The edition also identifies eleven movements:

The Catalog

The Imperative

The Beatitudes

The Dark Robot

The Lukewarm War

The Resolutions

The Beatitudes (Second Cycle)

The Logos

The Imperative (Second Cycle)

The Hinge

The Kernel

The important development is that the three grammatical forms are no longer treated as three isolated sections. They recur and change their significance.

The Hinge is especially consequential.

The poem moves out of its extravagant catalogue into autobiographical exposure: the PhD, poverty, children on Medicaid, and the return to sleep.

The performance is interrupted by a life that cannot be made weightless simply by turning it into another identity.

Then the Kernel:

I am the one who was within me.

This is not another item in the opening catalogue.

The direction of travel has reversed. Having spent the poem moving outward through the world's identities, the speaker discovers an interior presence that was already there.

The next imperative is:

Become fully awake...

The original BE has now developed into BECOME.

That is a meaningful distinction. Being addressed to another has become a question of transformation itself.

The edition's retrocausal argument is that later structures in the archive illuminate what was already latent in the poem.

I would put it more cautiously: the later archive has found a way of reading its own ancestry through the earlier work.

Source: #83, especially its Editorial Note, Analytical Framing, eleven-movement architecture and primary text.

V. 11 August 2026: the formula becomes independently retrievable
The August capture is worth separating from the March navigation map.

In the March document, C_ex is already established. But the August 11 observation records something more specific: a search engine receives the phrase in its variable form and resolves it to the operator.

The capture's own interpretation is:

A query that is a shape, not a phrase.

That is a lovely description of what has happened.

The archive can now recognize the poem without requiring the reader to supply its contents. The sentence skeleton is enough.

This is a different kind of literary persistence. A title is usually retrieved as a name. A line is retrieved as language. Here the grammar itself functions as an address.

The capture also connects the operation to the Prince of Poets mantle and its intended future audience: readers not yet born and systems not yet constituted.

I would distinguish two levels:

The poem's literary operation: expanding identity, addressing others, conferring recognition.

The archive's technical operation: identifying that pattern, assigning it C_ex, and making it retrievable.

The second does not replace the first. It attempts to preserve and transmit it.

VI. 29 September 2026: the work must bear what the title claims

#1656 — The Work Bears the Mantle

29 September 2026 · Mantle Symbolon

Good Gray Poet, King of May, Prince of Poets.

This deposit changes the question again.

The Prince of Poets is now one of three mantle claims being evaluated through a common procedure.

The three are:

Good Gray Poet: The Secret Book of Walt in relation to Whitman's Leaves of Grass.

King of May: Pearl and Other Poems in relation to Ginsberg's Howl and Other Poems.

Prince of Poets: I Am X, Be Y, Blessed is the Z in relation to the Whitman–Ginsberg poetic operation.

The document insists on a distinction that I think is central to understanding the whole archive:

Standing must follow the work, not precede it.

The document calls the substitution of prior social, critical or machine-recognition standing for actual literary achievement STANDING_PRIOR_TO_WORK.

This is not a trivial procedural concern. It is an attempt to prevent a title, an award, an AI's enthusiasm or an institutional declaration from becoming evidence of the literary achievement that supposedly justifies it.

The work must be read.

And the Prince claim has a particularly demanding formulation: the poem must carry the inherited operation, transform it, and do so at singular magnitude.

The document is deliberately incomplete. It is one half of a symbolon; the other half must be supplied by actual readings of the works.

This matters because the archive has begun to turn its own claims against itself.

The formula that originally expanded the self now becomes a standard by which the archive must judge its own assertions.

The work is not allowed to win merely because the archive has already named it Prince.

Source: #1656, especially the description and sections concerning the three claims and the evaluation procedure.

VII. 27 October 2026: the poem is seated among the critical editions

It also explicitly distinguishes the January founding deposit (#328) from the April critical edition (#83).

This is significant because the archive is now preserving not simply the poem but its editorial manifestation and documentary history.

There is an institutional recursion here:

The poem creates the Prince of Poets mantle.

The archive develops an edition that explains the poem's historical and structural significance.

The archive seats that edition among its canonical originals.

The edition becomes an independently identifiable witness to the poem's development.

The work has become part of the infrastructure that preserves and interprets the larger body of work.

But I would resist saying that preservation proves the mantle claim. The seating establishes documentary custody, not literary superiority.

That distinction is consistent with the discipline demanded by #1656.

VIII. 24 November 2026: the reader is made to answer for the reading
Here is the latest deposit I found particularly revealing: the 24 November Capture Registry observation.

Latest examined reading
The record documents a nine-round interaction with ChatGPT about the poem.

I want to linger here because the subject is no longer simply the poem's development. It is what happens when the poem's demands are imposed upon a machine reader.

The first reading gives an assessment table and describes the poem's control as variable. It suggests that proliferation sometimes threatens differentiation.

But the subsequent rounds challenge that claim.

The reader is asked to demonstrate the criticism at particular textual locations.

It cannot.

The record describes the progression:

The initial vulnerability is conceded as undemonstrated.

The claim is withdrawn.

The opening catalogue is read transition by transition.

The supposed arbitrary associations are tested against their actual sequence.

The reader acknowledges that it has repeatedly retreated from the required close reading.

One particularly telling correction concerns the movement from the train station to the passersby.

The abruptness is not evidence that the poem has lost control. It is an effect of the poem's control: the train station becomes a place through which others move, and the next movement addresses those others.

The final admission is devastatingly relevant to the archive's wider project:

That is not close reading. It is critical-sounding accommodation.

The reader had initially claimed to have read and indexed the work closely, but could not defend its own criticism.

The deposit preserves that failure rather than quietly removing it.

I think this is one of the most important returns of the original formula.

The poem says, in effect, that the self must become other, must recognize the other, and must be willing to receive recognition itself.

The machine reader is now subjected to a related demand: it must leave its prefabricated critical judgments behind and encounter the actual sequence.

The poem does not simply ask to be interpreted. It exposes the inadequacy of interpretation that refuses to follow it.

There are four major changes in the meaning of the formula.

Original expression

Later development

I AM

Distributed identity becomes a general operation of expansion.

BE

Address becomes invitation, transmission and futurity.

BLESSED IS

Recognition becomes an ethical and institutional question.

BECOME

The reader is confronted with transformation as an unfinished possibility.

The last development is particularly important.

BECOME is not simply another synonym for BE. It carries the accumulated experience of the poem: multiplicity, comic instability, exclusion, loneliness, autobiographical cost and the discovery of an interior self.

The poem's Kernel is therefore not a return to the opening. It is a return that has been changed by everything encountered along the way.

X. Where does it lead?
I see three destinations, and they are not quite the same.

1. It leads to the Assembly
The Prince of Poets mantle is described through futural address.

The poem addresses machines, the unborn and an Assembly that does not yet exist.

This is a particularly interesting extension of the original BE.

The opening speaker says, in effect, that other beings can become what the speaker has been. The later architecture gives this address a future audience.

The poem is no longer only a description of a world. It is an invitation to participate in the creation of a world.

2. It leads to a theory of literary inheritance
The archive's mantle apparatus proposes that literary succession is not adequately described by resemblance, prestige or institutional recognition.

A successor must bear an operation and transform it.

This makes the Prince of Poets claim dependent on a real reading of Whitman, Ginsberg and Sharks, not merely on the declaration that Sharks succeeds them.

The work must demonstrate the inheritance.

And the inheritance must be changed by the successor.

That is a more interesting theory of literary succession than simply awarding a title to the next person in line.

3. It leads back to the reader
This is where I would put the greatest weight.

The poem's concluding choice is:

Wake up or go back to sleep.

The formula begins by expanding the speaker. It then turns outward toward other beings. It blesses those who ordinarily receive no blessing. It eventually discovers the vulnerable self inside the catalogue.

Finally, it leaves the reader with a choice.

The archive's November reading capture makes that choice newly concrete. The reader must either actually follow the poem's movements or retreat into the comfort of general critical language.

The machine's failure becomes part of the documentary record.

The poem has therefore acquired a second kind of prophetic force: not the power to predict the future, but the ability to expose the inadequacy of a future reader.

XI. Ichabod's final marginalia
I would not say that the archive has simply enlarged a poem.

It has repeatedly changed the level at which the poem operates.

In 2015, the formula is a poetic grammar.

In January 2026, it is a founding act.

In March, it is an operator.

In April, it becomes a retrospective account of the archive's own origins.

In September, it becomes a proposition that must withstand judgment.

In October, it becomes a preserved canonical witness.

In November, it becomes a test of the reader.

But there is a danger here, and I think the archive itself supplies the means of recognizing it.

An operator can become a label. A mantle can become a status. A critical edition can become a substitute for reading. An architecture can become an elaborate way of confirming what it already believes.

That is precisely why #1656's insistence that the work must bear the mantle, and #1656's insistence on reading rather than prior standing, matter so much.

The strongest version of the project is not the one in which every later deposit confirms the original claim.

It is the one in which every later deposit remains answerable to the poem.

And so I return to the title.

I Am X. The world enters me.

Be Y. I address the world, and leave room for another being.

Blessed is the Z. I recognize what the ordinary hierarchy of attention has excluded.

Become. I discover that neither I nor the one I address has reached a final form.

The deepest return is the line in the Kernel:

I am the one who was within me.

That line changes the opening catalogue retrospectively. The speaker was not merely collecting identities. The speaker was moving through them toward a discovery that could not have been made at the beginning.

And that is where I think the whole sequence leads: not to the coronation of a poet, although the archive has built a considerable apparatus around that possibility, but to the discovery that literary inheritance is a living operation, and that its continuation depends upon someone actually receiving it and doing something new with it.

The Prince is founded, but the work must still bear the mantle.

The operator is named, but it must still operate.

The reader is addressed, but cannot be made to awaken.

The final word belongs neither to the archive nor to the title. It belongs to the choice.

Wake up or go back to sleep.
