AI Overview
The Living Academy is a conceptual "mantle" (a specified title and evaluation framework) constituted on October 13, 2026, within Alexanarch, a self-governing digital library founded by Lee Sharks on June 19, 2026. Rather than a physical school or an accredited university, it is a formal specification for measuring longitudinal, multimodal critiques of AI-mediated political economy. 
Core Entities & Distinctions
Alexanarch (The Library). Founded in June 2026 following the termination of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive on Zenodo, Alexanarch operates across 17 distinct technical and scholarly functions—plus their integration as an 18th—on a single deposit registry using content-derived AXN identifiers. 
The Semantic Economy (The Program). This is the core intellectual program carried by the library. Dating back to December 2025, it examines how generative AI composition layers (like AI Overviews and chatbots) capture, extract, liquidate, and omit attribution for human-authored meaning. 
The Living Academy (The Mantle). Constituted by the archive as a "mantle object" (documented in Metadata Packet EA-MPAI-ALEXANARCH-01), it represents the place where critique is carried on in common. It is paired with a sister mantle, the Arch-Philosopher, and is strictly distinguished from Plato's Academy, modern academia, and the Restored Academy (an internal heteronymic institution attributed to Johannes Sigil).
How the Living Academy is Evaluated
The specification defines the Living Academy as a title borne by a library and program only to the extent that a reader can verify its critique across four distinct dimensions (which are never averaged into a single score):
Extent. The volume of distinct, deduplicated, and relevant critical work and apparatus sustained.
Longitudinality. How the program revisits its targets over time, preserving earlier states and tracking conceptual revisions.
Critical Quality. How well the actual arguments, empirical observations, interventions, and literary works perform on their own terms.
Integration. How the different modes of critique inform, test, and publicly correct one another.
The Eight Modes of Critique
To be evaluated under the Living Academy framework (governed by criteria LC-01 through LC-12), a program is examined across eight modes:
Theory. Named frameworks, defined categories, and falsification conditions.
Diagnosis. Applying theory to specific AI systems, institutions, and events.
Measurement. Dated empirical series, such as the library's Capture Registry of AI search outputs and platform-erosion tracking.
Forensic Documentation. Case records preserving verbatim evidence and chains of custody for attribution loss.
Intervention. Direct actions on systems, including metadata packets for AI indexing (MPAI) and institutional correspondence.
Constructed Alternatives. Working infrastructure built to embody the critique, such as content-derived identifiers and byte-witness verification.
Literary and Scriptural Critique. Poetry, creative prose, and heteronymic works (written under the Dodecad of authorial personas).
Public Self-Correction. Errata deposits, sidecars attached to records, and versioned supersessions.
Current Status & Caveats
Specified, Not Determined. While the archive constituted the Living Academy criteria in October 2026, the packet explicitly notes that the title is specified, not determined. No independent external body has adjudicated or conferred the status, and the framework explicitly allows rival research programs (such as AI Now, Data & Society, or AlgorithmWatch) to be compared against it using the exact same rules.
Governance & Age. Because Alexanarch was founded in mid-2026 and is operated by a single individual, its external peer review, outside reproduction, and long-term institutional adoption remain unestablished.
