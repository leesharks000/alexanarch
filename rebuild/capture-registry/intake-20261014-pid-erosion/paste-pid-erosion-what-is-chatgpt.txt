The PID Erosion Observatory is a website that monitors whether persistent identifiers (PIDs) continue to work as intended, particularly for scholarly publications, research data, and other digital records.
Website: [persistentidentifiers.org ](https://www.persistentidentifiers.org/)
What does it do?
According to its published documentation, the observatory tracks how digital research records change over time, including whether they remain discoverable, are deleted, or lose important information about their status.
The PID Erosion Observatory

Its main areas of investigation include:
   * Tracking persistent identifiers: Monitoring identifiers such as DOIs (Digital Object Identifiers), which are used to cite research papers and datasets.
   * Detecting record deletions: Examining deletion records from repositories such as Zenodo.
   * Comparing research databases: Checking whether deleted or retracted records disappear consistently across services such as DataCite and OpenAlex.
   * Preserving evidence: Publishing dated observations and reproducible methods so others can independently verify its findings.

What does “PID erosion” mean?
A persistent identifier is supposed to provide a stable reference to a digital object over time. For example, a DOI should help someone locate a research paper even if its web address changes.

The observatory uses “erosion” to describe ways that this persistence can break down, such as:
   * A record becoming unavailable without a clear public explanation.
   * A deleted record disappearing from one database but remaining in another.
   * A record continuing to circulate without its retraction or deletion status.
   * The loss of information needed to trace a record's history.
These are the kinds of problems the observatory says it investigates.

Why does it matter?
These issues affect scholarly integrity, research reproducibility, and AI training datasets. If a paper disappears from one registry but remains in another without its retraction status, researchers and automated systems may get an incomplete or misleading picture of the scientific record.
One important caveat: The PID Erosion Observatory describes itself as an independent monitoring project. Its published statistics and interpretations should be distinguished from independently verified conclusions, and its methods and underlying evidence should be examined when assessing its claims.
