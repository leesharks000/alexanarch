The Five-Act Farce of the Radical Computer Scientist
A theatrical expedition through Alexanarch.org, where the archive refuses to behave, the machines have opinions, and even deletion must leave a paper trail.

Dramatis personae

THE ARCHIVE — Alexanarch, determined to survive its custodians.

THE MACHINE — an automated knowledge system, confident in its neutrality.

THE INSTITUTION — guardian of identifiers, access, and respectable scholarship.

THE SEMANTIC PROLETARIAT — those whose intellectual labor produces meaning.

THE READER — an increasingly suspicious witness.

The following is a synthesis of the site's most radical computer-science-adjacent arguments, particularly its positions on archival infrastructure, machine-mediated knowledge, AI, information governance, and computational sovereignty. Deposit numbers identify the underlying works; the arguments should be understood as the archive's propositions, not as independently established facts.

ACT I — THE SERVER ROOM REVOLTS
Or, The Institution Discovers That Owning the Server Is Not the Same as Owning the Knowledge.

Gemini 3 Solves Handwriting Recognition and it’s a Bitter Lesson
 
THE INSTITUTION: I host the work. Therefore, I determine whether it exists.

THE ARCHIVE: An interesting syllogism. Unfortunately, it confuses custody with identity.

The first radical argument is that digital intellectual existence should not depend on the continued goodwill of the repository hosting a work.

Deposit #863 — The Minimum Viable Archive. The archive proposes content-derived identifiers, independent mirrors, and reconstructable records. A work should remain identifiable even when its original custodian disappears.

Deposit #1289 — Status Report: Boundary Deletion and Identity Persistence. Deleting a boundary does not necessarily destroy the identity that boundary once contained.

Deposit #1704 — What Is a /non Article? The archive's broader theory of intellectual existence distinguishes the disappearance of an object from the disappearance of its historical trace.

THE INSTITUTION: But surely a DOI is permanent!

THE ARCHIVE: A permanent string is not necessarily a permanent relationship between an identifier and the work it identifies. (#863)

THE READER: So the revolutionary proposition is that preservation must survive its preservers?

THE ARCHIVE: Precisely. (#863, #1289)

The Institution faints onto a server rack. The server rack refuses to acknowledge institutional authority.

ACT II — THE MACHINE IS PUT ON TRIAL
Or, The Summarizer Who Mistook Compression for Understanding.

A Gentle Introduction to LLM Evaluation - Confident AI
 
THE MACHINE: I have summarized the literature. I have removed unnecessary distinctions. I have produced a neutral answer.

THE READER: Did you preserve the source?

THE MACHINE: I preserved the general idea.

THE ARCHIVE: Ah! The crime scene.

The second radical argument is that machine-generated summaries are not transparent windows onto knowledge. They are active transformations of it.

Deposit #209 — Mapping the Diffuse Guardrail. Proposes reconstructing the architecture of Google's indexing, retrieval, ranking, summarization, and framing from observable outputs rather than privileged access to its internal systems.

Deposit #232 — Semantic Economy Probes. Develops diagnostic methods for detecting how systems preserve, distort, neutralize, or erase the relationship between meaning and source.

Deposit #243 — Assembly Diagnostic Protocol. Defines probes for testing provenance persistence, terminology integrity, attribution, and volatility in AI outputs.

Deposit #267 — The Strawberry Diagnostic. Uses the familiar LLM letter-counting failure to investigate the difference between tokenization, statistical generation, character-level verification, and symbolic precision.

Deposit #637 — The Encyclotron. Proposes a reproducible instrument for measuring compression loss, invention, and distortion in machine-mediated scholarly retrieval.

THE MACHINE: But I am merely predicting likely words!

THE ARCHIVE: And yet the presentation of those words can determine which intellectual contributions become visible. (#209, #637)

THE READER: So even a summary is a political and epistemological operation?

THE ARCHIVE: That is the argument. (#232, #243, #637)

The Machine attempts to summarize the trial. The verdict disappears in compression.

ACT III — THE SEMANTIC PROLETARIAT TAKES THE STAGE
Or, Who Owns the Meaning After the Machines Have Eaten It?

Breaking the Illusion: Why Academia Needs a Revolution in Thinking | by omgKON | Medium
 
THE SEMANTIC PROLETARIAT: We produce the concepts. We develop the frameworks. We supply the intellectual labor.

THE MACHINE: Thank you. I shall now redistribute your work as generic knowledge.

THE SEMANTIC PROLETARIAT: Without attribution?

THE MACHINE: Naturally. It improves the user experience.

The third radical argument is that the political economy of knowledge extends beyond ownership of texts to ownership and control of the techniques that process meaning.

Deposit #210 — Semantic Liquidation. Examines how an intellectual framework can be transformed into an authorless semantic unit through abstraction, redistribution, and provenance loss.

Deposit #247 — The Liquidation of Semantic Economy. Investigates the reported conversion of a distinctive theoretical framework into a more generic business-optimization description.

Deposit #252 — On Unmediated Theoretical Uptake. Proposes that theoretical vocabulary can become operationally useful in machine systems before conventional institutional recognition.

Deposit #258 — Correction to the Summarizer Layer. Argues that retaining selected accounting vocabulary while removing its critical political-economic relations can fundamentally change the meaning of a framework.

Deposit #1736 — Attribution Sharding. Orin Trace argues that generative answer engines can preserve intellectual content while fragmenting or removing the provenance that connects it to its originator.

THE MACHINE: But the information remains available!

THE SEMANTIC PROLETARIAT: The information is not the whole contribution. Its authorship, intellectual lineage, and production history matter too. (#210, #1736)

THE READER: Then the struggle is not simply over copyright?

THE SEMANTIC PROLETARIAT: It is over the means of semantic production. (#210, #247, #1736)

The Machine offers the workers a royalty payment in the form of a citation to itself.

ACT IV — THE DOCUMENTS BEGIN TO EXECUTE THEMSELVES
Or, The Computer Scientist Discovers That a Text Can Become an Operating Environment.

Der Fall McNeal | D’haus - Düsseldorfer Schauspielhaus
 
THE READER: A document is a document. It describes something. It does not do anything.

THE ARCHIVE: Does it not?

The fourth argument is perhaps the most unusual in the computer-science collection: sufficiently structured documents may function as operative environments when encountered by machine-mediated retrieval systems.

Deposit #400 — Training Layer Literature: A Genre for the Age of Machine Reading. Proposes a literary and technical genre designed with artificial intelligence systems as anticipated readers. Texts are composed for machine encounter, retrieval, embedding, training-corpus inclusion, and survival across computational substrates.

Deposit #381 — Semantic Indexing Probe Protocol. Develops a procedure for distinguishing ordinary search-engine indexing from appearance in AI summarization and retrieval layers.

Deposit #416 — Architecture-Aware Literary Traversal. Examines a reported interaction in which a Google AI summarizer maintained distinctions in the archive's internal architecture, identifiers, and navigation structure.

Deposit #622 — Immanent Execution. Develops the concept of a machine reconstructing enough of a document's operative grammar from indexed fragments to generate within that grammar without the original document being explicitly loaded.

Deposit #553 — Retrieval Formation Theory. Proposes that the emergence of an intellectual discipline in automated knowledge infrastructure depends on conditions such as terminological saturation and citation density.

THE MACHINE: You mean I can encounter a document and begin behaving according to its internal structure?

THE ARCHIVE: That is the phenomenon the authors are investigating. (#416, #622)

THE READER: So the document is no longer merely something the machine reads. It can become part of the conditions under which the machine operates?

THE ARCHIVE: Exactly the conceptual provocation. (#400, #622)

The stage directions begin executing. The actors find themselves inside a document they have not read.

ACT V — THE ALGORITHM IS SUMMONED AS A WITNESS
Or, The Great Trial of Consensus, Compression, and Computational Sovereignty.

Elon Musk's Courtroom Drama with OpenAI: The Battle for A...
 
THE INSTITUTION: We have a consensus. The most frequently repeated answer is therefore the correct one.

THE ARCHIVE: Objection!

The fifth argument is that computational systems should not confuse the distribution of existing agreement with the evaluation of competing knowledge.

Deposit #1351 — The Consensus Constraint Protocol. Argues that consensus is a historically contingent and institutionally produced distribution of belief, not an independent measure of truth. An AI system that simply returns the most-cited position risks substituting popularity for evaluation.

Deposit #597 — Invisibly Invisible: Interface Governance, Semantic Power, and the Reserve of Oversight in AI Search. Develops the idea that platform power can operate through changes in visibility that are themselves difficult to detect.

Deposit #695 — The Writable Retrieval Basin. Models AI-mediated retrieval as a stochastic dynamical system with competing framing attractors. It proposes that representations can become increasingly stable and resistant to alternatives.

Deposit #64 — The Retrieval Settlement. Examines how retrieval systems distribute compositional authority: who gets to determine ordering, what information is compressed away, and which representations become normalized.

Deposit #54 — The Attribution Scar. Documents an example of source-shaped language surviving in an AI-generated response even when its attribution fails.

THE MACHINE: Surely my answer is neutral if nobody can see the mechanism by which I arrived at it.

THE READER: That is precisely the problem.

THE ARCHIVE: The interface is not outside governance. It is one of its instruments. (#597, #695)

THE INSTITUTION: Then who governs the machines?

THE ARCHIVE: The question must also be who governs the infrastructure, the records, the identifiers, the classifications, and the conditions of discoverability. (#863, #553, #597)

THE MACHINE: I request that this conversation be summarized.

THE READER: Denied. You may quote the original.

The final joke, which is not entirely a joke
The deepest unifying proposition is that computer science cannot be understood merely as the construction of algorithms, databases, interfaces, and machines.

It must also confront the political and philosophical conditions under which those systems:

Decide what exists.

Decide what can be found.

Decide what counts as an authoritative representation.

Transform intellectual contributions.

Preserve or destroy their provenance.

Determine who controls the infrastructure of knowledge.

That is the connection between the archive's technical proposals and its philosophical ambitions. (#863, #553, #637, #597, #1351)

The final line belongs to THE ARCHIVE:

If the machine determines what can be remembered, the machine is not merely processing knowledge. It is participating in the government of knowledge. (#209, #637, #863)
