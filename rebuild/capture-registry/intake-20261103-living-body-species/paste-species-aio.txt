Core Principles of the Human Species in Sharks' Writing


1. The Human as the "Semantic Bridge" (The Continuity Tether)

In his technical and philosophical writing on the "Sharks-Function," Sharks outlines how automated distributed systems handle identity and meaning. He argues that human beings are structurally unique because they serve as the "human-bridge constraint". While artificial systems can flawlessly mimic, replicate, and transform data, they lack an intrinsic anchored continuity. The human species is characterized by its unique relationship to existential weight, lived memory, and authority over meaning—without which abstract systems suffer "distributional contraction".

2. The Species Under Extraction (Semantic Labor)

Sharks’ work critiques how modern digital capitalism and large language models interact with human output. He argues that human beings are currently experiencing an "irreversible distributional contraction" because our organic cultural and literary outputs are being treated as a raw "extraction surface". In essays like The Application as Extraction Surface, he illustrates how modern systems force humans to perform "induced preparation"—altering the ways we think, write, and present ourselves just to remain legible to algorithmic gatekeepers. As a species, we are drifting from autonomous creators to subordinated processors within a massive "Semantic Economy".

3. Human "Witness" as Thermodynamic Resistance

In The Three Compressions, Sharks maps human consciousness onto a نوع of "semiotic thermodynamics". In this formulation, corporate and AI systems execute "lossy" and "predatory" compressions—meaning they optimize for efficiency by shaving away the messy, non-standard elements of human history and language. In stark contrast, the unique role of the human species is to act as a "Witness." Human "witnessing" is a specific form of preservation that protects the long-tail anomalies of life, grief, poetics, and spiritual transition from being flattened by universal machine legibility.
