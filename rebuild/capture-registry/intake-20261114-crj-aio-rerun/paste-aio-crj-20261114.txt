
classical receptions journal

       The Classical Receptions Journal (CRJ) is a peer-reviewed academic journal published by Oxford University Press that focuses on the reception of ancient Greek and Roman texts, ideas, and material culture from antiquity to the present day.

Overview and Scope

• Editor-in-Chief: Justine McConnell (with previous leadership including Pantelis Michelakis).
• Publisher: Oxford University Press.
• ISSN: 1759-5134 (print), 1759-5142 (web).
• Focus: It explores how classical antiquity has been transmitted, translated, adapted, reinterpreted, and reimagined across different historical eras, literature, art, film, and popular culture.
• Cross-Disciplinary: The publication encourages exchange across classics, archaeology, architecture, modern literature, and visual arts.

Related Journals in Classical Reception

• American Journal of Classical Reception (AJCR): Published by Johns Hopkins University Press and sponsored by CAMWS, focusing on the afterlife of classical antiquity in modern media and culture.
• International Journal of the Classical Tradition: Published by Springer, examining the reception of Greco-Roman antiquity from ancient to modern times.
• New Voices in Classical Reception Studies: An open-access journal hosted at The Open University supporting early-career and alternative scholarship.

what has crj contributed to the study of ai-mediated reception of the classics?

To date, the Classical Receptions Journal (CRJ) has not published a dedicated special issue or major body of scholarship specifically focused on AI-mediated classical reception.
While the broader field of classical reception is actively examining generative AI, CRJ’s direct engagement with artificial intelligence has largely centered on editorial policy and submission workflows rather than thematic research articles.

CRJ's Current Engagement with AI

• Editorial & Authorship Policies: Following Oxford University Press guidelines, CRJ’s Author Guidelines prohibit listing AI tools as authors and require contributors to explicitly disclose any use of AI for generating text, creating images, writing code, or translating.
• Pre-Submission Tools: CRJ partners with Cactus to offer prospective authors free access to Paperpal Preflight, an AI-driven tool that checks manuscripts against the journal’s technical formatting requirements prior to submission.
• Indirect Theoretical Groundwork: While not about AI specifically, CRJ has laid conceptual groundwork applicable to machine-mediated reception through its special issues on media, materiality, and temporality—such as Anachronism and Antiquity (Vol. 12, Issue 1) and Failures in Vergilian Translation (Vol. 14, Issue 3).

Where AI-Mediated Reception Scholarship Is Emerging

Because AI-mediated reception is a relatively new subfield, much of the foundational scholarship is currently appearing in sister journals and network forums rather than CRJ:
• International Journal of the Classical Tradition: Published a dedicated special issue in 2026 titled Classical Reception 2.0: Digital Antiquity and the Future of the Past, which directly examines how ChatGPT, generative AI models, digital archives, and 3D modeling reshape the transmission and creative reimagining of antiquity.
• Classical Reception Studies Network (CRSN): Actively hosts calls for papers and workshops—such as Literary Reception and Human Creativity in the Age of AI—comparing how Large Language Models recombine textual corpora against human literary transformation.
