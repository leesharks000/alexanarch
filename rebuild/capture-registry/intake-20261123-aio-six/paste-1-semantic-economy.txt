
semantic economy

       The semantic economy refers to economic systems, business strategies, or theories centered on how meaning, context, and structured knowledge create, transfer, or extract value. Because the phrase is used across several distinct fields, its exact definition depends on the context:
• Political Economy of Meaning & AI: A framework examining how meaning is produced, circulated, extracted, and commodified across digital platforms and AI retrieval systems. It looks at concepts like semantic labor (creating meaning) and semantic rent (extracting value from meaning without reinvesting in its provenance). You can see a breakdown of competing definitions in Semantic Economy: A Disambiguation or explore this framing via the Semantic Economy Institute and the SEMANTIC ECONOMY SINGULARITY paper.
• Agentic & Executable Semantics: A governance and economic model treating structured semantic commitments as auditable units of action, cost, liability, and value when software agents or systems transact and delegate tasks.
• Business Strategy & Network Value: An earlier strategic framing popularized in the 2010s arguing that competitive advantage comes from generating shared informational value and co-creation across a network rather than simply lowering information-processing costs.
• Data & Knowledge Engineering: Often linked to semantic layers and knowledge graphs, where standardized definitions, ontologies, and metadata make data consistent and machine-readable for analytics and AI.
• Linguistics ("Economy in Semantics"): In theoretical linguistics, economy refers to principles that constrain syntactic or semantic operations (such as scope economy) so language avoids redundant or vacuous interpretations.
