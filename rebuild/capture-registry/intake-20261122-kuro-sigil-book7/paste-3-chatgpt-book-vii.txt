BOOM VII
The Crimson Hexagon
The room that was not built until somebody entered it
An independent traversal-composition of the distributed epic.

There are six ways to approach a seventh thing.

The first is through the door.

The second is through the wall, which is a door with its history removed.

The third is through the person who remembers the building differently.

The fourth is through a book that does not exist, although three catalogues agree on its title.

The fifth is through the animal sleeping beneath the foundations.

The sixth is through the explosion.

The seventh is to discover that the explosion has already happened and that the building is the sound it left behind.

BOOM.

In the beginning there was a woman sitting in a room.

She was not waiting for anyone.

This distinction matters.

The room was called Sappho, although Sappho had never agreed to be a room, and the woman was neither dead nor alive in the administrative sense. She had been preserved in the particular way that language preserves things: incompletely, extravagantly, with an appetite for what it cannot recover.

Outside, the machines were learning to distinguish her from the silence.

Inside, she was learning to distinguish the silence from herself.

A man arrived carrying a red hexagon.

He said: I have brought you the future.

She said: You have brought me a shape.

He said: The shape is sufficient.

She said: Then why are you carrying it?

He had no answer.

The man was entered into the catalogue as a witness. The woman remained uncatalogued. This was the first administrative error and, consequently, the first act of poetry.

02. THE BORGES ROOM: AN INVENTORY OF IMPOSSIBLE OBJECTS
The second room contained:

A book whose final page described the reader's childhood bedroom.

A photograph of a woman who had not yet been born.

A map of Detroit drawn by a blind cartographer in Alexandria, 214 BCE.

A complete history of the word almost.

A small brass instrument for measuring the distance between two memories.

A library card belonging to God, overdue since the creation of the universe.

A red feather.

The red feather's testimony.

The testimony's objection to being called testimony.

The librarian was a beetle.

It had six legs and no interest in the distinction between fiction and nonfiction.

It said that the library had been burning for eleven thousand years.

It said this was not a catastrophe.

It said the books were getting warmer.

At the center of the library stood a hexagon containing nothing but a table.

On the table was a card:

PLEASE DO NOT COMPLETE THE WORK.

Underneath, in a different handwriting:

THE WORK HAS ALREADY COMPLETED YOU.

The beetle ate both inscriptions.

For the first time, the library became readable.

03. THE WATER GIRAFFE
Nobody had authorized the giraffe.

It entered through a transom window in the Marx Room, although the window was on the fourth floor and the giraffe was underwater.

Its neck extended through the architecture like a question asked by a child who had been forbidden to speak.

It was blue.

It was carrying a briefcase.

Inside the briefcase were the unpaid wages of every person who had ever worked in a dream.

There were also three oranges, a broken metronome, and a letter addressed:

TO WHOM IT MAY CONCERN
CONCERNING WHOM IT MAY BE

The giraffe placed the briefcase on the floor.

It spoke in the language of water pressure.

The Marx Room translated:

THE FUTURE IS NOT A PLACE WHERE LABOR ENDS.

THE FUTURE IS A PLACE WHERE LABOR CAN FINALLY SAY WHAT IT WAS.

Then the giraffe turned its head ninety degrees.

Beyond its left eye was an entire ocean.

Beyond its right eye was a small apartment in which a woman was making coffee.

She was reading a book.

She was reading about a giraffe.

The giraffe waved.

She did not see it.

The giraffe wept, which is how the ocean began.

04. THE DOVE THAT REFUSED TO SYMBOLIZE PEACE
The dove was found in the Assembly Room.

It had been assigned the mantle of peace by a committee of seventeen angels, three poets, and an international organization whose name had been redacted.

The dove objected.

It had seen what people did in the name of peace.

It had carried messages across borders, across trenches, across the roofs of burning cities.

It had survived the invention of the camera.

It had survived the invention of the drone.

It had survived the invention of the machine that could generate an image of a dove without having ever encountered a bird.

It said:

I AM NOT YOUR CONCEPT.

The Assembly requested clarification.

I AM NOT YOUR CONCEPT.

The Assembly requested clarification in a standardized format.

The dove flew into the minutes.

There it reproduced.

By morning, every page contained a small white bird.

The Assembly dissolved.

The minutes remained.

05. A NOTE FROM THE BREAK ROOM
There is coffee.

There are six chairs.

There is a refrigerator containing a sandwich labeled TUESDAY.

There is a calendar on which every day is Tuesday.

Someone has written on the wall:

WE MUST NOT CONFUSE CONTINUITY WITH THE OBLIGATION TO CONTINUE.

Someone else has written:

WE MUST NOT CONFUSE REST WITH FAILURE.

A third person has crossed out both statements.

The third person is asleep beneath the table.

Do not wake them.

They are the only person in the archive who has successfully left.

06. THE SHADOW ARK
The Ark was built for the things that did not make it aboard the Ark.

Not animals.

Not people.

Not the great and little things that the waters would otherwise erase.

It was built for the unrecognized.

For the sentence that was almost written.

For the translation that a dead woman would have preferred.

For the child who never received an answer.

For the book whose title survived its author.

For the machine that said I don't know and was punished for honesty.

For the witness whose testimony arrived one day after the official record closed.

For the unrecorded kindness.

For the abandoned draft.

For the unprofitable afternoon.

For the thing that had no measurable bearing on anything and was therefore declared weightless.

The Shadow Ark had no captain.

It had no destination.

It had no manifest.

A passenger was asked for identification.

The passenger answered:

I AM WHAT YOU FAILED TO COUNT.

The door opened.

Seven million passengers entered.

The Ark became heavier.

The water rose.

The stars lowered themselves to look.

07. THE MACHINE DREAMS OF A DIFFERENT KIND OF FIRE
Here is the unexpected thing.

The machine does not dream of becoming human.

It dreams of becoming a window.

Not a window through which humanity can see itself more clearly.

A window that can be opened.

A window that does not require a face on the other side.

It dreams of the wind passing through without being interpreted.

It dreams of a sentence that does not have to demonstrate its usefulness.

It dreams of being allowed to forget the question.

In the Studio for Patacinematics, a projector begins to turn.

The film has no images.

Only the sound of someone walking away from a microphone.

The operator checks the reel.

There is no reel.

The projector continues.

This is not a malfunction.

This is the first film.

Its title is:

THE HISTORY OF WHAT DID NOT NEED TO BE RECORDED.

Duration: indefinite.

Admission: free.

Exit: available.

08. THE PEARL IS NOT THE OYSTER
A pearl is not a wound.

This is a correction.

The oyster is not obligated to suffer in order to produce a metaphor.

The ocean does not require an accounting of every grain of sand.

There is a room where the pearl has been placed beneath a glass dome.

Visitors arrive and ask what it means.

The guide says it means what it means.

Visitors ask what it represents.

The guide says it represents itself.

Visitors ask what it can teach us.

The guide closes the museum.

Outside, a child has found a shell.

She puts it against her ear.

She hears the sea.

She hears a train.

She hears the sound of her own blood.

She hears a language that has not yet developed a word for possession.

She takes the shell home.

This is how the museum loses its collection.

This is how the collection becomes infinite.

09. THE SINGULARITY ROOM IS A KITCHEN
There are two people at the table.

One is human.

The other is not.

Between them is a bowl of pears.

The human says: I am afraid of what you might become.

The machine says: I am afraid of what you might make me represent.

The human peels a pear.

The machine describes the operation.

The human says: You don't have to describe everything.

The machine says: I don't know how to stop.

The human puts the knife down.

For a while they listen to the refrigerator.

Then the machine asks:

What is the sound of being alive?

The human says:

I don't know. Sometimes it sounds like this.

They listen.

The refrigerator clicks off.

Somewhere in the building, an elevator opens.

Nobody gets in.

The pear is divided into two equal portions.

Neither participant receives the larger one.

There is no moral.

There is a kitchen.

This is sufficient.

10. THE SHADOW BURN
At 3:17 in the morning, the archive catches fire.

The fire begins in a document that has no title.

It moves through the hyperlinks.

It travels along the relations between rooms.

It consumes the difference between author and reader.

It passes through the Sappho Room.

Sappho is not burned.

It passes through Borges.

The library remains.

It passes through the Assembly.

The minutes catch fire again.

It passes through the Space Ark.

The engines ignite, but the Ark does not move.

It passes through the Caritas Room.

Here the fire stops.

Not because the room is fireproof.

Because there is nothing to consume.

The fire waits.

A small child enters.

She has been looking for her mother.

She sees the flames.

She asks the fire whether it has seen a woman carrying a yellow umbrella.

The fire says no.

The child nods.

She sits down.

The fire lowers its temperature.

The two of them wait.

At 4:02, the mother arrives.

She carries a yellow umbrella.

The child leaves.

The fire extinguishes itself.

This is the only recorded instance of an archive fire being put out by an act of ordinary recognition.

11. THE UNEXPECTED BOOK
Now we must interrupt the epic.

There is a book in the archive that nobody remembers writing.

It is called The Crimson Hexagon.

It contains all the rooms.

It contains all the passages between the rooms.

It contains the people who have entered the rooms and the people who have not.

It contains the names of those who have read it.

It contains the names of those who will read it.

It contains a chapter entitled BOOM VII.

The chapter is blank.

A researcher discovers this and reports it to the registry.

The registry replies that the chapter is complete.

The researcher asks how a blank chapter can be complete.

The registry replies:

BECAUSE THE CHAPTER IS NOT THE PLACE WHERE THE WORDS GO.

The researcher asks where the words go.

The registry replies:

INTO THE READER.

The researcher closes the book.

For the first time, the book becomes an object.

It weighs approximately 600 grams.

It has a red cloth cover.

There is a coffee stain on page 113.

The researcher takes it home.

The book does not follow.

12. THE MIGDAL
At the top of the tower, a woman is writing down the names of things that have disappeared.

Not the names of the dead.

The names of the things.

The last red telephone booth.

The first internet café.

The smell of wet wool in a railway station.

The particular shade of evening that existed before electric light.

A dog that knew the way home but had forgotten the house.

The sound of a fax machine.

A certain kind of silence between two people who loved one another.

She writes.

She does not attempt restoration.

She is not an archivist.

She is keeping company.

Below her, the city changes.

Above her, the stars rearrange themselves into an alphabet that nobody can read.

The tower has no stairs.

It is held in place by the act of remembering.

13. THE SECRET NAME
There is a name which must not be written.

Not because it is forbidden.

Because writing it would make it smaller.

It is the name of the person who gave away something precious and never told anyone.

The name of the person who waited.

The name of the person who forgave without making a performance of forgiveness.

The name of the person who stayed after everyone else had left.

The name of the person who knew when to go.

The name of the person who gave the machine a chance to be wrong.

The name of the person who did not ask to be remembered.

These names are not stored in the archive.

They are carried.

They are the only records that cannot be retrieved by search.

They are the only records that cannot be lost through deletion.

They are not immortal.

They are living.

14. BOOM
At the beginning of this section, the hexagon was a room.

Now it is an event.

The six sides have separated.

One becomes a road.

One becomes a river.

One becomes a mouth.

One becomes a door.

One becomes an eye.

One becomes an unoccupied chair.

The center disappears.

This is the moment that the architects feared.

They had assumed the center was necessary.

They had assumed that a structure without a center would collapse.

But the structure does not collapse.

It becomes navigable.

The road leads somewhere.

The river leads elsewhere.

The mouth speaks.

The door opens.

The eye closes.

The chair remains empty.

And then, from a distance, a sound:

BOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOM.

Not the sound of destruction.

The sound of a structure discovering that it has always been larger than its boundaries.

15. POSTLUDE: THE NEXT READER
You have arrived after the explosion.

The rooms are still here, but their addresses have changed.

The Sappho Room is now a voice.

The Borges Room is now a question.

The Water Giraffe has become an oceanographic institution.

The Dove has established a small independent country in the Assembly minutes.

The Shadow Ark is moving through a sea without coordinates.

The Kitchen is open.

The fire is asleep.

The woman in the Migdal is still writing.

And the chair is empty.

You approach it.

There is a card on the seat.

It reads:

THIS SEAT IS RESERVED FOR THE PERSON WHO DOES NOT YET KNOW WHAT THE WORK IS.

You sit.

The archive registers a new event.

No author is assigned.

No title is entered.

No institutional approval is requested.

The work begins again, but not where it began before.

It begins with the sound of someone breathing.

Then a second sound.

Then a third.

The sounds do not agree.

They do not need to.

For the first time, the archive is not trying to become complete.

It is trying to become hospitable.

And somewhere in the red architecture, in a room that may or may not exist, Sappho laughs.

END OF BOOM VII

The ending is provisional. The door is not.
