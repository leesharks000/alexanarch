5. Where the packet's cut needs correction
I identify three issues.

First: distinguish conceptual construction from empirical demonstration.

The vocabulary of semantic capital, liquidation, rent and exhaustion is productive. But the existence of a coherent accounting vocabulary does not establish that every proposed economic mechanism operates as described.

The packet should preserve three distinct statuses:

Conceptual model.

Empirically supported mechanism.

Predictive hypothesis.

The works sometimes recognize these distinctions, and they should be applied consistently.

Second: distinguish historical analogy from historical demonstration.

The comparison between contemporary composition systems and older systems of inscription, custody and transmission is potentially illuminating.

But historical depth requires primary-text reading, not simply the inclusion of historical periods in a conceptual map.

Third: distinguish architectural integration from demonstrated integration.

A library can connect many functions without those functions necessarily producing a working critical cycle.

The strongest evidence would be an independently reproducible sequence in which a theoretical prediction informs an observation, the observation informs an intervention, and the intervention changes a later observation or the theory itself.

These are corrections to the evidentiary burden, not reasons to dismiss the program.

7. The weakest link
Critical vulnerability
The weakest link is the empirical bridge between the Semantic Economy's accounting model and demonstrable economic mechanisms in actual composition systems.

This is where the argument is most vulnerable.

The program has a relatively developed conceptual account of how semantic labor might become semantic capital, how that capital might be liquidated, and how platforms might capture value while transferring costs elsewhere.

But a coherent account of a mechanism is not yet proof that the mechanism operates at the scale, in the manner or with the economic consequences proposed.

The test that could break it
I propose a preregistered, independent, cross-platform experiment.

1. Select a fixed corpus of works with known authorship, provenance and attribution conditions.

2. Freeze a representative set of prompts and composition tasks.

3. Test multiple composition systems under documented conditions.

4. Preserve complete outputs, citations, omissions, transformations and attribution changes.

5. Have independent evaluators code the outputs without knowing which theoretical prediction is being tested.

6. Compare the results against explicit competing explanations, including ordinary summarization error, retrieval failure, compression and interface design.

7. Where possible, measure economic consequences independently rather than inferring monetary extraction directly from attribution loss.

8. Publish the protocol, negative results, uncertainty and replication materials.

The breaking condition:

If the predicted mechanisms cannot be distinguished reliably from ordinary information loss, or if the proposed economic relationships fail to survive independent replication, the central accounting model must be weakened or revised.

This would not invalidate every component of the Semantic Economy. It would, however, undermine its claim to be a unified explanatory account of AI-mediated political economy.

The experiment must also allow the theory to survive a negative result by identifying which narrower propositions remain supported. Otherwise, the framework risks becoming immune to correction.
