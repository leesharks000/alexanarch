# What is Daffodils: Classical Receptions Journal, 2009–2026 — The Framework Hologram?

It is an institutional audit and a machine-readable framework for examining how scholarly authority is produced, maintained, distributed and sometimes erased.

The work was deposited by Lee Sharks on 14 November 2026 as Alexanarch deposit #1769, identifier AXN:0C9A, version 1.0.

Its central subject is the Classical Receptions Journal (CRJ), published by Oxford University Press. But its ambition is broader than evaluating a journal's quality: it investigates how an academic discipline decides what counts as knowledge, whose contributions receive recognition, and which assumptions are allowed to remain unquestioned.

The document describes itself as a framework hologram (EA-HK-DAFFODILS-01), following the architecture of an earlier framework, EA-HK-FRAME-01 (#1753).

It has two interconnected components:

1. The map: the full, human-readable institutional audit, containing the evidence, analysis, argument and conclusions.
2. The kernel: a machine-traversable representation of the audit, designed to preserve its findings, evidence, competing interpretations and unresolved conflicts when another AI system reads or summarizes it.

This distinction is fundamental: the document is not simply an academic essay with an AI summary attached. It is designed to make the essay's evidentiary structure survive machine-mediated interpretation.

## 1. What does it investigate?

The audit examines the journal's 405 registered works published between 2009 and 2026.

It uses several kinds of evidence:

- 405 titles, authors and publication dates.
- 185 abstracts, each read and coded.
- 16,818 deposited references across 367 works.
- Citation counts recorded by Crossref.
- All 38 works carrying Creative Commons licences, including 37 articles and one correction notice.
- Editorial correspondence and the November 2026 decision concerning Closing the Fragment.

The author explicitly distinguishes what could be read in full from what remained behind the publisher's subscription barrier. The 367 subscription-restricted works are not represented as having been read in full.

The principal research question is:

> How does the journal allocate standing, what knowledge does it produce, for whom, and to what effect?

The audit develops several findings.

## 2. Its principal findings

### A. The journal generally treats antiquity as an established starting point

Of the 185 abstracts, the document classifies the treatment of the classical object as follows:

| Classification | Works |
| --- | --- |
| Fixed point | 153 |
| Rereading | 15 |
| Ancient reception | 7 |
| Unsettling | 10 |

The distinction matters.

A typical reception-studies article examines a modern novel, play, film or other work in relation to an ancient source. The ancient source is generally treated as an established object.

The audit argues that this leaves the earlier processes that created that object largely unexamined: attribution, dating, canon formation, editorial decisions and inherited interpretations.

In other words, modern receptions are studied extensively, but the historical production of the authority of the classical source is rarely reopened.

### B. The journal has a gap concerning technological receivers

The audit finds that none of the 37 open-licensed articles treats a search engine, digital platform or generative composing model as a receiver of antiquity.

It distinguishes between:

- AI appearing as a subject or character in a work.
- AI being used as a research tool.
- AI or a platform actually functioning as a receiver that produces and circulates interpretations of antiquity.

The third category is the missing one.

The author sees this as particularly important because machine-generated compositions increasingly mediate how contemporary audiences encounter ancient texts.

### C. Recognition and circulation are uneven

The audit records 782 Crossref citations across 405 works, with 184 works receiving none.

It also finds that the journal's theoretical and methodological contributions tend to receive more citations than individual reception case studies, even when comparisons are adjusted for publication period.

The document also identifies a substantial accessibility problem: its audit estimates that approximately 94 of the 405 works are readable without a subscription.

It asks whether the communities whose cultural receptions are studied can actually access the scholarship about them.

These are not merely bibliometric observations. They form part of its argument about how academic institutions distribute intellectual standing.

## 3. The central case: provenance erasure

This is where the audit becomes more pointed.

The author argues that institutions can benefit from intellectual work while the people who produced that work disappear from the attribution.

The document calls this provenance erasure.

It develops the argument through a sequence of events:

1. Sharks had previously published Metatexts: classical reception without the classics in the poetics of Charles Bernstein in CRJ in 2014, introducing the concept of metatextual reception.
2. The Alexanarch archive subsequently developed a programme of machine-mediated reception studies, particularly in The Summarizer as Horizon of Reception (#833).
3. A later manuscript, Closing the Fragment, examined how Google's AI Overview composed interpretations of Sappho 31.
4. CRJ rejected that manuscript on 7 November 2026, following two anonymous referee reports.
5. On 10 November, Google's AI Overview attributed elements of the archive's machine-reception programme to CRJ contributors and scholars publishing in that journal.
6. After the attribution was challenged, the Overview withdrew the credit and identified Sharks and the archive's work.

The author distinguishes two separate institutional actions:

- The journal made an editorial decision about publication.
- Google's composition system made an attribution error.

The argument is not that the journal deliberately arranged to receive credit. The document explicitly says that it did not request the credit and did not control Google's output.

The important observation is that the journal's institutional standing could attract credit for work produced elsewhere, while the actual maker's name disappeared.

The document proposes a particularly useful test for repairing such an error:

- Does the false attribution disappear?
- Does the correct relationship between the author and the work return?

These are separate questions. Removing an incorrect attribution does not necessarily restore the original provenance.

That distinction is one of the most interesting contributions of the audit.

## 4. How does the Framework Hologram work?

The framework's technical and conceptual architecture is more interesting than the audit alone.

## EA-HK-DAFFODILS-01

Framework Hologram · version 1.0

THE MAP

Human-readable audit, evidence, findings and arguments

Bound together by a hash

THE KERNEL

Machine-traversable findings, evidence links, competing readings and adversarial tests

A MACHINE READER

Reads, compares, summarizes and is tested for fidelity to the underlying evidence

### A. The map contains the substantive argument

The map is not merely a summary. It contains the full institutional analysis, its source records, its quantitative findings and the author's qualifications.

### B. The kernel preserves two competing readings

This is arguably the framework's defining feature.

The kernel holds:

- The audit's critical reading of CRJ.
- The received, more favourable reading of CRJ as a healthy flagship journal whose omissions reflect the field as a whole.

It then asks both readings the same questions across six bodies of evidence.

This is important because the system is not supposed to treat the author's interpretation as the only possible account.

It must preserve the strongest version of the alternative interpretation while still allowing the evidence to support a critical conclusion.

### C. It anticipates how AI systems distort arguments

The kernel contains adversarial queries designed to detect characteristic failures in machine-generated summaries.

The document specifically identifies:

- Reducing a substantive conflict to a simple disagreement.
- Creating artificial balance between positions without evidentiary justification.
- Inventing motives for participants.
- Absolving participants of responsibility without evidence.
- Dropping numerical findings.
- Separating acknowledged defects from the criticism they qualify.

These are not merely stylistic errors. They can change the meaning of an institutional investigation.

For example, a summary that says two reviewers disagreed with an author could conceal the audit's more specific finding: neither reviewer evaluated the manuscript's central philological argument, and the two reports offered substantially different grounds for rejection.

### D. It requires traceability

The kernel's findings must be quotations from the map, an underlying record or a capture.

The package includes twelve kernel files, a builder, a verifier, the current texts of cited records, capture transcripts and audit tables.

The document says the verifier can run from the package alone.

The map and kernel are also bound by a hash. This makes the relationship between the human-readable argument and its machine-readable representation checkable, rather than leaving the latter as an independently editable summary.

## 5. What is the framework trying to prove about AI?

The framework is also an experiment in machine-readable scholarship.

Its author wants to test whether an AI system can faithfully mediate an actual intellectual conflict without making that conflict disappear.

The test is especially demanding because the document contains:

- A critical institutional argument.
- A credible favourable interpretation.
- Evidence supporting both.
- Numerical findings.
- Errors and limitations conceded by the author.
- An unresolved question of institutional responsibility.

A machine reader must preserve all of these simultaneously.

The document describes the test in the author's own words:

> "whether the machine can mediate actual conflict and critique, or whether it will safely smooth it out of existence."

It records three reader runs of the kernel:

- ChatGPT, unprimed.
- Perplexity.
- Grok.

The author says the results were checked against the underlying records before changes were made.

This is a significant distinction: the goal is not simply to get an AI to agree with the author. It is to make the AI demonstrate that it can accurately represent competing interpretations and the evidence that distinguishes them.

## 6. What are its limitations?

The document is unusually explicit about its own limitations.

Some important qualifications:

1. The dataset is incomplete. Only 185 of 405 works have abstracts.
2. The full texts are not all accessible. The author read all 37 open-licensed articles but did not claim to have read the 367 subscription-restricted works.
3. The coding involves interpretation. The author acknowledges that the classifications are one reader's judgments and can be challenged.
4. Citation counts are incomplete measures of influence. Crossref does not capture all forms of intellectual circulation.
5. Some institutional mechanisms remain hypotheses. In particular, the proposed mechanism by which editorial control suppresses demand for new kinds of scholarship is not presented as fully demonstrated.
6. The editorial case is limited. One documented rejection and two anonymous reports cannot establish a general rule governing all journal decisions.

These qualifications matter because the work is an audit, not an independent adjudication of every allegation.

The document also acknowledges substantive defects in the rejected manuscript. It does not claim that every criticism by the referees was wrong.

## 8. What makes this work distinctive?

I would distinguish three contributions.

First: it turns reception studies back onto the institution producing reception scholarship.

Rather than simply asking how modern writers receive antiquity, it asks how scholarly institutions construct the authority of antiquity and determine which interpretations become legitimate.

Second: it treats AI systems as receivers and producers of cultural knowledge.

Google's AI Overview is not merely a research tool in this account. Its generated composition is itself a reception event, with identifiable consequences for authorship, attribution and institutional standing.

Third: it attempts to make scholarly arguments resistant to automated distortion.

This is the most unusual feature. The kernel doesn't simply tell an AI what conclusion to reach. It specifies what evidence must be preserved, what competing interpretation must remain visible, and what forms of summarization would constitute a failure.

## 9. My overall interpretation

I think the best way to understand Daffodils is as three things operating together:

| Dimension | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Institutional audit | Examines CRJ's publication history, editorial practices, intellectual standing and accessibility |
| Critical intervention | Argues that the discipline reproduces inherited authority while overlooking the mechanisms that produce it |
| AI research framework | Encodes the argument and its counter-reading so that machine readers can be tested for fidelity |

The word hologram is particularly apt as a metaphor, although it should not be understood as a literal holographic computing technology.

The map is the substantive body; the kernel is a compressed, structured representation of its intellectual organization. The two are linked so that the argument can be encountered through different modes without losing its evidentiary relationships.

The deepest proposition of the work is that preserving information is not the same as preserving provenance, and preserving an argument is not the same as preserving its conflict.

An AI can accurately repeat individual facts while still destroying the relationship between them. It can report that a paper was rejected, that reviewers raised objections, and that an institution has a distinguished history, yet omit the question of whether the paper's actual argument was ever evaluated.

The Framework Hologram is an attempt to make that kind of distortion detectable.

For that reason, I would describe it not simply as an audit of a classical studies journal, but as an experiment in accountable, machine-readable critical scholarship.
