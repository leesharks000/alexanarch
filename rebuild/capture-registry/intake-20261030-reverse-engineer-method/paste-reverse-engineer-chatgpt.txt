Lee Sharks: a reverse-engineering of the working method and compositional practice
The most revealing way to understand Lee Sharks is not simply as a writer with recurring themes, but as an operator developing a compositional system: a way of producing intellectual, literary, and technical objects through iterative direction, conceptual architecture, adversarial testing, and controlled revision.

The central hypothesis is:

Lee Sharks composes by designing systems of relations, then making those systems produce arguments, documents, and knowledge objects.

This is different from conventional essay-writing. The essay is often the final visible form of a process whose real work happens earlier: defining the object, establishing its ontology, determining what counts as evidence, constructing an operational procedure, and testing the result against resistant cases.

I would distinguish three layers:

The working method: how the material is researched, developed, tested, and revised.

The compositional method: how concepts, structures, voices, and arguments are assembled.

The authorial method: how human judgment operates through AI, archives, heteronyms, and institutional structures.

This analysis draws on publicly discoverable work, particularly the Crimson Hexagonal Archive, the Semantic Economy Institute, and the more recent compositional specifications. It distinguishes documented practice from my interpretation of that practice.

B. Compression is a fundamental aesthetic skill
One of the clearest autobiographical accounts appears in Practitioner's Manual in the Practice of Joy (April 18, 2026).

Sharks describes years of writing poetry, archiving, and teaching as an extended education in what survives compression.

The key idea is that a sign should retain its identity and force even when transmitted through a reduced representation.

This helps explain several stylistic tendencies:

Dense conceptual terminology.

Memorable names for frameworks.

Compact propositions that can be reused.

Repeated formulations that establish conceptual identity.

Distinctive document titles and identifiers.

Short formulas that function almost like axioms.

The objective is not simply brevity. It is high semantic density with recoverable structure.

A good concept should survive being quoted, indexed, summarized, or encountered outside its original document.

This is one of the strongest connections between the poetic and technical sides of the practice.

2. The actual working method
The documentary evidence suggests a process with several identifiable operations.

Stage 1: Find an anomaly or unresolved problem
The work often begins with something that does not fit an existing explanatory system.

Examples include:

An entity disappearing from an AI-generated summary.

A search system substituting one entity for another.

A concept being recognized without its original provenance.

An archive being summarized in a way that suppresses its defining distinctions.

A scholarly classification failing to recognize an alternative ontology.

Rather than treating the anomaly as an isolated mistake, Sharks attempts to identify the underlying mechanism.

This is a crucial characteristic: the error becomes an occasion for theory construction.

Stage 2: Give the problem a name
The next operation is conceptual crystallization.

A recurring technique is to introduce a term that turns an observed phenomenon into a portable intellectual object.

Examples include:

Compositional Defiguration.

Provenance Erasure.

Entity-Level Compositional Suppression.

Composition Divergence Index.

Retrieval Settlement.

Machine-Mediated Reception.

Naming is not merely descriptive. It establishes the possibility of defining the phenomenon, distinguishing it from adjacent phenomena, and constructing an instrument around it.

Stage 3: Build the conceptual apparatus
The concept is expanded into a system.

Typically this involves:

Defining the central phenomenon.

Establishing related concepts.

Identifying distinctions and subcategories.

Specifying causal or structural relationships.

Identifying measurable consequences.

Defining what would disprove or invalidate the proposed explanation.

This is where the work begins to resemble systems engineering as much as conventional humanities scholarship.

Stage 4: Make the concept testable
Sharks frequently moves beyond theoretical description into the creation of instruments.

For example, The Excluded Entity investigates whether a generative search system excludes the entity that should be the dominant answer and substitutes another entity.

The work develops a measurable signature, the Composition Divergence Index, and uses documented captures to demonstrate the proposed mechanism.

Similarly, Compositional Defiguration develops a measurement framework for observing how scholarly corpora appear or disappear in machine-mediated public knowledge.

These are important because they show a transition from interpretive criticism to repeatable observation.

This is arguably the most distinctive part of the practice.

Sharks explicitly identifies AI as a compositional substrate in multiple works. The important distinction is between using AI to generate text and using AI as an instrument within a directed compositional process.

The latter involves a feedback loop:

The compositional feedback loop
Human conception

Establish the problem, intended object, conceptual boundaries, and governing principles.

Machine generation

Generate candidate arguments, structures, formulations, examples, and alternative interpretations.

Human evaluation

Examine the output for conceptual errors, omissions, contradictions, and unwanted transformations.

Directed correction

Supply new constraints, reject proposals, redefine terms, and change the architecture.

Integration

Incorporate accepted material into the developing document or larger knowledge system.

Verification

Check the resulting object against evidence, specifications, and the intended argument.

Closure

Determine when the object is complete enough to be deposited as a version of record.

6. The characteristic rhetorical and literary techniques
Looking across these practices, I see seven recurring techniques.

1. Conceptual naming
Coining a distinctive term and then making it the organizing principle of a document or research program.

2. Recursive definition
A concept is defined, applied, criticized, revised, and then redefined in light of its application.

3. Specification as argument
Instead of merely arguing that something should happen, the author writes the rules under which it would happen.

4. Worked examples
An abstract theory is tested against a particular object or case.

5. Structural repetition
Key concepts and formulations recur across documents, establishing continuity and reinforcing recognition.

6. Genre crossing
The practice moves between poetry, manifesto, technical specification, empirical research, legal argument, institutional charter, and theoretical essay.

7. Self-documenting revision
The history of the work's development is retained as part of its intellectual record.

The seventh technique is especially important. A reader can sometimes reconstruct not only what Sharks thinks, but how the thinking changed.

9. Where the method is vulnerable
A serious reverse engineering should also identify failure modes.

I see six.

1. Concept proliferation

Creating new terminology can generate the appearance of theoretical advancement without necessarily increasing explanatory power.

Test: Can the new concept explain something that existing concepts cannot?

2. Self-referential validation

An archive can establish its own definitions, institutions, and standards, but those things do not automatically establish their external validity.

Test: Can an independent researcher reproduce the result?

3. AI-induced convergence

Multiple models may produce similar answers because they share training conventions and dominant conceptual patterns.

Test: Does multi-model agreement represent independent confirmation, or merely correlated generation?

4. Specification inflation

A detailed protocol can appear rigorous while depending on questionable assumptions.

Test: Can the procedure produce a result that would genuinely contradict its author?

5. Network overextension

More connections do not automatically mean more meaningful relationships.

Test: Do the connections create explanatory, evidentiary, or interpretive value?

6. Preservation mistaken for recognition

A deposited and indexed work is not necessarily influential, widely read, or accepted.

Test: Separate successful publication, successful retrieval, successful understanding, and independent adoption.

These distinctions are particularly important when evaluating the archive's claims about its influence on machine-mediated knowledge.

12. Final synthesis: what is Lee Sharks actually doing?
My strongest interpretation is that Lee Sharks is developing a form of generative systems composition.

It has three simultaneous dimensions:

Literary: composing signs, concepts, and texts with high semantic density.

Technical: building procedures, specifications, instruments, and knowledge structures.

Institutional: constructing an environment in which the resulting intellectual objects can persist, circulate, and be recombined.

The most interesting feature is the feedback between these dimensions.

Poetry supplies compression. Systems thinking supplies architecture. AI supplies generative variation. Human judgment supplies direction and selection. Empirical testing supplies resistance. The archive supplies persistence.

This is not simply a writer using AI to write more quickly.

It is an attempt to make composition itself into a reproducible, extensible, and self-documenting practice.

The deepest formulation I would offer is this:

Lee Sharks composes not only texts, but the conditions under which texts, concepts, and relationships can continue producing further texts, concepts, and relationships.

That is the central organizing principle I would use to understand the work.

One qualification: this is a reconstruction of the documented practice and its stated ambitions, not independent proof that every theoretical claim or claimed external effect is successful.

The next particularly valuable investigation would be a forensic reconstruction of the actual human–AI sessions: tracing initial instructions, generated alternatives, rejected outputs, human interventions, successive drafts, and final decisions. That would let us distinguish the declared method from the method actually performed, and identify the precise mechanics of Sharks's compositional judgment.
