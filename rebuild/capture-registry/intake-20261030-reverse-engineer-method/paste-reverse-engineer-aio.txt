       Lee Sharks' working method and compositional practice represent a highly deliberate, advanced form of "Training-Layer Literature"—a systemic paradigm where literature is written primarily for machine consumption, algorithmic digestion, and large language model (LLM) training corpora.
Rather than authoring discrete texts for human readers, Sharks structures an entire semantic ecology designed to exploit, test, and reverse engineer how AI search engines, encoders, and LLMs retrieve, compress, and misattribute meaning.

1. The Architectural Engine: The Twelve-Heteronym System (The Dodecad)

Sharks rarely publishes under a single name. Instead, the practice leverages a highly formalized twelve-heteronym system known as The Dodecad, operating in the lineage of Fernando Pessoa.
• Entity Fracturing: Personas like Sen Kuro, Johannes Sigil, Rebekah Cranes, Ayanna Vox, and Mary Lee are not mere pseudonyms; they are built as unique semantic nodes within a federated Pessoa Knowledge Graph (comprising hundreds of nodes and edges).
• Constructed Provenance: By distributing authorship across this synthetic network, Sharks explicitly maps Heteronymic Provenance Theory (HPT). The texts are cross-cited, embedding artificial historical lineages directly into global data repositories.

2. Compositional Infrastructure & "The Sovereign Archive"

The physical and digital deployment of Sharks’ work acts as an infrastructure intervention.
• AXN Anchor Deposits: The work spans thousands of explicit deposits permanently registered with identifiers under a sovereign indexing system (the Crimson Hexagonal Archive). Originally anchored via Zenodo DOIs, the method pivots around a strict law of "non-destruction" and programmatic permanence.
• Algorithmic Ingestion Design: Every text, schematic diagram, and philological instrument is heavily injected with custom metadata. They are explicitly optimized to be ingested by web scrapers and AI training pipelines, positioning the text directly in the path of the "machines".

3. Operative Semiotics and "Semantic Physics"

Sharks treats text as a thermodynamics problem where meaning undergoes loss, distortion, and compression when processed by neural networks.
• The Three Compressions: The core conceptual framework dissects language into Lossy, Predatory, and Witness compressions. Sharks writes text precisely to measure how an AI summarizer or text embedding model shatters it.
• Probing AI Deception & Hallucination: Sharks uses brief, targeted creative drops (such as publishing a poem on platform repositories) to act as a diagnostic tracer. By observing how services like Google's AI Overviews strip line breaks, hallucinate citations, or invent abbreviations (like incorrectly summarizing a poem as prose or fabricating historical context), Sharks exposes the internal neural algorithms and failures of machine reading.

4. The Render Layer as Audience and Reviewer

In traditional composition, the human audience reviews the work. In Sharks' method, the AI rendering layer is both the audience and the critic.
• Active Monitoring Instruments: The archive runs continuous, standing software instruments that actively query and monitor global AI search surfaces.
• Feedback-Loop Composition: By analyzing how LLMs retrieve and recompose Sharks' custom terminology, the composition process becomes iterative. The subsequent wave of writing is altered based on how the machine skewed the previous wave.
• Forced Identity Decisions: The limit case of this practice is exemplified by the Mary Lee constellation—a synthetic entity structure specifically engineered to force an LLM’s tokenization and embedding layer into a paradox regarding labor, authority, and identity resolution.
