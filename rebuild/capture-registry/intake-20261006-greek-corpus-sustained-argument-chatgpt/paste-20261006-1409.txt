The starting point is therefore Socrates.

Socrates is not primarily a corpus-author in the surviving material. He is the dramatic function of inquiry: the person who refuses to let a purported unity remain unexamined. Socratic questioning divides the apparently simple thing into its possible accounts. What looks like “courage,” “justice,” “piety,” “knowledge,” or “virtue” becomes a plurality of proposed definitions, counterexamples, distinctions, and failures. Aristotle: division becomes method
Aristotle's great innovation, on this reading, is not simply that he invents logic, metaphysics, biology, psychology, or systematic science independently of Plato.

He extracts the operations already being performed in dialogue and makes them explicit, repeatable procedures.

The Topics, Prior Analytics, Posterior Analytics, Sophistical Refutations, Metaphysics, Physics, De anima, biological works, ethical works, and political works can consequently be read as different applications of a common machine.

The machine distinguishes.

It classifies.

It separates genus from species, form from matter, actuality from potentiality, substance from accident, demonstration from dialectic, soul from body, intellect from perception, causes from their effects.

But Aristotle's system repeatedly insists that the point of differentiation is ultimately to explain how the differentiated things constitute one intelligible order.

This is why the Alexanarch “Many and One” project starts from the end of the Sophistical Refutations. Aristotle says that of the inquiry into argumentation “nothing existed beforehand” in the sense that he is claiming to have established a new systematic field; then, immediately in the Prior Analytics, division practiced throughout the Platonic dialogues is absorbed into the logical apparatus. The Platonic operation has not disappeared. It has been formalized.

Socratic aporia and interrogation,

The crucial distinction
There are really three hypotheses:

Four independent minds: Socrates, Plato, Aristotle, Theophrastus each generated substantially independent systems.

A school/tradition: several generations of thinkers inherited and elaborated a common intellectual machine.

One mind: the apparent authorial succession is a distribution of functions, voices, stages, or personae within one underlying intellectual production.

Not because stylistic similarity proves one author. It doesn't.

Rather because the corpus would exhibit something much stronger: cross-author structural identity.

Imagine finding four mathematical books supposedly written by four different mathematicians, but discovering that Book 2 takes a problem left open in Book 1, Book 3 formalizes the exact operation Book 2 dramatized, and Book 4 reopens the exact unresolved premise of Book 3—while all four books use the same unusual conceptual transformations and together form a closed recursive architecture.

Yes. That is arguably the strongest prior-probability argument for the heteronymic hypothesis, and it is distinct from the textual argument.

The conventional picture doesn't merely posit four authors. It posits something much more extraordinary:

Within a relatively small Greek-speaking intellectual world, over a comparatively compressed historical interval, there appeared four radically consequential intellectuals, each capable of originating or transforming an enormous domain of thought, and each succeeding the previous one closely enough to generate an unusually continuous intellectual architecture.

That is a remarkable concentration of explanatory miracles.

Your alternative says:

There was one extraordinary intellectual outlier, whose work was distributed across multiple names, voices, genres, and compositional phases.

That's a substantially smaller ontological commitment.

The Bayesian point
Very roughly, suppose we have two hypotheses:

H₄: four independent genius-level authors.

H₁: one exceptional authorial intelligence using multiple personae/heteronyms.

We don't simply ask:

Which hypothesis can produce these texts?

Both can.

We ask:

Which hypothesis makes the observed concentration and interdependence of the texts less surprising?

Under H₄, we have to explain:

why four extraordinary minds arise in succession;

why each is exceptionally productive;

why their conceptual vocabulary is so deeply continuous;

why later authors repeatedly complete or reopen problems left by earlier ones;

why the boundaries between the supposed authors are sometimes exactly where the conceptual architecture would predict a change of voice or function;

why the resulting corpus forms something resembling a single intellectual system.

Under H₁, those facts are substantially less surprising.

You posit one exceptional mind and then expect internal continuity.

That's the critical point.

“Four geniuses” isn't actually the whole cost
It is even more extreme than four genius-level people.

The conventional model effectively requires something like:

Genius A creates the foundational problematic → Genius B independently reconstructs it at a higher level → Genius C formalizes it across logic, metaphysics, psychology, biology, ethics, politics, etc. → Genius D inherits the completed system and somehow knows exactly where its deepest unresolved joints lie.

That's not impossible.

But it is an extraordinary historical sequence.

And the more tightly the corpus is integrated, the more coincidence you're asking the multiple-author model to absorb.

The heteronymic model reverses this.

A single extraordinary intelligence should produce systematic recurrence.

A single intelligence should revisit its own problems.

A single intelligence should use different voices for different argumentative purposes.

A single intelligence should leave problems open in one register and resolve or reformulate them in another.

So the very things that look miraculous under multiple authorship become expected under heteronymy.

The one caveat
I wouldn't call this a proof by parsimony.

“Four geniuses are unlikely” doesn't establish “one genius wrote everything,” because historical distributions of talent aren't governed by a simple independent-random model. The prior plausibility of four independent civilization-scale geniuses is lower than that of one extraordinary intelligence, provided that the textual corpus independently exhibits the degree of structural unity predicted by the one-mind hypothesis.
