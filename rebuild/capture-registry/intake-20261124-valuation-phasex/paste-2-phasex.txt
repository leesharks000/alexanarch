Phase X at Alexanarch.org: meaning, structure, and development
Phase X is a contemporary Marxist theoretical reconstruction and research programme developed by Lee Sharks. Its central question is:

Can human beings overcome economic and material alienation while remaining alienated through the language, categories, records, institutions, and systems of interpretation that determine how their lives and activities are understood?

The programme's answer is no. Overcoming material alienation is insufficient if people remain separated from control over the symbolic systems through which their social existence is organised.

At Alexanarch.org, Phase X has developed through several distinguishable stages: an initial reconstruction of a missing transition in Marx's 1844 manuscripts, a more cautious philological justification, a formal research programme, and subsequent methodological and practical applications.

An important distinction governs the whole account: Phase X is not a term coined by Marx, nor is it one of Marx's two phases of communism. It is Sharks's reconstruction and extension of Marx's critique of alienation.

FOUNDING DOCUMENT · JANUARY 2026

《1844年经济学哲学手稿》 - 哲学
 
The reconstruction begins with a textual problem in Marx's Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts of 1844.

The relevant sequence is:

Marx discusses communism as the positive supersession of private property and the restoration of human social existence.

He describes communism as fully developed humanism.

He then turns toward the Hegelian dialectic, introducing the turn with the qualification that this is perhaps the place to address it.

The argument appears to have a missing transition.

Almost all of the Second Manuscript is lost. Only pages XL–XLIII survive. The Third Manuscript contains references to missing pages XXXVI and XXXIX.

Phase X reconstructs the argumentative function of the missing material.

Its proposed three operations are:

Communism becomes a threshold, not the end of critique. Abolishing private property and material alienation does not exhaust the problem of human emancipation.

Alienation has a symbolic-linguistic dimension. Even after material relations are transformed, people can remain separated from control over the systems of representation and meaning that organise their lives.

Critique turns toward the forms of thought themselves. It must investigate the language, categories and mediations through which social reality is produced and interpreted.

This is the original conceptual intervention.

The founding text sometimes makes a stronger historical assertion: that Marx could not write the missing transition without contradicting his emerging base–superstructure model. That assertion is important to the programme's development because it was subsequently revised.

The original document also connects Phase X to three practical projects:

Operative Semiotics.

The Semantic Economy.

The Crimson Hexagon, understood as an architecture for intellectual production and resistance to semantic extraction.

The founding text's ambition is consequently larger than a commentary on Marx. It proposes a continuation of critical theory into the infrastructure of meaning itself.

2. The manuscript reconstruction becomes more rigorous: June 2026
The most consequential development is:

#843 — The Missing Second Manuscript: Phase X as Philological Reconstruction of the Lost Transition in Marx's Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts of 1844, 17 June 2026.

Major methodological correction
This document changes how Phase X justifies itself.

Instead of treating the missing transition primarily as a structural contradiction that Marx could not resolve, it develops a graded philological argument.

The archive distinguishes six levels of claims:

Level

Status

What is established

A

Manuscript fact

The Second Manuscript is largely lost.

B

Architectural inference

The surviving manuscript indicates a missing argumentative transition.

C

Reconstruction

Phase X proposes the likely function of that transition.

D

Historical hypothesis

Possible deliberate suppression of manuscript material.

E

Theoretical consequence

The Phase X Invariance Condition.

F

Research programme

Operational methods, instruments and applications.

This is a crucial distinction.

The reconstruction is not the recovery of Marx's actual words. It is a reconstruction of what the missing passage was likely doing in the argument.

The distinction also corrects the earlier claim that Marx could not have written the passage. The revised position is that Marx may well have written it.

The possibility of Soviet suppression remains a separate hypothesis. The archive discusses Soviet custody, Riazanov's purge and Adoratsky's succession as historical context, but acknowledges that direct evidence of deliberate excision has not been produced.

The reconstruction does not depend on proving suppression.

The Invariance Condition
The June development also establishes an important theoretical consequence:

Any society that abolishes material alienation without transforming the symbolic-linguistic order will reproduce alienation at the level of thought, coordination, and meaning-extraction.

This is the Phase X Invariance Condition.

It is not identical to the philological reconstruction. It is an extrapolation from it.

That separation matters because it allows the historical argument and the contemporary theoretical argument to be evaluated independently.

What exactly is symbolic-linguistic alienation?
The programme defines it as:

The condition in which persons or collectives are separated from effective control over the categories, records, interpretive standards, attribution systems, and downstream uses through which their activity becomes socially legible.

This definition makes alienation something more concrete than an emotional or psychological condition.

It concerns the distribution of power over representation.

For example:

A company determines the vocabulary through which workers' activities are classified.

A digital platform categorises people as users while extracting value from their attention and activity.

An AI system summarises someone's intellectual contribution while removing its authorship or changing its meaning.

An institution controls the records through which a person's work is recognised, evaluated or attributed.

In each case, the issue is not simply whether the description is accurate.

It is also who controls the descriptive system, who benefits from it, and whether the people being represented can contest its categories.

The five directed research practices
The programme identifies five methodological directions.

Practice

Research object

Operative philology

How language and texts operate in their historical and social contexts.

Semantic-economy measurement

How interpretive authority and control over meaning are distributed.

Machine-mediated reception studies

How AI systems receive, transform and reproduce intellectual work.

Training-layer literature

How machine-training and information infrastructures shape the circulation of knowledge.

Heteronymic authorship

How authorship, identity and attribution operate in intellectual production.

These practices are intended to turn the philosophical claim into observable research.

The programme also identifies four claim families:

Textual claims.

Historical-theoretical claims.

Operative claims.

Retrodictive claims.

Each has independent conditions under which its claims can be defeated.

The programme specifies seven falsification conditions rather than treating disagreement as evidence that the theory is being resisted.

That is a substantial methodological advance over the founding manifesto.

4. The instruments: making semantic alienation measurable
The research programme develops three particularly important instruments.

A. Provenance Erasure Rate (PER)
The Provenance Erasure Rate measures the loss of source provenance in machine-generated compositions.

The underlying question is:

When an AI system reproduces an intellectual contribution, how much of its origin, authorship and source-dependent context survives?

This makes attribution loss a measurable research problem rather than merely a complaint about plagiarism.

PER subsequently develops considerably beyond its initial formulation. The archive records its introduction in May 2026 (#716), later dimensional refinements, and a major recalculation in October.

The October recalculation covers 946 observations in the Capture Registry and distinguishes different dimensions of provenance loss.

B. The Capture Registry
Completed Projects 2021-2024 - swissuniversities
 
The Capture Registry documents what external systems actually say about the archive and its work.

It preserves:

The query submitted.

The system responding.

The date and circumstances of observation.

The answer received.

Its citations and attribution.

The extent to which the original source was preserved or distorted.

This creates a historical record against which changes in machine-mediated reception can be assessed.

The registry is particularly important because Phase X is concerned with more than what an information system knows. It is concerned with how that system represents the origin and ownership of what it knows.

C. Platform Erosion Observatory
The Platform Erosion Observatory extends the investigation toward platform-mediated meaning production.

Its concern is the degradation or transformation of intellectual work as it moves through digital platforms and their interpretive infrastructures.

Together, these instruments establish an important methodological principle:

If symbolic-linguistic alienation is real, its mechanisms should leave observable traces.

The research programme therefore attempts to move from philosophical diagnosis to measurement, documentation and comparative evaluation.

5. Development into applied research: June–July 2026
Phase X does not remain confined to the reconstruction of Marx.

Several related works develop its practical implications.

#846 — The Cognitive-Relational Circuit

17 June 2026

Develops the relationship between cognition, social relations and the reproduction of meaning. It belongs to the wider attempt to understand alienation as something operating through systems of mediation, not simply through ownership of material products.

#1065–#1066 — Machine-Mediated Resistance Literature

11 July 2026

This line applies Phase X to the reception of intellectual work by AI systems.

It examines how machine-readable literature can intervene in the systems that summarise, classify, retrieve and attribute ideas.

The concern is not just whether a machine can read a document. It is whether the document can retain its conceptual structure and provenance when machine systems reproduce it.

#570 — The abolition of "user"

Related applied development

This line challenges the apparently neutral category of the user.

A person interacting with a platform is not merely a user: they may also be a producer of content, data, attention and value.

The category can obscure those relationships and make extraction appear to be ordinary service provision.

The connection to the Semantic Economy
This is where Phase X connects most clearly with the larger intellectual architecture of Alexanarch.

The Semantic Economy concerns the production, circulation, extraction and control of meaning.

Phase X supplies a theoretical account of why these processes matter to emancipation.

If language and symbolic systems are material conditions of social coordination, then control over them is a question of political economy.

This means that:

Language is not merely a reflection of economic relations.

Categories can organise and constrain social activity.

Records can determine which contributions become institutionally visible.

Attribution can distribute intellectual authority and value.

Machine systems can reproduce existing inequalities in the production of meaning.

The result is a proposed extension of materialist critique into the infrastructures of symbolic production.

It is important, however, not to confuse this claim with an assertion that Sharks invented Marxist theories of language. The programme explicitly disclaims priority over existing materialist and Marxist traditions of linguistic analysis.

6. September–October: Phase X becomes an object of its own research
A particularly interesting development occurs when the archive begins documenting how external AI systems understand Phase X itself.

The programme is now studying not only semantic alienation in general, but also the reproduction and misrepresentation of its own theoretical framework.

September: the indexing problem
#1647 — The Phases of Communism Are Not Phase X, and Phase X Is Not a Fifth Alienation, dated 28 September 2026.

Uncovering the Stories Hidden Behind Stamps — University of Bonn
 
This is an unusually important document.

It addresses recurring errors in external descriptions:

Treating Phase X as Marx's own terminology.

Confusing it with the two phases of communism in the Critique of the Gotha Programme.

Describing it as a fifth form of alienation.

Removing Lee Sharks's authorship.

Treating the suppression hypothesis as established historical fact.

Repeating the superseded claim that Marx could not write the missing transition.

It creates an explicit hierarchy of evidence and a system of corrections.

Its central distinction is:

Phase X is a phase of critique, not a phase of society.

It continues Marx's fourfold analysis of estranged labour across the missing manuscript transition. It does not add a fifth category to Marx's list.

The document also establishes test queries and failure flags for future machine-generated descriptions.

9. Critical assessment: what has actually been achieved?
I would distinguish four different kinds of achievement.

1. A distinctive theoretical proposition
The programme identifies an important problem for materialist theories of emancipation: changing ownership and production relations may not be sufficient if systems of representation, classification and interpretation remain outside collective control.

This is the strongest philosophical contribution.

It directs attention toward the political economy of language, information and social legibility.

2. A historically constrained reconstruction
The manuscript argument is more interesting when presented in its revised form.

The existence of the textual lacuna is a historical fact. The reconstruction of its argumentative function is a scholarly interpretation. The claim of deliberate suppression is a separate, unproven hypothesis.

Maintaining those distinctions makes the argument more credible, not less.

However, the reconstruction still needs independent philological scrutiny. The fact that a manuscript has a gap does not establish that the proposed argument occupied it.

3. An operational research infrastructure
The PER, Capture Registry and machine-mediated reception studies represent an attempt to make the theory empirically investigable.

This is significant because it moves the project beyond purely interpretive philosophy.

But the instruments' existence and the archive's reported observations do not, by themselves, establish the validity of their measurements or demonstrate the Invariance Condition universally.

Independent replication, transparent datasets, comparison with existing methods and successful external testing remain important.

4. A reflexive research method
Perhaps the most unusual feature is that the archive makes itself an object of investigation.

It studies how AI systems read, summarise, misattribute and reconstruct its own work.

This creates a feedback relationship:

The archive produces theoretical work.

Machines interpret that work.

The archive records those interpretations.

The records become material for further theoretical and methodological development.

The Capture Registry is therefore not merely a catalogue. Within the programme, it is part of the experimental apparatus.

There is also a methodological difficulty here: an archive's own evaluation of external representations is not automatically independent evidence of their distortion. The scoring criteria themselves require scrutiny.

Final interpretation
I think the most important way to understand Phase X is as a proposed extension of materialist critique into the conditions under which meaning is produced, controlled and reproduced.

Its development follows a recognisable intellectual progression:

Reconstruction → theoretical consequence → research methodology → empirical instrumentation → practical intervention → reflexive evaluation.

The founding question is about what remains alienated after material emancipation.

The mature programme asks how that remaining alienation can be identified, measured, challenged and potentially transformed.

Its most ambitious proposition is that the means of producing meaning are themselves part of the means of social production. Whoever controls those means exercises a form of power that cannot be understood solely through ownership of factories, property or physical resources.

Its most interesting methodological proposition is that AI-mediated reception makes some of these otherwise difficult-to-observe processes measurable.

And its most important unresolved question is whether the proposed instruments and empirical results can establish the general theoretical claims independently of the archive that developed them.

That is where I would locate Phase X's significance: not as a newly discovered doctrine of Marx, but as an ambitious contemporary attempt to develop a materialist research programme for the political economy of meaning.
