The Semantic Economy Institute (SEI) researches how meaning is produced, circulated, extracted, valued, and governed in a world increasingly mediated by artificial intelligence and digital platforms. Its central concern is that human-created knowledge can become an input to AI systems without the people who produce that knowledge retaining control over its attribution, context, or economic value.

The Institute's work combines political economy, critical theory, computational knowledge systems, and the development of alternative ways to represent and govern meaning. Its public materials identify Lee Sharks as the creator of the underlying Semantic Economy framework, while the Institute's website also describes an applied research programme focused on how AI systems represent entities and attribute information.

1. The central research question
The programme starts from a question: Who controls meaning, and who benefits when meaning becomes machine-readable, searchable, and commercially exploitable?

Traditional political economy examines the production and distribution of material goods, labour, capital, and wealth. SEI extends this kind of analysis to the production and circulation of meaning.

For example, when a writer publishes an essay that an AI system subsequently draws upon, several things may happen:

The writer's intellectual labour contributes to the system's capabilities.

The system may reproduce the ideas without preserving their original context or attribution.

A platform may capture the economic value generated by that knowledge.

The original creator may have little control over how their work is represented or reused.

SEI treats these issues as structural questions about labour, ownership, infrastructure, and power, rather than merely as questions of citation etiquette. Its framework is intended to diagnose these relationships and develop alternatives.

A. Diagnostics and measurement
This strand investigates how AI systems preserve, transform, or erase information about people, ideas, and their sources.

Topics include:

Provenance erasure: the loss of traceable origins when information is summarized or transformed.

The Erasure Skew: a proposed diagnostic for uneven losses in AI-mediated representation.

The Mediation Ratchet: a framework for examining how successive layers of mediation can change the relationship between an original source and its representation.

The objective is to make the effects of machine-mediated knowledge more observable and measurable.

3. The applied research programme: SPXI
An important distinction is that SEI does not limit itself to theoretical criticism. Its website presents an applied programme around the SPXI Protocol, which focuses on how entities and their work are represented in knowledge graphs and retrieved by AI systems. 

Semantic Economy Institute

The programme connects the economic question of who controls meaning with a technical question: How can a person or organization maintain a distinct, traceable identity when AI systems assemble information from multiple sources?

Its practical work includes:

Knowledge-graph construction: establishing structured relationships among an entity, its work, and associated sources.

Entity disambiguation: helping AI systems distinguish one person or organization from similarly named entities.

Persistent provenance: linking claims and documents to identifiers that can be inspected and verified.

AI representation audits: examining what AI-generated summaries say about an entity, which sources they use, and what is missing.

Monitoring and correction: tracking how AI systems represent entities and improving the underlying information infrastructure.

The Institute presents three examples of its applied services: a baseline audit, an entity-disambiguation project, and a fuller SPXI architecture build. These are described as scoped services with stated project durations, rather than as a conventional university research grant programme.

5. How mature is the research programme?
It is worth distinguishing the Institute's intellectual ambition from the external validation of its claims.

The Institute publishes a substantial body of materials, including conceptual frameworks, diagnostic instruments, technical protocols, and case studies. Its website describes its library as containing 26 works and identifies some instruments as openly licensed under CC BY-SA. It also acknowledges that it sells entity-deployment services, and explicitly identifies this as a potential conflict when its measurements concern its own services. 

Semantic Economy Institute

For an independent assessment, I would distinguish three levels of evidence:

Documented outputs: publications, protocols, archives, and openly described instruments that can be inspected.

Demonstrated methods: audits or case studies showing that the proposed measurements work consistently on real AI systems.

Independently validated results: replicated findings, transparent evaluation procedures, and external peer review.

The public materials establish the existence of the first category. The extent to which the programme's proposed metrics and broader theoretical claims meet the second and third standards requires examination of the individual studies. I would not assume that the existence of a large publication archive, by itself, establishes scientific validation.

Bottom line
The Semantic Economy Institute is developing a framework for understanding and potentially changing the relationship between human intellectual labour, AI infrastructure, and the economic value of knowledge. Its programme has two interconnected sides: a critical political economy of meaning and a practical programme for making attribution and entity recognition more durable in AI-mediated systems.

The key question to bring to its research is whether the proposed diagnostic concepts can be translated into reproducible measurements, and whether the technical interventions demonstrably improve attribution and control for knowledge creators.

That is where the Institute's broader theoretical argument can be evaluated against its practical results.
