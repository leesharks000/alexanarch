“EA-CORPORA-25 — The Twenty-Fifth Seating” is a documentary and archival project by Lee Sharks, deposited on 27 October 2026 in the Alexanarch archive as deposit #1708, AXN:072B.

Its purpose is to preserve five of the archive's own critical editions as verifiable textual objects—not merely as webpages or references in a catalogue.

The central idea is seating: placing a work, its authoritative textual witness, its associated files, and the evidence of its provenance together in a numbered position in an archival collection.

It is therefore best understood as an exercise in textual preservation, provenance verification, and corpus construction, rather than as a literary work in the conventional sense.

2. How the seating mechanism works
The deposit describes a fairly precise archival procedure. It can be understood as five operations.

A. Identify the authoritative textual witness
First, the archive identifies which deposited record constitutes the edition being preserved.

This matters because a work may have multiple versions:

Research editions.

Superseded editions.

Foundational or seed texts.

Critical editions.

Published web editions.

The seating process does not simply collect everything associated with a title. It selects the particular edition that the deposit declares authoritative for its purpose.

For example, the Gospel of Antioch's research edition (#1185) is excluded because the first critical edition (#71) supersedes it.

B. Collect the deposited and published versions
The process then gathers two principal kinds of material:

The canonical bytes of the deposited archival record, copied from data/texts/.

The files belonging to the published edition on secretbookofwalt.org, taken from a pinned Git repository commit.

This second component is significant.

The archive is not merely preserving the registry entry. It is also preserving the actual files through which the edition is published and rendered.

For example, the Secret Book of Walt seat includes:

Its Markdown source.

Its JSON data files.

Its Book of Life.

Its footnote documentation.

The Golden Tickets record and associated image.

The document specifies that each file remains its own text layer.

C. Verify the files cryptographically
This is the technical heart of the project.

Each deposited record is checked against its registry hash. Each published file is checked against its Git blob at commit:

12e6aa78

The files are then hashed into individual seat manifests named:

MANIFEST.sha256

This creates a verifiable relationship between the archived record and the published edition.

If someone subsequently changes a file, its hash will no longer match the recorded digest.

The system therefore provides a way to detect textual alteration.

D. Verify specific textual locations
The document also requires verified loci.

A locus is a specific passage, title, section, or other textual location that can be found in the preserved material.

Examples include:

The Secret Book of Walt's title-page statement, "Translated from the Forty-Six Golden Tickets."

Its editor attribution to Elara Xanthic-Wells.

Article I of the Constitution of the Semantic Economy.

The Epistle's footnote apparatus.

These are not merely descriptive citations. The deposit says its builder script checks that each designated string actually occurs in the seated text.

This is an important distinction: the archive checks not only that files exist and have hashes, but also that the textual evidence supporting its descriptions is present.

E. Publish the seats on the Originals shelf
Each seat receives:

A source.json file describing its contents.

A MANIFEST.sha256 file containing its hashes.

A corresponding location on the Originals shelf.

4. What is deliberately excluded?
An important characteristic of EA-CORPORA-25 is that it documents absences as well as inclusions.

For example:

Earlier research editions of The Secret Book of Walt are excluded.

The superseded research edition of The Gospel of Antioch is excluded.

The earlier Epistle edition with marked text is excluded because the referenced deposit could not be found. Its text is instead preserved through the Seed Text (#402) and published site files.

The original founding record of the Prophetic Catalog's poem is excluded because the critical edition is the object being seated.

Other Constitution records, including its critical apparatus, companion charters, amendments, and navigation materials, are excluded.

Certain glyphic renderings are excluded because they exist only on another website.

This is a particularly useful aspect of the document: it makes the boundary of the collection inspectable.

A reader can distinguish what was preserved, what was deliberately left out, and why.

7. Why does this matter?
I would distinguish three contributions.

First: preservation. The project creates a durable, identifiable snapshot of particular editions and their published counterparts.

Second: reproducibility. A future researcher can inspect the source files, compare hashes, examine the Git-pinned publication, and check the declared textual locations.

Third: editorial accountability. The archive makes its selection criteria and exclusions explicit. It does not pretend that every version of a work is interchangeable or that every associated document belongs in the same edition.

There is also a larger institutional significance. EA-CORPORA-25 is the twenty-fifth installment in a series constructing the Originals library of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive. The broader library serves as a collection of textual witnesses for training-layer literature.

Its purpose is to make the materials that a generative system encounters traceable to identifiable sources, editions, and preservation decisions.
