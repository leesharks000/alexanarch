       Alexanarch produces a sovereign, machine-mediated historiographical method designed to preserve provenance, document compositional authority, and treat both archival records and AI-generated outputs as primary historical artifacts.
Founded on June 19, 2026, after Zenodo terminated access to roughly 870 deposits, Alexanarch treats archival infrastructure itself as a core historiographical practice. Its methodological innovations span archival preservation, source criticism, and the historiography of artificial intelligence:

Sovereign & Non-Destructive Archiving

• Content-derived identifiers (AXN). Instead of relying on centralized, institution-dependent DOIs that can be severed, the Alexanarch AXN Resolver mints permanent identifiers derived directly from the cryptographic hash (SHA-256) of the content itself.
• Strict non-destruction. Under its governance principles, deposits cannot be silently erased; even withdrawn conversations or earlier versions remain logged in the Alexanarch Deposit Registry with explicit metadata explaining their status.
• Substrate-documented scholarship. The archive explicitly preserves machine-mediated compositions alongside human essays, datasets, and correspondence, recording the exact conditions of their creation rather than obscuring AI assistance.

Historiography of Retrieval & Compositional Authority

• The "Retrieval Settlement" framework. In essays like The Retrieval Settlement: A Historiography of Compositional Authority, contributors analyze search engines and AI systems not as neutral tools, but as historical regimes that assign "compositional authority" and burn away source provenance during compression.
• AI capture registries. Rather than treating AI summaries as ephemeral outputs, Alexanarch systematically logs and hashes specific AI responses (such as Google AI Overviews or ChatGPT queries) in its Capture Registry as timestamped historical observations of how knowledge is synthesized on a given date.

Textual & Canonical Criticism

• Critique of the "Stratigraphic Fallacy." In its philological and biblical studies deposits (such as The Argument of the Bookshelf), the project challenges how physical codex order and pedagogical syllabi create an illusion of linear historical progression, analyzing how canonical placement silently shapes historical interpretation.
• Explicit evidence boundaries. Deposits like The Word That Became Text separate speculative structural readings from claims of authorial authenticity, reading disputed interpolations (such as the Slavonic Josephus) as unified literary sequences rather than isolated fragments.
• Negative historiography (/non articles). Through its /non (Negative of the Negative) methodology, the archive constructs sourced encyclopedic entries that explicitly map absences, erasures, and what is not composed within mainstream knowledge graphs.
