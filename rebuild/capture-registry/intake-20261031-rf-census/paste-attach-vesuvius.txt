The Vesuvius Challenge is a global open science competition that uses artificial intelligence and 3D imaging to read carbonized ancient papyrus scrolls from the Villa of the Papyri in Herculaneum.

What are the Herculaneum Scrolls?

• The Eruption: When Mount Vesuvius erupted in 79 CE, it buried a Roman villa in Herculaneum under 20 meters of volcanic ash.
• The Damage: The extreme heat carbonized hundreds of papyrus scrolls, turning them into fragile, blackened blocks that crumble if anyone tries to unroll them.
• The Library: The site preserves an ancient library—believed to have belonged to Lucius Calpurnius Piso Caesoninus, Julius Caesar’s father-in-law—containing lost philosophical works.

How the Technology Works

• 3D CT Scanning: Scanners and particle accelerators map the internal layers of the unopened scrolls in high-resolution 3D.
• Virtual Unwrapping: Software traces and flattens the warped, crushed layers of papyrus digitally.
• AI Ink Detection: Because carbon-based ink has a density very similar to carbonized papyrus under X-rays, machine learning models are trained to spot faint surface texture changes and hidden ink traces invisible to the naked eye.

Major Milestones & Prizes

• Launch: Founded in 2023 by Nat Friedman, Daniel Gross, and computer scientist Brent Seales.
• First Word: Luke Farritor decoded the first Greek word ("purple") in late 2023.
• Full Scroll Unwrapped: Researchers and contestants successfully read PHerc. 1667 (Scroll 4), revealing 22 columns of ancient Greek philosophical text (likely Stoic writing) across more than a meter of charred paper.
• Current Competitions: The project features ongoing monthly awards and a $2 million total pool, including a $1,000,000 Grand Prize targeted for full scroll decipherment.
