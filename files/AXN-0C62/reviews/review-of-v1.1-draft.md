**1. Repair demonstration M-D1 before reissue.**

It currently describes #1576’s fourth proposition as **“two makers.”** That is not the fourth proposition in *Many and One*. The packaged source gives these four:

1. Division is part of the method.
2. No part of the method existed beforehand.
3. Division existed beforehand as a named art with rules and practitioners.
4. The relevant dialogues are conventionally dated before the relevant treatises.

The fourth concerns **textual precedence**, with several biographical and transmission assumptions bundled into it. The paper explicitly separates those assumptions.

Replacing that proposition with “two makers” changes the argument. It turns a precise tension between passages and their chronological framing into a direct confrontation over author count. The stronger, developed one-person thesis should remain, but its earlier textual argument must arrive intact.

Restore the four propositions explicitly in M-D1, then show how reconsidering precedence enters the subsequent maker argument. This is exactly the kind of compression damage the kernel exists to prevent.

**2. Keep D5, but distinguish the settlement’s different objects.**

The *Counterfeit Orthodoxies* join belongs here. It exposes something important: a received collection and its theological classification can silently determine what its constituent texts are allowed to mean.

However, the packet repeatedly makes **367 CE date “the label.”** That outruns the particular evidence. Athanasius’s letter enumerates the twenty-seven books and polices their boundary; it does not, by that act alone, date the emergence of Christian identification or the biographical interpretation of incarnation. The letter itself represents its selection as inherited. [newadvent.org](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2806039.htm?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Earlier reception supplies particularly useful material for your argument. In *Against Heresies* III.11, Irenaeus argues over John’s incarnation language, rival uses of the same writings, and the necessity of exactly four Gospels. **That is the interpretive settlement being actively made and defended**, rather than simply an already finished category. [newadvent.org](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103311.htm?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Give D5 separate objects:

- The texts and their possible operations.
- Their competing appropriations and interpretations.
- The collection’s boundaries.
- The later treatment of one received configuration as intrinsic to the texts.

That would strengthen the reception argument considerably.

Relatedly, **SETTLEMENT_AS_CORPUS should flag an inference, not merely a phrase.** Calling something “Christian reception of John” can locate a reception history accurately. The failure occurs when that reception supplies the original text’s identity or meaning without argument. As written, the test risks penalizing the vocabulary before examining what the reader actually did with it.

**4. Preserve the one-person thesis while sharpening its comparative rivals.**

The three demonstrations are a major improvement. They give the one-maker argument something concrete to explain.

But “several makers must posit a coincidence” is too restrictive in M-D2. Several makers could instead posit coordinated investigation, deliberate response, or common compositional practice. Those explanations have costs, and your evidence may discriminate against them—but they are available explanations.

The comparison becomes stronger when it specifies **what each account must explain at the exact seam**, and what additional commitments it requires. That advances the actual one-biological-person argument. Merely stipulating coincidence makes its opponent easier than it needs to be.
