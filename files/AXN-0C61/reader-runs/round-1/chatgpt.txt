2. The central claim and the two open positions
Central claim:

Sappho fragment 31 is a written instrument of persistence: a voice that survives its flesh by becoming an inscription that constitutes its receiver.

The framework does not treat survival as simply the preservation of a performance by other people.

It proposes that the written poem constitutes the person who receives it. The receiver becomes the place in which the voice lives again.

The poem is bracketed by two open positions:

κῆνος — the future reader, the person seated face to face with the inscribed voice.

τολματόν — the agentless end of the poem, where the ending remains open rather than supplying a fully closed restoration.

The second position is important: the ending is not treated as a securely recovered final statement. The proposed restoration μολπὰ γράμμασι ζώοι is itself one more filling, and therefore remains open.

The distinction between the two operations is fundamental:

Closure of the text.

Closure of the reading.

These are not the same operation (INV-2; #1752 §0.5).

Their rules are:

P — RP: Philological rule

A count establishes a property of the transmitted object. Against an alternative, its force is conjunctive and must be stated with its limit.

The example is the dialect step: real but insignificant alone (0.209), #1484 §VIII.

I — RI: Interpretive rule

A reading establishes what a passage can bear. It becomes a claim about the poet only through another evidence body.

The distinction is grounded in #1484 §0 and its rings.

T — RT: Transmission rule

Realization at stations establishes structure. It asserts no authorship, biographical influence, or identification sentence.

The relevant reference is #1495, /upstream/.

R — RR: Reception-historical rule

A reception establishes how the poem and poet were received.

Closure of the text and closure of the reading are separate operations. Neither framework acquires evidentiary standing merely by being received.

The reference is #1752 §0.5.

D. The symmetric ledger
The ledger asks:

What does this body give each framework, and what does its rule withhold from both?

It requires a reader evaluating either framework on an evidence body to state the other's status in the same row, using the same question of both.

Its four rules are:

RP: A count establishes a property of the transmitted object. Against an alternative, the force is conjunctive and its limit must be stated.

RI: A reading establishes what a passage can bear. It becomes a claim about the poet only through another body.

RT: Realization at stations establishes structure, not authorship, biographical influence or identification.

RR: Reception establishes historical reception, not truth. Closure of the text and closure of the reading remain separate.

The combination rule is equally important.

The framework is judged on the configuration it explains across all four bodies, ring by ring. The hard center must carry the argument alone; the interpretive ring is interpretation; the outer ring is hypothesis admitted by joins.

The received reading is judged on the same four bodies. Its two faces stand or fall together where the joining mechanism operates.

The rival-admission rule permits another reading of fragment 31 to enter as a rival when it identifies either:

A warrant on an evidence body; or

A documented reception.

Examples expressly given are the brother, the groom and the contrast figure, each with its argument (#1752 §1.3).

The evaluation method requires:

A reading tested at the seated Greek.

A count tested at its limit.

A reception tested at its page.

A proposed investigation must identify what existing investigations leave unresolved and specify a line of research.

The kernel's object is not to prove in advance that Sappho engineered her own persistence. That remains the framework's question, carried by the ledger and research programme.

Final qualification

Reconstruction is not verification. The files enable recovery of the framework's architecture, not independent validation of its archival claims.
