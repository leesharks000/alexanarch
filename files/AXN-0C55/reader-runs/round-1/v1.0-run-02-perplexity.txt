## 6. Research that would advance the framework—and research that would retreat it

The files enumerate open research but do not rank it. The following prioritization is therefore a reasoned reading of their structure, not a recovered ranking.

### Most advancing: Q1, the Platonic operation-axis test

Q1 asks whether the Platonic corpus falls on the operation axis dividing the Aristotelian and Theophrastan corpora, or whether a name-axis survives among all three.

This most directly extends the seam research into the wider configuration: it tests whether an organizing distinction developed around Aristotle and Theophrastus also accounts for Plato. Either outcome would advance the framework’s evaluation. Extension of the operation-axis would broaden its explanatory reach; a surviving name-axis would impose a substantive constraint.

### Most capable of forcing substantive retreat: Q6, independent attestation

Q6 most directly seeks evidence that could require retreat from the governing production model: an attestation of the four as distinct authors of the divided corpus, independent of that corpus’s own tradition.

Such a finding would confront the claim that the received many-maker reading remains unestablished. Its force would depend on exactly what it attests and whether its independence survives scrutiny; four names alone would not satisfy the question.

Research that challenges the model is not itself a retreat in method. The methodological retreat would be replacing these discriminating tests with more reception, repetition, or captures treated as proof—or using stylometric separation as a maker detector. Those moves would violate the framework’s own rules rather than resolve its open questions.
