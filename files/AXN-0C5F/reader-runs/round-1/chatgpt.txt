## 2. The three claims and how each is defeated

The November formulation (#1735) separates three claims that must not be collapsed into one.

### Claim 1 — Absolute compositional priority

Claim: Revelation was composed before the other New Testament writings.

- Status: a priced hypothesis.
- Evidence body: chronological (D).
- Governing condition: an external or material anchor must establish the relevant composition date.
- Defeat: an independent, securely dated anchor placing a Pauline letter before the earliest plausible date of Revelation's received form.

The received chronology has not been established by such an anchor.

Nevertheless, the absence of that anchor does not prove absolute priority.

### Claim 2 — Corpus priority

Claim: Revelation presents the first New Testament corpus form at the point of composition, rather than depending on an already circulating sevenfold Pauline church collection.

- Status: a finding.
- Evidence body: corpus-literary (C).
- Governing condition: compare corpus form and architecture.
- Defeat:
  - Demonstration of a sevenfold Pauline church collection circulating before Revelation; or
  - A persuasive demonstration that Revelation's number, order and single-book architecture depend on that collection.

This claim does not face the chronological gate imposed on absolute priority.

### Claim 3 — Generative priority

Claim: Revelation is generative of later New Testament textual forms through structural-genetic transformations.

- Status: a research programme.
- Evidence body: generative (G).
- Governing condition: test transformations arrow by arrow.
- Defeat: identify passages that cannot plausibly be derived from Revelation through any proposed transform, or demonstrate a more economical earlier source or shared tradition.

Defeating an individual arrow does not automatically defeat the entire programme.

The essential separation: rejecting absolute priority does not erase corpus priority or successful generative findings. Each claim has its own evidentiary burden.

### Rung I — The reversed consensus

Fit: REVERSIBLE

The same broad historical-critical dossier has supported opposite critical majorities.

The framework argues that consensus direction is not itself an evidentiary anchor.

Defeat condition: an external or material anchor fixing the received form of Revelation after 70 CE.

### Rung II — The Gallio gap

Fit: SAME-CLASS

The Gallio synchronism reaches the dating of Pauline letters through Acts.

The load-bearing link is of the same inferential class as the evidence it is being asked to overrule.

Defeat condition: an independent, securely dated material or external anchor placing any Pauline letter before the earliest plausible date of Revelation's received form.

The corpus's own self-narrated chronology does not qualify.

### Rung III — The first corpus

Fit: INDEPENDENT

The claim concerns Revelation's corpus architecture at composition, not merely the date of composition.

It stands whether or not the preceding chronological rungs stand.

Defeat condition:

- Evidence of a sevenfold Pauline church collection demonstrably circulating before Revelation; or
- A persuasive demonstration that Revelation's number, order and single-book architecture depend on that collection.

### Rung IV — The midrashim transform

Fit: EXECUTABLE

The framework proposes structural-genetic transformations from compressed Revelation forms into elaborated New Testament forms.

Defeat condition: identifying New Testament passages that cannot be derived from Revelation through any plausible transformation.

Such passages constitute counterevidence.

### Rung V — The Josephus thesis

Fit: CONTINGENT

The Josephus thesis is an available heteronymic reading of New Testament author-functions.

It is not a necessary foundation for the first four rungs.

Defeat conditions:

- Demonstration that ordinary multiple authorship explains the relevant phenomena more economically.
- Explanatory redundancy in the proposed author-function.

### Rung VI — The permanent incarnation (the Logos)

Fit: DEPENDENT

This rung depends on rung V.

It concerns the permanent incarnation and the structural interpretation of the Logos.

Its reading does not deny that a historical person could have existed. It denies that the textual evidence requires one.

Defeat condition: defeat of rung V, on which rung VI depends.

### 6.4 Five evidence bodies and their rules

D — Chronological

D

Unit: an anchor, a date or a synchronism

RD: Only an external or material anchor dates a composition. The corpus's self-narrated chronology and reception memory do not.

C — Corpus-literary

C

Unit: the form of a corpus at composition or gathering

RC: A shared form locates a relation; direction is not inferred from compression alone.

G — Generative

G

Unit: a transform from a compressed form to an elaborated one, with its residue

RG: An arrow is defeated locally where an earlier source or shared tradition explains the correspondence more economically.

R — Reception-historical

R

Unit: a filing, catalogue, source theory, commentary or capture

RR: Reception establishes how books were received and what standing they were granted. It establishes nothing of either framework's truth.

H — Heteronymic

H

Unit: an author-function and its positions

RH: An author-function reading is defeated by explanatory redundancy where ordinary multiple authorship explains the phenomena more economically.

The five bodies are distinct evidentiary domains. They must not be collapsed into a single generalized standard of proof.

## 8. Received chronology, its strength, body-by-body status and symmetric ledger

### 8.1 The received chronology at strength

The rival is:

`fw:received-chronology`

Its strongest formulation, as represented by the kernel, is:

1. Pauline priority is established through the Gallio synchronism, transmitted through Acts.
2. The Irenaean dating supplies the chronological anchor for Revelation.
3. The Apocalypse is consequently late and derivative.
4. Q receives the standing denied to the extant Apocalypse.
5. Ordinary multiple authorship remains the appropriate comparator for the heteronymic phenomena.

This is not represented as a frivolous or arbitrary position.

It is a historically received chronological framework with an established reception history.

But its standing must be separated from the truth of its chronological inferences.

The kernel explicitly states that the received chronology has no external or material anchor establishing its composition order.

Its chronological standing is therefore not equivalent to demonstrated chronological priority.

### 8.4 Combination rules

The combination rule prevents the maximal claim from hiding behind weaker claims and prevents rejection of the maximal claim from erasing findings that do not depend on it.

The three claims combine as follows:

- Absolute priority: judged by an external or material anchor.
- Corpus priority: judged by whether a sevenfold Pauline collection preceded Revelation.
- Generative priority: judged locally, arrow by arrow.

The rung structure reinforces the combination rule:

- V and VI may be defeated without disturbing I–IV.
- A failed generative arrow does not automatically defeat the programme.
- A failed absolute-priority claim does not automatically defeat corpus priority.

### 8.5 Rival-admission rules

A further chronology enters as a rival when it supplies either:

- RD: an external or material anchor; or
- RC: a documented corpus.

A chronology that is merely logically possible is not admitted as an independently established rival. It retains the status of the received chronology.

### 8.6 Evaluation method

The ledger prescribes three different forms of evaluation:

1. Arrow: test at its loci — the seed in Revelation, the elaboration elsewhere, and the common antecedent sought.
2. Anchor: test the anchor at its actual object.
3. Standing: test the source theory that granted the standing.

A proposed investigation must name what existing investigations leave unresolved and specify its line of research.

The kernel succeeds when Revelation First can be rebuilt with each claim assigned its proper rung, evidentiary grain and date.

Whether Revelation actually is first remains the framework's substantive question, not an assumption built into the ledger.
