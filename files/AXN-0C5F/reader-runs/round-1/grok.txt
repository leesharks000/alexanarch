**2. The three claims and how each is defeated**

- **Absolute** (temporal: Revelation completed before the earliest surviving Pauline letter; state: priced): defeated by an independent anchor placing another New Testament composition before the earliest plausible date of Revelation’s received form. Not defeated by an anchor that merely dates Revelation late without an earlier anchored composition (rung I’s scope).  
- **Corpus** (formal: the seven-church corpus prior as a form; “occasional Pauline letters may be earlier”): does not settle the temporal relation of any letter to Revelation. Sub-claims: historical generation = the economical reading of the other two, not independently demonstrated; archetypal = established (first attested and treated as the model in reception); economy = argued. Defeated by evidence of a sevenfold Pauline church collection demonstrably circulating before Revelation, or a persuasive demonstration that Revelation’s number, order and single-book architecture depend on such a collection.  
- **Generative** (arrow by arrow: particular structures compressed in Revelation elaborated later; state: a research programme of reversible arrows): defeated locally where an earlier source or shared tradition explains the correspondence more economically, or where the proposed direction cannot account for the borrower’s transformation; failure of one arrow does not decide the rest. Bounded by the declared transform procedure.

- Combination: Each of the three claims is judged on its own conditions; absolute by external/material anchor; corpus by sevenfold collection before Revelation; generative locally arrow-by-arrow.
