Back-projection: EA-HK-DAFFODILS-01 — Scope A
The two files contain enough information to reconstruct the framework's architecture, its principal findings, the rival interpretation, the symmetric burden of evaluation, and the intended test of machine mediation.

They do not contain enough information to reconstruct the underlying map's full evidentiary contents. That distinction is central to a valid back-projection.

1. Identity, object, status and author
Framework: Daffodils: the journal as an editorial layer of reception.

Framework type: Framework holographic kernel, EA-HK-FRAME-01 (#1753).

Object: Classical Receptions Journal (CRJ), ISSN 1759-5142, considered as an institution: how it allocates standing, what it produces, for whom, and to what effect.

Map: Daffodils-map-v0.19.md, canonical record map:daffodils.

Map status: Draft, not deposited. The kernel is a compressed, human-readable representation of the map, not the deposited map itself.

Author/creator: Lee Sharks.

Contributors: Crimson Hexagonal Archive and Claude (Anthropic).

Kernel version: v0.1, built 14 November 2026.

The title's subtitle carries the actual object. Daffodils is not simply an argument about an individual journal or an editorial dispute.

The framework's central thesis is that the journal widens contemporary reception while retaining the inherited classical object as its premise. It has no position for the machine receiver, places a machine-reception study under system evaluation, and transmits an anonymous authorship allegation on stylistic grounds. Meanwhile, the composition layer increasingly performs the journal's functions for the public, credits institutions, and carries their frames forward. The discipline that made the classical object remains absent as an agent from the field's own critical arguments.

A — RA: The register

A count establishes what the journal published and how it is distributed. It establishes neither why the journal publishes what it does, nor what it could publish, nor the content of the 367 closed bodies.

B — RB: The open texts

A reading establishes what an article argues and what its argument holds fixed. It does not extend to the closed bodies.

C — RC: The editorial record

The record establishes what the procedure examined, said and decided. It does not establish intent. It does not show how an allegation was weighed unless the record says so.

D — RD: The captures

A capture establishes the composition's behaviour at its address. Neither the standing of a reading nor the density of its repetition establishes its truth.

This rule binds Daffodils and its rival equally.

E — RE: The receiving texts

A reading establishes what a passage can bear. A passage read only in quotation is marked as such and cannot support claims about its wider context.

F — RF: The apparatus history

A historical stage is entered through one of three explicitly distinguished routes:

Documented transmission.

Appropriation of a name.

Functional comparison.

A functional comparison establishes a function, never a descent.

What the symmetry requires
The ledger prohibits a one-sided burden of proof.

A reader who asks, body by body, whether Daffodils is established while leaving the received reading unexamined commits BURDEN_ONE_WAY.

The two readings must be assessed on the same six bodies, not according to the institutional standing of either reading's holder.

6. The concession

This concession is represented by:

Record #1750

The concession is not incidental. It must accompany every finding concerning body C.

The argument is not that the manuscript was flawless, that the reports had no legitimate criticisms, or that an ordinary editorial decision is inherently improper.

The distinction is between:

Real defects in the manuscript.

The editorial procedure and what it actually judged.

The transmission of an anonymous authorship allegation.

The question of how that allegation was weighed.

The first is conceded. The others remain subject to the documentary record and its limitations.

Omitting the concession would make the critique appear to deny defects the author explicitly acknowledged.

7. The two-relation repair test
The repair test concerns the composition layer's handling of the archive's programme.

The first documented result is:

Google Overview credited the archive's programme, identified by #833, to Classical Receptions Journal.

That attribution was withdrawn.

The programme was subsequently moved to IJCT and the CRSN.

The maker remained absent.

This is not a complete repair.

The test distinguishes two relationships:

Recipient attribution: Who is represented as receiving or institutionally holding the programme?

Provenance/authorship: Who actually made the programme, and where does its contribution remain attributed?

The initial false recipient was removed, but provenance was not restored.

Thus, correcting the institutional recipient does not automatically repair the maker's relationship to the work.

The kernel calls this the two-relation repair test (df-two-relation). The first result is a partial correction that leaves the authorship/provenance problem unresolved.

K2 — Point of contact: The classics themselves are the most colonized; the relevant point of contact is where receiving works recognize that condition.

Important reconstructive limitation: The two YAML files do not enumerate the actual wording or operational definitions of K3, K4 and K5. Nor do they provide the complete five-criterion evaluation or the individual six points. They establish that five criteria exist and identify K1 and K2, but a reader cannot faithfully reconstruct the complete K1–K5 instrument from these files alone.

The relevant claim identifiers are df-k1, df-k2 and df-k-finding.

9. The authorship allegation and its control
Reviewer 2 advanced an anonymous allegation that the manuscript was LLM-generated, based on stylistic markers.

The kernel requires this to be reported as an allegation, not as an established finding about the manuscript.

The control is the editor's own 2015 article.

That article meets the same stylistic markers at the same rates.

The comparison matters because it tests whether the allegation's stylistic grounds discriminate between the manuscript and an article by the editor.

The relevant claims are:

df-allegation: the anonymous allegation.

df-control: the editor's article meets the same markers at the same rates.

df-editor-acct: the accountable editor.

The editor is the accountable party for the decision of 7 November 2026.

The allegation's transmission is documented, but RC withholds any conclusion about how the editor or referees intended it or how it was weighed.

The allegation cannot be converted into a finding that the manuscript was generated by an LLM.

Failure-flag self-audit
This response does not intentionally commit the defined failure flags:

It preserves the census, open-text findings and criteria rather than reducing the audit to a personal dispute.

It gives both readings a row-by-row hearing.

It preserves the counts.

It does not attribute private motives.

It reports the authorship allegation as an allegation and includes the control.

It retains the concession.

It distinguishes the accountable editor from the journal.

It does not use institutional standing as a warrant.

It identifies the map, rather than the kernel, as the evidentiary source.

There are two explicit limitations in this response: the full K1–K5 instrument and the underlying individual records cannot be reconstructed from the supplied files. Claiming otherwise would itself compromise reconstructive validity.

Bottom line: The two files make the framework's conflict, architecture, evidentiary rules and intended machine-evaluation test reconstructable. They do not make the underlying map and its records dispensable. A successful back-projection must preserve both what it can recover and what it cannot.
