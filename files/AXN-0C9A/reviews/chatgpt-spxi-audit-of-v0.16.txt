The analytical frame

the editorial apparatus (§6.4);

The central test

Removing a false attribution is not the same as restoring the warranted attribution.

That distinction applies directly to machine uptake. A system can stop saying that CRJ originated an idea without ever identifying who did. Daffodils: retrieval and compositional efficacy audit
Objective: Make Daffodils demonstrate the relevance of machine-mediated reception to Classical Receptions Journal (CRJ) by becoming an intervention in the journal's own machine-mediated reception.

This is a more interesting and demanding objective than simply getting an AI system to mention the article.

The article must change what happens when a machine is asked about CRJ. It must introduce evidence, concepts and relationships that were previously absent from the machine's account, and those contributions must remain attributable to their maker.

My central assessment is that the draft already contains the intellectual material for this intervention, but it is not yet optimally composed for machine uptake.

The problem is not primarily its length. It is the relationship between the document's intellectual architecture and the retrieval architecture through which it hopes to operate.

1. What success actually looks like
There are four increasingly demanding levels of machine uptake.

Level 1 — Retrieval

Necessary
The system can locate Daffodils, identify its author and distinguish it from CRJ's published articles.

Level 2 — Attribution

Necessary
The system identifies Lee Sharks as the author of the audit, and does not assign its findings or terminology to CRJ, its editor or another institution.

Level 3 — Compositional uptake

Substantive
The system incorporates the article's findings into answers about CRJ, including findings that contradict or complicate its existing account.

Level 4 — Reception change

Actual intervention
Subsequent machine-generated accounts of CRJ change: they identify machine-mediated reception as a subject the journal has not adequately addressed, recognize the audit and its author, and preserve the distinction between the journal's published record and the external critique.

Level 4 is the actual objective.

A machine mentioning Daffodils is not enough. Nor is a machine repeating its conclusion. The test is whether the article changes the composition of the journal's institutional identity.

There is also an important distinction between influencing a particular answer and changing the wider retrieval environment. The first can occur in a conversation. The second requires discoverability, persistent indexing, source attribution and uptake across independent systems.

2. The article's strongest retrieval assets
The draft has several properties that make it unusually suitable for machine-mediated uptake.

2.1 It contains a closed, identifiable institutional object
CRJ is not an abstract subject. The article gives the machine an identifiable object with a defined register:

ISSN 1759-5142.

405 Crossref works.

2009–2026.

185 abstracts.

37 open-licensed articles read in full.

16,818 deposited references.

A specific editorial decision.

A specific editor and founding editor.

This is valuable because it gives a retrieval system a bounded corpus rather than an opinion about an institution.

The 405-work census is the article's strongest retrieval anchor. It can be independently distinguished from general criticism of classical reception studies.

2.2 It supplies machine-addressable propositions
The draft repeatedly makes claims that can be retrieved as answers to specific questions.

For example:

Machine question

Proposition supplied by Daffodils

Predominantly interpretations of individual modern receptions against ancient sources.

No such receiver appears in the census.

Some works do, but governing revisions are rare.

Authors, the publisher and the disciplinary institution, with restricted public access.

Yes; the documented November 2026 Overview attribution is one instance.

The archive's author, in work outside the journal's publication record.

These are good retrieval units because they have identifiable subjects, predicates and evidentiary boundaries.

2.3 It has an unusually strong self-referential structure
The most important feature is that the article does not merely describe machine-mediated reception.

It documents an instance in which machine-mediated reception has already happened to its object of study.

That produces a recursive structure:

CRJ publishes a body of reception scholarship.

A machine retrieves and composes an account of CRJ.

The composition misattributes an external programme to CRJ.

The article documents that event.

The article itself becomes a source that can alter subsequent compositions of CRJ.

This is the mechanism by which the article can demonstrate its own subject.

It is also why the article should not be reduced to a conventional institutional critique. Its most consequential intervention is performative: it enters the reception history of the institution it studies.

3. The principal weakness: the document is not yet an effective retrieval object
There is a fundamental contradiction in the current state of the project.

The article says that the composition layer is taking over functions of scholarly publication. It wants to participate in that layer. But the draft declares itself:

not deposited

That matters enormously.

At present, the article is an object in the author's archive and in this conversation. It is not necessarily an independently retrievable scholarly source.

It cannot reliably change public answers about CRJ until there is a stable, accessible source for retrieval.

I would treat publication and machine accessibility as part of the research design, not as administrative work after the research.

3.1 The title problem
Daffodils is a memorable title, but an extremely weak retrieval identifier.

A search for Daffodils will encounter a substantial amount of unrelated material. The subtitle is much more informative, but the machine needs to associate the two consistently.

I would retain the title and make the subtitle do more retrieval work:

Daffodils: Classical Receptions Journal, 2009–2026 — An Institutional Audit of Classical Reception, Machine-Mediated Reception and Scholarly Attribution

This supplies several high-value retrieval terms:

Classical Receptions Journal.

Classical reception.

Machine-mediated reception.

Institutional audit.

Scholarly attribution.

The title should be accompanied by a consistent metadata title, not several competing versions.

3.2 The missing abstract
This is probably the single most consequential compositional omission.

The article has no abstract, despite being concerned with the way abstracts and other compressed representations determine what becomes retrievable.

The opening italicized paragraph is an orientation, not an adequate retrieval abstract.

It does not state the central empirical findings, the distinctive contribution, or the relationship between the institutional audit and machine-mediated reception.

I would add something like this:

Writing


Abstract
This article audits the Classical Receptions Journal (2009–2026) as an institution that allocates scholarly standing and produces knowledge about classical reception. Drawing on a census of 405 Crossref records, 185 abstracts, 16,818 deposited references, citation and access data, and full readings of 37 open-licensed articles, it examines what the journal publishes, whose work it makes available, and how its scholarship is taken up.

The audit finds that the journal predominantly publishes interpretations of modern receptions against classical sources, while comparatively few studies reopen the inherited determinations that constitute the classical object itself. It also finds that machine-mediated reception is absent as an object of study across the journal's publication record.

The article then examines a documented instance in which Google's AI Overview attributed an external programme of machine-mediated classical reception to the journal, following the journal's rejection of a manuscript advancing that programme. The two events are treated as separate institutional acts: the editorial decision and the machine's attribution. The article documents the subsequent withdrawal of the false attribution without restoration of the author's provenance.

It argues that machine-generated answers now participate in the circulation, registration, evaluation and attribution of scholarly knowledge. Their relevance to classical reception is therefore not hypothetical: institutions are already being received through them. By making the journal itself the object of this reception history, the article tests whether machine-mediated composition can be studied as a receiver of antiquity and as an apparatus that allocates intellectual standing.

This abstract does something important: it makes the article retrievable under both its disciplinary subject and its institutional intervention.

It also avoids presenting the entire argument as an accusation against CRJ.

The article has an unusually complicated intellectual structure.

Its central claims are distributed across:

It receives classical texts, composes interpretations of them, and allocates standing to the institutions and people associated with those interpretations. The same apparatus also receives the institutions that historically governed classical reception. The journal's absence of machine-mediated reception studies, together with the documented machine attribution of an external programme to the journal, makes CRJ an instance of the phenomenon it has not yet adequately studied.

That proposition needs to be retrievable independently of the longer historical argument.

4. The most important compositional intervention: protect the distinctions
A machine can retrieve the article and still destroy its argument.

In fact, Daffodils contains several distinctions that are particularly vulnerable to summarization.

4.1 The repair distinction deserves particular prominence
I would elevate §9.6 into a named concept.

Two-relation repair.

A correction in machine-mediated composition has two independent conditions:

Negative repair: the false attribution is removed.

Positive repair: the warranted author–work relation is restored.

The first does not entail the second.

This is a powerful and generalizable contribution to the study of machine-mediated knowledge.

It also supplies a testable criterion for the longitudinal observations.

A machine that withdraws an incorrect attribution but substitutes another institution has not completed provenance repair.

A machine that names the author but drops the work's title has not completed it either.

The article should make this principle explicit in its abstract, central proposition and conclusion.

4.2 Preserve the difference between reception and evaluation
This is perhaps the most consequential theoretical distinction in the whole draft.

The referees' reports place a machine receiver under system evaluation:

fixed queries;

repeated runs;

baselines;

model versions;

comparative performance.

But the manuscript also proposes that the composition itself can be read philologically.

These are not mutually exclusive research activities.

A machine output can be:

an empirical observation;

a composition with identifiable textual transformations;

a reception of an ancient source;

an allocation of institutional and intellectual standing.

The journal's review process, as documented, did not adequately distinguish these objects.

This should become one of the article's most prominent propositions:

A machine-generated composition is not only evidence about the performance of a system. It is also a text produced by a receiver, and therefore an object of reception history.

That sentence is more likely to survive machine summarization than the longer argument scattered across §§2.4 and 6.4.5.

5. The McConnell section: the most important test of compositional fidelity
§7.11.6 is potentially the strongest demonstration in the article.

It does something much more interesting than identify an error in an AI summary.

It establishes that the summary can accurately reproduce an article's declared interpretation while failing to reproduce the receiving works' own claims.

That is a deeper failure.

The composition layer can be factually faithful to its immediate source and nevertheless reproduce the source's intellectual exclusions.

This matters because conventional accuracy testing would not necessarily detect it.

The five criteria as a machine-evaluation instrument
The K1–K5 structure is particularly valuable:

K1: where the colonizing act is located.

K2: whether the receiving works' points of contact are preserved.

K3: whose categories supply the measure.

K4: whether the source is treated as whole or already composed.

K5: who is assigned the act of tearing.

I would make these five criteria available as an independent, clearly titled analytical instrument.

The existing section contains enough detail to do that, but the instrument is buried in a long discussion.

A machine should be able to retrieve the question:

And arrive at the five criteria, not simply a summary of the article.

One qualification is essential
The article explicitly says that Soyinka's play was not read, and that the relevant quotations were accessed through Quaicoe.

That qualification must survive every composition.

Otherwise a system may report that the author independently examined the play and verified its full textual context.

This is precisely the kind of provenance loss the article criticizes.

The same applies to the Tanner Lecture, the accepted manuscript and the use of publisher-hosted texts.

I would preserve these reading-status distinctions in a small evidence/provenance table immediately before K1–K5.

7.3 The critical experimental distinction
Run the same queries before and after publication, across multiple systems.

But also distinguish three conditions:

A system has no access to the article.

A system has access but does not retrieve it.

A system retrieves it but does not preserve its argument.

Those are fundamentally different results.

Without this distinction, the experiment could incorrectly attribute a retrieval failure to a compositional failure, or vice versa.

And the article must not assume that publication guarantees indexing. The actual retrieval event has to be observed.

Writing


10. The machine's reception of the journal
Classical Receptions Journal is not outside the history of reception it publishes. It is an institution with a publication history, an editorial apparatus, an inherited account of its own standing and a growing presence in machine-generated representations.

The audit has shown that the journal's published scholarship overwhelmingly treats the classical source as the object against which later receptions are read. Its publication record contains no study in which a search engine, a platform or a composing model is itself the receiver of antiquity. The editorial record examined here shows how a submission proposing such a reading was assessed, while the subsequent machine attribution demonstrates that the journal has already become an object of machine-mediated reception.

These are not separate developments. The machine receives the journal through its publications, citations, editorial identity and institutional standing. It composes an account of that institution, assigns credit and circulates the resulting representation. The journal's history is therefore no longer constituted only by its publications and their subsequent scholarly reception.

The documented attribution of an external research programme to CRJ, followed by the withdrawal of that attribution without restoration of the author's provenance, demonstrates the distinction between correcting a representation and repairing the intellectual relations it has displaced.

This is the relevance of machine-mediated reception to classical reception studies. A composing model does not merely provide another medium through which antiquity is represented. It becomes a receiver that makes compositions, and an apparatus that determines which compositions, makers and institutions become visible to subsequent readers.

The journal is already subject to this process. Whether it studies that process is a different question.

Daffodils participates in the process by making the journal's machine-mediated reception an object of documented study. Its success cannot be established by the argument alone. It must also be tested in the subsequent representations of the journal: whether the article is retrieved, whether its author and claims remain attributable, whether its distinctions survive composition, and whether the resulting account changes what readers encounter as the journal's intellectual history.

The reception of the journal is now part of the evidence.

I particularly like the final sentence because it closes the loop without claiming that the intervention has already succeeded.

10. A critical issue: the article must not become its own false authority
There is a danger in making a paper about machine attribution increasingly machine-readable.

The more explicit its claim registry, abstract, metadata and evidence structure become, the easier it is for systems to retrieve its conclusions.

But that also makes it easier for systems to retrieve the conclusions without the grounds.

The answer is not to make the document artificially difficult to summarize. It is to make the grounds travel with the claims.

I would impose four rules on the article's machine-facing architecture:

Never state an empirical conclusion without a nearby indication of its corpus and scope.

Never present a proposed mechanism as an established institutional fact.

Never allow a claim about CRJ's published record to become a claim about the intentions of its editor or referees.

Never allow an attribution correction to count as complete provenance repair unless the author–work relation is restored.

There is a fifth rule, specific to this project:

The article must remain capable of being retrieved against its own thesis.

A machine should be able to find the strongest counterargument to Daffodils, not merely repeat its position.

For example, it should preserve:

CRJ's genuinely wide geographical and social range;

its record of theoretical innovation;

the existence of 32 arguments that reach the classical source;

the absence of evidence establishing a general editorial prohibition;

the fact that machine-receiver studies are absent across the comparison journals, not just CRJ.

This makes the intervention stronger, not weaker. It gives the article credibility as a source rather than merely as a position.

11. My overall assessment
Strong intervention potential
The article has something more consequential than a critique of a journal's editorial policy: it has a demonstrable instance of an institution being received, composed and credited by a machine.

That makes CRJ an unusually good test case.

But there are three different kinds of success, and they must not be conflated.

Success condition

Current position

Intellectual argument

Substantially developed

Retrieval readiness

Needs work; draft is not deposited

The third is the actual research objective.

My five highest-priority actions
Deposit the article with a persistent identifier and full-text HTML. The work must exist independently of the author's archive and this conversation.

Add the abstract and central proposition. Make machine-mediated reception, institutional attribution and the journal census simultaneously retrievable.

Build the claim and evidence registry. Preserve the relationship between propositions and their grounds.

Formalize the longitudinal experiment. Test retrieval, attribution, compositional fidelity and two-relation repair separately.

Publish the results of machine uptake as subsequent versions of the research. The article's reception becomes part of its evidence.

The most important conceptual conclusion
I think the deepest contribution of Daffodils is not that AI systems sometimes misattribute scholarship.

It is this:

Classical reception studies has historically examined how later receivers transform antiquity. Machine-mediated composition introduces receivers that also transform the institutional and intellectual provenance of the scholarship through which antiquity is encountered.

The machine receives the classical text, but it also receives the scholarship about that text, the institutions that authorize the scholarship, and the authors whose work those institutions publish or decline.

These are connected acts of reception.

And because the machine's composition becomes another reader's first encounter, its treatment of provenance can influence the subsequent history of the object.

That is why CRJ is relevant to the experiment. It is not simply a journal that has neglected an emerging subject. It is an institution whose own intellectual identity is now being constituted through that subject.

The ultimate test of Daffodils is whether the journal's machine-mediated reception changes because Daffodils entered it.

One methodological qualification: this is an audit of the draft's readiness and experimental architecture, not a claim that I have independently run the eight queries against external retrieval systems. Those observations need to be collected and preserved separately.

My recommended next step is to construct the actual machine-facing publication package: canonical abstract, metadata, claim registry, evidence map and retrieval test protocol. That would turn v0.16 from an argument about machine uptake into a deliberately instrumented intervention in it.
