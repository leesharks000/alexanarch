HOW ONE FINDING MOVED (SR-W2), recorded in order. (1) The deposited paper (v1.0) classed the sentence unsound because "the subject of deductive reasoning" changed the scope of the cited lines. (2) Opening the article whole, the session's first pass took the article's own sources (Smith: "the deduction ( sullogismos )"; Krabbe: "deductive reasoning (sullogismos)") as support for the gloss and withdrew the finding. (3) The operator rejected that: "deduction" and "deductive reasoning" are interpretive translations that relocate the interpretive move to translation; Aristotle defines syllogizing in excess of deduction; dividing and combining is the more accurate account of the undertaking. The session checked these against the sources and adopted them (SE 1's definition; Krabbe's and Smith's statements of what the definition requires; SE 17, 18, 20, 23 on division and combination). (4) A reader in round 01 argued the other way on one point: a translation can name the ancient category while its translator marks the distance from the modern one, so Smith's caveat does not by itself make "deduction" unfaithful; and the additional defining conditions make the class narrower, so "in excess" must say in what sense. The session kept the finding on the scope of the undertaking (183a37–b1), and records the fidelity of the translation as such as contested.

P1. The scope is this undertaking. Ταύτης δὲ τῆς πραγματείας (183b34) points back to the undertaking the retrospective has just described — the syllogistic capacity, questioning and answering, the treatment of arguments (183a37–b8). *Rules out:* rescuing the denial by relocating it to an undertaking defined independently of that description — "logic," "the formal theory of valid inference," a calculus the *Analytics* later supplies. *Reach:* it fixes the referent; it does not by itself decide whether every activity the undertaking examines counts as a contribution to it. That boundary is argued from the passage (P2, P8). *The escape it closes, first tried:* "he means the systematic study of logic." 
And from #1574 §3, verbatim:
The immediate undertaking is not presented here merely as the construction of a formal deductive calculus: its stated work also includes dialectical and peirastic procedures — the acquisition and defence of arguments from reputable premises, the conduct and ordering of questions, replies, and the solution of apparent refutations. At 184b1 the central methodological achievement of the broader πραγματεία is redescribed as an inquiry περὶ τοῦ συλλογίζεσθαι; syllogistic competence supplies the organizing capacity for those functions without being coextensive with every feature of the undertaking.
