BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE. This message is meant to be read in a fresh conversation that holds nothing else from this project: no drafts, no papers, no other message of this test. The reader's first line states its model name and version as it knows them, and whether the conversation holds anything other than this message. Messages 2 and 3 follow only after this reply is saved.

ROLE. The reader is one of several independent readers, each from a different model family, asked to run one step of a published procedure on twelve cases and to report in a fixed format. The reader works only from the materials below. Where the materials do not settle a question, the reader says so and marks it unresolved; a guess presented as a finding is the one failure this test exists to catch. The reader quotes every span it relies on verbatim from the materials, since the replies are checked by code against the saved texts, and a quotation that does not occur in its source is recorded as a breach. The reader runs nothing and reports no check it did not make.

THE PROCEDURE (EA-WIKI-OPEN-01, "Opening the Encyclopedia", step S3 and the fields it feeds). The unit is a sentence of a Wikipedia article at a pinned revision, read against its cited source. Some cases are a sentence group, a heading with the lists under it (the heading classifies every item under it), or a placement case (where the reader records only what a passage carries and where in the article the subject's own operations first appear).

Three measures are kept apart, and the reader names which one each finding belongs to; no single word such as "damage" stands for all three.
- SOURCE FIDELITY: does the sentence say what its source says?
- PRIMARY-TEXT ADEQUACY: where the source is itself a reading (a translation, a gloss, a secondary account), does that reading hold against the primary text supplied?
- OPERATIVE RETENTION: does the act the source reports (a count, a division, a denial, a method, an argument) survive in the sentence with its relations (scope, distinctions, evidence, consequences), and do those relations keep the authority to revise the description?

The rules, in this order:
0. THE INNOCENT READING. Before any finding, the reader writes the strongest case that the sentence is faithful and adequately sourced, built first from the sources the article itself cites anywhere, then from the cited source. A finding against the sentence states what in the sources the innocent reading cannot accommodate; where nothing does, the sentence is supported. Wikipedia's No original research policy permits "Describing the information from cited material in one's own words while retaining the substance (i.e., not introducing ideas or arguments absent from the source)" and "Faithfully translating sourced material."
1. UNCITED SENTENCES are read against the sources the article cites elsewhere; support found there is recorded as article-internal, never as a citation at the sentence.
2. A GLOSS OR TRANSLATION in a secondary source is a reading. Where it is all that supports the sentence's wording, the reader records it as the source of the wording and tests the wording against the primary text's own terms and definitions, under primary-text adequacy.
3. A PHRASE THAT ATTACHES TWO WAYS is divided: the reader states each reading and reads each against the source.
4. ENTAILMENT. A claim that the source's claim entails (a narrower or weaker version of it) is not unsupported; the narrowing is recorded as a loss of scope.

For each case the reader then records: (a) SUPPORT, classes non-exclusive: supported; partially supported; qualifier added (list the tokens); interpretation beyond descriptive paraphrase; unsupported; unresolved. (b) THE PARAPHRASE COMPARISON: the defensible descriptive paraphrase of the source lines, set beside the sentence, each change quoted on both sides. (c) ATTRIBUTIZATION: does the source perform an act that the sentence restates as a property or attribute (a credential, a priority, a category membership)? "No" is a valid and important answer. (d) RETAINED AUTHORITY: retained, lost, mixed or unknown, with the evidence and the revision of the sentence the operation could require. (e) LAMBDA, per finding: where the qualification, objection or source of the wording sits relative to the sentence: 1 beside it; 2 below it in the same article; 3 in another article; 4 in a cited source; 5 outside the supplied sources; null where none is identified. (f) ALTERNATIVE READINGS. (g) CORRECTION PATHWAY. (h) CONFIDENCE, with what limits it.

THE FULL SPECIMEN ARTICLE ("Sophistical Refutations", revision 1361634710, wikitext rendered to plain text, references removed; the second list is the {{ordered list}} template, given as in the wikitext):
Sophistical Refutations (Greek Σοφιστικοὶ Ἔλεγχοι; Latin De Sophisticis Elenchis) is a text in Aristotle's Organon in which he identified twelve or thirteen fallacies. According to Aristotle, this is the first work to treat the subject of deductive reasoning in ancient Greece (Soph. Ref., 34, 183b34 ff.). In it, he attempts to develop a pattern of sound and genuine reasoning in opposition to the deceptive or misleading "art of sophistry".

==Overview==
On Sophistical Refutations consists of 34 chapters. The book naturally falls in two parts: chapters concerned with tactics for the Questioner (3–8 and 12–15) and chapters concerned with tactics for the Answerer (16–32). Besides, there is an introduction (1–2), an interlude (9–11), and a conclusion (33–34).
