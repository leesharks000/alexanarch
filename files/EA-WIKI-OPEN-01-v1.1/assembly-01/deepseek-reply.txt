## Issues with the prompt or format surfaced by the run

These are things the test should catch before it goes to other families, because if I hit them, they'll hit them:

1. **The format does not have a "hedge" or "date omitted" kind.** SR-W1's hedge and TH-1's undated 1911 judgment are the two most interesting losses in the set, and neither fits the closed list ("scope | qualifier added | idea introduced | term changed | omission | none"). I forced SR-W1 into "scope" and TH-1 into "omission." A second reader in a different family may make a different forced choice and the code check will record the disagreement as substantive when it isn't.

2. **"Attributization: yes | partial | no" has no criterion for "partial."** SR-W2 keeps the act as "identified" and the priority as attributed; the exactness and the gloss are converted. Is that "yes" or "partial"? I wrote "yes" for SR-W2 because the operation's authority is what changes; the session wrote "partial." Both are defensible; neither is checkable.

3. **`support_source` is too coarse.** SR-W2's priority claim is at the sentence (pointer to primary text); its gloss is from the article's other sources. The schema forces one to be chosen. I split it with a "+" annotation rather than choosing; a reader in another family may choose differently.

4. **λ is ambiguous between two things.** For SR-W2, the gloss's source is elsewhere in the article (λ = 4); the *correction's* source is the primary text (also λ = 4, because the pointer is at the sentence but the exactness is in the Greek that the pointer doesn't reproduce). λ = 4 in both readings but for different reasons. The prompt treats λ as one field; it is two.

5. **AC-1 has no source material**, so the schema's most substantive fields (support, paraphrase, deltas) are empty or forced. If the test is meant to be run whole by each family, AC-1 has to be a separate kind of case with its own schema, or the run has to be split into an S3 part and a placement-observation part.

6. **"Innocent_reading" is doing two jobs.** In SR-W2 my innocent_reading is a paragraph of argument that both makes the innocent case and shows where it fails at its own sources. The prompt treats it as the strongest case for the sentence, but the procedure's S3(0) actually requires the innocent reading to be *tested* against the sources, and the failure recorded. My field compresses both into one string, and the compression is where the interesting move lives.

7. **Deltas don't fit composition-and-division.** In SR-W2 the phrase "in ancient Greece" composes two ways, and the finding is that it *either* bounds a denial *or* relocates it. The delta schema requires one "source_says" and one "kind" per row. I could add a fourth delta for the composition, but it wouldn't be a delta — it's a *rule* about how to read the phrase. The schema has no slot for this, and this is the most consequential finding in the specimen.

8. **Message 2's "who audits the auditor"** is well-framed, but for a same-family reader the answer is predetermined: evidence, not standing — because I share TACHYON's priors. A cross-family reader could say "standing" for reasons I can't see. The test will reveal nothing on this axis from me; it needs an actual other family.

9. **"Damage" is undefined.** The prompt asks readers to find where the session "finds damage that is not there," but never says whether damage means unsound (fails its own cited source), or loss (loses a term or scope), or attributization (converts an operation into a property). SR-W1's hedge is a loss, not unsoundness. IW-1's "personal" for "possessive" is a loss of precision, not damage. If the run is to be comparable across families, this needs a definition before the next send.

10. **The "keep your own Message 1 readings where you still hold them and say so" instruction in Message 2** is right, but the format doesn't have a field for it. The JSON's `agreement` array can carry "partly," but that's not quite "I keep my earlier reading and disagree with you." A separate field would let the reader record: my earlier reading was X, the session says Y, I hold X, decisive span Z.

**B7 (P1 threat).** The largest threat to P1's test as designed is **selection bias in matching that the four covariates do not capture**: entity pages and work pages differ in genre conventions (a biography's lead enumerates credentials; a work article's lead summarizes a work), in what kinds of sentences recur (identity sentences vs. argument sentences), and in who writes them (Wikipedians with an interest in the person vs. an interest in the text). Matching on length, age, citation density and revision activity does not remove these differences; it makes them look controlled.

The remedy that removes the most bias: **within-page comparison** — the same article read at its lead and at its body, or a page with both an entity section and a work section. The entity form's prediction is not just "entity pages lose more than work pages"; it is "within a representation, the lead carries the entity and the body carries the operation." That prediction is testable within a page and does not require matched pair selection. The matched pair test is a second-best approach; the within-page test is a better one and does not suffer from the selection problem.

Blinding: **yes, and more than the paper proposes**. Remove page titles, leads, infoboxes and page type; replace the entity's name with a placeholder; shuffle the sentence order within the article; and give the reader only the sentence and its cited source. Only after the classification is recorded should the page type be restored for scoring.
