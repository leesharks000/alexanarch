# Secondary Source: how the Apocalypse came to be received as derivative, secondary and marginal — a design

Working design, v0.2, 2026-11-05. Not deposited. Precedes a draft. v0.2 rebuilds v0.1 on the archive's existing Revelation First work, which v0.1 under-read: v0.1 presented the Muratorian locus as a finding of its own when it is the premise of Revelation First: Literary Genealogy (#1735 §IV.3), and restated the mechanism of The Argument of the Bookshelf (#1420) without crediting it. The companion study to Legitimately Many (#1733), on the same method: strata of reception, loci read in seated texts, counts kept apart, a locus table with a provisional coding, the operator's rulings in the apparatus. Loci marked [seated] were checked in the seat named; loci marked [to seat] enter the study only when read in a seated text; loci marked [read in #N] were read by an earlier deposit and are cited to it and re-read in seat.

## 0. The question, and what rests on it

0.1. The book presents itself as primary. It opens Ἀποκάλυψις Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ, ἣν ἔδωκεν αὐτῷ ὁ Θεός (Rev 1:1), names the act of writing and its address, Ὃ βλέπεις γράψον εἰς βιβλίον καὶ πέμψον ταῖς ἑπτὰ ἐκκλησίαις (1:11), blesses its reader (1:3), and seals its text against addition and subtraction (22:18–19) [seated: gnt-nestle1904/text/Rev.txt].

0.2. It is received as the last, the latest and the least: last in the codex, dated last or nearly last, disputed in the East, absent from the Byzantine lectionary, assigned to a second John, explained by its sources, and set apart as the strangest book of the collection.

0.3. **The stake (the operator, 2026-11-05).** In the operator's words: "if revelation comes first, then the logos it depicts is a work of literature before it is a historical person. and independent of it." The archive holds this as Rung VI of the argument ladder (revelationfirst.com/ladder/logos/): every pillar of the historical person is inferential, and on the Revelation First reading the gospels, if they are midrashim transforms of Revelation, are "testimony to the Logos, not to a biography" (the ladder's wording). The converse is what the received order carries. A book placed last, and read as derived from what precedes it, has its Christ read as derived too: the figure of Revelation 1 and the rider named ὁ Λόγος τοῦ Θεοῦ (19:13) are received as a seer's late visionary elaboration of a Jesus first known from the gospels and Paul. Secondary Source tracks that reception. Its object is the secondariness of the book and, carried with it, the secondariness of the figure.

0.4. The study holds the archive's thesis and states it (#1735; #1737; 66 CE as the working proposed date, held as proposed). The reception history does not have to prove the thesis. It records how the contrary became the received frame, and in particular how the order of the books came to stand for the order of the figure, the biography first and the Logos after.

## 1. What the archive already holds, and what this study adds

1.1. **The mechanism: The Argument of the Bookshelf (#1420, AXN:059D).** The terminal placement is an editorial and theological decision with two sufficient determinants, architectural climax and canonical hesitation ("The end of the codex is also, in a canon, the seat nearest the door", §2). The stratigraphic fallacy reads a designed sequence as a deposition sequence, and it is "transmitted, not committed" (§3): by the codex traversed front to back, by pedagogy, by the commentary series and the introduction, and by developmental narrative. The audit (§4) finds an asymmetry of treatment: early internal indicators held to maximal scrutiny, one ambiguous external sentence received as an anchor. The remedy (§5) is to mark the supply. v0.1's governing observation (order and standing set by filing and use, derivation read off them) is #1420's thesis extended from date to derivation, and is credited to it.

1.2. **The dating record: Revelation First: Literary Genealogy (#1735) §§II–III; ladder Rungs I–II.** Irenaeus, Adv. haer. 5.30.3, is one sentence with its subject unexpressed (ἑωράθη), a strong reception memory and no inscriptional date (§II.1; Rung I). The consensus reversed: the late nineteenth-century critical majority for a Neronian or Galban date (Schaff; Milligan's concession of 1893) gave way to Swete, Charles and the later default, with no new first-century object deciding it (§III; Rung I, "φ = REVERSIBLE"). Pauline priority is reconstructed through Acts, of the same inferential class as the evidence it is asked to overrule (§II.2; Rung II, "The Gallio Gap").

1.3. **The Muratorian direction: #1735 §IV.3 and ladder Rung III.** Paul, sequens prodecessoris sui Iohannis ordinem, writes by name to seven churches (lines 47–50; Metzger 1987: 305–7; Hahneman 1992 for the fourth-century dating). #1735 reads it as "direct evidence for an ancient construal of corpus priority", sets the canonological reading against it, and states what it does not prove. This is the essay's premise and the study takes it as given.

1.4. **The four questions: #1735 §IX.** Composition, collection, configuration as corpora, and generativity for later writing. Secondary Source works in the last two and in their reception.

1.5. **The transforms and the figure: the unfoldings.** The kernel and the midrashim transform (#1414, Rung IV), the heptad (#1416), the derivations M₂–M₅ (#1418), the James case (#1415), the upstream unfoldings into the Apocalypse's channel (#1511 with #1484, #1494, #1495), the heteronyms (Rung V, contingent by construction) and the Logos (Rung VI). These argue the direction of derivation. The reception study argues none of it; it records how the opposite direction was received as fact.

1.6. **What Secondary Source adds.** Three things the existing work leaves to be done. First, the reception record itself, locus by locus across the strata, with order, standing and derivation counted apart: #1420 names the mechanism and audits the date, and the study tests the mechanism at the loci. Second, the reception of the Muratorian construal: #1735 reads what the fragment says, and the study reads what the later strata did with it, whether it was cited, set aside as corrupt, filed as the fragmentist's error, or left unread. Third, the derivation of the figure: where and how the Christ of the Apocalypse came to be read as dependent on the Jesus of the gospels and Paul, which #1420 does not address and the ladder argues against without tracing.

## 2. Four things kept apart

The study counts four things and never lets one stand for another.

2.1. **Order:** where the book stands in a collection, a codex, a list or a lectionary.

2.2. **Standing:** how a list, a council, a father or an edition rates the book: acknowledged, disputed, spurious, read, unread, "in the second rank".

2.3. **Derivation of the text:** what the book is said to come from or come after: its sources, its date, its author's relation to the apostle, its relation to the gospels and letters.

2.4. **Derivation of the figure:** what the book's Christ is said to depend on: the Jesus of the gospels, Paul's Christ, Jewish angelology, Daniel's Son of Man, the imperial or astral imagery of the region. This count is separate from 2.3: a reader can date the book early and still read its Christ as derived, and the study records each count where it falls.

The hypothesis, credited to #1420 and extended: order and standing were set by filing and liturgical practice; derivation of the text was later read off them; and derivation of the figure was read off the derivation of the text. A book placed last and rated second is read as having come last, and its Christ as a late image of a figure known first elsewhere.

## 3. The strata

3.1. **The book's self-disclosure** [seated]: Rev 1:1–3, 1:11, 1:12–16, 19:11–16, 22:7, 22:10, 22:18–19.

3.2. **The earliest witnesses**: Justin, Dialogue 81 [to seat]; Irenaeus, Adv. haer. 4.20.11 and 5.30.3 [5.30.3 read in #1735 §II.1; to seat]; Melito and Papias as Eusebius reports them [to seat]; the Muratorian fragment [read in #1735 §IV.3]. For this stratum the study asks the questions #1735 does not: whether these witnesses treat the Apocalypse's Christ as derived from anything, and whether any of them reads Paul or the gospels through John.

3.3. **The disputes** [to seat]: Gaius of Rome (Eusebius HE 3.28, the book ascribed to Cerinthus); the Alogi in Epiphanius, Panarion 51; Dionysius of Alexandria in HE 7.25, the stylistic argument separating the book from the Gospel's author, made in the controversy over Nepos and the millennium. A reading of the book was answered by lowering the standing of its author. Mechanics only.

3.4. **The ledgers** [to seat; the Eusebian accounting summarized in #1420 §2]: Eusebius HE 3.25 (the book entered among the acknowledged and among the spurious, each "if it seems right") and 3.39 (Papias's two Johns); Laodicea 60 and the Apostolic Canons 85; Cyril of Jerusalem, Catecheses 4.36; Gregory of Nazianzus, Carm. 1.1.12; Amphilochius, Iambi ad Seleucum; Athanasius, Festal Letter 39; Hippo and Carthage. Each list is also read for its order and for whether any later list repeats the Muratorian construal.

3.5. **The codex and the lectionary** [to seat; the argument in #1420 §§2–3]: the book's terminal place in the codices that carry it, its transmission in a small fraction of the Greek manuscripts, its absence from the Byzantine lectionary. #1420 argues what this does to the date; the study records what it does to standing and to the figure: a Christ heard in the liturgy from the gospels every week and from the Apocalypse never.

3.6. **The printed Bible and the Reformation** [to seat]: Erasmus's Annotationes; Luther's 1522 preface to the Apocalypse and its 1530 replacement; the September Testament's unnumbered tail; Zwingli at Bern; Calvin's commentaries. Luther's 1522 preface is a central locus for 2.4: on the received description, it judges the book by whether Christ is taught and known in it, the measure of Christ being taken from the gospels and Paul. The wording enters only when read in WA Deutsche Bibel 7.

3.7. **Criticism** [to seat; the dating reversal read in #1735 §III]: the source-critical hypotheses of the 1880s (Vischer 1886; Spitta; Weizsäcker); the history-of-religions readings (Gunkel 1895; Bousset 1896, and Bousset's Kyrios Christos 1913 for the developmental account of early Christology); Charles 1920. Derivation becomes method, for the text and for the figure: the Apocalypse's Christ placed on a line of development from the Jesus tradition. Counter-witnesses in the stratum are read as such (Robinson 1976, for dating only).

3.8. **The composition layer** [seated: the Capture Registry]: answer engines on the Apocalypse and on the archive's thesis, including the Overviews that collapse Revelation First into Revelation Early (the operator's ruling of 2026-10-30: lead with the correction). The study adds queries on the Logos of Revelation and on the Christology of Revelation, to see whether the composition layer reads the figure as derived, under the registry's default capture conditions, signed out and incognito.

## 4. What the study measures and what it withholds

4.1. **The locus table.** One row per locus: stratum, seat, exact wording as seated, the count (order, standing, derivation of the text, derivation of the figure) and its filler, and a code on the six-way coding of Legitimately Many (does not address; presupposes; argues for; considers and excludes an alternative; revises; holds open). The coding is the session's provisional reading; the operator rules on it. Where #1735 or #1420 has already read a locus, the row cites that reading.

4.2. **The prediction, stated before the table is built, and frozen with its hash before any locus is coded.** If #1420's mechanism holds and extends: the early strata presuppose the book's place and leave both derivations unaddressed; argument about derivation of the text appears only where the book's standing was in question, as a defence of a standing already given; derivation of the figure appears later than derivation of the text and is read off it without the step being argued; and the Muratorian construal, once the later strata have the codex order, is not repeated and is not answered.

4.3. **Withheld.** The study does not decide the date, the author, the priority of the Apocalypse or the historicity of a person; it holds the archive's thesis and states it. It reads no motive: incentive and function only.
