# How to Write Matthew from Revelation

### The generative grammar of the first Gospel: a philological and compositional demonstration

Lee Sharks

*Draft v0.1, 26 November 2026. Not deposited. The research record behind every claim is The Unfolding Map, v0.45; its ledger of relations (52 rows, each checked in a tagged text) is this paper's evidence, cited by row. Greek is quoted from Nestle 1904 as seated in the archive and every quotation here was verified against that seat by machine.*

## 0. The question

0.1. The received account, in full. Matthew is a life of Jesus written in Greek late in the first century by an author who had Mark before him and reworked it. What Matthew has beyond Mark came from a sayings collection shared with Luke, or from Luke's use of Matthew, and from material of Matthew's own, written or oral, shaped by scripture. Revelation is another kind of book, a prophecy sent to seven assemblies in Asia, and where the two agree in wording they draw on a common stock: the Septuagint, the sayings of Jesus in circulation, the diction of early Christian prophecy. On this account nothing passes from Revelation to Matthew. Where anything passes between them it passes the other way, from the tradition of the sayings into the prophet's book.

0.2. This paper asks a different question and does not begin by disputing that account. Suppose a writer has Revelation, whole, and wants a Gospel. What must that writer do? Which operations, applied to which places, give which parts of Matthew? The question is answered by doing it. Each section takes a place or several places of Revelation, states an operation, performs it, and sets the result beside Matthew's Greek.

0.3. Three things are meant by "write" and they are kept apart. (a) A relation in Revelation and a relation in Matthew are the same relation with a stated change: this is shown by quotation and by counts over the whole New Testament. (b) The change is one of a small set of moves that recur: this is shown by the ledger. (c) A move, given to a reader who is shown only the place in Revelation and the rule, produces Matthew's passage: this has been done once, under seal, and is reported in section V. Most of what follows is (a) and (b). Only section V is (c).

0.4. The demonstration does not, taken alone, say who wrote first. Every pairing below can be read from right to left as the work of a seer who had Matthew and compressed it. What the demonstration gives is a procedure that another reader can run, alter, reverse or break, and a measure of how much of Matthew the procedure reaches. The argument for the order of the two books is made elsewhere in the archive and stands or falls on other grounds. Here the book is taken as a source and worked with.

0.5. The source is held open. A writer who knows a book does not move through it in order. Any place may be drawn on at any point of the new work, several at once, and the same place twice. Tests that looked for Revelation's sequence in Matthew's sequence failed three ways (section X), and they failed because composition does not work like that. The unit is the relation: who does what to whom, in what mode.

## I. Give the speaker a past

I.1. Revelation's speaker speaks now, from heaven, in the first person, as one about to come. The first operation gives that speaker a past: a teacher who went about a land, with followers, and was killed there. Call it P1. Its first consequence is the simplest. What the speaker says in the first person can stand as what the teacher said.

I.2. The thief.

| Matthew 24:43 | εἰ ᾔδει ὁ οἰκοδεσπότης ποίᾳ φυλακῇ ὁ κλέπτης ἔρχεται, ἐγρηγόρησεν ἂν |

I.6. The other account of the same thing. Bauckham (1977; again 1983) set the thief of Revelation 3:3 and 16:15 and the knocking of 3:20 beside the Synoptic parables of the parousia and read the direction as the received account does: parables of Jesus, used in early exhortation until the story fell away and the saying remained. His word for it is deparabolization. The observation is the same as I.2. The operation is the inverse. Section V returns to this, because there the story can be shown arising from the saying under a stated rule, and the falling away cannot be shown in the same manner.

I.7. Rule M1. A saying of the speaker in the first person, a promise, a warning or a formula, is kept as a saying of the teacher. Fourteen rows of the ledger keep.

## II. Give the life a beginning

II.1. A life needs a birth. Revelation has one, at its centre.

| | |
|---|---|
| Revelation 12:4 | ὁ δράκων ἕστηκεν ἐνώπιον τῆς γυναικὸς τῆς μελλούσης τεκεῖν, ἵνα ὅταν τέκῃ τὸ τέκνον αὐτῆς καταφάγῃ |
| Revelation 12:5 | ὃς μέλλει ποιμαίνειν πάντα τὰ ἔθνη |
| Revelation 12:6 | ἡ γυνὴ ἔφυγεν εἰς τὴν ἔρημον |
| Matthew 2:13 | φεῦγε εἰς Αἴγυπτον … μέλλει γὰρ Ἡρῴδης ζητεῖν τὸ παιδίον τοῦ ἀπολέσαι αὐτό |
| Matthew 2:6 | ἡγούμενος, ὅστις ποιμανεῖ τὸν λαόν μου τὸν Ἰσραήλ |

II.2. The second operation, P2: the host is a told life in a named land, anchored to scripture. Three things happen under it here. The adversary is named from the land: the one who stands waiting to destroy the child at its birth is a king, Herod. The flight keeps its verb and gets a place on the map. And the child "who is to shepherd" is said in scripture's words and cited as written, since the phrase was scripture's before it was Revelation's.

II.3. The frame. Matthew begins from Revelation 12. It ends, as sections VII and VIII show, from Revelation 11 and 20. The centre of the source is the two ends of the life. Wenkel (2020) describes the violent scenes of Matthew 2 and the discourse of chapters 24–25 as an inclusio of apocalyptic discourse round the Gospel. The ledger agrees on chapter 2 and puts the closing bracket later, at the cross and the tomb.

II.4. What P2 does not give. The genealogy, the magi, the star's journey, Nazareth. Goulder (1974) took Matthew's infancy narrative as the plainest case of the evangelist composing by midrash on scripture, with Mark his only written source. That account covers what this one does not reach in chapters 1 and 2, and this one covers a relation his does not name: why the child is hunted at birth and saved by flight.

## III. Make the vision happen

III.1. What a seer sees, a narrative must have happen. Two forms do this. A sign seen in heaven becomes an event on the ground (HAPPEN), or it is said by the teacher of days to come (FORETELL).

III.2. The face, the falling, the hand, the word.

| Revelation 1:16–17 | Matthew 17:2, 6–7 |
|---|---|
| ἡ ὄψις αὐτοῦ ὡς ὁ ἥλιος φαίνει | ἔλαμψεν τὸ πρόσωπον αὐτοῦ ὡς ὁ ἥλιος |
| ἔπεσα πρὸς τοὺς πόδας αὐτοῦ ὡς νεκρός | ἔπεσαν ἐπὶ πρόσωπον αὐτῶν καὶ ἐφοβήθησαν σφόδρα |
| ἔθηκεν τὴν δεξιὰν αὐτοῦ ἐπ’ ἐμὲ | ἁψάμενος αὐτῶν |
| Μὴ φοβοῦ | μὴ φοβεῖσθε |

Four relations in one order. The seer's part is given to three followers: "I fell" becomes "they fell", the hand laid on one becomes a touch on several, the singular imperative becomes plural. The falling, the touch and the word are Matthew's; Mark's transfiguration has none of the three.

III.3. The same four are used again at the tomb, divided.

| Revelation | Matthew 28 |
|---|---|
| σεισμὸς ἐγένετο μέγας | σεισμὸς ἐγένετο μέγας |
| ὡς νεκρός | ἐγενήθησαν ὡς νεκροί |
| Μὴ φοβοῦ | Μὴ φοβεῖσθε ὑμεῖς |

The earthquake of the seventh bowl happens at a tomb in the same three words. The seer's falling "as dead" goes to the guards, and "fear not" to the women. One place of the source, Revelation 1:17, is drawn on twice in Matthew, for a mountain and for a tomb, and at the tomb its two halves go to opposite parties.

III.4. Foretold.

| | |
|---|---|
| Revelation 6:12–13 | ὁ ἥλιος ἐγένετο μέλας … ἡ σελήνη ὅλη ἐγένετο ὡς αἷμα … οἱ ἀστέρες τοῦ οὐρανοῦ ἔπεσαν εἰς τὴν γῆν |
| Matthew 24:29 | ὁ ἥλιος σκοτισθήσεται, καὶ ἡ σελήνη οὐ δώσει τὸ φέγγος αὐτῆς, καὶ οἱ ἀστέρες πεσοῦνται ἀπὸ τοῦ οὐρανοῦ |
| Move | FORETELL: the seer's aorists become the teacher's futures. Both stand on Isaiah 13:10 and 34:4, and Mark 13:24–25 has the saying |

III.5. Gurtner (2012) asks how apocalyptic symbols are to be read once they stand in a *bios* and not in an apocalypse. P2 is that question put as an instruction. Mathewson (2011) reads Matthew 3:16–4:11 as a seer's visionary experience told in the third person. The ledger has two rows there from the letters (the trial by the devil; who bows to whom), and his reading and these rows describe the same stretch from two sides.

## IV. Give the teacher an economy

IV.1. Revelation has a relation of buying and selling with four bearers: the speaker, the Lamb, a rider, the adversary. Matthew tells all four, each turned its own way. Each construction below was searched through every verse of the New Testament; the lists are complete.

IV.2. The speaker's.

| | |
|---|---|
| Revelation 3:17–18 | Πλούσιός εἰμι καὶ πεπλούτηκα … πτωχὸς … συμβουλεύω σοι ἀγοράσαι παρ’ ἐμοῦ χρυσίον πεπυρωμένον ἐκ πυρὸς ἵνα πλουτήσῃς |
| Matthew 19:21 | πώλησόν σου τὰ ὑπάρχοντα καὶ δὸς πτωχοῖς, καὶ ἕξεις θησαυρὸν ἐν οὐρανοῖς |
| Move | OPPOSE the act: buy becomes sell. The end is kept: to be rich in what the speaker has |

IV.3. The Lamb's.

| | |
|---|---|
| Revelation 5:9 | ἐσφάγης καὶ ἠγόρασας τῷ Θεῷ ἐν τῷ αἵματί σου |
| Matthew 27:6–7 | τιμὴ αἵματός ἐστιν … ἠγόρασαν ἐξ αὐτῶν τὸν ἀγρὸν τοῦ κεραμέως εἰς ταφὴν τοῖς ξένοις |
| Move | EXCHANGE the buyer across the sides. By the same blood the Lamb buys people of every tribe for God, and the chief priests buy a field for the burial of strangers. "Buy" stands within a verse of "blood" at these two places in the New Testament and at no other. The story is Matthew's alone |

IV.4. The rider's.

| | |
|---|---|
| Revelation 6:5–6 | ἔχων ζυγὸν ἐν τῇ χειρὶ αὐτοῦ … Χοῖνιξ σίτου δηναρίου |
| Matthew 11:29–30 | ἄρατε τὸν ζυγόν μου ἐφ’ ὑμᾶς … ὁ γὰρ ζυγός μου χρηστὸς |
| Matthew 20:2 | ἐκ δηναρίου τὴν ἡμέραν |
| Move | The teacher takes the rider's seat. What the rider holds, the teacher offers as his own, and the dearth becomes rest. The price of a day's grain becomes a day's wage, in the same genitive of price. ζυγός is in no other Gospel |

The rider's one verse is also divided among the Gospels. The oil and the wine stand together at Revelation 6:6, 18:13 and Luke 10:34, with two denarii in the next verse, and nowhere else. Barley stands near a denarius at Revelation 6:6 and John 6:7–9, and barley is in no other book. Matthew has the coin for the day.

IV.5. The adversary's.

| | |
|---|---|
| Revelation 13:17 | ἵνα μή τις δύνηται ἀγοράσαι ἢ πωλῆσαι εἰ μὴ ὁ ἔχων τὸ χάραγμα |
| Matthew 21:12 | ἐξέβαλεν πάντας τοὺς πωλοῦντας καὶ ἀγοράζοντας ἐν τῷ ἱερῷ |
| Revelation 13:15 | ὅσοι ἐὰν μὴ προσκυνήσωσιν τῇ εἰκόνι τοῦ θηρίου |
| Matthew 22:20 | Τίνος ἡ εἰκὼν αὕτη καὶ ἡ ἐπιγραφή |
| Revelation 18:11–12 | οἱ ἔμποροι τῆς γῆς κλαίουσιν καὶ πενθοῦσιν ἐπ’ αὐτήν, ὅτι τὸν γόμον αὐτῶν οὐδεὶς ἀγοράζει οὐκέτι … μαργαριτῶν |
| Matthew 13:45–46 | ἐμπόρῳ ζητοῦντι καλοὺς μαργαρίτας … πέπρακεν πάντα ὅσα εἶχεν καὶ ἠγόρασεν αὐτόν |
| Move | OPPOSE. The beast shuts out of the market all but its own; the teacher shuts the market out of the temple. The image that must be bowed to, with a mark for buying, becomes the image on a coin, and the claim is divided. The merchants weep that no one buys their pearls; a merchant sells everything and buys one. ἔμπορος stands within a verse of a pearl at these places only |

IV.6. One stretch of Matthew, 19:16–20:28, now has four places of the source in it: the rich one who must trade (Revelation 3:17–18); the seat given, δώσω αὐτῷ καθίσαι μετ’ ἐμοῦ ἐν τῷ θρόνῳ μου, kept at ἐπὶ δώδεκα θρόνους and turned at τὸ δὲ καθίσαι ἐκ δεξιῶν μου καὶ ἐξ εὐωνύμων οὐκ ἔστιν ἐμὸν τοῦτο δοῦναι, the only other place where "give" governs "to sit"; the rider's coin; and, between them, the story of the next section. A writer with the source open draws on all the places that hold the relation in hand.

## V. Turn the name into a story

V.1. This is the one place where the procedure has been run forward and not only read back.

V.2. The seed is two verses.

> Ἰδοὺ ἔρχομαι ταχύ, καὶ ὁ μισθός μου μετ’ ἐμοῦ, ἀποδοῦναι ἑκάστῳ ὡς τὸ ἔργον ἐστὶν αὐτοῦ.
> ἐγὼ τὸ Ἄλφα καὶ τὸ Ὦ, ὁ πρῶτος καὶ ὁ ἔσχατος, ἡ ἀρχὴ καὶ τὸ τέλος.

V.3. Under P1 and P2 the speaker cannot say this of himself in a told life. A master in a likeness must do it. Then two things the vision says side by side collide. The wage is to each as his work is. And the first and the last are one. In a vision they never meet. Told as an economy they cannot both hold: if the ends are one, the pay cannot differ by the work. The conserving solution is to stage the collision (STAGE): workers who differ as much as possible in what they did, the same paid to all, and the paying so ordered that those who did most see it. The title, which no master can bear, becomes the order of the act (ENACT).

V.4. The rule was written as a pack: the two verses, the two parameters, six forced consequences each with the grammar that enacts it, three searches into the Septuagint. No word of the target was in it. A key of twelve points on the Greek surface was sealed before any run. Fresh readers, three language models from one maker, each in a separate context, were given the pack and nothing else.

V.5. What they wrote, and what Matthew has.

| Forced by the pack | Matthew 20 |
|---|---|
| the reckoning dated at day's end, from the statute on the hireling's wage (Deut 24:15) | ὀψίας δὲ γενομένης |
| a command to an underling to pay | Κάλεσον τοὺς ἐργάτας καὶ ἀπόδος τὸν μισθόν |
| the title as the order of the act | ἀρξάμενος ἀπὸ τῶν ἐσχάτων ἕως τῶν πρώτων |
| the objection of those who did most | Οὗτοι οἱ ἔσχατοι μίαν ὥραν ἐποίησαν, καὶ ἴσους αὐτοὺς ἡμῖν ἐποίησας |
| "my own", and the begrudging eye (Deut 15:9) | ἐν τοῖς ἐμοῖς; ἢ ὁ ὀφθαλμός σου πονηρός ἐστιν |
| the closing line, chiastic, future | Οὕτως ἔσονται οἱ ἔσχατοι πρῶτοι καὶ οἱ πρῶτοι ἔσχατοι |

Six runs with the full pack scored thirteen of thirteen on the sealed key. In three of the six the title's second pair had been exchanged in the source, as a control; it changed nothing, because the staged collision fixes the order whatever the title's order is, and the control is recorded as having failed to discriminate. All six wrote, word for word, ἀρξάμενος ἀπὸ τῶν ἐσχάτων ἕως τῶν πρώτων. None touched any of six near neighbours (the talents, the minas, the two sons, the tenants, the unprofitable servants, the unforgiving servant).

V.6. That alone shows little, since each reader knows Matthew and four of the six said so in their logs. The cuts show more.

| Source given | What was written | Runs |
|---|---|---|
| both verses | groups by the hour; the same wage; paid last to first; the dispute; the eye; "the last shall be first" | 6 of 6 |
| the title alone (22:13) | one group; one wage; paid first to last; no dispute; "the last shall be as the first" | 3 of 3 |
| the wage "to each as his work" alone (22:12) | three or four groups; paid in proportion, a drachma an hour, an obol a row; no order; no saying | 3 of 3 |
| the two parameters and ENACT, no forced consequences | a grain harvest; pay in proportion; first to last (1 point of 13) | 1 of 1 |
| a verse with the wage and the work and no title (Sirach 51:30) | no order of payment, no dispute, no saying (2 points of 13) | 1 of 1 |

Neither half of the seed holds the story. The title alone gives identity: the ends are alike. The rule of the wage alone gives arithmetic. The equal pay against unequal work, the order turned, the complaint and the reversal appear only when the two stand together and the consequence is drawn. One reader, given "to each" alone, recorded that it knew the missing title "from the next verse of the same book", declined to supply it, and wrote wages by the row.

V.7. What the pack could not produce was declared beforehand and held as declared: the denarius, the marketplace, the burden and the heat. The denarius has a seed, as IV.4 showed, in another place of the source. One forced consequence was found by a reader and not by the pack: the first group must have an agreed wage, or equal pay is a fraud and not a collision.

V.8. Set this beside I.6. Bauckham's account takes the story to be first and the saying its residue. Here the saying is given, a rule is stated, and the story comes, and it comes only from the conjunction. That does not prove the order of the two books. It does show that the first verse of the pair and the second, which Revelation sets in consecutive clauses, are sufficient and jointly necessary for the plot of Matthew 20:1–16 under a rule written in advance.

## VI. Make the end of the world into a parable

VI.1. A scene of the end, seen, becomes "the kingdom is like" (LIKEN), with the scene's members kept.

VI.2. The harvest.

| | |
|---|---|
| Revelation 14:15 | ἄλλος ἄγγελος ἐξῆλθεν … ἐξηράνθη ὁ θερισμὸς τῆς γῆς |
| Matthew 13:39 | ὁ δὲ θερισμὸς συντέλεια αἰῶνός ἐστιν, οἱ δὲ θερισταὶ ἄγγελοί εἰσιν |

"Harvest" stands in a verse with "angel" at these two places only. Matthew has the vision as a likeness and then explains the likeness by the vision's own terms.

VI.3. The fire.

| | |
|---|---|
| Revelation 20:15 | ἐβλήθη εἰς τὴν λίμνην τοῦ πυρός |
| Matthew 13:42 | βαλοῦσιν αὐτοὺς εἰς τὴν κάμινον τοῦ πυρός |

"Thrown" with "fire" in one verse is in Revelation (seven verses) and the Gospels (ten, six of them Matthew's) and in no other book. In Revelation those thrown are the beast, the devil, death and Hades, and whoever is not written. In Matthew they are the fruitless tree, the lawless within the kingdom, and "you". The patient is exchanged from the adversary to the hearers.

VI.4. The wedding. ἦλθεν ὁ γάμος τοῦ Ἀρνίου; Μακάριοι οἱ εἰς τὸ δεῖπνον τοῦ γάμου τοῦ Ἀρνίου κεκλημένοι. Matthew 22:1–14 is a king's wedding for his son, with those called. It also has what a wedding does not need: armies sent, murderers destroyed, τὴν πόλιν αὐτῶν ἐνέπρησεν, a guest examined for his garment, bound and thrown out. Each has its place in the stretch of Revelation that runs from the war of the kings through the burning of the city to the wedding and the binding (17:14; 18:8; 19:7–9; 19:14; 19:21; 20:2–3). Six places gathered into one likeness. Luke's supper (14:16–24) has the calling and none of the war.

VI.5. Rule F3, and the composition rule with it: a stretch of the output gathers every place of the source that holds its relation, whatever their distance.

## VII. Give the teacher an adversary's death

VII.1. P1 has a second consequence, and it is the deepest. Once there is one person with a past, there is one person to whom things can be done. What Revelation distributes among several bearers, on both sides, can be told of him.

VII.2. The speaker's love, in the adversary's mouth.

| | |
|---|---|
| Revelation 3:19 | ἐγὼ ὅσους ἐὰν φιλῶ ἐλέγχω καὶ παιδεύω |
| Matthew 26:48 | Ὃν ἂν φιλήσω αὐτός ἐστιν· κρατήσατε αὐτόν |

φιλέω in the first person under ἐάν or ἄν stands at Revelation 3:19, here, and at Mark's parallel, and nowhere else. The speaker's sentence is spoken by the betrayer; its object is the teacher; and what follows the loving is seizure.

VII.3. The dragon's custody.

| Revelation 20:2 | Matthew |
|---|---|
| ἐκράτησεν τὸν δράκοντα | ἐπέβαλον τὰς χεῖρας ἐπὶ τὸν Ἰησοῦν καὶ ἐκράτησαν αὐτόν |
| ἔδησεν αὐτὸν | δήσαντες αὐτὸν ἀπήγαγον |

VII.4. The rider's crown.

| | |
|---|---|
| Revelation 6:2 | ἐδόθη αὐτῷ στέφανος, καὶ ἐξῆλθεν νικῶν |
| Matthew 27:29 | πλέξαντες στέφανον ἐξ ἀκανθῶν ἐπέθηκαν ἐπὶ τῆς κεφαλῆς αὐτοῦ |

VII.5. With IV.3 (the Lamb's purchase done by the priests) and I.5 (the beast's law of the sword said by the teacher), chapters 26 and 27 hold: a line of the speaker's, a deed of the Lamb's, a gift of a rider's, a law spoken over the beast, and the seizing and binding of the dragon. Five bearers in the source. One person in the output, and every one of the five is done to him or said by him as he is taken.

VII.6. Rules M2 and M3. A deed done to the adversary is done to the teacher, by the adversary's people in the land. A costly act of the speaker or the Lamb toward the faithful is done by the adversary to the teacher, or refused.

VII.7. A caution that belongs here. The exchange of the adversary's treatment onto the teacher was tested by count across the Gospels. It held on one instrument, with Acts' arrested heroes as control, and did not come again on a second instrument built to extract every relation. Acts 12 also holds more of the dragon's custody than any Gospel passage: Peter seized, chained, in prison, and an angel who looses where Revelation's angel binds. The pairings in this section stand as read, each on its own words.

## VIII. Open the tomb

VIII.1. The custody of Revelation 20 has a second half, and Matthew alone has it.

| Revelation 20:1–3 | Matthew 27:63–28:2 |
|---|---|
| ἵνα μὴ πλανήσῃ ἔτι τὰ ἔθνη | ἐκεῖνος ὁ πλάνος |
| ἔκλεισεν καὶ ἐσφράγισεν ἐπάνω αὐτοῦ | σφραγίσαντες τὸν λίθον |
| ἄγγελον καταβαίνοντα ἐκ τοῦ οὐρανοῦ | ἄγγελος γὰρ Κυρίου καταβὰς ἐξ οὐρανοῦ |
| ἐπάνω αὐτοῦ | ἀπεκύλισεν τὸν λίθον καὶ ἐκάθητο ἐπάνω αὐτοῦ |
| μετὰ ταῦτα δεῖ λυθῆναι αὐτὸν | the tomb is empty |

VIII.2. In Revelation an angel comes down, the deceiver is shut in and sealed over, so that he deceive no more, and he must afterwards be loosed. In Matthew the chief priests call Jesus "that deceiver", seal the stone so that the last deception not be worse than the first, and an angel comes down from heaven, rolls the stone away and sits over it. The patient is exchanged (M2). The helper's act is opposed: the angel who shuts becomes the angel who opens.

VIII.3. The rival source, heard in full. Daniel 6 has a den, a stone laid on its mouth, a king's seal, and a deliverance by an angel, and it is in the Septuagint that Matthew cites throughout. Entered as the rival for this passage in the archive's engine, where a derivation must supply the target's members above every control, it supplied one member of seven. The deceiver, the descent from heaven and "over him" are not in it.

## IX. The procedure

IX.1. In order.

1. Hold Revelation open. Its unit is the relation, with the bearer it has at that place.
2. **P1.** Give the speaker a past: a teacher in a land, with followers, killed there.
3. **P2.** Make the host a told life in a named land. Name the adversary from the land. Cite as written what is scripture's.
4. Set the frame: the birth and flight of Revelation 12 at the beginning; the death, custody and opening of Revelation 11 and 20, with the first vision's "fear not", at the end.
5. For each stretch of the life, gather every place of the source that holds its relation.
6. Apply to each relation the move its kind requires:

| If the relation in Revelation is | then |
|---|---|
| a first-person saying of the speaker | keep it as the teacher's saying |
| a deed done to the adversary | it is done to the teacher; at the judgment, to hearers who fail |
| a costly act of the speaker or the Lamb toward the faithful | the adversary does it to the teacher, or it is refused |
| a rider's charge, given in the passive | the teacher says it of himself, or it is turned on him |
| commerce, an image, wealth trusted in | the teacher opposes it, keeping its terms |
| the faithful's part | the trial goes to the teacher; the seeing and falling to followers |

7. Give it a form: stage two colliding sayings as a likeness; enact a title as an act or a list; liken a scene of the end to the kingdom; foretell a sign; or let it happen.
8. Let the vision's furniture go (horses, bowls, trumpets, living creatures, counted thirds) and keep the rare word.

IX.2. Three of the six moves are one consequence of P1. The adversary's sufferings, the Lamb's costly acts and the faithful's trial all need somewhere to land once there is a person with a past, and they land on that person.

## X. What is not written, and what failed

X.1. Reach. The ledger has 52 rows. They reach 119 of Matthew's 1,067 verses, 11 per cent. A count made with no reader (pairings that Revelation and Matthew share and no book outside the Gospels has: 287 of them) brings it to 225 verses, 21 per cent. The rows fall on the infancy, the trial in the wilderness, the mission charge, the parables of chapter 13, the keys, the transfiguration, chapters 19 to 28.

X.2. Unreached. Chapters 1, 6, 7, 9, 12, 14 and 15 have no row: the genealogy, the body of the Sermon, the healings, the controversies, the feedings. The procedure says "for each stretch of the life" and does not say what the stretches are or their order, beyond the frame. The narrative Matthew shares with Mark is outside it. Whatever account is given of Matthew, Mark is in it.

X.3. Unexplained within reach. Where a relation fits two conditions, the procedure does not say which governs. The seat is kept at 19:28 and refused at 20:23.

X.4. Failed, under predictions sealed beforehand. Revelation's order as Matthew's order: three ways (by section, by rotation at the centre, by runs in either direction). One mapping of seats over the whole book: the seats kept; the three seats that live in "I" and "you"; a general extraction of every relation, five predictions of five. A law for each Gospel by the frequency of its operations. The assignment of the four riders' strands to the four Gospels as first counted (it came within chance, and came out as Irenaeus has it only under a rule made afterwards). These are in the research record with their numbers.

X.5. Other accounts. The two-source hypothesis, the Farrer–Goulder hypothesis and the two-Gospel hypothesis each say how Matthew stands to Mark and Luke. None places Revelation among Matthew's sources, and this paper does not displace any of them on the ground they cover. It adds a relation they do not consider and shows it is workable as a procedure. Goulder is the nearest in kind: a writer with one source, expanding it by stated habits of composition. His source was Mark and his habits were midrash and the lectionary. The procedure here has a different source and reaches different parts of the book; the two could both be true of one author.

X.6. Direction. Stated once more and left. The rows are relations between two texts. Section V shows that a stated rule produces Matthew's parable from Revelation's two verses and from nothing less. No row shows that the rule was run in the first century, or in which direction.
