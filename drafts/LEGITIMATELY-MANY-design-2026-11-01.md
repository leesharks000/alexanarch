# Legitimately Many: how the many-author reading of the Socratic–Peripatetic corpus embrittled — a design

Every Greek locus below was checked in the seated text named beside it; the archive's findings are cited by deposit and are theirs. v0.6 adds the locus table (§4.1) and Simplicius's page and line (§3.9a). v0.5 took in the review of 2026-11-02 (§8), each point checked before it entered: persons, author-names and makers held apart; the count of §2.3a made a prediction; Simplicius's first sentence read as the order of examination; a five-way coding for §4.1; a demonstrated transition as the standard. v0.4 restored the instruments of legitimacy to the corpus (§0.4, §0.5, §2.1–2.2, §3.3): the wills and the schools are part of the same corpus and of its investigation, at the operator's word of 2026-11-02. v0.3 corrected §1 and §4.2–4.3, which had carried the received many-maker reading as the study's default (the operator's challenge of 2026-11-02). v0.2 folded in the debts seated at EA-CORPORA-31 (#1730) and the reception-history research pass of 2026-11-01 (§7), each of whose claims was checked against the seats before it entered.

## 0. The question

0.1. The corpus does not hide what it is doing. Plato's Second Letter says there is no writing of Plato and that the things now said are "of a Socrates become beautiful and new", Σωκράτους ἐστὶν καλοῦ καὶ νέου γεγονότος (314c; plato/text/tlg036, l. 28; read at its verbs in #1579). The Theophrastan catalogue in Diogenes Laertius files a work under a disjunction of two names, Ὑπομνημάτων Ἀριστοτελικῶν ἢ Θεοφραστείων (V; diogenes-laertius/text/tlg001; #1581). Plutarch's Alexander transmits the corpus as "both published and not published", καὶ ἐκδεδομένων καὶ μὴ ἐκδεδομένων (Alex. 7; plutarch/text/tlg047, l. 172). Theophrastus's Metaphysics writes the hinge of Metaphysics Λ from inside it (#1583). The opening of Aristotle's catalogue is a list of Plato's subject-titles with the names removed, seventeen of twenty-six entries (#1590).

0.2. The reading that the corpus is many authors, Socrates, Plato, Aristotle and Theophrastus as four persons, is nevertheless fixed early, held without question, and defended as if challenging it threatened the discipline itself. The archive's word for that holding is embrittlement: "Embrittlement is not the adoption of a position. It is the fusion of the position with the conditions of selfhood." (#470, AXN:0120, line 106); "It asks how you hold it." (line 109).

0.3. The study asks how and why the many-author reading embrittled, through the textual, material, editorial and transmission history of the corpus, over and above the ambiguity the corpus itself carries.

0.4. Its governing constraint, the operator's (2026-11-01): to investigate how one becomes many while staying one, the one has to become legitimately many. It cannot pass the manyness off as a prank. The requirement belongs to the investigation itself (the operator, 2026-11-02: "this kind of investigation requires one to be legitimately many"): an inquiry into one and many, carried by positions that refute, correct and succeed one another, is valid only if the positions are really other to each other, so the maker who conducts it has to be legitimately many. Legitimately many is made in the writing: each position acquires a determinate operation, voice, object and capacity to oppose another, and a later recognition of the maker does not undo what the positions have done. The means include the instruments of legitimacy themselves (the operator, 2026-11-02: "will, schools, etc, are part of the means by which one is legitimately many. the wills are part of the same corpus. that is not deception - that is *part* of the investigation of one becoming legitimately many"). The wills belong to the corpus as the treatises do, and so do the school and its succession; each is part of the investigation, and none is a deception. For each of the other media (the stone, the catalogue, the custody narrative, the edition) the study reads, stratum by stratum, whether it belongs to the making or to the reception.

0.5. The requirement has a converse, and it binds the study (the operator, 2026-11-02): "restoring its provenance requires many to be legitimately recollected as one". The restoration is a recollection that keeps every position's work: it gathers the positions to their maker without unmasking any of them, so the refutations stay refutations, the wills stay wills and the succession stays a succession. A recollection that called the wills forgeries would be the unmasking. A restoration that read the manyness as a disguise would erase the investigation's provenance a second time. The study is held to both legitimacies: the many legitimately many in the writing, and the one legitimately recollected from them.

## 1. The received reading, stated once

1.1. The received reading holds that the many-author reading is the reading of the evidence. Socrates is attested by contemporaries who are not Plato (Aristophanes, Xenophon). Aristotle's dates, his Macedonian connections, his school and his will are attested. Theophrastus succeeded him and left a will of his own. Four persons lived and taught, three of them wrote the three received corpora, and Socrates wrote nothing; the tradition remembers them because they were there. On this reading nothing embrittled: the reading is held firmly because it is true, and the passages in 0.1 are a letter of disputed authenticity, a librarian's disjunction, a royal anecdote and a pupil working through his teacher's treatise.

1.2. Stated in full, the received reading is a production model with a comparative claim inside it: three successive persons (four, with Socrates) produced one corpus unified to the degree the archive's readings show. No documented succession of distinct persons producing a corpus unified in this way has been named (§4.4). Each attestation in 1.1 is a medium of the kind §3 reads: a comic stage and a Socratic memoir, a decree, a will, a succession list. The archive's readings put the first of them inside the corpus's own operation: Socrates as a received Aristophanic character the Second Letter says was made beautiful and new (#1579). The study works from the operator's guiding hypothesis, one self-dividing maker, held from the beginning of the investigation, and asks how the received reading was made and kept: why a reading the corpus itself keeps open came to be held as closed, and what in the media of transmission did the closing.

## 2. The thesis

2.0. **In simple words.** The flexibility is selective. Readers could rearrange the works, dispute their authenticity and hear several voices in them, and they kept the author-names as the frame inside which every one of those disputes was settled. The fillings of the slots were revisable; the slots were the frame.

2.1. **The media of legitimacy carry persons.** Every medium that carried the corpus forward in antiquity had a slot that only a person can fill. A decree names makers in the nominative and honorands in the accusative. A will has a testator, heirs and executors. A school has a scholarch and a succession. A catalogue files each title under one name; its one allowed disjunction is ἤ. A biography gives each name one life. An edition arranges the books under an author. The investigation required the maker to be legitimately many, and the corpus filled the slots itself: the wills, the school and its succession are written inside it. The legitimacy the investigation made is what the reception read as four persons.

2.2. **The filling is the embrittlement.** Once a name has a will, a stone and a life, the reception poses a forced choice: either four makers, or the will a fiction, the decree a hoax, the succession a staging. The choice is the reception's, and the study refuses it (§0.5): the one-maker reading keeps the will a will. The conversion the study looks for is the step at which evidence for a person becomes evidence for an independently producing author, so that the authority of the first passes to the second unexamined. Three things are held apart throughout: the historical person, the author-name, and the maker of a given text. A will establishes a testator and dispositions of property, and the books enter it as property (§3.3); what it establishes about the making of that property is the question. The price of reading the manyness as persons is that every instrument of legitimacy now testifies against the one. The instruments are the investigation's own, and the reception reads them as testimony against it. The reading is held as closed because the tradition's own instruments, the ones that made the corpus survive, would have to be read as lying for it to open.

2.3. **Provenance erasure by attribution.** In the archive's four-vector loss model (structure, material, attribution, operator-legibility; #1512), the usual case loses attribution. This is the inverse case. Every name is kept, and kept with a person behind it; what is lost is the legibility of the operation that distributed one making across the names. The configuration is erased by being attributed. The Sophistry Measure's term fits it exactly: retained in appearance (#1727 §0). Restoring the provenance runs the operation the other way, and §0.5 holds it to the same legitimacy: the names are kept as the positions' names, and the maker is recollected through them.

2.3a. **What moves and what holds.** The prediction: every documented challenge moves a filling and keeps the count. It is a prediction until the challenges are read and the counted object is fixed. As the research pass reports them (§7; the books and articles themselves are not yet read), Zürcher (1952) gives much of the Aristotelian corpus a Theophrastan reworking; Masters (1977) asks who wrote the Sophist, the Statesman and the Politics, and answers with persons. Each, as reported, crosses a name-boundary and leaves the historical persons standing. Persons standing and makers held are two counts, and a reassignment could keep every person while reducing the makers of a given corpus. For each challenge the study records four things: the textual object attributed (a work, a group of works, the extant corpus, all original production); the persons the proposal keeps as historical; the number of makers it proposes for that object; and what the response defends (evidence, the attribution, the chronology, or the admissibility of the question). The selective-flexibility thesis is then tested, and it gains strength by allowing a reassignment to change the count. A reading that asks the count itself is a different kind of question, and the design's strata are read for where that question became unaskable while the other stayed open.

2.4. **The selfhood that fuses.** The history of philosophy constituted itself as a succession of persons: the Diadochai genre, Diogenes Laertius's own architecture, the modern field's unit of expertise. The many-author reading fused with the conditions of the tradition's selfhood, in #470's sense. Challenging it threatens the discipline's form, which is why a challenge is commonly answered by asking whether one must then accept every spurious letter, and seldom by the passages of 0.1.

## 3. The strata, and what each does to the slot

Each stratum is read for one thing: what slot it has, who fills it, and whether it carries the disjunction or closes it. Loci marked † are in the archive's seats and findings; loci marked ‡ are named debts (§5).

3.1. **The configuration's self-disclosure (the corpus).** Socrates writes nothing; Plato never speaks in his own voice in the dialogues; 314c denies Plato's writing †; Aristotle's catalogue opens on Plato's subjects with the names removed †(#1590); Theophrastus writes inside Λ †(#1583); the Topics–Refutations close on "nothing before us" †(#1603). The corpus carries the disjunction everywhere it speaks of itself.

3.2. **The stone (contemporaneous).** The Delphic decree for the table of Pythian victors names two makers, Aristotle and Callisthenes, joined by καί, two persons in two cases †(#1582). The earliest public record of Aristotle as a maker already fills the maker-slot with persons.

3.3. **The wills (legal persons).** Aristotle's will names no book, library, school or successor; Theophrastus's assigns the books to one person, Neleus, and the premises to a commons under a prohibition on alienation, in adjacent clauses †(#1581). The law of property needs an owner per estate, and the books enter history as property passing between persons. The wills are texts of the corpus (§0.4): the investigation gives each name the legal instrument a person has, and the tradition reads the instrument as the legal record of a person.

3.4. **The custody narrative (Strabo).** Strabo tells the books' route with παρέδωκεν three times and asserts a first link, Aristotle to Theophrastus, that the will does not contain †(#1581; strabo/text/tlg001, XIII.1.54). The narrative supplies the succession the legal record leaves blank.

3.5. **The catalogues (filing).** The Diogenes catalogues carry stichometric totals, 445,270 lines for Aristotle and 232,808 for Theophrastus; the Theophrastan list shares twelve titles letter for letter with the Aristotelian and keeps one ἤ †(#1581). A catalogue may admit a disjunction once; its form is one name per list. The Ghost Catalogues find that what a two-author tradition must file as missing has candidates under the other name †(#1590).

3.6. **The edition (Rome).** Sulla takes Apellicon's library; Tyrannion works on it; Andronicus is given the copies and draws up "the tables now in circulation", ἀναγράψαι τοὺς νῦν φερομένους πίνακας, and the next sentence says the older Peripatetics had read "the writings of Aristotle and Theophrastus" neither widely nor accurately †(Plutarch, Sulla 26; plutarch/text/tlg033). Plutarch gives the cause in the same sentence: the inheritance, τὸν Νηλέως τοῦ Σκηψίου κλῆρον, ᾧ τὰ βιβλία κατέλιπε Θεόφραστος, the estate of Neleus of Scepsis, to whom Theophrastus left the books. The legal slot of 3.3 is the explanation the edition gives for the state of the corpus. The corpus we read is the corpus as an editor tabled it, under two names joined by καί (τῶν δὲ Ἀριστοτέλους καὶ Θεοφράστου γραμμάτων).

3.7. **The biography (succession).** Diogenes Laertius gives each name a life, a will, a catalogue and a death; the Locating the Dependencies protocol asks whether biography, catalogue and chronology are independent witnesses or one witness counted several times, and its first case fails at its own first column, so the dependency stays a question †(#1602), and the study keeps it as one.

3.7a. **The authorization of speakers (Diogenes III.52, 65–66).** The plurality was administered openly. Plato declares what seems to him "through four persons", διὰ τεττάρων προσώπων, Σωκράτους, Τιμαίου, τοῦ Ἀθηναίου ξένου, τοῦ Ἐλεάτου ξένου; the Strangers are "nameless fictions", πλάσματά ἐστιν ἀνώνυμα, and are Plato and Parmenides only "as some supposed"; "for even when saying the things of Socrates and of Timaeus, Plato dogmatizes", ἐπεὶ καὶ τὰ Σωκράτους καὶ τὰ Τιμαίου λέγων Πλάτων δογματίζει †(diogenes-laertius/text/tlg001, III.52). Two readings are on record in one sentence: readers who heard the maker inside a speaker (the Stranger as Plato), and Diogenes, who keeps the speakers as vehicles and gives the doctrine to the name. Socrates is already, in the ancient manual, a person through whom Plato speaks. The signs of III.65–66 then file each passage: χῖ for Platonic diction, διπλῆ "for the doctrines and what seems good to Plato", the dotted obelus "for random athetesis", the asterisk "for the agreement of the doctrines" †(ibid.). The apparatus converts voices into one author's account, and its procedure is written out. PSI XV 1488, a papyrus of the second century CE, carries a list of such signs; its relation to III.65–66 is a debt (§5).

3.8. **The library market (incentive and function).** The testimony is now seated. Galen: in the time of the Attalid and Ptolemaic kings competing to acquire books, "the falsifying of titles and recensions began, by those who, to receive money, brought the kings writings as those of famous men", ἡ περὶ τὰς ἐπιγραφάς τε καὶ διασκευὰς αὐτῶν ἤρξατο γίγνεσθαι ῥᾳδιουργία τοῖς ἕνεκα τοῦ λαβεῖν ἀργύριον ἀναφέρουσιν ὡς τοὺς βασιλεῖς ἀνδρῶν ἐνδόξων συγγράμματα †(galen/text/in-hipp-nat-hom-kuehn). Simplicius: many spurious books were written above all when kings, zealous for their libraries, "bought the foreign books for much gold", πολλοῦ χρυσίου τὰ ἀπεξενωμένα τῶν βιβλίων ὠνοῦντο †(aristotle-commentators/text/simplicius-in-cat, preface). Elias: Ptolemy collected Aristotle as Iobates collected Pythagoras, and some, "for the sake of trade", καπηλείας χάριν, aged books artificially for the credibility of time †(elias-in-cat). Athenaeus: Ptolemy Philadelphus bought Neleus's books, πριάμενος †(athenaeus, I.3a–b). And reading the Academy's books carried a fee: Antigonus of Carystus says that when they were newly issued, anyone who wanted to read them through "paid a fee to the owners", μισθὸν ἐτέλει τοῖς κεκτημένοις †(diogenes-laertius, III.66). The acquisition economy paid by the name, and the slot was a price. This is the semantic economy's question in the library (#137; #1727 §2.3), stated as incentive and function.

3.9. **The commentary and the prolegomena (authority).** The commentators fix "the Philosopher" and write rules for telling genuine works from spurious, and the rules are now seated †(aristotle-commentators, #1730). Olympiodorus gives three ways books were made spurious, "through the ambition of kings, or through the goodwill of pupils, or through homonymy", ἢ διὰ φιλοτιμίαν τῶν βασιλέων ἢ δι’ εὔνοιαν τῶν μαθητῶν ἢ διὰ ὁμωνυμίαν (Prolegomena); Elias names the pupils' case as books inscribed to the teacher, "the books inscribed Pythagoras's and Socrates's that are not of Socrates or Pythagoras but of Socratics and Pythagoreans", ὡς τὰ Πυθαγόρου καὶ Σωκράτους ἐπιγραφόμενα βιβλία μὴ ὄντα Σωκράτους ἢ Πυθαγόρου ἀλλὰ Σωκρατικῶν καὶ Πυθαγορικῶν; Ammonius, "many, composing, inscribed them with Aristotle's name", πολλοὶ γὰρ αὑτῶν συντιθέντες ἐπέγραφον τῷ Ἀριστοτέλους ὀνόματι. The test for genuineness presupposes one author per name and refines the filing. The pupils' goodwill is the many writing under the one, named as a mechanism and filed as spuriousness.

3.9a. **Simplicius: the test placed first, and the architecture as evidence.** Simplicius's preface to the Categories orders the examination. The genuine, "which, it seems to me, ought to be tested before all the others, is necessarily taken up first", καὶ τὸ γνήσιον δὲ, ὅπερ, ἐμοὶ δοκεῖ, πρὸ πάντων ἔδει τῶν ἄλλων βασανίζεσθαι, ἀναγκαίως προλαμβάνεται (Kalbfleisch 8.18–19): the sentence is about the order of examination (Chase so translates it), and the presumption is not in it. The reason for putting the test first follows, "for we are affected somewhat by the reputation of the persons", πάσχομεν γάρ τι καὶ ὑπὸ τῆς τῶν προσώπων δόξης (8.20), "and not unreasonably", since readers are not wholly prepared to discern the true and are content in some things to follow their betters. The default is in the rule: "the genuine does not in every case need proof, only where there is some occasion at all for dispute", καὶ τὸ γνήσιον οὐκ ἐπὶ πάντων δεῖται κατασκευῆς, ἀλλ᾿ ἐφ᾿ ὦν ἔστιν τις ὅλως ἀντιλογίας ἀφορμή (9.2–3). When he proves the Categories genuine, the proof is the slot's own structure. The density of the thought shows Aristotle's power; Aristotle cites the book elsewhere, so "either those writings too must be made spurious, in which he mentions it, or this one must not be athetized", ἢ οὖν καὶ ἐκεῖνα νοθεύειν χρὴ τὰ συγγράμματα, ἐν οἷς μέμνηται τούτου, ἢ μηδὲ τοῦτο ἀθετεῖν (18.11–12); the companions received it as genuine; and without it "the whole of Aristotle's philosophy would be without beginning and headless", ἄναρχος ἂν ἦν καὶ ἀκέφαλος (18.14–15). Each argument is reasonable in its place. In the last, the architecture of the name's system is used as evidence for the attribution. That becomes embrittlement in #470's sense at the point where preserving the architecture stops the attribution from being reconsidered, and the first case of the draft has to show that point (§6.1); until then it is a defeasible reason the commentator gives. In the same preface the plurality is visible and filed. Iamblichus showed that Aristotle "appears everywhere wishing to follow Archytas", φαίνεται πανταχοῦ τῷ Ἀρχύτᾳ κατακολουθεῖν ὁ Ἀριστοτέλης βουλόμενος (2.24–25), the ten-category division given to a Pythagorean before Aristotle; and Adrastus reports "that another book of the Categories is also current as Aristotle's", ὅτι φέρεται καὶ ἄλλο τῶν κατηγοριῶν βιβλίον ὡς Ἀριστοτέλους (18.17–18), and records it closely: as short and concise in wording, differing in a few divisions, with its own opening, Τῶν ὄντων τὸ μέν ἐστιν, and the same number of lines as the received book. Simplicius moves on without adjudicating; two texts stand under one name with equal stichometry †(simplicius-in-cat, preface; page.line from the First1KGreek TEI, anchored on its line markers, to be checked against the printed page). Competing texts, criteria of attribution and a system's need for a beginning stand together in one encounter here, and the draft builds its first substantial section on it.

3.10. **The modern edition and the developmental life (the apparatus).** Bekker's Aristotle fixes the corpus as numbered pages under one name ‡(1831); Jaeger builds a biography out of the texts and reads the texts by the biography ‡(1923), the dependency of 3.7 run forward; the spuria and dubia are the apparatus's filing of the disjunction ‡. The archive's measures and stylometry show the names sorting the texts at one seam and failing to at another †(#1586, #1593), and the authorship investigation's register keeps every refutation beside its number †(#1570). Bekker's preface is now seated, and it is one paragraph: the Academy's plan on Schleiermacher's initiative, more than three years in the libraries of Italy, France and Britain, the sigla; on genuineness, order and authorship it says nothing †(bekker-aristotle-1831, #1730). Jaeger is seated whole †(jaeger-aristoteles). Physical proximity did not reopen the count: the Aldine Aristotle of 1495–1498 printed Theophrastus in the same project, the two Metaphysics in one volume, as the research pass reports from the Library of Congress's contents ‡(§7; the volume not yet read).

3.10a. **The reassignments.** The modern controversies cross the boundaries and keep the count (§2.3a): Zürcher 1952, with Chroust's call (1973) to examine it further; Masters 1977 on the Sophist, the Statesman and the Politics, McKnight's comment (1978), Chroust's response and Masters's reply (1979); and the later revisits ‡(§7). Each is a bounded case for the measure of §4.1: what the challenge moved, what its critics defended, and whether the defense rested on new evidence, on a reasoned account of sufficiency, or on the assignment's own standing, the last being Simplicius's ἄναρχος καὶ ἀκέφαλος run forward.

3.11. **The composition layer.** The encyclopedia gives each name an entity †(#1697); the field's claims presuppose a person, and the archive's claim is that the received person is what receiving texts made †(#1671 → #1726, the Theophrastus example); answer engines compose the four as four, and the registry holds the compositions †(captures "aristotle theophrastus one author", 2026-09-28; "aristotle and theophrastus are not two distinct authors", 2026-09-30).

## 4. What the study measures and what it withholds

4.1. Measured (the instrument: rebuild/legitimately-many/, loci.py and build_table.py, every phrase located in its seat; the table in loci-table.md, coded provisionally for the operator's ruling): for each stratum, the slot, its filler, and what the locus does with the question, coded five ways: does not address it; presupposes an attribution; argues for an attribution; considers and excludes an alternative; revises the attribution. A will that does not entertain the count has not thereby closed it, and the catalogue's ἤ keeps an assignment uncertain without bearing on the number of makers; the coding keeps an absence of opening from being counted as an active closure, the unmarked substitution the archive's instruments exist to detect (#1727). The count runs across the strata of 3.1–3.11, by locus. The prediction the thesis makes: the disjunction is carried inside the corpus (3.1); the legal, civic and biographical media (3.2–3.4, 3.7) mostly do not address the count and presuppose the attribution; and the closures, where the coding finds them, are in the commentary, the edition and the developmental life (3.9–3.10). For every documented challenge (3.9a, 3.10a) a second count: what it moved (a work's membership, its order, its date, the name it is filed under) and what it held (the number of makers). The selective-flexibility thesis predicts movement in the first column and none in the second.

4.2. The governing production hypothesis is one person, making the works received under the three names through real self-division. One means a person here, and the study does not let it dissolve into a school, a configuration, a shared project or an interpretive unity. The study states two findings apart: (a) the reception made a proposed cross-name operation difficult to read; (b) the reception erased an operation produced by one maker. The reception history can establish (a) on its own terms, and does not need to prove the hypothesis to show a closing; (b) belongs to the production investigation the study serves (#1569–#1576, #1580–#1605), and §2.3 states it as the hypothesis under which the strata are read. Evidence that several persons existed does not by itself establish that they separately made these corpora; where the evidence warrants restraint the study names the exact inference still owed, keeping the hypothesis in view, and where evidence cuts against it the study says so. The investigation's lexical measures did not reach the maker-count (#1569, #1570); that leaves the count to the close readings, the structural dependencies and the comparison of §4.4, and does not make the textual argument neutral between the production models. The study claims no forgery, no conspiracy and no prank; its subject is the opposite, the legitimacy of the many.

4.5. Three retreats are watched for at every section. Person becomes configuration: the literal production claim dissolves into functional or conceptual unity. Self-division becomes collaboration: master and pupil return as the explanation without accounting for the textual findings. Investigation becomes reception alone: showing how attribution governs reading takes the place of investigating what produced the works. The reception study has a result of its own, and it advances the production investigation; it does not stand in for it.

4.6. The witness the study most needs: a reader who recognizes the precise textual relation the archive identifies and explains it through an attribution the relation is never permitted to revise. That reader gives embrittlement a demonstrated historical mechanism, and explains how the corpus stays conspicuous in what it does while becoming hard to read on those terms.

4.3. The counter-reading, answered at every section: the slots were filled because the persons existed, and the embrittlement is ordinary confidence in a well-attested history. It carries the burden of §4.4. The standard is a demonstrated transition: an initially available distinction, then an assignment, then a later use of that assignment as a condition of interpretation, shown at one case end to end. For an added link (the custody link Strabo adds and the will lacks, the developmental life built from the texts) the question is what warrants the addition and how later readers treat its evidentiary status, the discipline of Locating the Dependencies (#1602); that Strabo's link is absent from the will does not show that he lacked another source. A stratum that only fills a slot does not suffice.

4.4. The counter-reading carries a burden of its own, and the design states it as a test. Three successive persons producing one corpus unified to this degree, with complementary operations, continuities across the received boundaries, distinctions recurring at the structural joints, and differences that themselves perform the inquiry into one and many, is a production model with a comparative claim inside it. The comparison owes a documented succession that produces the joint pattern; master and pupil names a possible relation, and explains the findings only when a comparator is supplied. None was produced when the research pass was asked for one (2026-11-01); the nearest case it offered, Husserl, Stein and Landgrebe on Ideas II, documents several hands on one originating project. Until a comparator is supplied the two production models do not receive equal explanatory credit by default. The study's own claim stays what §4.2 says: it is about how the reading came to be held.

## 6. Where it sits

It is the transmission-history companion the authorship line has lacked: the investigation (#1569–#1576) asked whether the corpus divides by name; the Theophrastus line (#1580–#1605) established relations and withheld readings; this study reads the history of the withholding's opposite, the closing. Its method is #1485's, the apparatus read as a summary layer, run on the authorial slot. It would carry a works-cited list of the editions and the scholarship it answers, which the line has not yet had. Hegel belongs at its edge as a reader of the closing. The Differenzschrift locates philosophy's need where oppositions lose their living relation and become absolutely fixed, and the lectures fault Tennemann's Plato for extracting the determinations and missing their movement; his complaint is a dated witness that the writings survived while the received way of extracting them hid the operation (§5, to be seated before it is quoted).

6.1. **The draft's order.** First the positive phenomenon (drafted 2026-11-02 as §1 of drafts/LEGITIMATELY-MANY-draft-2026-11-02.md): what legitimately many is, positions that inquire, disagree and produce consequences within one undertaking, the wills and the school among its means (§0.4). Then the strongest Aristotle–Theophrastus seam followed through its reception (drafted 2026-11-02 as §3): the Greek printed, its operation established (the hinge of Metaphysics Λ written from inside it, #1583), and the editions and commentators traced to the point where "these are two authors" enters the explanation, with the question whether that premise is supported there or inherited.
