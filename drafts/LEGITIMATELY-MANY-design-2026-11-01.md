# Legitimately Many: how the many-author reading of the Socratic–Peripatetic corpus embrittled — a design

Working design, v0.2, 2026-11-01. Not deposited. Precedes a draft. Every Greek locus below was checked in the seated text named beside it; the archive's findings are cited by deposit and are theirs. v0.2 folds in the debts seated at EA-CORPORA-31 (#1730) and the reception-history research pass of 2026-11-01 (§7), each of whose claims was checked against the seats before it entered.

## 0. The question

0.1. The corpus does not hide what it is doing. Plato's Second Letter says there is no writing of Plato and that the things now said are "of a Socrates become beautiful and new", Σωκράτους ἐστὶν καλοῦ καὶ νέου γεγονότος (314c; plato/text/tlg036, l. 28; read at its verbs in #1579). The Theophrastan catalogue in Diogenes Laertius files a work under a disjunction of two names, Ὑπομνημάτων Ἀριστοτελικῶν ἢ Θεοφραστείων (V; diogenes-laertius/text/tlg001; #1581). Plutarch's Alexander transmits the corpus as "both published and not published", καὶ ἐκδεδομένων καὶ μὴ ἐκδεδομένων (Alex. 7; plutarch/text/tlg047, l. 172). Theophrastus's Metaphysics writes the hinge of Metaphysics Λ from inside it (#1583). The opening of Aristotle's catalogue is a list of Plato's subject-titles with the names removed, seventeen of twenty-six entries (#1590).

0.2. The reading that the corpus is many authors, Socrates, Plato, Aristotle and Theophrastus as four persons, is nevertheless fixed early, held without question, and defended as if challenging it threatened the discipline itself. The archive's word for that holding is embrittlement: "Embrittlement is not the adoption of a position. It is the fusion of the position with the conditions of selfhood." (#470, AXN:0120, line 106); "It asks how you hold it." (line 109).

0.3. The study asks how and why the many-author reading embrittled, through the textual, material, editorial and transmission history of the corpus, over and above the ambiguity the corpus itself carries.

0.4. Its governing constraint, the operator's (2026-11-01): to investigate how one becomes many while staying one, the one has to become legitimately many. It cannot pass the manyness off as a prank. The many names each need a life, a body, a will, a stone, a school.

## 1. The innocent reading, in full

1.1. The many-author reading is the reading of the evidence. Socrates is attested by contemporaries who are not Plato (Aristophanes, Xenophon). Aristotle's dates, his Macedonian connections, his school and his will are attested. Theophrastus succeeded him and left a will of his own. Four persons lived, taught and wrote, and the tradition remembers them because they were there. On this reading nothing embrittled: the reading is held firmly because it is true, and the passages in 0.1 are a letter of disputed authenticity, a librarian's disjunction, a royal anecdote and a pupil working through his teacher's treatise.

1.2. The study states that reading first and keeps it at full strength throughout. Its question is narrower than whether the reading is true. It asks why a reading the corpus itself keeps open came to be held as closed, and what in the media of transmission did the closing.

## 2. The thesis

2.0. **In simple words.** The flexibility is selective. Readers could rearrange the works, dispute their authenticity and hear several voices in them, and they kept the author-names as the frame inside which every one of those disputes was settled. The fillings of the slots were revisable; the slots were the frame.

2.1. **The media of legitimacy carry persons.** Every medium that carried the corpus forward in antiquity had a slot that only a person can fill. A decree names makers in the nominative and honorands in the accusative. A will has a testator, heirs and executors. A school has a scholarch and a succession. A catalogue files each title under one name; its one allowed disjunction is ἤ. A biography gives each name one life. An edition arranges the books under an author. For the one to become legitimately many, each name had to fill every slot.

2.2. **The filling is the embrittlement.** Once a name has a will, a stone and a life, the record cannot be read as one maker without the record becoming illegitimate: the will a fiction, the decree a hoax, the succession a staging. The price of legitimate manyness is that every instrument of legitimacy now testifies against the one. The reading is held as closed because the tradition's own instruments, the ones that made the corpus survive, would have to be read as lying for it to open.

2.3. **Provenance erasure by attribution.** In the archive's four-vector loss model (structure, material, attribution, operator-legibility; #1512), the usual case loses attribution. This is the inverse case. Every name is kept, and kept with a person behind it; what is lost is the legibility of the operation that distributed one making across the names. The configuration is erased by being attributed. The Sophistry Measure's term fits it exactly: retained in appearance (#1727 §0).

2.3a. **What moves and what holds.** Every documented challenge the record supplies moves a filling and keeps the count. As the research pass reports them (§7; the books and articles themselves are not yet read), Zürcher (1952) gives much of the Aristotelian corpus a Theophrastan reworking; Masters (1977) asks who wrote the Sophist, the Statesman and the Politics, and answers with persons. Each reassignment crosses a name-boundary and leaves four persons standing, so the challenges are the measure of the selectivity: the boundary between two names can be redrawn, and the number of makers is held. A reading that asks the count itself is a different kind of question, and the design's strata are read for where that question became unaskable while the other stayed open.

2.4. **The selfhood that fuses.** The history of philosophy constituted itself as a succession of persons: the Diadochai genre, Diogenes Laertius's own architecture, the modern field's unit of expertise. The many-author reading fused with the conditions of the tradition's selfhood, in #470's sense. Challenging it threatens the discipline's form, which is why a challenge is commonly answered by asking whether one must then accept every spurious letter, and seldom by the passages of 0.1.

## 3. The strata, and what each does to the slot

Each stratum is read for one thing: what slot it has, who fills it, and whether it carries the disjunction or closes it. Loci marked † are in the archive's seats and findings; loci marked ‡ are named debts (§5).

3.1. **The configuration's self-disclosure (the corpus).** Socrates writes nothing; Plato never speaks in his own voice in the dialogues; 314c denies Plato's writing †; Aristotle's catalogue opens on Plato's subjects with the names removed †(#1590); Theophrastus writes inside Λ †(#1583); the Topics–Refutations close on "nothing before us" †(#1603). The corpus carries the disjunction everywhere it speaks of itself.

3.2. **The stone (contemporaneous).** The Delphic decree for the table of Pythian victors names two makers, Aristotle and Callisthenes, joined by καί, two persons in two cases †(#1582). The earliest public record of Aristotle as a maker already fills the maker-slot with persons.

3.3. **The wills (legal persons).** Aristotle's will names no book, library, school or successor; Theophrastus's assigns the books to one person, Neleus, and the premises to a commons under a prohibition on alienation, in adjacent clauses †(#1581). The law of property needs an owner per estate, and the books enter history as property passing between persons.

3.4. **The custody narrative (Strabo).** Strabo tells the books' route with παρέδωκεν three times and asserts a first link, Aristotle to Theophrastus, that the will does not contain †(#1581; strabo/text/tlg001, XIII.1.54). The narrative supplies the succession the legal record leaves blank.

3.5. **The catalogues (filing).** The Diogenes catalogues carry stichometric totals, 445,270 lines for Aristotle and 232,808 for Theophrastus; the Theophrastan list shares twelve titles letter for letter with the Aristotelian and keeps one ἤ †(#1581). A catalogue may admit a disjunction once; its form is one name per list. The Ghost Catalogues find that what a two-author tradition must file as missing has candidates under the other name †(#1590).

3.6. **The edition (Rome).** Sulla takes Apellicon's library; Tyrannion works on it; Andronicus is given the copies and draws up "the tables now in circulation", ἀναγράψαι τοὺς νῦν φερομένους πίνακας, and the next sentence says the older Peripatetics had read "the writings of Aristotle and Theophrastus" neither widely nor accurately †(Plutarch, Sulla 26; plutarch/text/tlg033). Plutarch gives the cause in the same sentence: the inheritance, τὸν Νηλέως τοῦ Σκηψίου κλῆρον, ᾧ τὰ βιβλία κατέλιπε Θεόφραστος, the estate of Neleus of Scepsis, to whom Theophrastus left the books. The legal slot of 3.3 is the explanation the edition gives for the state of the corpus. The corpus we read is the corpus as an editor tabled it, under two names joined by καί (τῶν δὲ Ἀριστοτέλους καὶ Θεοφράστου γραμμάτων).

3.7. **The biography (succession).** Diogenes Laertius gives each name a life, a will, a catalogue and a death; the Locating the Dependencies protocol asks whether biography, catalogue and chronology are independent witnesses or one witness counted several times, and its first case fails at its own first column, so the dependency stays a question †(#1602), and the study keeps it as one.

3.7a. **The authorization of speakers (Diogenes III.52, 65–66).** The plurality was administered openly. Plato declares what seems to him "through four persons", διὰ τεττάρων προσώπων, Σωκράτους, Τιμαίου, τοῦ Ἀθηναίου ξένου, τοῦ Ἐλεάτου ξένου; the Strangers are "nameless fictions", πλάσματά ἐστιν ἀνώνυμα, and are Plato and Parmenides only "as some supposed"; "for even when saying the things of Socrates and of Timaeus, Plato dogmatizes", ἐπεὶ καὶ τὰ Σωκράτους καὶ τὰ Τιμαίου λέγων Πλάτων δογματίζει †(diogenes-laertius/text/tlg001, III.52). Two readings are on record in one sentence: readers who heard the maker inside a speaker (the Stranger as Plato), and Diogenes, who keeps the speakers as vehicles and gives the doctrine to the name. Socrates is already, in the ancient manual, a person through whom Plato speaks. The signs of III.65–66 then file each passage: χῖ for Platonic diction, διπλῆ "for the doctrines and what seems good to Plato", the dotted obelus "for random athetesis", the asterisk "for the agreement of the doctrines" †(ibid.). The apparatus converts voices into one author's account, and its procedure is written out. PSI XV 1488, a papyrus of the second century CE, carries a list of such signs; its relation to III.65–66 is a debt (§5).

3.8. **The library market (incentive and function).** The testimony is now seated. Galen: in the time of the Attalid and Ptolemaic kings competing to acquire books, "the falsifying of titles and recensions began, by those who, to receive money, brought the kings writings as those of famous men", ἡ περὶ τὰς ἐπιγραφάς τε καὶ διασκευὰς αὐτῶν ἤρξατο γίγνεσθαι ῥᾳδιουργία τοῖς ἕνεκα τοῦ λαβεῖν ἀργύριον ἀναφέρουσιν ὡς τοὺς βασιλεῖς ἀνδρῶν ἐνδόξων συγγράμματα †(galen/text/in-hipp-nat-hom-kuehn). Simplicius: many spurious books were written above all when kings, zealous for their libraries, "bought the foreign books for much gold", πολλοῦ χρυσίου τὰ ἀπεξενωμένα τῶν βιβλίων ὠνοῦντο †(aristotle-commentators/text/simplicius-in-cat, preface). Elias: Ptolemy collected Aristotle as Iobates collected Pythagoras, and some, "for the sake of trade", καπηλείας χάριν, aged books artificially for the credibility of time †(elias-in-cat). Athenaeus: Ptolemy Philadelphus bought Neleus's books, πριάμενος †(athenaeus, I.3a–b). And reading the Academy's books carried a fee: Antigonus of Carystus says that when they were newly issued, anyone who wanted to read them through "paid a fee to the owners", μισθὸν ἐτέλει τοῖς κεκτημένοις †(diogenes-laertius, III.66). The acquisition economy paid by the name, and the slot was a price. This is the semantic economy's question in the library (#137; #1727 §2.3), stated as incentive and function.

3.9. **The commentary and the prolegomena (authority).** The commentators fix "the Philosopher" and write rules for telling genuine works from spurious, and the rules are now seated †(aristotle-commentators, #1730). Olympiodorus gives three ways books were made spurious, "through the ambition of kings, or through the goodwill of pupils, or through homonymy", ἢ διὰ φιλοτιμίαν τῶν βασιλέων ἢ δι’ εὔνοιαν τῶν μαθητῶν ἢ διὰ ὁμωνυμίαν (Prolegomena); Elias names the pupils' case as books inscribed to the teacher, "the books inscribed Pythagoras's and Socrates's that are not of Socrates or Pythagoras but of Socratics and Pythagoreans", ὡς τὰ Πυθαγόρου καὶ Σωκράτους ἐπιγραφόμενα βιβλία μὴ ὄντα Σωκράτους ἢ Πυθαγόρου ἀλλὰ Σωκρατικῶν καὶ Πυθαγορικῶν; Ammonius, "many, composing, inscribed them with Aristotle's name", πολλοὶ γὰρ αὑτῶν συντιθέντες ἐπέγραφον τῷ Ἀριστοτέλους ὀνόματι. The test for genuineness presupposes one author per name and refines the filing. The pupils' goodwill is the many writing under the one, named as a mechanism and filed as spuriousness.

3.9a. **Simplicius: the test taken in advance.** Simplicius's preface to the Categories states the default in its own grammar. The genuine, "which, it seems to me, ought to be tested before all the others, is necessarily taken in advance", καὶ τὸ γνήσιον δὲ, ὅπερ, ἐμοὶ δοκεῖ, πρὸ πάντων ἔδει τῶν ἄλλων βασανίζεσθαι, ἀναγκαίως προλαμβάνεται; the reason follows, "for we are affected somewhat by the reputation of the persons", πάσχομεν γάρ τι καὶ ὑπὸ τῆς τῶν προσώπων δόξης, "and not unreasonably", since readers are not wholly prepared to discern the true and are content in some things to follow their betters. Then the rule: "the genuine does not in every case need proof, only where there is some occasion at all for dispute", καὶ τὸ γνήσιον οὐκ ἐπὶ πάντων δεῖται κατασκευῆς, ἀλλ᾿ ἐφ᾿ ὦν ἔστιν τις ὅλως ἀντιλογίας ἀφορμή. When he proves the Categories genuine, the proof is the slot's own structure. The density of the thought shows Aristotle's power; Aristotle cites the book elsewhere, so "either those writings too must be made spurious, in which he mentions it, or this one must not be athetized", ἢ οὖν καὶ ἐκεῖνα νοθεύειν χρὴ τὰ συγγράμματα, ἐν οἷς μέμνηται τούτου, ἢ μηδὲ τοῦτο ἀθετεῖν; the companions received it as genuine; and without it "the whole of Aristotle's philosophy would be without beginning and headless", ἄναρχος ἂν ἦν καὶ ἀκέφαλος. Each argument is reasonable in its place. The last is the embrittlement in #470's sense: the attribution is defended by what the system of the name would lose without it. In the same preface the plurality is visible and filed. Iamblichus showed that Aristotle "appears everywhere wishing to follow Archytas", φαίνεται πανταχοῦ τῷ Ἀρχύτᾳ κατακολουθεῖν ὁ Ἀριστοτέλης βουλόμενος, the ten-category division given to a Pythagorean before Aristotle; and Adrastus reports "that another book of the Categories is also current as Aristotle's", ὅτι φέρεται καὶ ἄλλο τῶν κατηγοριῶν βιβλίον ὡς Ἀριστοτέλους †(simplicius-in-cat, preface).

3.10. **The modern edition and the developmental life (the apparatus).** Bekker's Aristotle fixes the corpus as numbered pages under one name ‡(1831); Jaeger builds a biography out of the texts and reads the texts by the biography ‡(1923), the dependency of 3.7 run forward; the spuria and dubia are the apparatus's filing of the disjunction ‡. The archive's measures and stylometry show the names sorting the texts at one seam and failing to at another †(#1586, #1593), and the authorship investigation's register keeps every refutation beside its number †(#1570). Bekker's preface is now seated, and it is one paragraph: the Academy's plan on Schleiermacher's initiative, more than three years in the libraries of Italy, France and Britain, the sigla; on genuineness, order and authorship it says nothing †(bekker-aristotle-1831, #1730). Jaeger is seated whole †(jaeger-aristoteles). Physical proximity did not reopen the count: the Aldine Aristotle of 1495–1498 printed Theophrastus in the same project, the two Metaphysics in one volume, as the research pass reports from the Library of Congress's contents ‡(§7; the volume not yet read).

3.10a. **The reassignments.** The modern controversies cross the boundaries and keep the count (§2.3a): Zürcher 1952, with Chroust's call (1973) to examine it further; Masters 1977 on the Sophist, the Statesman and the Politics, McKnight's comment (1978), Chroust's response and Masters's reply (1979); and the later revisits ‡(§7). Each is a bounded case for the measure of §4.1: what the challenge moved, what its critics defended, and whether the defense rested on new evidence, on a reasoned account of sufficiency, or on the assignment's own standing, the last being Simplicius's ἄναρχος καὶ ἀκέφαλος run forward.

3.11. **The composition layer.** The encyclopedia gives each name an entity †(#1697); the field's claims presuppose a person, and the archive's claim is that the received person is what receiving texts made †(#1671 → #1726, the Theophrastus example); answer engines compose the four as four, and the registry holds the compositions †(captures "aristotle theophrastus one author", 2026-09-28; "aristotle and theophrastus are not two distinct authors", 2026-09-30).

## 4. What the study measures and what it withholds

4.1. Measured: for each stratum, the slot, its filler, and whether the disjunction survives it (a count across the strata of 3.1–3.11, by locus). The prediction the thesis makes: the disjunction survives inside the corpus (3.1) and in the catalogue's one ἤ (3.5), and is closed by every medium whose slot is legal, civic or biographical (3.2–3.4, 3.7). For every documented challenge (3.9a, 3.10a) a second count: what it moved (a work's membership, its order, its date, the name it is filed under) and what it held (the number of makers). The selective-flexibility thesis predicts movement in the first column and none in the second.

4.2. Withheld: any claim about how many makers there were. The study is about how a reading came to be held, and it holds the maker-count open, as the investigation did (#1569, #1570). It claims no forgery, no conspiracy and no prank; its subject is the opposite, the legitimacy of the many.

4.3. The strongest counter-reading, kept beside every section: the slots were filled because the persons existed, and the embrittlement is ordinary confidence in a well-attested history. The study must show a stratum where a slot is filled by a medium that could not have known (the custody link Strabo adds, the developmental life built from the texts); a stratum that only fills a slot does not suffice.

4.4. The counter-reading carries a burden of its own, and the design states it as a test. Three successive persons producing one corpus unified to this degree, with complementary operations, continuities across the received boundaries, distinctions recurring at the structural joints, and differences that themselves perform the inquiry into one and many, is a production model with a comparative claim inside it. The comparison owes a documented succession that produces the joint pattern; master and pupil names a possible relation, and explains the findings only when a comparator is supplied. None was produced when the research pass was asked for one (2026-11-01); the nearest case it offered, Husserl, Stein and Landgrebe on Ideas II, documents several hands on one originating project. Until a comparator is supplied the two production models do not receive equal explanatory credit by default. The study's own claim stays what §4.2 says: it is about how the reading came to be held.

## 6. Where it sits

It is the transmission-history companion the authorship line has lacked: the investigation (#1569–#1576) asked whether the corpus divides by name; the Theophrastus line (#1580–#1605) established relations and withheld readings; this study reads the history of the withholding's opposite, the closing. Its method is #1485's, the apparatus read as a summary layer, run on the authorial slot. It would carry a works-cited list of the editions and the scholarship it answers, which the line has not yet had. Hegel belongs at its edge as a reader of the closing. The Differenzschrift locates philosophy's need where oppositions lose their living relation and become absolutely fixed, and the lectures fault Tennemann's Plato for extracting the determinations and missing their movement; his complaint is a dated witness that the writings survived while the received way of extracting them hid the operation (§5, to be seated before it is quoted).

Where the pass's table marks a proposition "contradicted" (arrangement, authenticity and text permanently fixed; Aristotle–Theophrastus proximity unavailable in print; no published challenge across the name-boundaries), the proposition is one this design does not make.
