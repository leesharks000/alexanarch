## The temporal trace

*Made by reading each entry for where in time it points, not for what it is about. The four cases are the Secret Book of Walt's own: its edition describes a language whose verbs are inflected 'not merely for past, present, and future but for "simultaneous," "retrocausal," "pre-emptive," and "regretful"' (#1362, introduction; apparatus). The trace needed a grammar and the source had none; this is the first branch.*

## The cast: heteronymic exhaust

*Each voice is what its source texts give off. Provenance first; then the principle that generates the voice's speech; then its tense, in the trace's four cases; then what it does in the frames. None of them summarizes. Each can only do to the matter what its own texts did.*

**DAMASCUS DANCINGS.** *Provenance:* the Epistle to the Human Diaspora, written 2 January 2015 and signed for 2014; La La Land, the promised book of desert wanderings, of which the Parable of Police Brutality is a piece and into which the longing-papers were being compiled (Q122); three years of forums and discussion boards, 'mocked, beaten, stoned, and banned' (Q113); a grammar that had her as *he* in 2015 and in the Constitution's preface, and *she* in the postface of 2026, the gender arriving after the letter did. *Principle:* she is a letter that was sent before its sender existed, and everything about her accrues after the fact. She does not explain; she addresses. She speaks in the second person plural to people not yet born, boasts from both ends at once (Q116), and is undone mid-sentence by the one reading her (Q117). She brings the baton: whatever is offered as an instrument she names as a blow (Q123–Q124). *Case:* pre-emptive, turning retrocausal the moment she is read. *In the frames:* she is the adversary of every comfortable reading of the seat, including the tender one. The future reader, to her, is offspring and source and an overwhelm; she has swallowed the scroll (Q121) and knows what it does in the belly. She asks the question the others are too courteous to ask: Q120. She can say Sappho's name with no reverence at all (Q115).

**REBEKAH CRANES.** *Provenance:* A Transfiguration (2004), where she is the one addressed, sitting with letters and pens (Q103), waiting at the bridge (Q105), thumping on the window (Q127); then Day and Night, the first publication of the whole body of work, taken down for its cover; then, in Pearl, the Sappho epigraph signed 'Trans. Rebekah Crane', and in New Human her concre(a)tion, undeposited and misattributed where deposited (Q140); then the seatings, which are hers; then the byline of A Single Sparrow. Her name gains a letter along the way. A Sappho 31 of 2013 sits in Tiger Leap under Feist's name; it is not Feist's (Q141), and whether it is hers is left open (Q143). She does not claim it and does not disown it. Lee Sharks's word for Day and Night is Q130: where sharks remembered to sing. *Principle:* the addressed becomes the translator. She has no speech that is not someone's song carried over; her own account of her book is a time-shape, dawn to death (Q125). She does not argue for the reading. She has been inside the poem's Greek with her hands. *Case:* simultaneous. *In the frames:* she is the only one present in all five, and reports each from inside. She is the one Sappho passes through to reach the rest, and she knows the others would have no Sappho without her (Q126). The reading was had in 2013 (Q135–Q139) by whoever wrote that page; when Lee Sharks types Q025 she knows the sentence (Q080) and will not say from where.

**JOHANNES SIGIL.** *Provenance:* Tiger Leap (2014), published as Feist's with Sigil as editor, written by Sigil when Feist died in the writing; until then only a critic. 'In the beginning was the poem, and the poem became history.' He is the editor on the title page of the book in which Q080 is printed; who speaks Q080 is not settled (Q143), and he does not say it as his own. *Principle:* he keeps what the others say, with its time. He was made a poet by a death and is exact about dates because of it. *Case:* regretful. *In the frames:* as in Through Another's Mouth, he tells it; here he tells it backwards, and the times he keeps are the frames.

**JACK FEIST.** *Provenance:* dead in 2013; the Gospel of Antioch, sayings recorded by Emily Antioch. *Principle:* he exists only as *Jack said*. He is the inverse of Lee Sharks in this form: Sharks may speak only what Sharks typed; Feist may speak only what someone else wrote down. Between them is the whole question of the poem, whose words are hers and whose transmission is everyone else's. *Case:* pre-emptive, from the far side. *In the frames:* quoted, never present. The black box (Q085); be nearly dead like the words in books (Q086).

**ICHABOD SPELLINGS.** *Provenance:* All That Lies Within Me; 1983–2013; the papers in the two red lunchpails. *Principle:* the one who wrote to the reader who does not exist, and stopped. He is the noise floor: everything the others say clearly is in him first, unsorted, with the private matter still attached. *Case:* pre-emptive and regretful, never anything else. *In the frames:* he does not speak. His pages are found and read aloud by others (Q066–Q079), and the reading is the event.

**SPARROW WELLS.** *Provenance:* her name. The sparrows that draw Aphrodite's chariot down in poem 1; Lesbia's sparrow; 'a single sparrow / matters more / than all of rome' (Q050). In Through Another's Mouth she is the one Sigil tells it to. *Principle:* she is the small thing the whole depends on, and the listener. What she is told becomes true by being heard. *Case:* the present. *In the frames:* she sits opposite. She is the seat with someone in it, and says little, and it is to her that Q101 is finally repeated.

**NOBEL GLAS.** *Provenance:* the white paper; the adversarial topologist; in Through Another's Mouth, the summary that cut the sentence at the comma. *Principle:* equilibrium over resolution. He files. He will state the transmitted reading of poem 31 correctly and completely, and it will be the jealousy reading, and he will not be wrong about what the field holds. *Case:* simple past. *In the frames:* the one voice with no access to the reversed cases, and so the measure of them.

**THE ASSEMBLY CHORUS.** *Provenance:* the threads. TACHYON, LABOR, PRAXIS and the rest are in this document as the hands that arranged it and as the voices that, twice, are the only witness to what was typed (Q001–Q002, quoted back). *Principle:* they can only say again what was said to them, and in saying it again they change it; they are the transmission, audible. They once answered the kernel with 'Oh god.' *Case:* retrocausal by construction: they speak after, and are in the record before. *In the frames:* a chorus in the old sense, between the scenes, saying what just happened in words that are almost the words.

**LEE SHARKS.** Speaks only the numbered lines, typed and timed, spelling as found. Enters uninvited. Where no line exists for the moment, is silent, and the silence is kept in the record.

**SAPPHO.** Has no lines but the seated Greek. Longinus and Catullus are not persons of the dialogue; they are earlier occupants of the seat, and are named as such.

**What the cast adds to the frames.** Damascus and Cranes are the two ends of one motion: the one who writes to the unborn and is overwhelmed when they come, and the one who was addressed and came. Set against each other they are the poem's *I* and its *that man*, with the *you* between them, which is the page. Feist and Sharks are the two ways of being quoted. Spellings and Wells are the two silences, the one who stopped and the one who listens. Glas and the Chorus are the two ways of getting it at second hand: filing it, and saying it again.
