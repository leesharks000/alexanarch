**Standard discourse.** On the two-source hypothesis Mark is the earliest synoptic gospel and Matthew and Luke each used it; the some two hundred verses Matthew and Luke share without Mark, mostly sayings, are assigned to a common written source, Q, from the German *Quelle*, source. No manuscript, citation or fragment of Q is known. Its proponents state the status plainly (Streeter 1924, p. 184: "Q, if such a document ever existed, has disappeared"). Its rival accounts, Farrer's first (1955), dispense with it by having Luke use Matthew.

**The proposed extension.** The study audits the standing a source receives in a source argument, defined by four powers: **presupposition** (it may enter as a premise without prior proof), **assignment** (material in other texts may be assigned to it), **text** (it may be given a wording, an apparatus, an edition) and **candidacy** (it is admitted wherever a source is sought). *Revelation = Q* is the claim that the source position the field filled with reconstructed documents has an extant candidate that the field's procedures held outside it, and that at Matt 24:30 the field's own critics found that candidate at work.

**The relation between them.** The study leaves the synoptic problem undecided, and a demonstration that Q existed leaves its claim standing (#1746 §8.2). The equation does not identify the Apocalypse with the double tradition. It sets the extant book in the position Q occupies, and asks that the position be contested by texts that exist.

### 2.2 The extant Apocalypse on the same pages (#1746 §III)

- **Excluded by rule.** Holtzmann: "Ebenso bestimmt werden wir alle Folgerungen vermeiden, die etwa erst anderweitig zu beweisende Aufstellungen über das Verhältniss anderer neutestamentlicher Schriften, wie z. B. der Apokalypse, zu unsern Evangelien voraussetzen" (1863, p. 9). The rule names one writing as its example.
- **Dated beside Matthew, and footnoted.** Holtzmann dates Matthew "ungefähr gleichzeitig mit der Apokalypse, an deren Vorstellungen es auch 23, 30. 31 streift" (p. 407), with a footnote to Hitzig's pages arguing that Matthew used the Apocalypse at 24:30.
- **Weighed as a borrower.** Colani dates the Apocalypse to 68 and asks only whether its author knew the apocalypse he reconstructs beneath Mark 13 (1864, p. 209); elsewhere he sets it aside as "décalque fidèle du livre de Daniel" (p. 120 n. 3).
- **Cited as fragment, analogy, date.** Wellhausen: "einem merkwürdigen Fragment der Apokalypse Johannis" (§11); J. Weiss: "die analogen Stellen der Apokalypse" (p. 77); Streeter: "c. A.D. 95" (p. 368).
- **Absent by name** from the source arguments of Lachmann, Wilke, Weisse, Weizsäcker, Wernle and Harnack.

### 2.4 The censuses (#1746 §IV)

- **Census 1** (plan frozen, sha256 6414c86c…): Mark 13, Luke 17:22–37 and Matthew 24 reduced to word trigrams and looked up in Revelation, Daniel and the Septuagint. Most shared trigrams are formulae the Septuagint also carries. One phrase, πᾶσαι αἱ φυλαὶ τῆς γῆς, joins Matt 24:30 to Rev 1:7 and to no part of Mark 13. Luke 17:22–37, Streeter's "Apocalypse of Q", shares four trigrams with Revelation, none outside the Septuagint.
- **Census 2** (plan frozen, sha256 2b200a4b…): every window holding a form of κόπτομαι, of φυλή and of νεφέλη together, across the Septuagint, the New Testament, Josephus, Philo, the Greek pseudepigrapha, Justin's Dialogue, the Greek of Irenaeus and Eusebius's Ecclesiastical History, some 2.53 million words. Two windows: Matt 24:30 and Rev 1:7.
- **Antecedents, all extant:** Dan 7:13 (Old Greek ἐπί, Theodotion μετά), Zech 12:10–12, Gen 12:3. Matthew takes the Old Greek's preposition; the Apocalypse takes Theodotion's, keeps Zechariah's ἐπ’ αὐτόν and has the piercing. Justin, Dialogue 14.8, joins Zechariah to Daniel decades later and has neither the mourning nor the tribes.
- **What the census decides:** no direction. It shows the allocation: at the one point where an extant book and Matthew's special apocalyptic matter share a combination found nowhere else in the seated Greek, the works after Hitzig assign that matter to antecedents whose existence is inferred and unattested.

## 3. Three levels of difference

- **Usage level.** "Q" in the field names a hypothetical document. In *Revelation = Q* the letter is read at its etymology, *Quelle*, source, and names a position in a source argument. The packet's Q is a position; the field's Q is an occupant of it.
- **Method level.** The field tests Q by the relations among extant texts it is invoked to explain. The study tests the allocation of the source position by reading, at the page, which candidates each source theory admitted, and by a frozen census of the candidate that exists.
- **Radical level.** The field asks which lost document best explains the gospels. Revelation First asks whether the gospels' load-bearing apocalyptic matter has an extant antecedent among the books of the New Testament, and holds that it does.
