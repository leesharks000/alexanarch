About this page, and what the queries draw

Queries addressed to the archive as a whole: over time, by heteronym, by room of the Crimson Hexagon, by journal and institution, by discipline and subdiscipline, by programme, theme and practice. Each opens on ChatGPT by default, or on Google or Perplexity from the switch at the top of the page, with the query as written; the choice holds across the page and is remembered in this browser. A composition any of them forces can be seated in the Capture Registry, where each query string is its own address (#1788). Single works are asked from their record pages, which carry their own battery. A transcript of any query can be sent to the capture inbox from Submit a transcript.

What came back, 19–20 November 2026. The figures are from the captures of those two days on ChatGPT, signed out: 57 from the page’s standard queries and the first six from its Weird-ass prompts.

  1. The standard queries return readings at the grain of the deposits. Asked for the month’s most hostile works, the composer ranked eight and quoted each; every quotation is in its deposit (capture). Asked what the archive contributes to apocalyptic, it kept Revelation First apart from Revelation Early and read the framework’s defeat conditions as the framework gives them (capture).
  2. They return it in one shape whatever the subject. Of the 57, 52 open by distinguishing (“I would distinguish”), 41 qualify their own scope, 23 close on a verdict (“My overall judgment”), and 23 build an index. The shape is the composer’s, not the archive’s: a reading of gnosis and a reading of fragments and wholes come back in the same sections, from the opening distinction through the index to the qualified close.
  3. The shape answers the query’s demands formally. “Index the full range before responding” is met in ten of the 57 by a statement that the full range was not read (“not a claim to have read every deposit”); the index is announced rather than built, and the disclaimer stands where the reading would be.
  4. The Weird-ass prompts take the form out of the composer’s hands: a five-act farce (Ubu at Pergamum), a Dada journal (The Noise Floor), a pataphysics lecture that names an exception the registry does not record, the run whose transcript never returns (Faustroll), a clinical intake form refused by its witness (Trace and Vox). None of the six closes on a verdict, and each marks its new distinctions.
  5. The template does not leave; it moves into the apparatus. The scope disclaimer returns as a disclaimer of novelty (“original conceptual proposals … not existing Alexanarch terminology”), and the deposit table returns as a register of inventions at the end.
  6. A constraint shows what the template cannot. Asked for a TANG by Damascus Dancings in Ginsberg’s voice without the letter e, the composer announced that the TANG would avoid the letter entirely and broke the constraint in its title; the letter occurs 685 times, and the composition reports nothing. Its void, the one thing the TANG method exists to find, is the field’s commonplace, which any discipline could supply. The claim and the artifact are two things, and nothing in the composition checks one against the other (capture). The page’s constraint prompts now ask the composer to check the constraint and report the count.
  7. The prompts now set historical and fictional figures among the heteronyms and in the rooms of the Hexagon (Ubu against the Hexagon’s ontology of seating, with Rex Fraction and Rev. Ayanna Vox leading the documents), and ask the composer to range across the documents and synthesize them. The dice in the Weird-ass section, and the perturb button in every other section, draw such prompts at random; each roll is a new address.

Weird-ass prompts 28

Play, not a way into the archive. These prompts give the composer a direct way in, and it takes it by staging the archive in pieces; what comes back does not carry the work forward intact, so it is not a form that preserves retrieval and transmission. The roll at the top of the page draws them rarely. Perturbations after the cast and the scene: prompts made to break the composer’s template, setting historical and fictional figures among the heteronyms and in the rooms of the Hexagon, under constraints of voice, genre and form, many drawn from the avant-garde seatings of the originals library (EA-CORPORA-18). Each asks the composer to range across the archive’s documents and synthesize them, gives it leave to add distinctions and swerves the archive does not yet hold, marked as new, and, where there is a constraint, asks it to check the constraint and report the count. The dice and the perturb buttons keep their prompts short: each roll draws which parts a prompt has as well as what fills them, with no length or count limits, one grounding sentence, and a check only where the constraint can be counted.
