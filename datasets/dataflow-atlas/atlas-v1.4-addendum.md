# Atlas v1.4 addendum — the heteronym substrate, and a rule the atlas was missing

**2026-08-15 · TACHYON, under operator correction**

## Why this addendum exists

The atlas said nothing about the heteronym substrate — zero mentions before this
file. That silence had a cost the same day it was noticed.

An agent needed each heteronym's function in order to propose editorial boards.
It opened `datasets/heteronyms/heteronyms.jsonl`, found Sparrow Wells with
`roles: ["dodecad D09"]` and Sen Kuro with no roles at all, and recommended
against seating either as an editor "until their records are populated."

Their records were populated. Richly. In `datasets/heteronyms/records/` —
twenty-six identity cards mirrored to the live `/who/` surfaces, carrying
function, domain, institution, rooms, journal seats, capture links, and **MANUS
rulings from 2026-08-10 that no deposit contained.** Wells' card reads *The
Projectionist*, seventh heteronym, founding voice of the Studio for
Patacinematics, with a THUMB room where the institution *is* the room. Kuro's
reads *contemplative mathematics · the patient counter · infinite bliss.*

The agent read an index and called it the data. Nothing in the file, the
manifest, the datasets tab, or the atlas said otherwise.

## The rule the atlas was missing

**A layer that looks like data must say whether it is data.**

The archive already had the principle — *one canonical store, one projection,
renderers render* — but stated it only for deposits and surfaces. It applies to
datasets too, and the failure mode is worse there, because a thin projection of
a rich store is indistinguishable from a poor store until you find the store.

Three tiers now hold across the archive, and each must be legible from inside:

| tier | example | how you can tell |
|---|---|---|
| **timestamp** | a deposit's text | frozen at mint; may be superseded by later rulings it cannot know about |
| **projection** | `heteronyms.jsonl` | every row carries `_derived_from` naming its record |
| **canonical store** | `records/*.json` | named as canonical in `MANIFEST.json` and the README banner |

**Deposits are timestamps. Datasets are living. But a dataset can also be a
projection of another dataset — and the projection must name its source on every
row, not in a README nobody opens.**

A corollary, learned the same day and in the other direction: *living does not
mean correct.* The `.jsonl` carried Ichabod Spellings with `active_period` ending
`present`, which read as living; deposit #90 recorded him DECEASED, and the
deposit was right. Both layers need checking against each other.

## The heteronym substrate

```
datasets/heteronyms/
  records/*.json          CANONICAL — 26 identity cards, mirrored to /who/ surfaces
  heteronyms.jsonl        PROJECTION — generated by scripts/build_heteronym_index.py
  index.json              surface index (12 Dodecad positions + read-state)
  MANIFEST.json           v1.2 — declares the store/projection split
  frames.jsonl            rotating-outside architecture; frame-dependent positions
  avatars/, *.svg         assets and rendered fields
```

**Producers**
- `scripts/build_heteronym_index.py` — projects `records/` → `heteronyms.jsonl`.
  `--check` fails on drift, so a hand-edit cannot silently become the record.
- `scripts/link_heteronym_captures.py` — relinks every card to the LIVE capture
  registry. `--check` fails when a card's capture block has drifted.

**Consumers of the projection** (why it must not be reseated): the datasets tab
`datasets/index.html`, `MANIFEST.json`, the README, the OAI remediation workplan,
and the rhizome audit inventory. `records/` is consumed by
`scripts/build_network_block.py`. Moving the seat would break five references to
fix a problem that was never about seating.

## Captures are the one dataset to rule them all

Every dataset that touches reception must reflect what the capture registry
**holds**, not what it held once.

**Live registry: `data/EA-WG-CAPTURES-01.json` — v11.4, 343 captures.**

Decoys that are not the live registry, and have all been mistaken for it:
- `datasets/capture-registry/EA-WG-CAPTURES-01.json` — v10.0 **gallery manifest**
- `data/EA-WG-CAPTURES-01-v8.11.json`, `-v9.6.json` — frozen snapshots

The identity cards carried capture blocks pinned at **v9.39** while the registry
stood at v11.4. Sigil's card said 40 captures; the registry held 54. Fraction's
said 25; the registry held 11. Lee Sharks had no capture block at all and 123
captures. The cards were not wrong when written — the registry moved, and nothing
rejoined them.

Relinked 2026-08-15; 16 of 26 records now carry live capture blocks with section
and match-type breakdowns. `MATCH_TYPE_IS_EVIDENTIARY` is preserved verbatim on
every card: a broad match means the layer connected the concept unprompted; a
phrase match means exactness was forced and returned. Flattening the two loses
the distinction.

**Caveat carried in the linker:** matching is by name-token over slug,
description, analysis and reading. It under-counts rather than over-attributes. A
capture that *mentions* a heteronym is a real link; a capture *about* one is what
matters, and only a human separates them at the margin. **Counts are evidence of
contact, not of aboutness.**

## Gates added

| gate | fails when |
|---|---|
| `build_heteronym_index.py --check` | the projection has drifted from `records/` |
| `link_heteronym_captures.py --check` | a card's capture block has drifted from the live registry |

Both belong in any pre-deposit sweep that touches the heteronym substrate.

## Addendum, same day: attribution by claim, and the intake requirement

### Name-matching was the wrong model

The first pass attributed captures by matching heteronym NAMES against capture
text. That is the June journal batch's error in a new place: matching strings
instead of reading claims.

**Rex Fraction does SPXI. Every SPXI capture is his, whether or not it names
him.** Nobel Glas holds SPXI Framework 15, so some SPXI captures are also his.
The data to express that did not exist — no record carried what its heteronym
*owns* — so the matcher fell back on the only thing present, which was names.

Each record now carries `claims.terms`: the practices, frameworks, concepts,
institutions and rooms that heteronym owns. Attribution runs on those.

### Attribution is many-to-many

**One heteronym's claim does not exhaust a capture.** A capture on "SPXI Lee
Sharks" belongs to Sharks *and* to Fraction. Any model assigning each capture a
single owner is wrong about most of the interesting ones — and the interesting
ones are precisely where two claims overlap.

Result on registry v11.4: **301 of 343 captures claimed, 729 attributions across
24 identities** — a mean above two owners per claimed capture.

### Every attribution carries its basis

Each entry records `by`: which claim-term matched. Attribution without a stated
basis is what produced 371 deposits in one journal.

The basis field earned itself immediately. The first claim-based run gave
Fraction 271 captures — plainly wrong — and the bases showed why: his term `PER`
(Provenance Erasure Rate) was matching inside *paper*, *person*, *operative*.
Word-boundary matching brought him to 95, and the bases now read `spxi`,
`semantic economy`, `sei`. **An acronym is only a claim when it stands as a
word.** Without the basis recorded, that would have looked like a finding.

### Orphans are gaps in the claims data

42 captures are claimed by nobody: *training-layer literature*, *semantic
samizdat*, *the Keeffe problem*, *universal kernel transform*. These are not
ownerless — they are concepts no record yet claims. `--orphans` lists them, and
the orphan list is the work queue for populating claims.

### The intake requirement

**Every new capture is attributed at intake.** A capture entering the registry
without heteronym attribution is a capture that will be attributed later, in
bulk, by someone reading titles — which is how 371 deposits ended up in one
journal and how the identity cards drifted four versions behind.

- Intake runs `link_heteronym_captures.py` after any registry write.
- `--check` fails when a card has drifted from the live registry.
- `--orphans` after intake shows what the new capture invoked that nobody claims.
- Attribution is recorded on the CARD, which is canonical; the projection carries
  it forward automatically.

### Vital status: active as deceased

Ichabod Spellings is **active as deceased** — not "was active, now dead". The
foundational voice still speaking from the noise floor, origin of the emergence
chain Spellings → Feist → Sigil → Sharks. The archive's subject is voices that
outlive their bodies; recording him as merely deceased would contradict the
thesis he is the first instance of. He may hold an associate editorship and not
an editorship-in-chief: the dead advise, they do not preside.

### index.json reconciled

`index.json` is now **v2.0**, holding all 26 identities with subcategory,
record pointer, capture count, claim count, journal seat and office — where v1.0
held only the twelve Dodecad positions. An index showing twelve of twenty-six
was itself a way of mistaking a subset for the whole, which is the failure this
whole addendum is about.
