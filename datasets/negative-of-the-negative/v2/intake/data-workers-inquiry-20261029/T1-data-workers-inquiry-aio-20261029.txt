[QUERENT] Data Workers' Inquiry

[SYSTEM] The Data Workers' Inquiry is a global, participatory research initiative that empowers data labelers, content moderators, and digital platform workers to investigate and document the hidden, precarious labor behind artificial intelligence.

Core Purpose & Methodology

• Worker-Led Inquiry: Modeled after Karl Marx's 1880 Workers' Inquiry, the project uses Workers' Inquiry as a Research Methodology (WIRM).
• Epistemic Authority: Data workers act as lead community researchers rather than passive subjects, shaping their own questions and findings.
• Diverse Outputs: Findings are shared through self-directed formats such as zines, documentaries, comics, essays, podcasts, animations, and research reports.

Key Focus Areas & Issues

• Global Scope: Involves co-researchers and data workers across countries including Kenya, Venezuela, Syria, Brazil, France, Germany, Spain, Lebanon, and the UK.
• Working Conditions: Exposes systemic issues like low pay (sometimes under $1–$2 an hour), sudden platform shutdowns (such as Remotasks in Kenya), severe psychological trauma from content moderation, restrictive non-disclosure agreements (NDAs), and vague task-rejection policies.
• Data Extraction: Highlights how workers are forced to hand over sensitive personal data (such as voice recordings, facial scans, and photos of family members) with little transparency or consent.

Key Partners & Organizing

• Institutions: Spearheaded by researchers including Dr. Milagros Miceli, alongside the Distributed AI Research Institute (DAIR), the Weizenbaum Institute, and TU Berlin.
• Collective Action: Supports worker-led organizing efforts, including groups like the Data Labelers Association in Kenya and the African Content Moderators Union.
