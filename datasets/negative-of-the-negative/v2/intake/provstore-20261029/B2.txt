.i18n.translations.ada.form.policy.title: ADA Testing Accommodations Policy
.i18n.translations.ada.form.policy.intro: Applicants who request testing accommodations pursuant to the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) must make the request on the ADA Accommodations Forms. Prov administers the examinations in a manner that does not discriminate on the basis of disability against a qualified Applicant. An Applicant who is otherwise eligible to take the Prov Examination may file a request for testing accommodations.
.i18n.translations.ada.form.policy.definitions_heading: Definitions
.i18n.translations.ada.form.policy.definitions[0]: The Americans with Disabilities Act provides comprehensive civil rights protection for qualified individuals with disabilities. An individual with a disability is a person who: (1) has a physical impairment or a mental impairment that substantially limits a major life activity, (2) has a record of such an impairment, or (3) is regarded as having such an impairment.
.i18n.translations.ada.form.policy.definitions[1]: A qualified individual with a disability means an Applicant with a disability who, with or without reasonable modification to Rules, Policies, or Practices; the removal of architectural, communication, or transportation barriers; or the provision of auxiliary aids and services, meets the essential eligibility requirements for licensure for the specific client being tested.
.i18n.translations.ada.form.policy.definitions[2]: Major life activities include functions such as caring for oneself, performing manual tasks, walking, seeing, hearing, speaking, breathing, learning, and working. An individual who currently uses illegal drugs is not protected by the ADA when a decision not to provide accommodations is made based upon his/her current illegal use of drugs.
.i18n.translations.ada.form.policy.definitions[3]: Physical impairment means any physiological disorder or condition, cosmetic disfigurement, or anatomical loss affecting one or more of the following body systems: neurological, musculoskeletal, special sense organ, respiratory (including speech organs), cardiovascular, reproductive, digestive, genitourinary, hemic and lymphatic, skin and endocrine.
.i18n.translations.ada.form.policy.definitions[4]: Mental impairment shall mean any mental or psychological disorder, such as mental retardation, organic brain syndrome, emotional or mental illness, and specific learning disabilities.
.i18n.translations.ada.form.policy.definitions[5]: Reasonable accommodations mean an adjustment or modification of the standard testing conditions that ameliorates the impact of the Applicant’s disability without doing any of the following: (1) fundamentally altering the nature of the examination such that the ability to determine through the examination whether the Applicant possesses the essential skills and aptitudes that the Board has determined are appropriate to require; (2) imposing an undue burden on the Board; (3) compromising the security of the Examination; or (4) compromising the integrity, the reliability, or the validity of the Examination.

.i18n.translations.ada.faq.eyebrow: Frequently Asked
.i18n.translations.ada.faq.heading: Common
.i18n.translations.ada.faq.heading_highlight: questions.
.i18n.translations.ada.faq.body: Answers to what candidates ask most about requesting testing accommodations.
.i18n.translations.ada.faq.items[0].q: Who is eligible for testing accommodations?
.i18n.translations.ada.faq.items[0].a: Any applicant otherwise eligible to sit for a Prov examination who has a physical or mental impairment that substantially limits a major life activity. Major life activities include seeing, hearing, learning, reading, concentrating, thinking, walking, and others. You must submit Form A and have Form B completed by a qualified medical or psychological authority.
.i18n.translations.ada.faq.items[1].q: How far in advance should I apply?
.i18n.translations.ada.faq.items[1].a: All forms and supporting documentation must be received by Prov at least three weeks before your desired testing date. Late submissions may not be reviewed in time, so we recommend submitting as early as possible.
.i18n.translations.ada.faq.items[2].q: Do I need to reapply for accommodations for each exam?
.i18n.translations.ada.faq.items[2].a: No. Once your accommodations have been approved, you do not need to submit a new application for each subsequent exam. If your accommodation needs change, please contact our ADA Accommodations Team.
.i18n.translations.ada.faq.items[3].q: What documentation do I need from my provider?
.i18n.translations.ada.faq.items[3].a: Form B must be completed by a physician, psychologist, or other professional licensed to diagnose and treat your impairment. A recent IEP or 504 Plan may be submitted in place of Form B. Otherwise, recent evaluations are typically required — within the past five years for learning, attention, or psychological disabilities, and within the past year for physical, visual, or hearing disabilities. A copy of the provider's curriculum vitae (CV) must also be attached.
.i18n.translations.ada.faq.items[4].q: How are my testing accommodations determined?
.i18n.translations.ada.faq.items[4].a: Each accommodation request is reviewed individually based on your needs, supporting documentation, and the requirements of your testing program. Accommodations may vary by exam or licensing board, and in some cases, approval is provided by the applicable licensing board or regulatory agency rather than Prov.
.i18n.translations.ada.faq.items[5].q: How long does the review process take?
.i18n.translations.ada.faq.items[5].a: Complete requests are reviewed within three weeks of receipt. Incomplete submissions take longer — your reviewer may request additional records, so submitting everything together speeds the process.
.i18n.translations.ada.faq.items[6].q: Will my disability information stay confidential?
.i18n.translations.ada.faq.items[6].a: Yes. All information submitted in connection with an accommodation request is treated as confidential and used solely to evaluate eligibility. By signing Form D, you authorize Prov and your provider to exchange information, and recipients are forbidden from re-disclosing it.
.i18n.translations.ada.faq.items[7].q: What if I'm denied?
.i18n.translations.ada.faq.items[7].a: If your request is not approved, you'll receive a written explanation. You may submit additional documentation for reconsideration, or contact our ADA coordinator to discuss next steps. Decisions are made based on the documentation provided and ADA standards.
