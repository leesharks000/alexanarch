About us

Turkopticon’s mission is to organize data workers to build collective power, improve working conditions, and hold platforms, companies, and institutions accountable to the labor they rely on. 💠Our Organization is led by data workers, ensuring our advocacy, resources, and support reflect the real and current conditions workers face. 💠We provide assistance, resources, and advocacy to all data workers without discrimination and with respect for each worker’s human worth. 💠We believe data workers are stronger together. We build connections across platforms, borders, and experiences to support one another. 💠We are committed to a structure where leadership is shared, voices are valued, and decisions are made with transparency and care.

    Who is accountable for the workers behind AI? Behind every AI system and social media platform is a global workforce of content moderators, data labellers and other digital workers whose labour is essential, yet whose rights and working conditions often remain invisible. On 18 December 2026, Equidem's researcher Jason Nemerovski will join an international, interdisciplinary conference at the French Senate in Paris, titled 'Accountability in AI Global Value Chains: Data, Labour, and Environment'. Drawing on Equidem's research, including our report 'Scroll. Click. Suffer: The Hidden Human Cost of Content Moderation and Data Labelling', Jason will contribute to discussions on the legal, policy and practical solutions needed to strengthen corporate accountability and ensure greater protection for workers across global AI value chains. Based on interviews with 113 workers across Colombia, Ghana, Kenya and the Philippines, our investigation documented serious psychological harms, exploitative working conditions, restrictions on workers' ability to organise, and how outsourcing arrangements involving technology companies such as Meta can obscure responsibility for labour rights violations. As AI continues to expand, we must ask: Who bears the human and environmental costs of technological progress? How can we ensure that companies benefiting from these global value chains are held accountable? We look forward to joining researchers, legal experts and advocates for this important conversation on advancing corporate accountability and building a more responsible and rights-respecting AI industry. 📍 French Senate, Paris 📅 Friday, 18 December 2026 | 9:00–17:30 The event is free to attend, with prior registration required. Simultaneous French–English interpretation will be available. 🔗 Register here: https://lnkd.in/guDKFu46 📖 Read Equidem's research: https://lnkd.in/enWjuQQh #AIAccountability #LabourRights #HumanRights #DigitalLabour #CorporateAccountability #ResponsibleAI

    Can we create technology that truly serves people if we keep the people who build it hidden from view? I attended the Summit on People-Centred Technologies in Africa last week, convened by Open Society Foundations, Andela, Code For Africa (CfA)Code For Africa (CfA), Build Up ^ and Ushahidi, and joined a breakout session on Governance, Rights, and Ethical Guardrails, where we explored practical ways to make technology safer, more inclusive, and more accountable. As the session unfolded, I realised just how vital content moderators and data labellers are to every one of the four interconnected themes we explored: 🔹 Theme 1 - Protection of Human Rights Defenders: Workers who expose harmful practices within technology systems need protection from retaliation and surveillance when they speak up. 🔹 Theme 2 - Misinformation, Surveillance & Political Misuse: Content moderators are making decisions about misinformation, political speech, and harmful content in every electioneering period, yet they often have little voice in the policies they implement. Hate speech can spread because the platform lacks a policy on local languages being shared… 🔹 Theme 3 (strongly connects) - Data Governance, Privacy & Algorithmic Accountability: Data labellers and moderators are part of the AI supply chain, yet their data rights, privacy, and working conditions often receive little attention; they are also data subjects and rights holders… 🔹 Theme 4 - Inclusion, Accessibility & Community Ownership: We talk about African ownership and community stewardship, but Africans are not only consumers of technology. African workers already contribute the labour, language, and contextual knowledge that power global digital platforms and AI systems that the world marvels at…(we have been mapping LIDAR systems for the driverless cars back in 2015 before FSD was implemented) So one practical guardrail I’d propose is that we start assessing the entire AI supply chain, not just the final product. Could we have something like an AI Labour and Data Rights Impact Assessment that looks at privacy, labour rights, psychological safety, participation and redress? - The Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD), aka the EU Supply Chain Act, can act as a guide in developing such. People-centred technology should reach further than just end users or impacted communities. It must shine a light on the workers who build, train, moderate, and maintain these systems. As we shape governance frameworks and ethical guardrails, these workers deserve a seat at the table where we decide our digital future. And as Dr Siphosami Malunga put it, Africa needs to look for African solutions from within. ‼️ TL/DR: If we’re serious about people-centred technology, the people who make it possible cannot remain invisible. ‼️ 🚨 Check comments for a link to my research on how data workers’ biometric data is being extracted. #PeopleCentredTechnology #AI #DataRights #DigitalRights #ContentModeration #DataLabeling

    Today marks the end of Amazon Mechanical Turk, but it does not mean the work that happened there is disappearing. Some requesters are transitioning their work to other platforms, including Prolific and Clickworker. Others have created their own websites where workers can continue completing their tasks directly. As workers navigate this transition, we want to help share places where former MTurk requesters and projects can still be found. Here’s one site workers may want to know about: Piper - https://www.piperpays.com/ You may know them by their Mturk name PickFU. If you know of other requesters or projects that have moved off MTurk, feel free to share them with us.
