book of revelation
The [Book of Revelation](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESYwHrOzAVuV4gchX-WYshll5_yDPCbpzDC7x8n0g-jKFjp9ZvoDbX9DDkKRef5WvaE-Fb6dCngh1zcCCSLIRERoftggm7896GnlYSaEn7eXJCbiWskSJJseIhba84E0oQjW7Yxg) is the final book of the New Testament, written as an apocalyptic prophecy by John while exiled on the Greek island of Patmos.
Core Message and Meaning

* The Victorious Lamb: The central message reveals that Jesus Christ—depicted as a slain yet triumphant lamb—has already conquered evil through his sacrifice and resurrection.
* Hope Under Pressure: It was originally written to encourage first-century Christians facing persecution and pressure to conform to Roman imperial culture.
* Ultimate Triumph: The book assures believers that God sovereignly controls history and will ultimately eradicate evil, uniting heaven and earth in a new creation.

Structure and Major Symbolism

* Letters to Seven Churches: It opens with specific messages of praise, warning, and encouragement to seven historical churches in Asia Minor (Ephesus, Smyrna, Pergamum, Thyatira, Sardis, Philadelphia, and Laodicea).
* Recursive Cycles: It uses rich Old Testament imagery structured around repeating sets of seven—including [seven seals, seven trumpets, and seven bowls] of judgment.

* Vivid Figures: The text features symbolic characters like the Dragon, the Beast (famously associated with the number 666), and Babylon (representing corrupt human empires like Rome).
* The New Jerusalem: It concludes with a vision of a renewed cosmos where God dwells with humanity forever.

Main Interpretive Frameworks

* Preterist: Views the book's symbols as descriptions of events tied strictly to the first century, particularly the Roman Empire and the fall of Jerusalem.
* Futurist: Interprets chapters 4 through 22 as literal, yet-to-be-fulfilled prophecies concerning the literal end of the world and Christ's physical return.
* Idealist / Symbolic: Views the text as a timeless, allegorical depiction of the ongoing spiritual conflict between good and evil.
* Historicist: Reads the book as a symbolic timeline tracking the history of the Christian Church from the apostolic era to the final return of Christ.
