        The main causes of World War II in Europe—and the factors behind Germany's eventual collapse ("roke" / defeat) can be divided into the outbreak of the war and the reasons for Germany's defeat.

Causes of World War II (How the War Started)

• Treaty of Versailles (1919): The harsh peace terms forced on Germany after World War I demanded massive financial reparations, severe military cutbacks, and territorial losses, breeding deep national resentment and a desire for revision.
• Economic Instability: The Great Depression of 1929 shattered the German economy, causing massive unemployment and hyperinflation, which destabilized the democratic Weimar Republic.
• Nazi Ideology and Lebensraum: Adolf Hitler and the Nazi Party rose to power promising to restore German pride, overturn Versailles, and conquer Lebensraum ("living space") in Eastern Europe for the Aryan race.
• Appeasement: Britain and France pursued a policy of diplomatic concessions (such as allowing the annexation of parts of Czechoslovakia) in hopes of avoiding another devastating war, which instead emboldened Hitler's aggression.
• The Invasion of Poland: On September 1, 1939, Germany invaded Poland, prompting Britain and France to declare war and officially spark World War II in Europe.

Causes of Germany’s Defeat (Why Germany Lost)

• Overextended Supply Lines: The immense scale of the Soviet Union stretched German logistics past their breaking point, leading to severe shortages of fuel, food, and warm clothing (especially during the halt before Moscow in 1941).
• Two-Front War: Opening the Eastern Front against the Soviet Union while fighting Britain and later the United States split the Wehrmacht's forces and industrial output.
• Economic and Industrial Disparity: The Allied coalition—powered by the massive manufacturing might of the United States (via U.S. Lend-Lease), the human and material resources of the Soviet Union, and the British Empire—far outproduced the German war economy.
• Catastrophic Military Setbacks: Key defeats like the surrender at Stalingrad (1943) and the failed offensive at the Battle of Kursk halted German expansion and forced a permanent retreat.
• Allied Air Superiority and Bombing: Round-the-clock Allied strategic bombing campaigns devastated German cities, crippled infrastructure, and crushed industrial production and civilian morale.
