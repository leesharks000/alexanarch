13 Best AEO and GEO Agencies in 2026

AEO and GEO are services that are typically sold separately. However, there’s a common overlap in how agencies describe each of these terms. Many of them may offer one or both even if they serve a similar function - to help your brand be visible in AI search.

This article provides an overview of how and when AEO and GEO became separate marketing services, why agencies offer one or both, and what you can expect from an agency that offers either service.

Additionally, this post includes information on independent AEO and GEO research studies. Finally, we've created a checklist of questions you should ask your potential vendor before signing a contract.

The agency list you can find in this article will continue to grow, and we will update it soon.

AEO And GEO Are The Same Thing

Where The Two Terms Came From

When we look back to the early days of these two terms, the differences become much clearer.

GEO originated from academia. Researchers affiliated with Princeton University, IIT Delhi, Georgia Tech, and the Allen Institute for AI published a research paper, "Generative Engine Optimization," on arXiv in November 2023. This research was later presented by the authors at the ACM SIGKDD Conference in Barcelona, August 2024. GEO was a research-based product long before agencies started selling it as a service.

AEO has a different history. The term grew after years of SEO work, specifically around Featured Snippets and Voice Search, years before the AI search was common. The goal was to create content structured in a way that allowed search engines and voice assistants to locate, understand, and return a direct answer.

GEO became more widely used in marketing after Andreessen Horowitz (a16z) published a May 2025 newsletter focused on the concept. That helped push GEO into the wider marketing conversation.

There is one other piece of context worth noting. In the same newsletter, a16z disclosed that it had investments in several companies working on GEO measurement. That does not invalidate the term or the underlying research, but it is relevant context when looking at how GEO entered the commercial market.

For more background, see our existing piece on SEO, GEO, AEO and LLM optimization, which this article builds directly on.

Why They Describe The Same Work

Here it’s all about how sources frame the same conclusion.

Profound, a company that sells AI search measurement software published “AEO vs GEO: Why they are the same thing.” In this article, Profound states that these two terms describe the same underlying work.

Digday made a similar point, describing AEO and GEO as different acronyms that agencies use for the same broader search.

Wikipedia says that as of early 2026 there’s no agreed academic definition that undoubtedly separates these two terms and that both can be used in practice.

We also found sources outside the agency and vendor market with the same conclusion.

Forrester Principal Analyst Nikhil Lai said that AEO is meaningfully different from traditional SEO, but it’s not different enough to be a separate discipline. He also says that marketing around AEO and GEO often overstates that difference to create a space for new tools and services.

Google’s 2026 documentation takes a similar attitude, saying that optimizing for generative AI search is still part of optimizing the search experience, which Google continues to describe as SEO.

From another viewpoint, there are legitimate counterpoints. Kai Spriestersbach states that LLMs and other types of search engine technology will process and produce content differently than traditional Search Engines. This means that there will be a need for different measurement tools and methodologies. From this perspective, GEO is more than just a new name for SEO.

For more on what answer engine optimization actually involves, see our piece on what answer engine optimization actually is.

Here's the honest split, and it's not really a criticism once you understand it: roughly eighty percent of what gets sold as AEO or GEO is established SEO work under a new name, and about twenty percent is genuinely new measurement work.

Schema markup, entity optimization, content restructuring, and citation building through digital PR are all disciplines that existed well before anyone started saying "AEO." Google and Forrester both say the fundamentals of good SEO still apply here, so an agency doing solid SEO work under new branding is still doing the right work, just with slightly misleading marketing around it.

The Genuinely New Part Is Measurement

Prompt tracking and citation monitoring, meaning the practice of measuring whether and how often your brand shows up in an AI assistant's answers, genuinely didn't exist as disciplines two years ago, and they can't be done with a traditional SEO toolset.

This is the honest answer to whether AEO is a real, distinct thing: the optimization work is mostly SEO, the measurement work is genuinely new, and the measurement is actually the harder problem to solve well. See our roundups of AI Overview monitoring tools and our explainer on the AI visibility pyramid for more.

The Highest-Leverage Input Is Earned Media

A University of Toronto study submitted to arXiv on September 10, 2025, found that AI search tools show a systematic and fairly overwhelming bias toward earned media (meaning coverage on third-party sites you don't control) over content a brand publishes on its own site or social channels.

If that finding holds up, it reorders the priority list for this entire category: what moves citations most isn't restructuring your own pages, it's getting written about elsewhere. Worth flagging clearly: this is a preprint, meaning it hasn't yet been peer-reviewed, so it's a strong early signal rather than settled fact. Directionally, it points toward putting budget into digital PR rather than into more on-page content work.

Does It Actually Work

The Test That Found Nothing

Ahrefs ran a controlled study on this specific question, and it's worth reporting fully because no other list in this category mentions it. Between August 2025 and March 2026, they tracked roughly 1,885 pages that added JSON-LD schema markup against about 4,000 comparable pages that didn't.

The result: no meaningful citation growth in AI Mode or ChatGPT, and actually a 4.6 percent decline in AI Overview citations for the pages that added schema. Ahrefs also demonstrated that tools like ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, and AI Mode mostly extract the visible HTML content on a page when retrieving information, and frequently ignore the JSON-LD schema entirely.

The plain conclusion: schema markup is good hygiene, not a lever that moves AI citations. Worth knowing, since schema implementation shows up in nearly every AEO agency's deliverables list, including on the search results for this exact article's topic.

The Forty Percent Figure, Properly Qualified

The number quoted across almost this entire category traces back to the same Princeton paper mentioned earlier. Its headline finding: certain techniques, specifically citing sources, adding quotations, and adding statistics, improved a page's visibility in AI answers by 30 to 41 percent, with increases over 40 percent reported across various queries.

Here's the caveat that almost nobody publishing this number includes. The same paper's Table 2 shows that identical technique cutting a rank-1 page's visibility by 30.3 percent, while lifting a rank-5 page's visibility by 115.1 percent. In other words, the effect is heavily rank-dependent, and it can actively hurt a page that's already the top-cited source for a query.

The test itself was run on a simulated generative search engine with a zero-sum, five-source setup, not on live ChatGPT or Perplexity. The edits tested were generated by an LLM, not applied by a human writer. And notably, an authoritative tone produced no significant improvement on its own, while keyword stuffing frequently performed worse than doing nothing at all. The headline number is real. The caveat belongs in the same breath as the number, not in a footnote three paragraphs later.

How Would You Even Verify It

Here's the measurement problem stated plainly enough to actually act on. AI answers are non-deterministic, meaning the same question can get a different answer, with different sources cited, from one query to the next. eMarketer research found that 40 to 60 percent of the sources an AI system cites for a given query change from one month to the next. Two respected industry trackers disagree significantly on how often AI Overviews even appear in the first place: BrightEdge put the figure near 48 percent in early 2026, while Semrush put it near 15.7 percent as of November 2025.

The practical takeaway: if two established trackers can't agree within thirty percentage points on how often a feature even shows up, any single AI visibility score reported for one specific brand deserves real skepticism, and a one-time spot check tells you essentially nothing.

Ask any agency for a locked prompt set, named engines, and a stated sampling frequency, and ask directly whether you'd be able to reproduce their result yourself. See our roundup of LLM optimisation tools for tools that support this kind of ongoing tracking.

Eight Questions

Can you show me a live citation dashboard for an existing client?
Not a screenshot. A live view in Profound, Peec or Otterly.

A good answer arranges it. Reluctance here is the whole answer.

Which prompts, which engines, and how often will you sample?
Unlocked prompts mean the number can be made to move without the work moving.

A good answer names the prompt set, the engines and the cadence before you sign.

Can I reproduce your reported result myself?
Given 40 to 60 percent monthly source churn, a result you cannot reproduce is a result you cannot trust.

A good answer is yes, with instructions.

Show me two dated examples of citation-rate movement, not traffic.
Traffic and rankings are the easy metrics and the ones least connected to this work.

If every case study shows traffic, they are selling SEO under a new name.

What proportion of your effort goes to earned media versus on-page?
The strongest independent finding points to third-party sources over your own pages.

A good answer weights off-site heavily and can explain why.

What is your position on schema?
Ahrefs found no citation lift and a small decline in AI Overviews.

A good answer treats schema as hygiene. An answer that leads with schema is a flag.

What are the contract term and exit terms?
Watch for twelve-month minimums with vague deliverables in an unproven category.

A short initial term or a paid diagnostic first is a signal of confidence.

What would you tell us not to bother with?
An agency that thinks everything in this category works is selling, not advising.

A good answer contains at least one popular tactic they think is a waste of money.

Eight questions worth asking any AEO or GEO agency before you sign anything, all on the same call if you're comparing multiple agencies:

Can you show me a live citation dashboard for an existing client? Not a screenshot, an actual live view in a tool like Profound, Peec, or Otterly. Reluctance here tells you most of what you need to know.

Which prompts, which engines, and how often will you sample? Unlocked prompts mean the reported number can move without the underlying work actually moving.

Can I reproduce your reported result myself? Given that 40 to 60 percent of cited sources change monthly, a result you can't reproduce is a result you shouldn't trust.

Can you show me two dated examples of citation-rate movement, not traffic? Traffic and rankings are the easy metrics to report, and they're the ones least connected to whether this specific work is actually doing anything.

What proportion of your effort goes to earned media versus on-page work? The strongest independent finding available right now points to third-party sources mattering more than your own pages.

What's your position on schema markup? Ahrefs found no citation lift and a small decline in AI Overviews. An agency that leads its pitch with schema work is worth a second look.

What are the contract term and exit terms? Watch for twelve-month minimums paired with vague deliverables in a category this unproven.

What would you tell us not to bother with? An agency that thinks everything in this category works is selling you something, not advising you.

Red Flags

A short list worth watching for. Any guarantee of specific AI citations or a guaranteed placement inside ChatGPT's answers, which is essentially the AEO version of a guaranteed number-one Google ranking and isn't something any agency actually controls, since the platforms decide independently.

Case studies that only show traffic numbers. A sample report that looks identical to a standard SEO dashboard with a new label slapped on it. The phrase "AI-ready" used as a buzzword with no specifics behind it. Twelve-month contract minimums offered before any kind of diagnostic. And a strategy that focuses only on your own domain, ignoring the third-party sources these AI models actually pull from.

The strongest independent finding available points to third-party authoritative sources mattering more than your own pages. If your site is already well optimized, more on-page work is probably the lowest-return option on the table. Look at PR-native and citation-focused agencies rather than technical SEO shops.

Your site is technically difficult to work with. What you need is technical depth: JavaScript rendering, crawl access, and site architecture work. If AI crawlers can't actually retrieve your content in the first place, nothing downstream matters. This is where enterprise technical agencies genuinely earn their premium pricing.

The website itself is the constraint. What you need is design and build capability alongside the search work. This happens to be our category, and we're naming it because it's a real constraint some companies genuinely have, not because we want it to be yours. If your site works fine already, hire someone else. Fix the publishing constraint first, before buying more optimization work that just queues up behind it.

You're not convinced any of this is real. What you need is a paid diagnostic, not a twelve-month contract. Given the evidence covered in this article, that's a genuinely reasonable position to hold. Buy three months of measurement and one audit, then decide from there. An agency unwilling to sell you a standalone diagnostic is telling you something worth listening to.

Here's the underlying principle. Every list of best AEO agencies is written by someone selling AEO, including this one. The only defensible thing an agency can actually offer isn't a ranking; it's a set of criteria you can apply yourself to any agency you're considering, including the one handing you the criteria.

The evidence in this category is thinner than the marketing around it suggests. Ask for the dashboard, lock the prompts, and stay a little suspicious of anyone who sounds completely certain.

Running The Search
