 Ancient Philosophy — Journal Profile
 Ancient Philosophy is a peer-reviewed academic journal devoted to the study of ancient Greek and Roman philosophy and science. Published since 1980, it appears twice a year, in spring and fall issues, and it is published by the Philosophy Documentation Center (PDC) in the United States on behalf of Mathesis Publications. It carries print ISSN 0740-2007 and online ISSN 2154-4689 — the pair confirmed by the publisher's descriptions, by OpenAlex, by SCImago-derived listings and by this record's own identity statements. The journal is edited by Ron Polansky (full name Ronald Polansky) in the Department of Philosophy at Duquesne University in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. In the publisher's own summary, the journal is "devoted to the publication of original articles, discussions and reviews in ancient Greek and Roman philosophy and science", and it "aims to contribute to philosophy by encouraging and enriching reflection on the paths laid down in antiquity".
 That combination — a single ancient field, covered in both its philosophical and its scientific aspects, by a specialist editor working from a university department and a scholarly publisher devoted to philosophy — has made Ancient Philosophy one of the standard specialist journals of classical philosophy for more than four decades. Reference sources record that it has published well over a thousand articles and reviews in its field since 1980; OpenAlex's index of the journal now holds 2,103 works. It is not a general classics journal and not a general philosophy journal: everything in it concerns Greek and Roman thought, from the Presocratics through late antiquity, together with the ancient sciences that grew inside that thought.

 Identity first: which journal this record is — and which it is not
 This record needs its disambiguation stated early, because the field contains confusingly named neighbours and the confusion is documented on this very site. Ancient Philosophy (this journal, ISSN 0740-2007 and 2154-4689, United States, founded 1980) is a different journal from Revista de Filosofia Antiga, the Brazilian open-access e-journal published by the Universidade de São Paulo since 2007 under electronic ISSN 1981-9471, which presents itself in English as the Journal of Ancient Philosophy. The São Paulo journal's own deep profile on this site says so explicitly, in a passage written precisely to prevent the mix-up: "Do not confuse this journal with Ancient Philosophy, the American journal founded in 1980, edited from Duquesne University and published by the Philosophy Documentation Center; its ISSNs, 0740-2007 and 2154-4689, are different, and a citation or a metric belonging to it does not belong here." That passage is also why this journal's twin-check flagged the São Paulo record: the shared ISSNs appear there as disambiguation prose only, in a profile whose own identity rests on a completely different ISSN. The two journals publish in overlapping subject territory — Greek and Roman philosophy — but they have different publishers, editors, languages of operation, access models and identifiers, and nothing belonging to one should ever be filed under the other. The same care applies to other near-names in the field, among them Apeiron (a De Gruyter journal for ancient philosophy and science, print ISSN 0003-6390) and the Brill book series Philosophia Antiqua, which is not a journal at all.
 History and character
 Ancient Philosophy was established in 1980, and its publishing arrangement has been stable in outline ever since: a journal owned in effect by its scholarly enterprise (Mathesis Publications) and produced and distributed by the Philosophy Documentation Center, the Charlottesville-based nonprofit that serves as philosophy's central bibliographic publisher, best known for the Philosopher's Index. The editorship sits not at the publisher but in a university department — Ron Polansky at Duquesne University in Pittsburgh — a pattern typical of humanities journals, where the editor is an active scholar in the field and the publisher supplies production, subscription and platform services. An American Philosophical Association journal survey of the title records the same arrangement from the journal's side: publisher Mathesis Publications, editor-in-chief Ronald Polansky, contact through the Department of Philosophy at Duquesne.
 The journal's rhythm is the traditional humanities one: two issues a year, spring and fall, carrying original articles, shorter discussions — the field's term for focused critical engagements with texts, arguments or other scholars' readings — and reviews. It has a printed version as well as its online edition, and its backfile runs from 1980 to the present. OpenAlex records its first indexed publication year as 1980 and its most recent as the current year, matching the founding date in reference sources, while the Scopus coverage stored in this site's record reads "1980, 1996–2025" — a pattern that describes the database's indexed holdings (a first year, then a continuous modern run), not interruptions in the journal itself, which has appeared continuously. Readers hunting an item from the 1980s or early 1990s should go to the journal's own archive and contents listings rather than infer absence from a database coverage line.
 One more feature of the journal's character deserves record, because it shapes what a submission should look like. Ancient Philosophy has never been a journal of trends: its pages track the permanent questions of its field — the interpretation of Plato's dialogues and Aristotle's treatises, the reconstruction of Presocratic and Hellenistic thought, the editing and understanding of fragmentary and commentary traditions — and a strong article in it is expected to remain useful for decades, not for a citation season. That is why the field's own measures of worth (the quality of the textual argument, the command of the scholarly literature in several languages, the durability of the reading offered) diverge so visibly from the two-year metrics tabulated below, and why experienced classicists advise younger scholars to judge this journal by its tables of contents and its review pages rather than by any single number.
 Scope: what the journal publishes
 The scope is stated in one sentence by the publisher — ancient Greek and Roman philosophy and science — and its two halves both matter. The philosophy half spans the whole ancient tradition: Presocratic thought, Socrates and Plato, Aristotle and the Hellenistic schools, Roman philosophy, and the commentators and late-antique traditions that transmitted them. The science half is what distinguishes the journal from a purely philosophical title: ancient mathematics, astronomy, medicine, natural philosophy and related disciplines are in scope as part of the same intellectual world, studied with the same textual and philosophical tools. OpenAlex's topic analysis of the journal's actual corpus reflects exactly that shape: classical philosophy and thought dominates overwhelmingly, with substantial clusters in classical antiquity studies, medieval philosophy and theology (the journal's material reaches into the reception and continuation of ancient thought), epistemology, ethics and metaphysics, and medieval and classical philosophy.
 The article forms are the field's standard three. Original articles present new readings, arguments or evidence on ancient texts and problems. Discussions take up a specific text, passage or scholarly claim at shorter length and with a sharper point. Reviews assess new editions, translations and monographs — a service of real weight in a field where a new critical edition or commentary can reset a whole debate. A submission that is really about medieval, modern or contemporary philosophy without its centre of gravity in the Greek and Roman material belongs elsewhere; a submission on ancient science, or on the ancient roots of a later debate, is squarely in scope.

 Reading these numbers correctly matters more than the numbers themselves. Ancient philosophy is a slow-citation field: its articles argue about texts that are two millennia old, they are cited over decades rather than months, and much of the field's scholarly traffic runs through books and commentaries that article-citation metrics do not count. A two-year mean citedness of 0.19 and an impact factor around 0.3–0.4 are therefore not marks of weakness; they are the field's normal range, shared by its respected titles. The signals that specialists actually weigh are the journal's category standing — Q1 in Classics in SCImago's listings — its presence in the field's bibliographic infrastructure, and its four decades of continuous publication under a named scholarly editor. Conversely, the gap between the SCImago h-index of 28 and the OpenAlex h-index of 58 is a corpus-size artefact of exactly the kind this site now expects: OpenAlex indexes a much longer and wider run of the journal (2,103 works) than the Scopus-derived count, so its h-index is correspondingly larger. Report the database with the figure, or do not report the figure.
 Indexing and discoverability
 The journal is indexed in Scopus, with the coverage pattern noted above, and it sits inside philosophy's own discovery infrastructure through its publisher: the Philosophy Documentation Center is the producer of the Philosopher's Index, and the journal's contents are listed through PDC's services and the journal's own site, whose tables of contents run from 1980 to the present. OpenAlex additionally records the journal in ERIH PLUS and in several national evaluation lists, including the Norwegian and Polish lists and Italy's ANVUR Class A — registrations that matter to European classicists and philosophers whose institutions score publications by such lists. It is not in the Directory of Open Access Journals: a DOAJ search by its print ISSN, repeated for this profile in November 2026, returned no record, as expected for a subscription title. JSTOR-style archive access and library holdings should be checked through the reader's own institution, since arrangements vary and are not stated in the sources verified here.
 Editorial leadership and the publisher
 The editor is Ron Polansky of Duquesne University, a scholar of Aristotle and Plato, and the journal's contact point of record is the Department of Philosophy at Duquesne in Pittsburgh. That single-editor model, verified in both the APA's journal survey and standard references, means submissions and enquiries go to a working specialist rather than to a publisher's editorial office, and it explains the journal's consistent character across four decades. The Philosophy Documentation Center, on behalf of Mathesis Publications, handles publication: PDC is a nonprofit scholarly publisher whose entire operation serves philosophy, which is why this journal, the Philosopher's Index and much of the discipline's bibliographic apparatus sit under one roof. The stored Journals World record previously showed the publisher only as a numeric code, "2968"; on the evidence above, the named publisher is the Philosophy Documentation Center, and that is the name that belongs in citations.
 Publishing model and costs
 Ancient Philosophy is a subscription journal with a printed version and an online edition (eISSN 2154-4689); it has no "online first" system according to the APA's survey, meaning articles appear in their assigned spring or fall issue rather than dribbling out ahead of print. OpenAlex lists no article processing charge for the journal and records it as not open access at journal level, and no APC figure is published in any source verified for this profile: authors should treat any third-party page that invents a fee for this journal with suspicion, and confirm current terms with the editor or PDC directly. In a field whose journals are traditionally subscription titles run by scholarly publishers, that profile — subscription, no published author fee, society-scale operation — is the norm rather than an exception.
 Guidance for prospective authors
 Fit is textual and philosophical at once. The journal publishes work on Greek and Roman philosophy and science that engages the ancient evidence directly — the texts, in Greek or Latin where the argument turns on them, the standard editions and commentaries, and the modern scholarly conversation. A paper that uses an ancient author as decoration for a contemporary argument will read as off-scope; a paper that settles a question about what an ancient text says, means or implies is the journal's home ground. Discussions offer a genuine shorter route for a focused correction or reading, and the reviews pages are the field's notice-board for new editions and books, though reviews are arranged through the editor rather than submitted on speculation.
 Practically: the journal appears in spring and fall issues and does not operate an online-first pipeline, per the APA survey, so authors should expect issue-based timing rather than immediate posting, and should address submissions and queries to the editor at Duquesne through the journal's own channels, confirming the current instructions on the journal site first. Because the editorship is a single named scholar, a brief enquiry about fit for an unusual submission — ancient science, reception material, a very long piece — is reasonable and in the field's tradition. Authors citing the journal should use the abbreviation Anc. Philos. and the ISSN pair above, and should keep its identity strictly separate from the São Paulo Journal of Ancient Philosophy (ISSN 1981-9471) discussed in the disambiguation section: misciting one journal as the other is a documented, avoidable error.
 Frequently asked questions
 What are the ISSNs of Ancient Philosophy? Print ISSN 0740-2007 and online ISSN 2154-4689.
 Who publishes it, and who edits it? The Philosophy Documentation Center (United States) publishes it on behalf of Mathesis Publications; it is edited by Ron Polansky at Duquesne University, Pittsburgh. The numeric publisher code in older versions of this record is not a name; the named publisher is the Philosophy Documentation Center.
 Is it the same as the Journal of Ancient Philosophy? No. That English name belongs to Revista de Filosofia Antiga (Universidade de São Paulo, eISSN 1981-9471), a Brazilian open-access journal founded in 2007. Ancient Philosophy is the American journal founded in 1980. The two are different journals with different ISSNs, as the São Paulo journal's own profile on this site also states.
 How often does it appear? Twice a year, in spring and fall issues, since 1980.
 What is its ranking? Q1 in Classics in SCImago's listings, with an SJR of 0.363 for 2024; its Journal Impact Factor is reported at 0.3 for 2024 and 0.38 for 2025 — figures in the normal range for ancient-philosophy scholarship, for the reasons explained above.
 Is Ancient Philosophy open access? No. It is a subscription journal in print and online, absent from the Directory of Open Access Journals, with no published article processing charge in the verified sources.
