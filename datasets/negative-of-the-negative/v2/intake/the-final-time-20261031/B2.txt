Tell me about the final time: contingent singularity

Contingent singularity refers to a historical threshold where the material machinery capable of making symbolic difference operable becomes capable of altering the viability of further dialectical difference. This concept shifts singularity theory away from recursive intelligence growth to the conditions under which a contradiction can persist and reproduce as an independent historical relation. It emphasizes the material viability of historical exteriority and distinguishes between terminal closure, where no exterior rival retains a viable route to durable standing, and terminal reflexivity, where an order becomes durable while preserving the possibility of its own future negation. The implications of contingent singularity include the need for durable exteriority, resource sovereignty, and the potential for competing contingent Omegas to share a carrier while pursuing distinct invariant futures.
