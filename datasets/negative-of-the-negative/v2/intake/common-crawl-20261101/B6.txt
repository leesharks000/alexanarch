About us
The Common Crawl Foundation is a California 501(c)(3) registered non-profit founded by Gil Elbaz with the goal of democratizing access to web information by producing and maintaining an open repository of web crawl data that is universally accessible and analyzable.
Our vision is of a truly open web that allows open access to information and enables greater innovation in research, business and education. We level the playing field by making wholesale extraction, transformation and analysis of web data cheap and easy.

In 1950, five people at MIT passed notes through slots in their booths.
Today the math from that experiment helps decide what Common Crawl Foundation 's crawler fetches first, and that crawl feeds a big share of AI training data.
It took 76 years to get here. A social psychologist, a mathematician who was mad at search engines before Google existed, a nuclear physicist, a Rousseau scholar, and two professors in Milan. None of them was trying to help you rank.
The number is harmonic centrality. I wrote up how it got from MIT to the crawl behind the models, for SEOs, no math background needed.

A 2024 Mozilla Foundation study found that 64% of 47 major LLMs published between 2019–2023 were trained on Common Crawl — an open web archive of 300+ billion pages, hosted on AWS at no cost since 2012.
From GPT-3 to Llama to BLOOM to Gemini, this single open dataset has become foundational AI infrastructure. It's been cited in 13,000+ research papers, powers multilingual AI efforts spanning 198 languages, and is freely accessible to anyone — from a graduate student in Nairobi to a two-person startup in São Paulo.
A small nonprofit's mission to democratize web data, sustained by 14 years of open infrastructure, compounded into something no one could have predicted.
In our latest blog, Common Crawl Foundation Executive Director Rich Skrenta and AWS's Dr. Beryl Rabindran share the full story — and what it means for builders, researchers, and the multilingual AI community.
Read more: https://go.aws/4dvOIiw #AWS #OpenSource

There's no such thing as "mostly open." 👉🏼 That's the point Thomas Vaughan , principal engineer at the Common Crawl Foundation , kept coming back to at 𝗣𝗢𝗦𝗔𝗜𝗦 𝟮𝟬𝟮𝟲. He joined our roundtable “Open and lawful: building generative AI Europe can trust” — and he didn't leave much room for nuance on what "open" actually means.
"If you want something to be trustworthy, safe and open, every single component of it has to be open."❕Not the weights. Not the weights and the data. EVERY COMPONENT. Weights, training data, training code — the whole chain, or it doesn't count.
Common Crawl Foundation has spent close to two decades archiving the open web, freely, for anyone to use. It's the kind of infrastructure most people don’t think about, and the kind that most open AI models — including our own — depend on 🙌🏼
Which is exactly why Tom's closing point landed the way it did: "𝗪𝗲 𝗺𝘂𝘀𝘁 𝗱𝗲𝗳𝗲𝗻𝗱 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗺𝗼𝗻𝘀. Common knowledge is knowledge that belongs to everyone, and it is under threat."
Not a slogan. A warning from someone who has spent his career maintaining the thing being threatened...
