[QUERENT] Negotiating Tech

[SYSTEM] Negotiating a tech offer means treating the total compensation package—not just the base salary—as a flexible starting point.

Core Rules of Tech Negotiation

• Don't say a number first: Avoid sharing your current compensation or salary expectations early in the process. Ask the recruiter for the role’s budgeted band instead.
• Buy time: Never accept or reject an offer on the initial phone call. Respond neutrally with excitement, then ask to review the details in writing.
• Negotiate via email: Putting your counter-proposal in writing helps you control the message, avoid pressure, and clearly list your rationales.
• Use data sources: Research real-world compensation bands using platforms like Levels.fyi (via Tech Interview Handbook) or H-1B salary databases rather than unverified general sites.
• Understand offers won't be rescinded: Major tech companies rarely revoke an offer just because you asked for more.

What to Negotiate Beyond Base Salary

Tech companies have different degrees of flexibility depending on the lever:
• Restricted Stock Units (RSUs) / Equity: Often has the most room for adjustment, especially at mid-to-senior levels where equity makes up the bulk of your total compensation.
• Sign-on Bonus: Easier for companies to approve than raising your permanent base salary. Use it to cover unvested equity or bonuses you are walking away from at your current job.
• Start Date: Time your start date to coincide with your current employer’s next stock vesting cliff to avoid leaving money on the table ("double-dipping").
• Level / Title: If you are operating at a higher scope, push for an uplevel (though this requires strong evidence or a 30-60-90 day plan).
• Non-monetary perks: Remote work flexibility, extra PTO days, or relocation assistance.

Leverage & Strategy

• Competing offers: Having another active offer is your strongest piece of leverage. Be transparent with recruiters about your interview timelines to force alignment.
• The "Reason" rule: Always back up your counter-offer with a logical rationale (e.g., total compensation alignment with market data, a competing offer, or increased responsibilities).
• Build internal advocates: Speaking with the hiring manager or skip-level manager to discuss your vision for the role can turn them into internal champions who advocate for a better offer on your behalf.
