Anne Carson is a acclaimed Canadian poet, essayist, classicist, and translator who won the 20th/2026 Nobel Prize in Literature for her "bold and inventive oeuvre that, in playful dialogue with the classical tradition, has created new forms for contemporary literature". Born in Toronto in 1950, she is celebrated for genre-defying works that blend poetry, prose, scholarship, and modern feminist perspectives with ancient Greek and Roman literature.

Background & Career
Early Life and Education: Born on June 21, 1950, in Toronto, Canada. She learned ancient Greek in high school with the help of an instructor and earned her BA, MA, and PhD from the University of Toronto, alongside studying at the University of St Andrews.

Academic Career: Has worked as a professor of Classics, Comparative Literature, and Creative Writing at prominent institutions including Princeton University, McGill University, and New York University.
Literary Style: Known for unorthodox, hybrid structures that cross the boundaries between lyric poetry, academic essay, visual art, and translation.
