Cave Of Hira And The First Quranic RevelationThe first revelation in Islam occurred in 610 CE when the angel Gabriel (Jibreel) appeared to Muhammad (around age 40) in the Cave of Hira on Mount al-Nour near Mecca and revealed the opening five verses of Surah Al-Alaq (Quran 96:1–5).

Setting: Muhammad had been spending periods of solitary meditation and worship in the Cave of Hira.

The Revelation: After embracing Muhammad tightly and repeating the command three times, Gabriel conveyed the first verses of Surah Al-Alaq, beginning with "Read in the name of your Lord who created" and highlighting creation, knowledge, and teaching by the pen.

Aftermath and Significance
Comfort and Verification: Shaken and overwhelmed, Muhammad returned home to his wife, Khadijah, who comforted him. They visited her cousin Waraqah ibn Nawfal, who identified the visitor as the angel sent to earlier prophets such as Moses and foretold Muhammad's prophethood and upcoming challenges.

Timing: Traditional accounts place the event during Ramadan (often linked to Laylat al-Qadr, the Night of Power), with scholars offering varying estimates for the exact date in 610 CE (such as August 10, 610 CE, or December 610 CE), while some Shia traditions associate the start of prophethood (Mab'ath) with 27 Rajab.
