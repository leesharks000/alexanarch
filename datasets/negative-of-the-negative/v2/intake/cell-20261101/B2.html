About CELL

An international, open-access collaboration providing centralized access to electronic literature databases, archives, and institutional collections worldwide.

The Index & Wikibase

The centerpiece of CELL is The Index: a curated, peer-reviewed catalogue bringing together records from independent electronic literature databases in one Wikibase. It provides open, machine-readable access to their collections, while contributions to Wikidata extend this shared knowledge beyond the electronic literature community.

Multilingual Inclusivity

By federating databases with distinct linguistic, thematic, and geographical focuses, CELL supports a more inclusive approach to electronic literature research. Wikibase and Wikidata’s multilingual infrastructure help researchers discover and connect works across languages, collections, and research traditions.

Scholarly Community

CELL supports active scholarly exchange via a quarterly newsletter, conference sessions at the Electronic Literature Organization, shared ontologies, and hosted Wikibase environments for emerging research groups.

INSTITUTIONAL STEWARDSHIP

Hosted at the Center for Digital Narrative

CELL is currently hosted and directed by the Center for Digital Narrative (CDN), a Norwegian Centre of Excellence at the University of Bergen, Norway. The consortium operates in direct governance with representatives from all federated member databases and in close partnership with the Electronic Literature Organization (ELO).

HISTORICAL CONTEXT

Milestones

2009–2010

Inception & Founding Assembly

Initiated by the Board of the Electronic Literature Organization (ELO). The founding meeting in Sydney, Australia was convened by Anna Gibbs and Maria Angel through the “Creative Nation” grant from the Australian Academy of the Humanities.

2012–2013

The CELL Element Set

Consortium partners established an international metadata standard for creative works of e-lit, codifying a canonical minimum description for born-digital literature across differing institutional architectures.

2015

First Cross-Database Search Engine

NT2 Lab at Université du Québec à Montréal (Sylvain Aubé & Robin Varenas) and Whisky Echo Bravo engineered the initial cross-search tool, aggregating records from distributed member repositories.

Ongoing

Stewardship at Center for Digital Narrative

CELL transitioned to the stewardship of the University of Bergen in Norway, hosted at the Center for Digital Narrative (CDN). The Index was rebuilt on modern Wikibase infrastructure with real-time Wikidata federation.

History

CELL was initiated in 2009 by the Board of the Electronic Literature Organization (ELO) to facilitate the coordination of international projects in the emerging field of electronic literary arts and scholarship. The vigorous database-building practices in the field of electronic literature have been key in establishing the field, while demonstrating the diversity of electronic literature across cultures and perspectives. The ELO recognizes the contribution of Anna Gibbs and Maria Angel for convening the initial CELL participants at a founding meeting in 2010, supported by a grant entitled “Creative Nation,” from the International Science Linkages Program of the Australian Academy of the Humanities, Sydney, Australia. This was not an isolated moment, as conversations about collaboration started before and continued after. During the 2012-13 academic year, the consortium established a metadata standard for creative works of e-lit (“The CELL Element Set”). The document emerged from discussion of the existing standards of member projects, identifying a canonical minimum metadata description for a creative work of e-lit. Robin Varenas and Sylvain Aubé at the NT2 lab in Montreal were responsible for the technical development of the search tool, in collaboration with Whisky Echo Bravo. The search tool that went live in 2015 used partial data from several CELL members to enable cross-database search.

Founding director Joseph Tabbi wrote a Manifesto for CELL. Davin Heckman was the long term managing director since 2017, leading many vital discussions over the years and applying for grants. Discussions about the future of CELL took place at ELO in seminars dedicated to CELL, especially about the establishment of name authority. After multiple years of inactivity due to a lack of resources, CELL was transferred to the stewardship of the University of Bergen, Norway, now home of its founding director Joseph Tabbi.

CELL project has been supported financially over the years through an array of sources: NEH Digital Humanities Start-up grant; Programme Québec-É tats-Unis by the Ministère des Relations internationales, de la Francophonie et du Commerce extérieur du Québec; Center for Literary Computing at West Virginia University, including a 2014 PSCoR Grant; the Electronic Literature Organization; the University of Bergen; and the University of Siegen. All members of the Consortium contribute to the overall success of the project.

Institutional & Grant Support

Over the years, CELL has been sustained through generous contributions and competitive grants: NEH Digital Humanities Start-up Grant; Programme Québec-États-Unis (Ministère des Relations internationales, de la Francophonie et du Commerce extérieur du Québec); Center for Literary Computing at West Virginia University (including 2014 PSCoR); the Electronic Literature Organization; the University of Siegen; and the University of Bergen.
