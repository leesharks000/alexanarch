We Finally Know What the Interstellar Object Crossing the Solar System Really Is — and It’s Unique

The interstellar object 3I/Atlas has just reappeared from behind our Sun. And some people believe they’ve spotted signs of an explosion in the latest images released by astronomers. But what’s drawing even more attention now is a radio signal detected by researchers…
Discovered last July, 3I/Atlas has been making headlines ever since. First because it was confirmed to be of interstellar origin. Then because it displayed several surprising characteristics. So surprising, in fact, that some — including Avi Loeb , the well-known astrophysicist at Harvard University — suggested it might even be an extraterrestrial spacecraft.
At the end of last month, the comet — because astronomers recently confirmed its true nature — disappeared behind our Sun. That disappearance sparked plenty of speculation. Then it reappeared after passing its closest point to our star, the perihelion, on October 29. And once again… nothing particularly unusual seemed to have happened. Which was almost surprising in itself.

So Avi Loeb examined the post-perihelion images. By analyzing the jets visible in those images, he estimated the amount of material the comet ejected — and concluded that it must have broken apart. According to him, astronomers should now look for at least sixteen fragments . And if they fail to find them, that could only mean one thing: 3I/Atlas is actually an extraterrestrial spacecraft. Most other researchers firmly disagree. They maintain that the comet is doing just fine — and that nothing challenges its entirely natural origin.

The radio signal astronomers were waiting for
But what about the other piece of information revealed over the past few days? Astronomers using the MeerKAT radio telescope in South Africa have detected a signal coming from 3I/Atlas. A radio signal that Avi Loeb and his followers might be tempted to interpret as one emitted by a huge craft from beyond our Solar System.
Once again, astronomers explain the observation quite differently. To understand it, you need to know that when water molecules (H₂O) are expelled from a comet, they break down into hydrogen atoms (H) and hydroxyl radicals (OH) that remain in its coma. These hydroxyl radicals tend to absorb radio waves at two specific frequencies: 1665 and 1667 megahertz . In The Astronomer’s Telegram , astronomers report that they detected a signal from 3I/Atlas precisely at those frequencies — just five days before its perihelion passage.

3I/Atlas, a comet with much left to reveal
This radio signal is therefore not surprising for a comet approaching the Sun — especially one that previous studies have shown to be losing large quantities of water.
So even if Avi Loeb still refuses to accept it, everything points to 3I/Atlas being of natural origin. That in no way diminishes the scientific value of this visitor. It is, after all, only the third interstellar object astronomers have ever been able to study. And it may yet reveal crucial information about the planetary system it came from.
