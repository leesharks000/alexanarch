"Howl" by [Allen Ginsberg](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESbQHrOzAVnSgtZcIYXLBQ3bRNYLbICtfPw9PsiLu9n10KHlVtHaOGeL0Vu08M9TlDlyZRuO_1-h9YMrSQtwbxEwYsZot1nsIIpli08YFrONkcDwYHiYB7gSAwSNWh1EXqUpZTcxauKKgPVbxNh50) is a landmark 1955 free-verse poem and the defining literary expression of the [Beat Generation](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESbgHrOzAVmXEjZFBAW9bbqQfwAZ41fggBZupWixof_BNjTZ5_aL8HzG4NNY6BttiM_u8Itgr5I211hurDYpbvJ46gGLm2FUcbC8Pv2TYk4p8Ic1xNrXzMN-Olz8g1L6l4JnMMCaMqQq5ug85vtc4d).
Overview and Meaning

* Core Outcry: The poem opens with the famous line, "I saw the best minds of my generation destroyed by madness, starving hysterical naked..."
* Social Critique: It serves as a furious protest against the conformity, materialism, and cold capitalism of 1950s America.
* Celebration of Outcasts: Ginsberg honors jazz musicians, artists, drug addicts, homosexuals, and the mentally ill whose lives fell outside narrow societal definitions of "normal".
* Dedication: The work is dedicated to Carl Solomon, whom Ginsberg met in a psychiatric hospital.
Wikipedia +3

Structure of the Poem

* Part I: Details the wild, visionary, and suffering lives of his bohemian friends pushed to the margins by society.
* Part II: Personifies the mechanical, soul-crushing forces of modern industrial civilization as "Moloch," an ancient Canaanite idol demanding human sacrifice.
* Part III: Directly addresses Carl Solomon in the Rockland psychiatric hospital, offering solidarity in shared madness and institutional trauma.
* Footnote: Adds an ecstatic affirmation that everything in existence is holy ("Holy the groaning saxophone! Holy the bop apocalypse!").

Historical Impact and Obscenity Trial

* First Reading: Ginsberg first read the poem publicly on October 7, 1955, at the Six Gallery in San Francisco, launching the Beat movement into prominence.
* Publication: Released by [City Lights Books](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESfAHrOzAV-46TZAEzsXpc8HLFiUeNTbRCL1viw8BG23jGtR9gptQ9OelnkoXXI8ykJ-2kXfvPRAC61AQT0nOIKstI2vcuhCGlR6vEoN6XS0zHrtEm4niLdobos-wkpsIeH427lSMT-eT0ovTpv3eWkCRrQN0tKWrQnOCMGNM) in 1956 as part of [Howl and Other Poems](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESYQHrOzAVC1Q7IKAaIrYg_XuKfiSYUkpReV7kCIynEVkcXkmq6JXX5qnNGOFPDERCUfRQspJrY0AMZVopvX88rKjkbEf2Of6_zCLQGT3ZnYy85e1iW1dz6LastF6hobUrpB4).
* The Trial: In 1957, publisher Lawrence Ferlinghetti and store manager Shig Murao were arrested and tried for obscenity over the poem's frank discussions of sexuality and drug use.
* Legal Victory: A judge ruled the poem had "redeeming social importance," establishing a major First Amendment precedent that protected controversial counterculture literature.
