[QUERENT] Canaries in the Coal Mine

[SYSTEM] A "canary in a coal mine" is an early warning sign or indicator of hidden danger, trouble, or failure. [[1](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESYwHrOzAVEqRzLnZhV5mcWA7F_hu8yR8AcWPyxWvFaKw3jfarHSB8vnYaKGaK6j36zRikDdgt_7KM9X1QbNsZaMLy8KX2Li7tH5GCczZ4JahVHGbZKis6QUBGipyTJFWarekt9Q), [2](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESgwEB6zswFYyvGx7Cx76Ua6EORs_JuhC-pluixBm4SEb_W34t08bvCwadkdTVL8XyPUouZozV9gAybf8JcTTKmlZbSWKejiP-joAvKTtlNzzYSfR3y4McBwdXBTYVyNFkGoHI84lhlCFEmtJkPKmtILoyvRmOUHcxLItE9jfJqUABzU1N6g)]
Historical Origin

* Toxic gas detection: In the late 19th and 20th centuries, coal miners brought caged canaries into underground tunnels. [[1](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESYgHrOzAVH1drH5NI4jXNCYpOPyKxfv1305tVWQ86XWSovAFqRfU2V8S85Kbxq3Y1UhfLBMkMzEX1dsL-qNm79T9oMWjw1GtXVXutW8gLkicuZeV3aK4fztuccKcsmHN0taTv), [2](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESYwHrOzAVa_5SekDXQbkaCZ8gmIC4BtGeH3ADFaiopOiykFS4cyJtZXmmT6FFn7kvoSgOMG2LmFIaJXaKOjJ5ZehBnpms5iknHbqaHx5JlP6anx4QwbnDjnHG6Dxd1ln6Tr2OYw)]
* Physiology: Canaries have a rapid breathing rate and high metabolism, making them much more sensitive to lethal, odorless gases like carbon monoxide and methane than humans. [[1](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESlQEB6zswFSO5H75EU10vINs9SOmfnxiaGnrN_H3H4Z4DkU39fNQYFj0FEoMOKoEDKoSYteMEFaPB5qi0mY5Sn36b6tCUfUNPKNREcQUGBmXD8D5rTd9lc6pP12KHq7grmiuglVJE0zMGK2-35Iul72rBV24Nb0QY0C57oVYZGR_K2eCAkLKJmtRxUUM1DXxx2gPyXJ1S6A), [2](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAES2AEB6zswFcFG8Oa3_RCjdVIHPuTOJ8mwMQLAnWielJU9GovMM5khTG3tNwWqDPfdMkesFgPui4ggrEUxa8D03kfIAWTQNJo6pCniVKc5Ud_hhqllV8daNccEeeBkm6jy0Z9lad5DiwRfMl-wvzy4tSH0bhDTsg4cuECHOUuNUE6QaYyQnGePhWldSCP74MrFzMA6oZnyWGoNZMeJVNMW-EDStZG_sf1KhnRToqf7Vr1wVL5FUnq3_YUru7d4eFcod1qtteGP4lXBo7LEIg597JfqDlk60Ni2x2I)]
* The warning signal: As long as the bird sang and acted normally, the air was safe. If the canary grew distressed, fell silent, or collapsed, it signaled that poisonous gas was building up and miners needed to escape. [[1](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESaAHrOzAVTOpgmdrF8RpGKlFLXDfKJ023XeBlxSYaHGsLb5N_KtiDD_h0IMyn0uZNSMAH_--vKuqO9RcgMBkW3a2BcjMkuuIC7KWZNx8lqVWWhENFHOXscc8VtTmlV3mL9raewBBfrbcW), [2](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESogEB6zswFT9qJXl1mov9iH0FSD06go8edvUa1sPoh-j4oPaiZnr8gOVUZ_aA-edkzAQNg1AMRQQOqriZrjBpJ8HOfCDIwMuH5BPWKzxKCYQc-ut0AogTcTe8PvrxyGTJZy4BSPY6iNdbcQVXWS71b1AceLAVjFtqOs1Z4PxeYRg_-7QBKQcnTz3rv9OjeH9FFkwLs6PUzt9_jI-dfwqlkk92m6U), [3](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESYgHrOzAVH1drH5NI4jXNCYpOPyKxfv1305tVWQ86XWSovAFqRfU2V8S85Kbxq3Y1UhfLBMkMzEX1dsL-qNm79T9oMWjw1GtXVXutW8gLkicuZeV3aK4fztuccKcsmHN0taTv)]
* Replacement: The practice continued until electronic gas detectors replaced live birds in the late 20th century (around 1996).
