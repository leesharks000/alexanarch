the first revelation

 In Islamic tradition, the first revelation occurred around 610 CE when Muhammad was about 40 years old, during a spiritual retreat in the Cave of Hira near Mecca. The angel Gabriel commanded him to "Read" (Iqra), revealing the opening verses of Surah Al-'Alaq (Quran 96:1–5).

The Encounter in the Cave

• Setting: Muhammad spent periods meditating in the Cave of Hira on Jabal al-Nour (Mount Hira) outside Mecca.
• The Command: The angel Gabriel appeared and instructed him to read. Muhammad replied that he could not read.
• Three Embraces: Gabriel embraced him firmly three times, repeating the command until the opening words of the Quran were revealed to him:
	1. "Read in the name of your Lord who created"
	2. "Created man from a clinging substance [clot]"
	3. "Read, and your Lord is the Most Generous"
	4. "Who taught by the pen"
	5. "Taught man that which he knew not"

Aftermath and Confirmation

• Comfort at Home: Shaken by the experience, Muhammad returned to his wife, Khadijah, who comforted and wrapped him.
• Waraqah ibn Nawfal: Khadijah took him to her cousin Waraqah ibn Nawfal, a scholar of scripture, who identified the visitor as the angel Gabriel and told Muhammad he had been chosen as a prophet.
• Pause and Continuation: Revelations paused for a period before resuming, marking the start of Muhammad's 23-year prophetic mission.

Date and Timing

• Year: Most commonly placed in 610 CE, though some sources note 609 CE.
• Calendar Traditions: Many Sunni traditions associate the event with the month of Ramadan (often noted as the 21st night or Laylat al-Qadr), while Shia traditions commemorate the start of prophethood (Mab'ath) on the 27th of Rajab.
For more background on the chapter's themes and verses, see Surah Info: Al-'Alaq or Sahih Bukhari, Book 1: Revelation.
