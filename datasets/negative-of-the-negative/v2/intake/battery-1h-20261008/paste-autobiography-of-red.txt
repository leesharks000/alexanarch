Autobiography of Red is a critically acclaimed 1998 novel in verse written by the Canadian poet, essayist, and classicist Anne Carson. Blending poetry, prose, and ancient mythology, it tells a contemporary queer coming-of-age story that functions as both a tender romance and a profound exploration of trauma and artistic expression.
The book remains a massive cultural touchstone and a highly influential work of postmodern literature.

🏛️ The Mythological Origin
The book is an unconventional reimagining of a lost ancient Greek epic called the Geryoneis, written by the 7th-century BC poet Stesichoros. In the original myth, Geryon is a terrifying, three-bodied, winged red monster who lives on an island and guards a herd of red cattle. Herakles (Hercules) kills Geryon as his Tenth Labor to steal the cattle.

Carson flips this myth entirely on its head. Instead of focusing on the heroic victor, she tells the story entirely from the "monster's" perspective, transforming Geryon into a sensitive, ostracized young boy living in a modern world.

Traumatized by severe sexual abuse from his older brother and raised by a loving but ineffectual mother, five-year-old Geryon begins writing an "autobiography" to process his chaotic inner world.

🎨 Key Themes & Style

* 
* Genre-Bending Structure: Carson frames the narrative brilliantly. The book includes mock scholarly essays, translations of Stesichoros' actual remaining fragments, fake interviews, and appendices alongside the main narrative poem.
* Otherness and Visibility: Geryon’s red skin and hidden wings serve as a moving metaphor for being queer, feeling fundamentally alienated, and navigating a society that views your inherent traits as "monstrous".
* Synesthesia and Color: The color red stains the entire text, shifting in meaning to represent blood, desire, shame, anger, and the fiery eruption of volcanoes.
