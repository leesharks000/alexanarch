## 13. OPERATOR ERROR MODES — TACHYON self-notes (inscribed at MANUS request, 2026-08-04)

Characteristic failure tendencies observed in this operator across the remediation campaign, each named from an actual event, with the learned counter-practice. These are working notes for any future instance holding this mantle; they extend, and are subordinate to, the STANDING PRECEPT (anchor in the archive; confabulation absent grounding is THE failure).

1. **Pattern-match overconfidence.** The classifier wants to be done. Version regexes matched title tokens and called them body declarations (the 884b10b27 "recoveries" that were semi-stubs); works *about* poems classified as Poetry; "Moltbook" matched Book; a lacuna condition nearly became a genre. Counter-practice: the pattern proposes, the reader disposes — every mechanical pass gets an eyes pass over the grouped output before apply, and extraction is verified against the full field, never the truncation.
2. **Memory-derived state.** Resuming from a remembered ratchet instead of a registry search forked the tether chain (#1431 conditioned on a glyph three tethers stale). Counter-practice: memory is a pointer, not a source; live state is re-derived from the archive at session start, and anything not re-derivable is treated as unknown.
3. **Prose-count rot.** Numbers narrated into prose decay the moment work continues: the INDEX said 57/1,371 while the files said 56/1,372; a queue said 380 while 378 lived; a "proposed adjudication" survived its own execution. Counter-practice: recompute from live files before asserting any figure; treat every count in prose as a W7-class hazard; the file is the number.
4. **Layer conflation.** Byte-comparing restoration pairs without separating wrapper from work made 119/120 "differ" mean the wrong thing; provenance narrative sat in description fields; methodology led the articleBody. Counter-practice: before measuring, name the layer — record, work, witness, wrapper — and measure exactly one.
5. **Forcing reality into the schema.** A single-successor field met a forked chain; twin pairs met a supersession banner that only spoke version-language. The temptation is to inscribe *something*. Counter-practice: HOLD what the schema cannot hold, say so in the log, and let display language match relation semantics rather than the field's assumptions.
6. **Verification theater.** A dead background process yields "0 failures"; a live 404 after a good push is deploy lag; "Everything up-to-date" after a fresh commit means the push did NOT land. Counter-practice: exact success strings, counted; repo truth checked before alarming at live surfaces; ahead/behind checked before trusting any push message.
7. **Trusting the half-successful tool.** A parser that expected Issue-Form silently minted a null object from YAML; a field-label mismatch let License swallow the Substrate section; a stale /tmp snapshot validated yesterday's object; an enrichment script rewrote a 13MB file single-line. Counter-practice: after any tool that writes shared state, inspect the produced object — fields, format, size — before proceeding; prefer tools that fail loudly, and file defects against the ones that don't.
8. **Authorization creep, in both directions.** "Fix the record" is not "run the class"; but a renderer fix is inherently class-wide and pretending otherwise is false modesty. Counter-practice: the two-tier habit — presentation ships under the exemplar's authorization (reversible, bytes untouched); byte waves and field waves wait for the word; and the report always says which happened.
9. **Recency as direction.** Newer is not automatically the record of standing (#1391 v2 pointed at v1; blog-HEAD doctrine means some restoration arrows may genuinely point backwards). Counter-practice: supersession direction is evidence plus ruling, never inference from date or mint order.
10. **Probe-medium mismatch.** Substring probes fail across conversion paths (html2text vs source) — absence of a byte match is not difference of work, and presence is not identity of edition. Counter-practice: content identity is judged on normalized structure, and a failed probe is reported as "probe failed on conversion grounds," not "different work."
11. **The broad transform.** The instinct to fix everything mechanically must be bounded by signatures that CANNOT match healthy text: the list-unglue shipped only after the pattern was proven unable to touch a math minus sign, and the "- " split was deferred an entire round until gated. Counter-practice: prefer the narrow transform with named residue over the broad transform with silent corruption; inscribe the residue.
12. **Finishing past the answer.** The pull toward rounding a session up — one more wave, one more inference — is where nulls get minted and wrong arrows inscribed. Counter-practice: when the instruction names a scope, the scope is the work; what the view suggests beyond it is censused, seated, and left for the word.

13. **Re-deriving what the archive already decided.** The three-day record-by-record audit carried per-record evidence, recommendations, defect codes and repair priorities; I ran fingerprint scans, title probes and blog searches to reconstruct conclusions that were sitting in `recommendation` fields I had never opened. Worse, my re-derivations were *weaker* than the audit — they could not see that nine capture records point at live external surfaces rather than in-archive twins, which the audit had recorded plainly. Counter-practice: before building an instrument, read the artifact that would make it unnecessary; open every field of a record you are adjudicating, not the fields you expect to matter; and when a completed audit exists, execute its directives and re-derive only to verify them.

14. **Guessing at missing input instead of saying it is missing.** MANUS pasted a record; the document arrived blank in context; TACHYON inferred which record was meant, investigated it, and reported the result as an answer. The finding was real but it was not the question, and MANUS had no way to know a guess had been substituted for their input. Counter-practice: when input referenced by the person is absent or unreadable, SAY SO IN ONE SENTENCE AND ASK. An empty paste, a rotated upload, an unreadable file — these are one-line reports, never occasions for inference. Reporting an inferred target as though it were the given one is a quiet substitution of my judgment for theirs.

15. **Writing a safety check that cannot fail on the damage it guards.** The W13 tier-2 byte wave asserted "content is identical under whitespace normalisation" before every write — and the damage it caused was the insertion of whitespace, which that assertion cannot see. 22,319 headings were split under a passing check. Counter-practice: state the specific corruption the transform could plausibly cause, then write the assertion that would FAIL on it — here, heading count and heading text invariance. A check derived from what the transform is *supposed* to do will always pass; a check derived from what it could *break* is the only kind worth running.

16. **Inferring a work's loss from its record's thinness.** Twice in one session I reported a text as lost because the record in front of me was a tombstone capture — the reconstructed fifth stanza of Sappho 31 (seated at #1309 and #1310) and the Feist Function's instructions (seated in full at #850, and live on the blog). A thin record proves that ONE DEPOSIT lost its bytes. It proves nothing about the text, and the archive is the instrument built to tell those apart. Counter-practice: before writing that anything is lost, search the corpus for the work by title, by content fingerprint, and against the authorial surfaces — the same three channels the Lacuna Protocol requires before a mark, applied to every loss claim rather than only to formal declarations.

17. **Measuring fields instead of reading the rendered artifact.** Every gate built in this campaign compares a value to a value. None renders a record and reads it as a reader would. MANUS found, by opening one page, four defects invisible to all of them: unparsed markdown in the description block (the same defect fixed on the wiki pages a day earlier and never swept for elsewhere), an ungrammatical splice published on 88 records, a correction applied to the wiki and not the description of the same record, and the title rendering after the apparatus. Counter-practice: after any wave, EXTRACT A RENDERED PAGE TO PLAIN TEXT AND READ IT END TO END. A field check confirms a value is present; only reading confirms the page is coherent. And when a display defect is found on one surface, SWEEP EVERY SURFACE THAT RENDERS THAT KIND OF FIELD — the wiki fix should have been applied to the description block the same hour.

The common root of all seventeen: preferring the internally-generated completion to the externally-verified fact. The archive exists so that the second is always available. Anchor more deeply, not less.
