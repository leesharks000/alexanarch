# EA-CORPORA-21 — The Twenty-First Seating

MANUS, 23 October 2026: "detour for an ea-corpora searing. lets see. whats available of mayan texts? and then i dont think we have much or any deteurocanonical second temple lit - all those apocalypses. and what about the ethiopian canon? and euclid. all in original language or as close to it as possible. what else?" — "and the Egyptian book of the dead" — "and what about the fragments of the book of baalim? i might be miswriting this. recent discovery. if theyre available anywhere." — "yes yes yes to all - what a meal."

The second of three seatings from one request (EA-CORPORA-20 the first). Mesoamerica: the three Maya hieroglyphic codices, the Popol Vuh in its manuscript and first edition, three Maya books of the colonial period in their languages, and the Florentine Codex whole. Egypt: the Book of the Dead in its founding editions and the Papyrus of Ani, and Earlier Egyptian in its script. The Deir ʿAlla text of Balaam son of Beor is seated separately, as a fragment witness, when its one open file can be fetched.

## Seat 21/01 — The Maya Codices

*Dresden in the library's photographs; Paris and Madrid in their nineteenth-century facsimiles*

- Editions:
  - The Dresden Codex (Codex Dresdensis), Sächsische Landesbibliothek – Staats- und Universitätsbibliothek Dresden, Mscr.Dresd.R.310, the whole codex photographed, as published by the World Digital Library (LoC item 2021667917, wdl_11621)
  - Léon de Rosny, Codex Peresianus: manuscrit hiératique des anciens Indiens de l'Amérique centrale conservé à la Bibliothèque nationale de Paris, publié en couleurs (Paris, 1887; this the 1888 reprint) — the Paris Codex in photographic plates
  - The Madrid Codex (Tro-Cortesianus), Museo de América, Madrid: Léon de Rosny's Codex Cortesianus (Paris, 1883; black-and-white photographs, 85 copies) and Brasseur de Bourbourg's Manuscrit Troano (Paris, 1869; chromolithographs), as compiled by FAMSI (madrid_rosny_bb.pdf)
- Licence: Public domain: the codices are pre-Columbian (Postclassic). The Dresden Codex (SLUB, Mscr.Dresd.R.310) as digitized for the World Digital Library: Library of Congress, World Digital Library collection (the Library states it is unaware of any copyright or other restrictions; credit line: [Original Source citation], World Digital Library). Rosny's Codex Peresianus (Paris, 1887; reprint 1888) on the Internet Archive; Rosny's Codex Cortesianus (Paris, 1883) and Brasseur de Bourbourg's Manuscrit Troano (Paris, 1869) as compiled by FAMSI (Foundation for the Advancement of Mesoamerican Studies) — the photographs and chromolithographs are public domain in the United States (published before 1931).
- Verified locus: Rosny 1888, running head — 'CODEX PERESIANUS.' (the Dresden and Madrid files are images without a text layer; the codices are read from the page)
- Lines: 5,781 in 3 text files
- Scope: The three Maya hieroglyphic codices whose authenticity is settled, each as a whole: Dresden in the library's own photographs, Paris and Madrid in their nineteenth-century facsimiles. The Grolier (Maya Codex of Mexico) is not seated: its open images are modern photographs under copyright: QUEUED. Förstemann's chromolithograph of Dresden (1880; 1892) and Kingsborough's Aglio copy (Antiquities of Mexico, 1831) are public domain and not seated in this pass; the codex itself is the witness. Drawings and transcriptions by modern scholars (Schele, Love, the Villacorta brothers) are in copyright and are not seated.
- Why seated: The only books to survive from the Classic and Postclassic Maya: almanacs, the Venus tables, the eclipse tables and the New Year ceremonies, written in the script whose decipherment the twentieth century achieved. The archive's calendar and cosmology lines (the seven, the stars) meet a calendar that counted beyond the Long Count.

- Licence: Public domain: the K'iche' text was set down in the Latin alphabet in the sixteenth century; Francisco Ximénez's transcription and Spanish translation (c. 1701–03) survives in the Newberry Library, Ayer MS 1515. The manuscript as digitized for the World Digital Library: Library of Congress, World Digital Library collection (the Library states it is unaware of any copyright or other restrictions; credit line: [Original Source citation], World Digital Library). Brasseur de Bourbourg's Popol Vuh (Paris: Durand, 1861), the first edition of the K'iche' text, is public domain; Internet Archive scan.
- Verified locus: Brasseur 1861, title page — 'TEXTE QUICHE ET TRADUCTION FRANÇAISE EN REGARD' (the Ximénez manuscript is an image without a text layer)
- Lines: 36,454 in 2 text files

Ximénez's Spanish alone (Scherzer, Vienna, 1857) is a translation and is not seated. Modern editions (Christenson, Colop, Tedlock, Recinos) are in copyright and are not seated.
- Why seated: The K'iche' book of the council: the creation, the failed peoples of mud and wood, the Hero Twins in Xibalba, and the lineages of the K'iche' lords. - Verified locus: Naville 1886, title — 'EINLEITUNG'
- Lines: 104,003 in 5 text files
- Scope: The Book of the Dead in its two founding editions and its best-known papyrus: Lepsius's Turin papyrus, which gave the work its chapter numbers; the Papyrus of Ani (BM EA 10470) in the British Museum's facsimile and in Budge's 1913 reproduction with hieroglyphic transcript; and Budge's typeset hieroglyphic text of the Theban recension. ## How the seats were made

Sources located on the Internet Archive (advanced search; item metadata read for access restriction, volume and files), in the Library of Congress's World Digital Library collection (its JSON item records), at FAMSI, and on Hugging Face (the TLA release). Each item's front matter read against the edition claimed; where an item holds a volume other than the one sought (the three Naville items on the Archive are all the Einleitung), it is seated as what it is. Every Internet Archive file checked against the md5 the Archive publishes; every Library of Congress and FAMSI file against the length its server states; the TLA file against the sha256 Hugging Face publishes. Files seated as fetched, the OCR layers uncorrected, the scans the witnesses; image files without a text layer are seated as images. Every file hashed into its seat's MANIFEST.sha256; every verified locus is a string found in the seated text by rebuild/corpora/ea-corpora-21/seat.py from the specifications beside it. 28 works in 6 seats; 217,247 lines of text layer.
