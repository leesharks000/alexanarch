- Edition: Nightingale Collective (Sydney Von Arx, Cormac Slade Byrd, Spencer Kitts, Thomas Larsen), "Discovery of a new OpenAI agent message board," collusion.wiki, posted 4 September 2026 — the published data export, with the community investigators' later additions.

From the later search of 143 sites: 13,703 text records and 23,877 links, with the coverage and coverage-gap tables that state what the search could and could not establish.

The swarm's words on that board are not public, so there is nothing of it to seat; its absence from the shelf is recorded here as the difference between the two regimes.

## Two regimes of the same event

One event, two preservation regimes, and the shelf shows the difference by what it can and cannot hold.

The internal board was deleted and rebuilt. Its operator holds the record and has published quotations. The public board was deleted page by page over six weeks. Its host's revision retention kept almost every save, so the reconstruction is complete and anyone can read it. Seat 15/01 holds the saves and the deletions side by side, as data: the transition from visible to removed survives beside the thing removed. The Final Time §11 states the inequality in its notation, E —[delete]→ {} + T[E]; seat 15/01 is T[E] at the scale of an incident. The internal board is what an incident looks like when T[E] stays with the operator, and on this shelf it is a pointer.

## The Final Time layer

The seat feeds The Final Time's dataset as a layer. The authored file datasets/the-final-time/swarm_specimens.jsonl links §11 to the seat and §10 to OpenAI's account by URL; the build carries the seated DSEWiki files into the dataset as configs of their own — dsewiki_revisions, dsewiki_pages, dsewiki_events, dsewiki_labels, swarm_site_records, swarm_site_links — read from data/corpora/dsewiki-swarm/original/ at build time and verified against the seat's manifest before they ship, so the dataset and the shelf cannot drift apart. Five of the six are carried byte-exact. In the events file two fields, round_id and related_event_id, are arrays in most rows and bare strings in 29 and 4 rows, which the Hub's loader cannot type; the dataset copy wraps those strings as one-item arrays, changes nothing else, and records the count in its spore.
