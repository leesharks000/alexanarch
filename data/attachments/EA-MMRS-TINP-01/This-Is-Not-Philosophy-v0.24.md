**Abstract.** This notebook measures what the journals of philosophy now publish and asks what the measurement says about the discipline that produces them. A census of twenty journals in Crossref and two series in Persée, ancient, continental and analytic, codes each article's object and, from its abstract, what it does with that object: re-read a passage, resolve an apparent inconsistency, reconstruct a doctrine, make a claim about the text itself, relate authors, carry the material past antiquity, or argue a position of its own. Every code carries a verbatim span of its abstract, machine-checked. In *Ancient Philosophy*, 8 of 844 articles open the question of what the object is; across the sister journals since 2021, 4 of 283 Plato/Aristotle abstracts do. The three fields are three placements of one unit, the article that places a position: the ancient field repairs a named object, the continental field pairs names, the analytic field takes a position in a debate, and the form of the abstract converges on the analytic. The question was once ordinary. In the *Archiv für Geschichte der Philosophie* of the 1890s one ancient title in five puts a work's standing in question, and its pages carry the question at that rate until the war; by the 1920s it is gone. Philology kept it longer, and unevenly. A model asked for plausible abstracts reproduces each field's unit, raises its characteristic move, writes no claim about the text in sixty ancient abstracts, and deals one inventory of topics across the mastheads. The notebook argues that disciplinarity is the stable form of the article economy: the journal certifies the person through a unit that cannot fail, and the field takes the shape of what can be certified. The leading case is the author's own *Socrates as Orthonym*, the inverse image of the field's unit: it takes the names as its question. In three approaches to the journal, carrying three states of its evidence, the condition of admission was the same, the claim withdrawn. The notebook argues from the structure of the question that no amount of evidence or argument from the extant record could bring it into the field for consideration, and names what would refute that. The author's correspondence with the editor of *Ancient Philosophy* is read as the gate's own specification of that unit, and the case and the gate are read by the *Critique of Pure Inference* (#1766). The texts the measures read are seated as EA-CORPORA-39 (#1771).

## 0. The question

0.1 **The question.** The author (15 November 2026): "the real question here is how disciplinarity murdered philosophy as such. much has been done on the analytic / continental divide etc... whats the shape of the field?"

0.1a **Why the question is philosophy's.** The conventional reading first, in full. What a text is, who wrote it, when, whether it is one work or several, is philology's question; philosophy takes the text as given and asks whether its arguments are sound. The division is a reasonable division of labor, it lets each discipline do its own work well, and on this reading the census measures only that philosophers do philosophy and leave philology to philologists. The notebook's reading: the question of what the object is was philosophy's question before it was any discipline's. The Socratic *ti esti* asks what a thing is before anything is said to follow from it. Aristotle begins his inquiries from what is said and by whom, and closes the *Sophistical Refutations* with a claim about the standing of his own inquiry, what of it existed before him. Where the corpus is the evidence for the doctrine, as it is wherever a dialogue is attributed, a treatise ordered or a fragment assigned, the identity of the object is a premise of every reconstruction built on it, and an argument read without asking which text it is in, whose, and in what order carries that premise unexamined. A discipline that treats the premise as settled has removed it from examination, whatever division of labor it calls the removal. That is the sense of the title. "This is not philosophy" is the gate's verdict on the submission that reopened the premise (§0B), and the census's verdict on what passes the gate: a field in which every argument stands on a premise no article may examine. The premise is the partition of these corpora among composing persons. The census holds articles that examine a single work's standing (§4.8); it holds none that examines the partition.

0.2 **The prediction.** The author (15 November): "my prediction is that we'll see a tightening of ontological reach even within the contracting range of topics." Here a *topic* is the ancient object an article takes up, such as the *Republic* or the *Metaphysics*. *Reach* is what the article does with that object: re-read a passage, repair an inconsistency, reconstruct a doctrine, or make a claim about the text itself.

0.3 **What is sought.** The author: "im looking for model collapse". The notebook treats this as a technical term. It names a system trained on its own outputs, whose distribution narrows and whose tails disappear. Here it names the mechanism the notebook proposes (3.3). The census measures the distributions; the recursive dependence between them is still to be traced.

0.4a **The second prediction.** The author (15 November): "the fields have the most animosity to ai where their own work is already the most algorithmic. these are operations ai could spit out by the thousands and tens of thousands with no need of human clinamen". The test is in §4.14–4.15.

0.4 **The horizon.** The author plans to collect "the entire range of seven articles" in "a combined audit of what the fields cant carry". This notebook supplies the instrument for that audit and its first test case outside CRJ: *Ancient Philosophy* and five sister journals.

0.5b **Who coded.** The notebook's "readers" are instances of Claude (Anthropic), run in parallel in this session, one batch of abstracts each. ## 0A. The leading case: *Socrates as Orthonym*

0A.1 **Why this case leads.** *Who Speaks for the Classics* (#1770 §5.3.1–5.3.2) sets the philology its reports did not examine beside each report, so that a reader can assess what the gate did not. This notebook does the same with the case its gate refused: the author's *Socrates as Orthonym* (#123, 23 May 2026), submitted to *Ancient Philosophy* in July 2026 and declined on 28 August, and the line of evidence that grew from it to the framework hologram of 8 November (#1754). It leads because it is extreme. It is the inverse image of the field's unit. The census's unit takes the name as given and works under it, re-reading a passage, repairing a consistency, reconstructing a doctrine, three-quarters of the time inside one author (§4.7). The case takes the names as its question: how many made the corpus filed under Socrates, Plato and Aristotle, and later Theophrastus. The census measures the field from its centre. The case measures it from the farthest point an argument can stand, and so locates the boundary.

0A.2 **The conventional reading first, in full.** That one person made the corpus received under four names is an outlandish claim. The persons are attested by a continuous ancient tradition of lives, wills, succession and testimony. An extraordinary claim needs extraordinary evidence, and an editor who declines to spend referees on it exercises the judgment the post exists for. The editor stated the reading at its strength, by reductio: "Why bother to claim one guy did it all? Why not have one guy writing all modern philosophy?" (28 August). On this reading the case shows only that a journal does not publish improbable stories, and a census of what it does publish has no need of it.

0A.3 **What the case claims, and what it brought.**
- **The claim.** #123: "the foundational corpus of Western philosophy *can be read as* a single distributed authorial project rather than only as three independent authorships", with Socrates the orthonym, "characterized by the choice *not to write*", Plato the survival-heteronym, Aristotle the systematizing-heteronym. Its limit: "It does not claim that Socrates, Plato, and Aristotle were biologically identical, or that they constituted a single historical person."
- **The evidence it gathered.** Across six months and some forty deposits, four bodies, each kept apart and each with what went against it recorded (#1754 §7.8):
  - *interpretive*: the passages read as settling two leave it underdetermined (#1576); one work continues across the point where the name changes (#1583);
  - *stylometric*: the first axis of variation across the two corpora is operation, and the names fall at chance unsupervised (#1586); supervised, the names are recovered at 0.798, falling to 0.564 when subject is controlled (#1593); the Theophrastan fragment → Λ ranks first of 1,482 directed edges (#1605);
  - *manuscript-philological*: the catalogue's Ὑπομνημάτων Ἀριστοτελικῶν ἢ Θεοφραστείων; twelve shared titles; a will that names no book (#1581); a decree that names Aristotle as the maker of a list (#1582);
  - *reception-historical*: the count of two filed before it was argued; "on the 79 loci that bear on the count of composing persons, the received count is affirmed as a proposition at two, both machine compositions, both on the warrant of the wills and lives" (#1733).
- **The production model**, named on 1 October: "The corpus is what one self-dividing maker produces" (#1658).
- **The received reading on the same bodies** (#1754 §7.9): established by none of the four, and owing "a comparator, a documented succession of distinct persons producing a corpus unified to this degree", of which none has been named (#1733).

0A.4 **What the gate cannot carry, in scale.** The units the census finds opened, from the smallest:
- an emendation, a line or two of Greek, on the order of 10¹ words;
- the largest opening since 2010, the standing of one work ("On the Inauthenticity of the Critias", the authorship of [Arist.] *De spiritu*), on the order of 10³–10⁴ words;
- *Socrates as Orthonym*'s object, the partition into persons of the Platonic and Aristotelian corpora, on the order of 10⁶ words (estimate).

That is about five orders of magnitude beyond the field's typical unit and two beyond its largest. The author's "five orders of magnitude" holds against the census, measured from the unit the field ordinarily works in. The size is a proxy. The finding is the difference of type: the largest object the field opens is the individual work, and the partition of a corpus into persons is a different kind of object. The size follows from the type; it illustrates the difference and does not establish it.

0A.6 **The finding.** The author (16 November): "regardless of how outlandish the reader perceives the claim, no amount of evidence or argumentation could bring it into the field, even for consideration. it is not a thought that can be carried or a question that can be asked." Three exchanges cannot by themselves show what every development of the evidence would meet. The claim does not rest on them. It follows from the structure of the question, and the letters are its instance.
- **What evidence can do for a count of makers.** The archive's own protocol: "no supervised separation, however strong, licenses an inference to distinct makers, in either direction" (#1593). Textual evidence locates operations, dependencies and seams. It does not count makers, and the limit binds two as tightly as one.
- **What the admissible rival is.** The received reading at its strength is "a model of several connected contributors with editors" (#1754 §7.9). The class sets no upper bound on connection. Any unity the texts show can be described as makers closely connected, and any result the one-maker reading earns can be restated inside it. The gate names the class as its condition of admission ("closely connected … could be O.K.") and names its operation as the ground of the decline ("the same results without the hypothesis").
- **What the default needs.** Nothing. The received count is held without the comparator it owes, "a documented succession of distinct persons producing a corpus unified to this degree" (#1733), and is asserted in the letters as "lots of confirmation" and "pretty much evidence".
- **So.** Evidence from the texts cannot move the question off the default, because no textual result discriminates the one maker from makers closely connected. The default needs no evidence to stay. Consideration is therefore never reached by evidence: the question is set outside before its evidence is weighed, and would be at any quantity of evidence of the kinds the record holds. That is what "not a thought that can be carried" means, stated as mechanics.

0A.6a **The attempt to prove it wrong.** The claim is refuted by a case in which a field admitted, for consideration, that persons attested as distinct were one maker. The cases sort by direction.
- *One name into many makers* is admitted on evidence, repeatedly: Wolf's *Prolegomena ad Homerum* (1795); Casaubon's dating of the Hermetic corpus (1614), which dissolved an ancient author into a later literature; the Hippocratic corpus read as many hands under one name; Koch and Stiglmayr's demonstration (1895) that Dionysius the Areopagite wrote after Proclus; Jaeger's Aristotle divided by period (1923). The field carries partition.
- *Many names into one maker* is admitted too: Chatterton's Rowley, Macpherson's Ossian, Kierkegaard's pseudonyms, Pessoa's heteronyms, James Tiptree Jr. as Alice Sheldon. In each, what decided it was a witness outside the texts: the maker's own papers or statement, a documentary record of the person, or the absence of originals the name required. None was decided by the texts alone.
- *The ancient case has no such witness.* The persons are attested by the tradition's own channel: the lives, the wills and the catalogues reach the reader through the school (#1733). The one material witness, the Delphic decree, names Aristotle as the maker of a list (#1582). An autograph, if one were found, would be read as a hand, and a hand is not an author (#1588).
- **What would refute the claim, then.** (1) An admitted case, in any field, of independently attested persons merged into one maker on textual evidence alone; none was found here, and the search is open. (2) A result the class of closely connected makers cannot absorb; the archive's own protocol rules it out for stylometry, in both directions (#1593). (3) A new witness from outside the tradition. That is a discovery and not an argument, and it is the one route the analysis leaves open: it would bring in a fact, and the question would follow the fact. On the record as it stands, the claim holds.

0A.6b **The comparator.** The author (16 November): "not a single example of a corpus functionally unified in that way and to that degree produced by multiple successive makers. not one. closest is d & g. and that is an experiment in authorial fusion, not successive 'closely connected' people. not one. every single corpus of that degree of functional unity that we can see: one maker. unicorns." *In that way* means distinct named voices, each with its own function, building one architecture across the names. *That degree* means the corpus of 0A.3.
- **The documented cases.** Where the making of such a corpus is documented, there is one maker: Pessoa's heteronyms, Kierkegaard's pseudonyms, the archive's own configuration. These are #123's analogues, and here they serve as the reference class. The editor gave the rule himself: "But in the case of others you mention, such as Kierkegaard or Pessoa, we know the origin. There is the one guy" (24 July).
- **The near cases, and where each fails.** 
  - Deleuze and Guattari: two makers writing at once, a fusion declared as an experiment, documented as such. It is not succession.
  - Bourbaki is the strongest candidate: successive members over decades, one treatise, a fixed method. It runs the other way. Many makers produce one voice under one name, by a documented collective procedure. It does not produce distinct named functions across names.
  - The *Encyclopédie*: many makers at once, under editors who are named.
  - Successive traditions (the Hippocratic and Pythagorean corpora, the Talmud's generations of named voices) are known by their heterogeneity, their strata or their recorded disagreement. That is the opposite of the unity in question.
  - Corpora whose successive making is itself the scholarly default (the Pentateuch under the documentary hypothesis) cannot serve as the comparator. They stand on the same unwitnessed inference, and one unverified attribution of many makers cannot certify another.
- **What follows.** The received reading owes "a documented succession of distinct persons producing a corpus unified to this degree" (#1733), and the kind it posits has no known instance. Textual evidence cannot discriminate the count (0A.6). The reference class can, and every documented member of it has one maker. So the one-maker reading is the expected reading on the documented cases, and the received count posits a unicorn. The field holds the unicorn as its default, needing no evidence, and refuses as "an unlikely story" the reading that matches every documented instance. In the terms of the *Critique* (§5.2.3), the received account "survives on the bare possibility of an unspecified alternative", and here the alternative has never been observed.
- **What would refute it.** A documented corpus of that kind made by successive makers. The search is open, and its candidates are recorded above with the reason each fails.

0A.7 **Read by the *Critique of Pure Inference* (#1766).**
- **Standing and warrant are different variables** (§3.13). The gate assigns the claim its standing, a story to be entertained, and the warrant is not reached: the version read was the wrong one, and the next paper was read for a few sentences. "Exclusion does not by itself establish truth. Acceptance does not supply the support that acceptance failed to examine." The received count holds its standing by the same route.
- **Asymmetric immunity** (§5.2.3): "Uncertainty turns sophistical when its distribution protects a conclusion regardless of the evidence. A challenger must establish every step with extraordinary precision, while the received account survives on the bare possibility of an unspecified alternative." Here the received account survives on evidence unspecified, and the challenger owes "results that justify your hypothesis". The letters show the distribution at their own scope; the five conditions of §5.2.5 are not run here.
- **The entry point** (§5.5.3): "the distinction between the existence of an argument and the truth of its conclusion. The regime can accurately represent a real argument whose conclusion it rejects. If it cannot do that, its classification prevents the evaluation it appears to have performed." "The same results without the hypothesis", given on a version that did not contain the worked case, is a classification of that kind.
- **Proposition I** (§11.0j): "validity inside a representation cannot certify the representation's hold on its object". The articles the census counts are valid inside the received partition, and none of them can test the partition's hold on the corpus, because the partition is their premise (0.1a). The census is that proposition measured over a field; the case is the one argument that puts the representation's hold in question, and the gate's answer is that the question is not one.
- **Against this reading** (§5.5.5). It fails if the letters name evidence that would admit the claim, or if a version of the claim with the count kept was assessed on its warrant. They name results once (0B.5), and the version that offered them was not the one read.

## 0B. The exchange

0B.1 **The conventional reading first, in full.** The editor of a specialist journal, Ronald Polansky of *Ancient Philosophy*, without referees he could find for an unusual submission, read the paper himself. He corresponded at length and gave practical advice: length, clarity, abstract, a narrower object, the state of the secondary literature. When the manuscript did not meet the journal's standards, he declined it, and he left the door open for a different paper. On this reading nothing in the exchange is a mechanism. It is an editor doing his job with more patience than most.

0B.2 **The specification, in the editor's words.** Polansky's demands, in order:
- **Vocabulary.** "I frequently request that authors rid papers of philosophical jargon" (23 July). Later: "Avoid off-putting technical vocabulary beyond that of the passages you are considering" (31 August), and "Do not have any words that are not easily recognized in the abstract" (2 September).
- **The object.** "My recommendation... is instead to do some close analysis of an ancient text rather than such broad stroke sort of writing" (28 August). "I would much rather see close analysis or one or two" (2 September).
- **The frame.** "It should begin with a discussion of the present state of thinking about the relationship of the two works, i.e., coverage of available secondary discussion" (9 September).
- **The size.** "Anything over 40 pages is problematic" (29 July). "33 or so pages single-spaced, i.e., crazy long" (2 September).
- **The claim.** "If the paper is not about Aristotle and Theophrastus being one person, but instead that the two works mentioned are closely connected, this could be O.K." (9 September). "Could we see a tamer version of this paper" (2 September).
- **The register.** "PLEASE STOP WRITING IN THIS ECCENTRIC WAY" (31 August). "WHY NOT, THEN, PLAY THE SORT OF GAME NEEDED TO BE ACCEPTED FOR PUBLICATION RATHER THAN GAMES THAT YOU INVENT?" (31 August). "Do the sorts of things that you may resist, but that I might welcome" (31 August).
- **The stake.** Of the closing section of *Many and One*, the count of makers, which returns a null in either direction: "I saw the stuff from the other paper about one body or two bodies. We really can do without any of this. Who cares besides you, and can you really offer them much reason to care? It seems quite questionable. So, could we see a tamer version of this paper" (2 September; in the same letter: "I have not read more than a few sentences of this paper"). Of *Socrates as Orthonym*: "We seem able to arrive at the same results without the hypothesis" (28 August), and "Why bother to claim one guy did it all?... So, what really is the point other than to entertain an unlikely story?" (28 August).

0B.3 **What the specification is, read against the census.** Each demand has a measured counterpart in §4:
- the object narrowed to one or two texts, close analysis → A, the passage re-read (§4.6–4.7), and *Phronesis*'s passage-locus titles doubling from 11% to 20% (§4.5);
- the present state of the secondary discussion first → the template, "scholars hold X; I argue Y" (§4.15);
- vocabulary bounded by the passage's own → the claim stated in the object's own terms, so it cannot leave the object;
- the tamer claim → D-confirm admitted, D-open refused (§4.8–4.9). "Closely connected" keeps both names. "One person" removes one. The admissible version is the one the census already contains in quantity: "Aristotle's Metaphysics Z as First Philosophy", "The Unity of Plato's Trilogy";
- the game "needed to be accepted" → the move the stylometry finds converging across fields (§4.15).

The demands are the specification of the field's unit, stated by the person who administers it.

0B.3a **Production divorced from content and form.** The author (15 November): "he sees production as completely divorced from content and form." The stake-demands state it. Who made the corpus, and how many made it, are treated as making no difference to what the texts say or how they are built: "the same results without the hypothesis"; "who cares besides you". The same letters treat production as decisive for the submission. The decline of 28 August lists among its grounds a prior version on Academia ("whether there should be multiple publications of versions of a paper") and "whether papers composed by A.I. participation should be considered as regular submissions". Production is irrelevant when it is the canon's and disqualifying when it is the submitter's. That asymmetry is the field's unit in its plainest form. The object is the text as received, and operations on it (A, B, C) are the work. The question of how the text was made (D, the count of makers) is detachable and dropped. And a text whose making is in question at intake is excluded before its content is reached. The author's reply of 28 August named the converse: "*how* it was produced can matter, but not *instead* of engaging the thing in itself."

0B.4 **Eccentric and standardized are one measure.** The editor's objection was that the words were too eccentric. The census finds the field's output standardized. These are the same distance read from two sides. Eccentricity is distance from the template, and the gate removes what is distant. A field whose intake filters the tails produces outputs that converge. In the model-collapse literature the tails are lost because they are under-sampled. Here they are refused at intake. The author's phrase is "banning submission by topic/argument". The mechanism in the letters is precise: the topic is admitted and the claim is not. "Closely connected" is the same topic with the claim removed. A paper that drops the claim can be submitted, and the claim, removed before review, cannot be assessed. The demands are reasonable in form and close the route in effect, the same split the CRJ audit found between procedural check and substantive judgment.

0B.5 **The objection kept.** "We seem able to arrive at the same results without the hypothesis" is the one demand the author took as the real review (28 August): a hypothesis must earn a result the received partition strains for. It belongs with §7, as the standard the combined audit holds each field's excluded question to. The field's own unit does not have to meet it, because the field's unit was never a hypothesis. It is the default.

## 1. The received account: seven lines

1.1 **Indictments from inside.** The discipline has accused itself in public.
- Frodeman and Briggle (*NYT*, The Stone, January 2016, "When Philosophy Lost Its Way"; excerpted at Daily Nous) date the break to the research university. The "institutionalization of philosophy made it into a discipline that could be seriously pursued only in an academic setting". This was "the act of purification that gave birth to the concept of philosophy most of us know today". It was "about divorcing knowledge from virtue". Their verdict: "philosophy should never have been purified." "The point of philosophy now is to be smart, not good." "Having become specialists, we have lost sight of the whole."

1.2 **Institutional and sociological histories.**

## 2. The gap

2.1 Each line holds one link of a chain, and no line holds the chain.
- 1.5 explains how the object was built: as a *system*, retro-projected onto the ancients.
- 1.2 and 1.6 explain how the institution is maintained: editorial policy, the paper form, the culture of justification, the self-supporting community.
- 1.1 and 1.4 record the loss.
- 1.7 argues over the membership list.

2.2 The synthesis this notebook proposes joins three things that the lines above treat apart: the **construction** of the object (18th–19th centuries), its **maintenance** by the article economy (20th–21st), and its **transmission to the machine** (now). The third link is new, and the received account cannot see it, because it is written in the article form the machine is trained on.

2.3 **Thesis of the notebook.** Disciplinarity is produced, and what produces it is the economy of the article: the journal certifies the person through the unit (5.1), the unit that cannot fail is the one that certifies, and the field takes the shape of what certifies. That economy did not end philosophy by changing its doctrines. It made the *unit of work* the article that places one position: in the ancient field most often by repairing a named object, in the continental field by pairing names, in the analytic field inside a debate (4.14, 6.1). That unit, iterated, behaves like a model trained on its own outputs. The operations it performs were already narrow by 2010 and hold there: three-quarters of the field's work stays inside one author (§4.7). The objects keep narrowing (§4.2–4.5). And the tail goes: the article that reopens what the text is (§4.8). The machine then inherits a corpus that has already collapsed.

2.4 **The archive's own line, and what this notebook adds to it.** The received account (§1) does not hold the chain; the archive has held parts of it, as theory and as instrument, before this census measured anything.
- *The Certified Center* (Johannes Sigil and Nobel Glas, #1540, AXN:063A, 24 August 2026) read the *P&PA* policy on the day it was posted. It quoted the journal's two functions, "the promulgation of new knowledge, and identification and credentialing of talented researchers", and drew the consequence 4.16a and 5.1 reach here: the journal "legislates on A", the authorship process, not on Q, the quality it concedes. It named the arrangement *Ratificatory Human Governance* (the classifier supplies the fact, the journal the force) and *Retroactive Classifier Standing*, and argued that a discipline whose credential is machine-scored contracts as model collapse does, tails first, with the loss "experienced as improvement". Its §VI sets the obelus, athetesis as a signed, kept, reversible mark, as the custody principle through which philosophy's primary corpus survives, and says the discipline "has forgotten it". Its §VII asks for "a tail-preservation observatory over the admitted corpus", and its §IX predicts that the institutions in a position to take that measurement will not take it.
- *The Wrong Unit* (Nobel Glas, #1573, AXN:0665, 1 September 2026): per-item instruments read the head of a distribution while collapse accrues in its tail, so "a mode-seeking model beats a diverse one on any single turn"; the instrument that reads the state variable counts effective modes and cross-prompt convergence.

It dates what that paper stated as a framing thesis: athetesis was an ordinary article form in the *Archiv* of the 1890s (4.12, 4.12h), gone from its pages by the 1920s (4.12g), gone from the *REG* after 1945 and thinned in *CQ* after 1970 (4.12c, 4.12f). And it moves the path to collapse back a century. Two collapses are distinguished here. The institutional one is variance reduced by selection, the editor's and the referee's, under the credential, from the 1910s on; the computational one is a model trained on what that selection admitted, from 2023 on. The field contracted first; the model inherits the contracted distribution and, on the evidence of 4.17, sharpens it; the detector regime of 2026 is the newest instrument of the same credentialing function, pruning a distribution the article economy pruned before it.

## 3. The mechanism

3.1 **Construction.** Brucker's "system" assumes that every ancient philosopher intended a whole (1.5). The fetishized philological object does most of its consolidating in the 19th century (the author, 15 November): a fixed text under a name, with a corpus edited and bounded. Two consequences follow for later work. The name becomes the unit of inquiry. And any apparent inconsistency becomes a defect for scholarship to repair, rather than a fact about the text.

3.2 **Maintenance.** The article is the field's unit. Its protocol is set by editors (Katzav and Vaesen), and its first obligation is to justify itself to the field (Dotson). Its safest product is a higher-order truth about a game already constituted (Dennett). The norm of charity turns 3.1's inconsistency-as-defect into a genre: *consistency repair* (reach category B, §4.6).

3.3 **The machine.** A model trained on the article stock receives the object already whole, already named, and already repaired. Asked about the field, it reproduces the field's own distribution, and the field's next articles are now written alongside that model. The archive's captures document this recursion on the classics side: an Overview restates a field's self-description as fact. The computational link is now measured once, at the output (4.17): asked for twenty plausible abstracts per journal, the model reproduces each field's unit, raises its characteristic move, writes no D in sixty ancient abstracts, and deals one inventory of topics across mastheads. What is measured is what these generated batches contain. The training mixture, the retained share of original data and any loss across generations are not measured; the mechanism below is proposed. The institutional link (2.4) is the census. The mapping onto the collapse literature is the notebook's extension, stated as such. In the terms of *Diversity Contraction* the field is a substrate whose regeneration term is its tail, the article that asks what the object is; in Shumailov's recursion that is the retained share of original data, γ, and with γ = 0 the loss is unbounded. The corrective the collapse literature names is accumulation: Gerstgrasser et al. (2024) distinguish replacing earlier data with successive synthetic generations, under which the loss grows, from accumulating those generations beside the retained original data, under which it stays bounded. Extended to this case, the original data is the field's own record before the flattening, and EA-CORPORA-39 seats it for the machine reader (§8). That is the notebook's proposed intervention; its effect on any model is untested. The gates of 4.16 close the same channel from the other side: in *The Certified Center*'s words, "the corrective channel is gated by the pruning instrument".

- **Three-quarters anchored to a name, as in the ancient journals.** Work anchored to a named author runs 74–84% in every period. In the ancient journals, operations inside one author run 72–76%. The proportion is the same on both sides; the operations differ.
- **The continental operation is the pairing.** E (X's reading of Y, X and Y on Z) runs 24–36%. F (X applied to Z) runs 8–12%. Together that is 36–46% of the field: a product of the name list with itself and with a list of topics.
- **B, the ancient genre, is absent.** Consistency repair runs 1–4%. The continental field does not repair its names; it pairs them.
- **D is the growth of the corpus.** Of 14 D works, 10 are neutral reports on newly published or unpublished material: Derrida's seminars, the *Gesamtausgabe*, Kojève 1939, Fink's RIAS lecture, Schürmann's estate. Three open the text: the Trakl marginalia (2021), an editorial error in GA 39 (2014), and Derrida's archive and *Geschlecht III* (2020). On adjudication, Husserl's 1896 manuscript (2024) moved to C: it re-dates the origin of a doctrine, not the standing of a text. The name's corpus expands, and its standing is not in question.
- **One quarter is G.** The field's independent thesis is 16–26% of its abstracts, and it does not grow.

4.14d **Reading.** In the analytic journals the name is not the unit: 91–93% of the work is G. Their unit is the position in a debate. The three fields are therefore three placements of the same unit:

4.15a **Reading.**
- **The convergence is in the operation.** What abstracts say they do grows more alike within the continental field and across the two fields. What they talk about and how they phrase it does not.
- **The template's home is the analytic journal.** The claim formula runs 47% (2000–09) to 63–68% after 2010. Ancient philosophy is the field operationally closest to it (0.29, stable in every period). The trajectories differ and are kept apart. Continental against analytic rises steadily: 0.19, 0.19, 0.21, 0.24. Continental against ancient falls to a trough and recovers, 0.25, 0.19, 0.21, 0.26, with little net change over the interval.
- **The continental field is taking up the template.** The claim formula rises from 16% to 39% (*Research in Phenomenology*: 16% to 42%). The full template, "scholars hold X; I argue Y", rises from 1–2% to 7%. In the ancient journals the template is already 15–20%, and in *Phronesis* the claim formula is 73%.
- **For the second prediction (0.4a): one half measured here; the other half compared, exploratorily, in §4.16.** What is measured is one half: the dimension that converges is the stated move, the abstract's rhetorical template. Topic and phrasing vary. The other half, the hostility to AI, is measured at one point only, the ancient gate (§0B.3a, AI participation among the grounds of decline). The analytic journals, where the template is strongest, are unread on it. A template is not a reproduction: producing a defensible claim in the template still takes the work. What the census can show is the asymmetry once both halves are measured: an institution that rewards a recognizable procedure and excludes machine composition. That needs the gates of the other arms read as §0B reads this one (7.7).
- *Limits:* the ancient 2000–09 cell is *Phronesis* alone. Within-journal language similarity shows no trend, so the fall in LANG across the ancient cells is an effect of composition. The concentration and novelty measures in `stylo.txt` depend on n and carry no claim.

4.13 **What the census cannot see.**
- The baseline (4.12) is titles only and rests on the lexicon's recall: a title that questions a work's standing without any of its words (for example, a bare "Plato's Seventh Letter") is missed in every period alike. Before 1950 the philosophy baseline is *AGPh*, in its Zeller-era form, and the *Revue philosophique de Louvain* (4.12e); comparisons across that line set two journals against six.
- Titles are not arguments. Regex coding misreads (see 4.3). An abstract is not the article. Reach is coded from what the abstract claims, and some abstracts are truncated in Crossref.
- Crossref holds no deposited references for *Ancient Philosophy*, so its self-citation is unmeasured. For the sister journals, journal-title fields in the references are too often empty to measure field-citation.
- The recent zero-citation shares reflect citation windows, not neglect.

4.16a **Reading.**
- **On the editorial layer, the cases differ in kind.** In the analytic flagships the record holds prohibition, detection, retraction, author bans, and a coordinated response among the flagship editors. In the ancient field it holds one case, at the editor's desk. In the continental field no journal-level rule was found in the limited search the gates seat records (39/13), and the field takes AI up as an object of critique in its own articles: 8 of 886, against 0 in the ancient field and 5 in the analytic. Whether these differences follow the strength of the template is what the comparison leaves open.
- **The publisher layer does not discriminate.** De Gruyter Brill's rule covers the ancient journals and *Research in Phenomenology* alike. The difference between the arms is in what the fields' own editors add.
- **The template was reproduced before it was banned.** The *P&PA* policy followed an article in the same journal "written mostly by Claude" (Simon Goldstein, "Epistocracy and the Commitment Problem", August 2026), published, per the editor-in-chief, as "an experiment rather than as an expression of a settled PPA policy." At the template's home, a model-written paper passed review, and the response was prohibition with detection. That is the second prediction's mechanism observed once: the procedure is reproducible enough to pass, and the gate answers by policing production.
- **Status: an exploratory comparison, consistent with the prediction on the editorial layer; not established.** *The Certified Center* (#1540, 24 August 2026) read the *P&PA* policy on the day, from the same Daily Nous post, and drew its consequences at length (2.4). *(e) Reading.* The resemblance 7.5 asked for is established at the output, and the generated batches do more than resemble: they amplify, substitute and narrow. The spread it does show is a spread of objects drawn from a single shared inventory, which is why it converges across journals. This tests the reproducibility half of the second prediction (0.4a), at the level of the abstract, and not the association between reproducibility and hostility: the field's operations, as each abstract states them, come out twenty at a time from a one-line prompt, and what does not come out is the question of what the object is. Whether the articles behind such abstracts could be produced the same way is the *P&PA* case (4.16a), not this test. Limits: one run per journal; one model, signed out, its underlying version not recorded; twenty abstracts a journal, written in one batch each, so that within-batch likeness is partly the shared generation context and the comparison is between these batches and the sampled publications; one coder; the prompt asks for "plausible" abstracts, so it asks for the type, and the test measures what the model takes the type to be; a resemblance at the output does not by itself trace the transmission (3.3).

## 5. The article economy: who benefits, and what it produces

5.1 **The article economy.** A repaired object yields an article that cannot fail. Reopening the object puts the whole stock at risk. The interest is stated by an institution: *P&PA*'s prohibition rests on the journal's credentialing role, since "Submitting AI-authored essays makes the task of identifying talented researchers harder" (4.16a). The journal certifies the person through the unit; a unit that can be produced without the person breaks the certificate, whatever it contains. The labor the unit costs beyond its contribution is the admission price (*Socially Necessary Scholarly Labor*, #751: literature review, positioning, defensive citation, length, genre conformity). The editor's specification of the unit (§0B) is that price itemized at one desk: shorter, one or two texts, a tamer version, an abstract with no word not easily recognized.

5.2 **The norm of charity.** It turns the field's assumption (3.1) into a virtue that referees can enforce.

5.3 **The name as organizational chart.** Positions, series, companions and handbooks are all indexed by name. A text whose standing is open has no place on the chart.

5.4 **Publishers.** The object sold as whole is the one that sells.

5.5 **The machine.** A flat object is the most compressible one. The field's collapse is the model's convenience (4.17).

5.6 **The discipline as product.** 5.1–5.5 are not five beneficiaries of a discipline that exists apart from them; they are what the discipline is made of. The credential needs a unit that cannot fail (5.1); the unit needs a standing object (3.1), which the norm of charity guards (5.2); the standing object is indexed by name (5.3) and sold whole (5.4). Disciplinarity is the stable form of that circuit: what the census measures as the shape of the field is the shape of what can be certified. The model test (4.17) shows the circuit's output with the person removed: the type, sharpened, and in this run with no tail.

## 6. Against the received account

6.1 The analytic–continental divide is a quarrel over method. Measured (4.14–4.15), with the analytic arm (4.14c–d): the analytic field is 91–93% positions in a debate, so the name is not its unit. Its *form* is the one the others converge on. Further: on both sides about three-quarters of the work is anchored to a named author (ancient journals, operations inside one author 71–76%; continental journals, work anchored to a name 74–84%). The operations differ. The ancient field repairs the name (B), and the continental field pairs names (E, F). The continental field moves toward the analytic template: the operation profiles grow closer (0.19 to 0.24), and the continental claim formula rises from 16% to 39%. Against the ancient field the continental trajectory is a trough and recovery with little net change. The divide is real at the level of the operation, and the template is its point of convergence with the analytic field.

6.2 The canon debate is a quarrel over membership (1.7). The census shows that what is done to a member after admission (§4.6) narrows whoever is admitted.

6.2a The case (§0A) and the exchange with the editor of *Ancient Philosophy* (§0B) show the mechanism at the point of entry. The specification of the unit is stated, demand by demand, and the excluded claim is refused by topic.

6.3 What the article economy removes, and the discipline it produces then lacks, is not a doctrine and not a set of names. It is the capacity to ask what the object is. Catana's verdict on the "system" ("obscure more than it reveals") describes the instrument. The census measures what it obscures: 8 of 844 articles in *Ancient Philosophy*, and 4 of 283 Plato/Aristotle abstracts across the sister journals since 2021. In the philosophy journal it was gone from the pages by the 1920s (4.12g–h); in the census, where it survives, it is concentrated in one journal, *Elenchos* (4.10), whose institute was founded on an edition of fragments, with scattered openings elsewhere. The phenomenology is the one *The Certified Center* names: "a discipline cannot miss the arguments it no longer produces". The editor's "who cares besides you" (§0B) is that sentence spoken from inside. The national explanation was tested once and weakened (4.12d). The stronger candidate is the philological remit: the question persisted in philology after it left the philosophy journal (4.12c), though not in all of it; the *REG* lets it go after 1945 (4.12f), and the text survives there where the work's standing does not.
