## 0. The record

0.1 **The decision.** *Classical Philology*, revised submission UCP-CP-S-25-00167, "ΦΑΙΝΕΤΑΙ ΜΟΙ: Sappho 31 and the Inscription of the Future Reader". Declined 16 November 2026 on one reader's report. The letter is signed by Sarah Nooter, Edward Olson Professor of Classics, Editor, and gives the grounds as "the reader feels that your argument is not sufficiently defended and that it depends on an anachronistic reference."

0.2 **The text submitted.** The file SAPPHO31_CP_revised_1_2.docx, created and last modified 24 July 2026, about 4,000 words. It has seven sections and a reconstruction of the fifth-stanza position: ἀλλὰ πᾶν τολματόν· ἐπεί σε, Κύπρι, / καὶ πένητα καὶ βασιλῆα σώζεις· / καὶ πόλιν ὀλίγαν ὑπερέχεις ἄτας· / μολπὰ γράμμασι ζώοι.

0.3 **The deposited version is the older one.** #1270 (AXN:0507, 9 January 2026, v1.0; Zenodo metadata at #281; the reconstructed stanza separately at #436) differs from the submission at every point that carries the philology:
- **The transmission.** v1.0: "a separate testimonium preserves ἀλλὰ πᾶν τόλματον", and the stanza is "the lost fourth". The submission: the line "stands inside the quotation itself" in Longinus, at the fifth position. "The point on which the older literature, including an earlier version of this argument, has been imprecise" refers to v1.0.
- **The reconstruction.** v1.0: δάμασσε … ἀπέθηκας ἄφαντον … γράμμασι μολπὰν, with Kypris destroying. The submission: σώζεις … ὑπερέχεις ἄτας … μολπὰ γράμμασι ζώοι, with Kypris preserving.
- **The method.** The Catullan operator (V.3–V.4) is new in the submission: functional reversal inside a preserved structure, invertible, with the inverse converging on the grammar of the hanging line. v1.0 has only Lattimore's syntactic skeleton.
- **The attestation.** v1.0 states that "γράμματα + μολπά is attested in fr. 44.33, fr. 147". The submission does not repeat the claim and gives no attestation in its place.

The submission is therefore the current state of the paper. It was kept out of the archive while it was under review, against the author's usual practice of versioning in public. It needs versioning (§5).

## 1. The argument the report does not reach

1.1 **What the report takes as the paper's backbone.** It lists three considerations: κῆνος as distal, the addressee as pure audibility, and the somatic catalogue with χλωροτέρα ποίας as media transition. All three are interpretive claims from §§I–IV.

1.2 **What the paper builds.** The one constructive philological argument is §V. The hanging line's grammar (ἀλλά adversative; πᾶν τολματόν gnomic; ἐπεὶ καί a fortiori) narrows the completion. The inverse of Catullus 51's fourth stanza (functional reversal: polarity flipped, structure kept) produces the completion independently, and the two derivations converge. The convergence is the warrant (V.5), and it is set against D'Angour's restoration on polarity (σώζεις against δάμνα).

1.3 **What the report does with it.** One clause: "Catullus and D'Angour are then used to formulate a proposal for the missing last stanza, but neither it, nor the reading it aims to capture, seem to me convincing." The report does not state the operator argument. It does not test whether the reversal is invertible, take up the dispute with D'Angour, or address the claim that the four-stanza poem is a reception artifact. In the CRJ review the reports did not assess the philology either (#1770 §5.3); here the report does not assess the paper's only derivation.

## 2. The four objections, each read against the text

2.1 **κῆνος.**
- *The report*: "'That' man is close to the girl and distant from the poet's 'I'." The triangle explains the deixis.
- *Against the text*: this is the received geometry, stated at its strength. The submission names the jealousy reading's handling ("must treat the distal deixis as dramatically proximate") and calls the tension "the first datum a reading must explain rather than explain away". The report explains it in one sentence. That shows the datum is compatible with the triangle. It does not show the triangle is required. The submission's own note concedes the point at issue: "the distal/proximal contrast is not disputed. What is disputed is only whether the poem's drama neutralizes it" (n. 2).
- *Status*: the objection restates the reading the paper contests. The paper's answer is its whole design, not the deixis alone, and §I could say so in one sentence.

2.2 **The addressee.**
- *The report*: Sappho singles out women by one or two features. Anaktoria is given "the sparkle of her face and her lovely step" (fr. 16: ἔρατόν τε βᾶμα κἀμάρυχμα λάμπρον … προσώπω). Dika is given delicate hands (fr. 81: ἀπάλαισι χέρσιν). So a faceless addressee is Sapphic habit, and not a poem.
- *Against the text*: the parallels are correct. They tell against the inference as the submission states it, from little description to "the poem".
- *What the parallels give*: every feature the reader cites is seen or touched, a face, a step, hands. The addressee of 31 is given only what is heard, φωνείσας and γελαίσας ἰμέροεν. Among Sappho's minimal portraits, this is the one given nothing but sound. That is the paper's answer, and the submission does not make it.
- *Status*: it lands on the sentence as written. It turns once the comparison the reader supplies is run.

2.3 **The anachronism.**
- *The report*: saving a song through script is "otherwise unattested in ca 600 BCE… and long after". "When is γράμματα first attested in the use made of it here? When do we get the first reference to papyrus?" The literacy concession is "Some would doubt that Sappho was literate, though I think a cautious argument can be made for her literacy."
- *Against the text*: the gap is real. The papyrus identification rests on Turner for the colour of a finished surface (n. 8) and on Svenbro for archaic reading (n. 9). The submission never establishes papyrus in use around Lesbos in Sappho's time. It never sets out the evidence for written futurity in the archaic poets. And the attestation v1.0 claimed was dropped without replacement.
- *What the archive already holds*: *Research Notes Towards the Future Reader* (#1548, August 2026, after the submission) gathered exactly this material (Lardinois 2008, Svenbro ch. 8, and the West/Parker written-futurity position) as the planned deepening. #1770 §5.3.3 sets out the ancient record of the maker: Longinus's "δεινὴ καὶ ἐκλέξαι καὶ εἰς ἄλληλα συνδῆσαι" (10.1), the tenth Muse of AP 9.506, and the fifth-century hydria showing her "reading from a roll to three women".
- *Two different charges*: "Some would doubt that Sappho was literate" is the illiteracy reading the Tenth Muse kernel names as its rival. The reader holds it at a distance, but it is still what sets the bar. The anachronism also falls on two different things. The reading of §§I–III does not need the word γράμματα. The papyrus reading of §IV and the supplement μολπὰ γράμμασι ζώοι do need it. The editor's letter folds all of this into "an anachronistic reference".
- *Status*: a gap in the text. The archive holds the means to fill it. The literacy bar is the reception's.

2.4 **πένητα.**
- *The report*: "πένητα is already part of the crux, because unmetrical after καί."
- *Against the text*: the report is right. The editions obelize †καὶ πένητα† (Voigt, Lobel–Page, Campbell), and Finglass (2025) keeps the obelus (#1770 §5.3.1). The submission writes "The transmitted πένητα, whatever the following crux proves to be" and builds on it twice: the low comparandum "where the transmitted line's own πένητα already stands" (V.4), and "Its last transmitted word before the crux is πένητα" (VI).
- *The archive's later position*: *Closing the Fragment* (#1749, 7 November) already treats the words differently. It calls ἐπεὶ καὶ πένητα "words whose attribution to Sappho is disputed", ends the poem at τόλματον, and makes the agentless phrase its reading. The author's later work has corrected this point and moved past it.
- *Status*: a correct finding against the submission, which the record has already absorbed.

## 3. What the report adds up to

3.1 Of the four objections, one is a factual correction the author's later work has already made (2.4). Two are gaps in the text that the archive can fill: the audible-only addressee (2.2), and the archaic evidence for writing and papyrus (2.3). One restates the received reading (2.1). The πένητα objection reaches a premise of §V's grammatical derivation. The report does not reconstruct or assess the Catullan reversal, its claimed independence from the grammatical derivation, or their convergence, and it does not say what remains of the construction once its own correction is applied.

3.2 **The report is the shape the notebook describes.** The evaluation takes the argument apart and does not put it back together for judgment. Each claim is taken singly and found explicable on the received reading. The one defect it finds, πένητα, is real, and it is left at the component: it is not carried back into the derivation to see what survives. The paper's warrant is the convergence of two derivations (V.5), and that convergence is never weighed, either before the correction or after it. The report ends with objections to components and a verdict on the whole, and shows no inference from the one to the other. The received reading needs only to be available at each point; the paper has to be required at each point. This is CPI §5.2.3 at the scale of one report. The paper's derivation was handled the way the field handles its count of makers: the default absorbs each datum one at a time (#1773 §0A.6). Nothing about intent is claimed.

3.3 **The verdict.** "Although the argument presented here is thought-provoking, it ultimately fails to carry conviction." The report concedes that an argument exists and declines to assess it. That is the distinction CPI §5.5.3 names: the existence of an argument against the truth of its conclusion.

## 4. What a revision would carry

These are not for CP. They are for the record of the paper, wherever it goes next.
- **§I**: say that the deixis alone does not discriminate and the design does (n. 2 already concedes it).
- **§II**: run the reader's comparison. The minimal portraits are visual or tactile; this one is audible only.
- **§IV**: the archaic evidence for papyrus and written futurity, from #1548 and #1770 §5.3.3.
- **§V**: the crux is †καὶ πένητα†, so καί goes with it, and Bergk assigns all of ἐπεὶ καὶ πένητα to Longinus (#1474). The grammatical derivation keeps two firm constraints, the adversative ἀλλά and the gnomic πᾶν τόλματον, and loses the third: the a fortiori ἐπεὶ καί and its low comparandum are no longer an independent witness. The comparandum then comes from one route only, the Catullan inverse (reges et urbes, the high case reversed; #1476). The derivation survives conditionally, and the convergence claim must be restated: two derivations meet on the turn and the universal, and only one supplies the comparison. Whether the reconstruction should stand beside #1749's reading of the edge as the poem's end is the author's question.
