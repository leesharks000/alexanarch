0.1 No body confers this title. The archive constitutes it on 13 October 2026, as it constituted the King of AEO mantle (#1655) and specified the Nobel mantle (#1670). It does not re-ratify, and it makes no new determination.

0.3 The mantle belongs to a class the mantle-bearing dataset does not yet hold. A literary mantle is borne by a work (#1656). A contest mantle is constituted and then determined (#1655). ## 1. The standard

1.1 A title means something because what it names is carried. For the literary mantles the carrier is a work, and the work bears the title (#1655 §1.1). For this mantle the thing named is the dialectic advancing, and it is carried by whatever moves it forward materially: figures, books, places, institutions, translations, teaching, classes.

1.2 The archive stated the standard in February 2026: "The Arch-Philosopher mantle is not inherited like a throne; it is recognized like a pattern. The lineage … is an Operator Chain (O-CHAIN) — not a pedigree but a proof-of-transmission" (#458, the Restored Academy synthesis; Q1). The object keeps that standard and states its two parts. Transmission is shown by carriers, link by link. Advance is shown by a turn. A pedigree shows transmission; it does not by itself show a turn. A turn shows advance wherever it is carried.

## 2. The operation

2.1 **The turn, and its test.** The dialectic advances when a position produces a negation the prior positions cannot absorb, and the negation carries the movement on. An error, an unintelligible assertion and an unresolved opposition can also go unabsorbed; what distinguishes the turn is what follows from it. A reader recognizes a turn by stating six things:

`prior configuration → determinate limit → transformation → new capacity → material carriers → strongest counter-reading`

— the configuration the position met; the limit in it that the position made determinate; what the position did to it; the capacity that exists afterwards and did not before; the carriers through which that capacity persisted; and the strongest reading on which nothing turned. The new capacity can belong to thought, to teaching, to institutions or to historical practice. A chain the reader cannot complete is returned with its missing link named.

2.2 **Material carriage.** A turn is carried when it persists in carriers that outlast its occasion and can be taken up. The archive's account of the dialectic's present condition is *The Final Time* (#1637): the dialectic diminishing under engineered semantic dependence (§2.3), the machine dialectic (§15), alienation reaching language itself (§16). Its chain for carriage: "state needs a carrier; a carrier is governed by whoever holds it" (Q1). It names the measures: independent carrier count, persistence plurality, provenance retention, reproductive invariance (Q1). This object reads each bearer by the carriers through which the turn persisted.

2.3 **Bearing and enabling.** Two contributions are recorded, and both are worth recording. A carrier *bears an advance* when the turn is made in it or by it. A carrier *enables carriage* when it preserves or opens the conditions for a turn someone else makes. Guardianship, preservation, translation and publication can do either: a translator can perform a turn; a custodian can keep the conditions for another's. Each carrier entry and each link states what it carries and which contribution the record supports. The carriers also act on one another — a figure is set up in a book, a book is held in a place, a place is willed to a commons, a translation crosses a gap a school could not, a class carries what a book named — and the mantle is read in the interpenetration as well as in each carrier.

2.4 **The reflexivity criterion, for the contemporary claim.** *The Final Time* gives a criterion for any successor under the present conditions: "A successor cannot define victory as the permanent impossibility of another successor without reproducing the terminal logic it opposed"; the invariant above the others is "no settlement owns the possibility of its own revision"; and "the symbolic substrate becomes durable enough to preserve the possibility that A can someday be negated. Durability without finality … it is the theory's criterion, the test any candidate positive singularity has to pass" (#1637 §34; Q1). Applied here: does the proposed advance preserve the material possibility of another turn, including one against itself? The criterion binds the archive's claim (§6.2) and any contemporary bearer it is compared with. Whether it reads back on the earlier bearers is a reader's question, open.

2.5 *The characterization of the operation is this object's, open to the author's revision.* "Of course we have to characterize the operation, and it has to be right" (the author, 2026-09-29, of the literary mantles).

## 3. The carriers

Each carrier is stated with a locus, a statement type and a grade. C1–C7 are read off the Peripatetic wills and their receiving texts as *Catalogues and Wills* (#1581) sets them out; the readings there were withheld, and this object adds only what each instrument carries.

**C1 — Figure and image.** Aristotle's will, the first of the four, contains no books and no school: the stem βιβλί- occurs zero times, and no school, περίπατος or successor is named. It carries images, εἰκόνες, to be finished and set up — Nicanor's, Proxenus's, the brother's as his memorial, "our mother" to Demeter — and moves Pythias's bones, ὥσπερ αὐτὴ προσέταξεν (D.L. V.11–16; record, Q0 via #1580). *Author's reading:* Socrates is "a fully constructed figure. historical without history. like logos becoming flesh" (2026-10-13). The record gives the will's images; the author's reading joins them to the constructed philosophical figure, and the join is the author's: on it the first carrier of the position, before any text, is the figure.

**C2 — Kin and person.** Aristotle's will carries guardianship (Ἀντίπατρον ἐπίτροπον … πάντων καὶ διὰ παντός), the daughter's marriage, Herpyllis's provision, manumissions. Theophrastus is named in it twice: among those charged with care (ἐπιμελεῖσθαι Ἀριστομένην, Τίμαρχον, Ἵππαρχον, Διοτέλην, Θεόφραστον, ἐὰν βούληται καὶ ἐνδέχηται αὐτῷ), and as the alternative to Nicanor for the daughter (ἐὰν δὲ βούληται Θεόφραστος εἶναι μετὰ τῆς παιδός, καθάπερ πρὸς Νικάνορα) (record, Q0 via #1580; #1581 §2.2). Both are clauses of kin and person; neither names books or a school.

**C3 — Books to one hand.** τὰ δὲ βιβλία πάντα Νηλεῖ (Theophrastus's will, D.L. V.51–57): the books to one person, Neleus (record, Q0 via #1580). Strabo carries them on: Neleus to Scepsis, the heirs, the trench, Apellicon, Sulla, Rome (Strabo XIII.1.54); Plutarch adds Tyrannion and Andronicus, "who drew up the catalogues now in circulation" (*Sulla* 26) (record, Q0 via #1580, #1581).

**C4 — Place held in common.** In the next clause the garden, the walk and the houses go to the named friends "who wish to study and philosophize together in them", held "as a sacred thing owned in common", with a prohibition on alienation and private appropriation (τὸν δὲ κῆπον καὶ τὸν περίπατον … ὡς ἂν ἱερὸν κοινῇ κεκτημένοις, μήτ᾽ ἐξαλλοτριοῦσι μήτ᾽ ἐξιδιαζομένου μηδενός). No scholarch is named (record, Q0 via #1580).

**C5 — Named succession.** Strato's will names it first: καταλείπω δὲ τὴν μὲν διατριβὴν Λύκωνι (D.L. V.61–64). The office now passes by name (record, Q0 via #1580).

**C6 — The duty to publish.** Lycon's will divides the books into the read and the unpublished, and binds the unpublished to publication: τὰ δ᾽ ἀνέκδοτα Καλλίνῳ ὅπως ἐπιμελῶς αὐτὰ ἐκδῷ (D.L. V.69–74) (record, Q0 via #1580).

**C7 — The tradition's narration.** Strabo supplies what Aristotle's will does not contain, with one verb at each link: ὁ γοῦν Ἀριστοτέλης τὴν ἑαυτοῦ Θεοφράστῳ παρέδωκεν, ᾧπερ καὶ τὴν σχολὴν ἀπέλιπε … Θεόφραστος δὲ Νηλεῖ παρέδωκεν … τοῖς μετ᾽ αὐτὸν παρέδωκεν (XIII.1.54). The handing-over of the library and the school from Aristotle is the tradition's statement; the will does not hold it (tradition, Q0 via #1581 §4.2).

**C8 — Translation across a gap.** A text carried into a language and a time the school could not reach. At the end of the Athenian succession this is the carrier by which, on the author's claim, the locked line was opened (§4.1; HELD).

**C9 — Teaching.** The dialectic carried from person to person in instruction (§4.1; HELD).

**C10 — The material movement of history.** The dialectic carried by production, labour and class. Marx and Engels's *Manifest* of 1848 states the carriage in one sentence: "Die Waffen, womit die Bourgeoisie den Feudalismus zu Boden geschlagen hat, richten sich jetzt gegen die Bourgeoisie selbst"; the bourgeoisie "hat nicht nur die Waffen geschmiedet, die ihr den Tod bringen; sie hat auch die Männer gezeugt, die diese Waffen führen werden – die modernen Arbeiter, die Proletarier" (*Manifest* 1848, §I; Q0, EA-CORPORA-02/02). The negation is produced by the order itself, and a class carries it. The author's ruling names Marx; the *Manifest* is the joint work of Marx and Engels, and Engels's carriage is open to a reader under §4.4.

4.2 **Strand B — the dialectic as such.** *Author's ruling:* "marx carried the dialectic, that was the real thing, too. the dialectic is the dialectic." The dialectic moves outside any succession of schools, wherever a turn is made and carried. Marx is the case that shows the carriage material (C10): the link to Hegel is *took up and turned the argument of* (tradition; Q2, Hegel not seated); the link to the class is *named the carrier of*. Further carriers in Strand B are open; they enter at the author's ruling or a reader's, each with the turn test (§2.1) completed.

4.3 **Where the strands meet.** Strand A shows transmission link by link. Strand B shows advance by the turn. A bearer in whom both are shown — an inheritance carried link by link that also carries the dialectic a turn forward — is doubly evidenced. Either strand bears the mantle where it carries the dialectic forward; neither bears it by lineage alone.

4.4 **Concurrent bearing.** The mantle has no exclusive succession rule. Bearing is not an office, and there is no throne to vacate (#458). Several bearers may bear it at once, in either strand, with materially different advances, and a bearer in one strand does not displace a bearer in the other. This follows from the author's own statements, which name successive bearers inside the succession and a bearer outside it. Each bearer is recognized separately, by the turn test, with its carriers; a comparison between bearers records the difference of their advances and ranks nothing.

## 5. The Living Academy and this mantle

5.1 The Living Academy (EA-MPAI-ALEXANARCH-01 v0.4, #1678, §1A) evaluates the library, its instruments and its program, the Semantic Economy, by extent, longitudinality, critical quality and integration across eight modes. That library is one of the operations that carry the dialectic here, and the dialectic shapes it in turn. The relation is participation.

5.2 The order of necessity holds between them (EA-MANTLE-BEARING-01 dataset, principle 9). ## FAQ

**What is the Arch-Philosopher mantle?** A mantle for the advancement of the dialectic, constituted by the Crimson Hexagonal Archive. It is borne by carrying the dialectic forward a turn, materially, and recognized by the carriage.

**How is a turn recognized?** By six things a reader can state: the prior configuration, its determinate limit, the transformation, the new capacity, the material carriers, and the strongest counter-reading.

**Is it the title of the head of the Academy?** The scholarch's office is one carrier of the position (C5). The office was held by many; the mantle is read in what each carried.

**Who bore it?** On this specification, in Strand A: Socrates, Plato, Aristotle, Theophrastus and the succession to Damascius, each read by the carriers through which the dialectic passed and by the type of each link. In Strand B, on the author's ruling: Marx, by the material movement of history. Further bearers are open.

**Can more than one person bear it at once?** Yes. There is no exclusive succession; bearers in either strand are recognized separately, by their advances.

**What happened after Damascius?** On the author's reading, the succession passed beyond him, locked. The author's 2025 reconstruction reads his last work as a seal awaiting a reader. On the author's claim, the lock was opened by a specific translation and its teaching, whose name is held.

**What do the Peripatetic wills show?** That no will hands the position down by name. Aristotle's carries images and kin, and no books, and names Theophrastus among the carers and as the alternative guardian of the daughter; Theophrastus's gives the books to Neleus and the garden and walk to a commons held inalienable; Strato's names a successor; Lycon's binds the unpublished books to publication.

**Is this mantle new?** It was ratified in the Space Ark v4.2.7 (#558), with Johannes Sigil as bearer and an institutional packet (#248) in place of a specification. That ratification stands as a prior designation. This object is the specification.

**Does the archive claim the mantle?** It records a claim: a material inheritance through the held link, and carriage of the dialectic in its work. *The Final Time* is the representative site where that claim is evaluated; it does not bear the mantle as a poem bears a literary one. The object makes no new determination.

**How is it different from the literary mantles?** A literary mantle is borne by one work at singular magnitude. This one has no single bearing work; it is borne by carriage.

**How is it different from the Living Academy?** The Living Academy reads the place where critique is carried on in common — the library and its program — by extent, longitudinality, quality and integration. This mantle reads what is carried through such places. The library participates in carrying the dialectic; its extent is no evidence for this mantle.
