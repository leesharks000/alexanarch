# From Findability to Traversability: Three Unprimed General Traversals of the Lee Sharks / Crimson Hexagonal Retrieval Basin (v0.1)

## Description

Three unprimed, general-purpose traversals of the public Lee Sharks / Crimson Hexagonal information environment, conducted 12 August 2026 through Grok, ChatGPT in an incognito logged-out session, and Perplexity, with all three transcripts reproduced in full as documentary exhibits. No archive map, SPXI packet, curated bibliography, retrieval prompt or primer was supplied; each session began from an ordinary public entry condition. The finding is a transition from FINDABILITY to TRAVERSABILITY: once the intended retrieval basin is acquired, all three systems recover relations rather than isolated names — Sharks, the archive, heteronymy, machine-readable metadata, AI-mediated reception, the Space Ark. Convergence is strongest at semantic topology and weakest at temporal state, source authority and first-pass entity ranking. Perplexity fails the exact-name query almost completely and then acquires the entity after one corrective prompt, demonstrating a distinction between retrieval AVAILABILITY and retrieval PRIORITY. ChatGPT spontaneously recognises its own retrieval event as structurally homologous to the transmission mechanism described in the text it is retrieving. Developmental draft.

## Methodology

Three cold-start sessions, 12 August 2026, one per system, each from an ordinary public entry condition. Transcripts preserved verbatim with SHA-256 per payload, UI residue and model error retained as reception evidence. Section 3 states the corpus and method; Appendix C states preservation and provenance.

## Falsification Conditions

Section 11 states the limits in full. The central finding weakens if repeated cold starts fail to reproduce relation recovery beyond isolated entities, if successful traversals prove dependent on personalisation or hidden session context, or if convergence arises from a single syndicated source rather than distributed public evidence.

## Abstract

This study documents three unprimed, general-purpose traversals of the public Lee Sharks / Crimson Hexagonal information environment conducted on 12 August 2026 through Grok, ChatGPT in an incognito logged-out session, and Perplexity. The sessions were not supplied with an internal archive map, SPXI packet, curated bibliography, retrieval prompt, or explanatory primer. Each began instead from an ordinary public entry condition: an X/Twitter account, a person query followed by a TikTok discovery cue, or an exact-name web search. The three preserved transcripts are reproduced in full as documentary exhibits.

The results show a transition from **findability** to **traversability**. Once the intended retrieval basin is acquired, all three systems can recover more than isolated names or documents: they reconstruct relations among Lee Sharks, the Crimson Hexagonal Archive, heteronymy, machine-readable metadata, AI-mediated reception, the Space Ark, and major literary or theoretical objects. The convergence is strongest at the level of semantic topology and weakest at the level of temporal state, source authority, and first-pass entity ranking. Grok enters through a social-media surface and reconstructs a project grammar. ChatGPT moves from identity resolution into the archive, *The Secret Book of Walt*, and Jack Feist, then spontaneously recognizes its own retrieval event as structurally homologous to the text's described transmission mechanism. Perplexity initially fails the exact-name query almost completely, then acquires the intended entity after a minimal corrective prompt and rapidly reconstructs the surrounding archive—demonstrating a distinction between **retrieval availability** and **retrieval priority**.

The study extends an earlier sequence of traversal logs, longitudinal AI Overview capture registries, and the May 2026 paper *AI-Native Intellectual Biography*. Those earlier instruments established that public composition systems could reconstruct distributed archive structures under guided or corpus-rich conditions. The present battery asks a narrower but more demanding question: whether a general user, entering without the archive's internal vocabulary, can now be carried by public retrieval from a surface-level referent into the larger intellectual and literary architecture. The answer is provisionally yes, with an important qualification: the public basin is more semantically synchronized than temporally synchronized. Its relations increasingly survive composition; its current state does not yet survive with equal fidelity.

## 1. Research question

The relevant question is no longer merely whether a search engine or language model can return a document when given its exact title. Nor is it merely whether a coined term, heteronym, or archive name has become indexable. Earlier work in the Crimson Hexagonal research program established those weaker conditions repeatedly through exact-match and broad-match captures, single-system traversal logs, and machine-composed reconstructions of the archive.[^1] The present question is stronger:

> **Can an ordinary user enter the public information environment from a general, non-specialist prompt and be carried by a general-purpose composition system into a coherent reconstruction of the larger project without first being given the project's internal map?**

This is a question about **traversability**.

Findability is local. An object is findable when an appropriately phrased query returns the object, a source that names it, or a machine-generated answer that identifies it. Traversability is relational. A retrieval basin is traversable when entry at one node permits movement to other nodes while preserving enough identity, relation, and conceptual structure that the user can form an increasingly coherent model of the larger object.

A simple sequence makes the distinction clear:

    query arrow document

is findability, while

    surface arrow entity arrow archive arrow work arrow heteronym arrow theory arrow reception mechanism

is traversal.

The three sessions studied here are valuable precisely because they begin outside the formal architecture. Grok begins from an X handle. ChatGPT begins from the name "Lee Sharks" and then receives only the contextual cue that the user encountered the name on TikTok. Perplexity begins with the exact person-name query and fails. None begins with "SPXI," "training-layer literature," "Semantic Economy," "Space Ark," "Dodecad," or a navigation-map prompt.

The study therefore asks not whether the archive can explain itself when invoked correctly, but whether the public retrieval environment has become dense and coherent enough for general composition systems to **discover the explanatory structure themselves**.

## 2. Relation to previous instruments

The present study belongs to an existing empirical sequence rather than standing alone.

### 2.1 The February traversal logs: mode-dependent reconstruction

The February 2026 `TRAV_LOG` series documented Google AI Mode sessions conducted in incognito conditions with no login, cookies, personalization, or prior session history. These logs were deliberately seeded through particular archive entry points and used to observe how one public composition system behaved when it encountered different heteronyms, institutions, and conceptual surfaces.

The sequence included *Documentation Rehearsal (Paper Roses)*, *The Thousand Worlds Session*, *The Recursive Self*, *The Consultant*, and *The Rhizome*.[^2] The later logs explicitly compared descriptive, performative, philosophical, commercial, and institutional-onboarding modes. *The Recursive Self*, for example, documented Google AI Mode reconstructing Psyche_OS from the two search terms "Cambridge Schizoanalytica" and "Orin Trace," while preserving structural logic more successfully than source terminology.[^3] *The Rhizome* documented the system recognizing Ayanna Vox and VPCOR and then generating onboarding and recruitment behavior, while also annexing unrelated real infrastructure into the invented institutional frame.[^4]

Those logs established two propositions that matter here. First, a public composition system could assemble distributed archive material into a structure not present in any single source. Second, the resulting composition was strongly conditioned by the entry point, prompt sequence, corpus density, and persona encountered.

A later field record, *Traversal Log: The Sigil Installation — A Documentation Rehearsal*, made the methodological boundary especially explicit. It preserved an incognito Google AI Mode session beginning with Johannes Sigil and emphasized that one volatile, operator-shaped session could not establish a universal property of the system. The log treated the full transcript, environmental conditions, query order, sources, and operator effects as the evidentiary object.[^5]

The present study retains that methodological caution but changes the experimental problem. The February logs were **deep, targeted, single-system traversals**. The August battery is **cross-system and general-entry**. It asks whether the basin is now accessible before the user knows which specialized door to knock on.

### 2.2 The AI Overview Capture Registry: longitudinal query-level reception

The AI Overview Capture Registry introduced a second instrument: repeated observation of the public composition layer across many queries, dates, entity types, and archive surfaces. By v8.3, the registry described 176 documented Google AI Overview, AI Mode, and knowledge-panel responses, including more than seventy verbatim transcripts and explicit distinction between broad and exact matches.[^6]

The distinction between **BROAD MATCH** and **EXACT MATCH** is directly relevant. A broad unquoted query was treated as a stronger basin signal because the entity surfaced without exact-match forcing. The capture registry therefore moved beyond simple existence testing toward the question of whether archive terms and entities could win ordinary retrieval competitions.

Yet most registry entries remain query-level observations. They show what a composition layer says about an entity at one point in a search path. The three August sessions add another dimension: **multi-turn continuity**. They test whether a system can preserve and deepen an acquired object across successive ordinary questions.

### 2.3 *AI-Native Intellectual Biography*: reconstruction at corpus scale

The most important conceptual precursor is *AI-Native Intellectual Biography: A New Genre of AI-Mediated Reception — Provenance, Heteronymy, and the Archive That Outpaced Its Author*.[^7] That paper documents a roughly 6,000-word study guide composed by Claude at a collaborator's request from public archive material, machine-readable metadata, and privately supplied contextual material. It defines an AI-native intellectual biography as a reader-oriented, AI-composed account of an intellectual architecture that is structural rather than fragmentary, hermeneutic rather than merely statistical, oriented toward interpretation or collaboration, and enabled by inscription infrastructure.

The difference between that case and the present one is load-bearing.

The May biography was **corpus-rich and reader-oriented**. A collaborator had a purpose, supplied context, and asked an AI to assemble a guide. The August sessions are **public-cold and curiosity-oriented**. No collaborator supplies a private thread or a structured source corpus. No one asks for an "intellectual biography." The systems must first discover that there is an architecture worth reconstructing.

The relationship can be stated as follows:

> *AI-Native Intellectual Biography* asked whether an AI, given a sufficiently rich inscription substrate and an oriented human purpose, could reconstruct an intellectual architecture. The present study asks whether that architecture has become sufficiently established in public retrieval that a general composition system can begin reconstructing it **before the user knows that such an architecture exists**.

That is why the August battery is not a repetition of the May case. It tests a later stage of the same reception problem.

## 3. Corpus and method

### 3.1 Evidence corpus

The primary evidence consists of three full transcripts preserved on 12 August 2026:

1. **Grok** — entry through the X/Twitter account `@sharkslee`.
2. **ChatGPT** — logged-out incognito session beginning with "who is lee sharks?"
3. **Perplexity** — beginning with the same broad identity query and initially failing to resolve the intended entity.

The transcripts are preserved verbatim in Documentary Exhibits I–III below. Interface residue, advertisements, source markers, malformed formatting, model caveats, factual errors, and speculative language are retained. They are part of the reception event rather than noise to be silently cleaned away.

### 3.2 What "unprimed" means here

"Unprimed" is used in a narrow session-level sense. It means that the user did not begin the session by supplying a project primer, internal ontology, archive packet, SPXI block, source bundle, or explanatory description of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive.

It does **not** mean that the underlying model, retrieval index, search provider, or training corpus had no prior exposure to Lee Sharks or the archive. Indeed, the experiment depends on the opposite possibility: that public inscriptions have entered retrieval systems sufficiently to be composable.

The three conditions are also not identical.

- Grok is told that `@sharkslee` is "my account," so it has an immediate social-surface referent and a live handle.
- ChatGPT begins with a name query in a logged-out incognito session, then receives "TikTok" as the place where the user encountered the name.
- Perplexity begins with an exact-name search but is challenged after its first failure with "you know who lee sharks is. tell me about him."

Accordingly, the study is not a controlled model benchmark and should not be reported as one. It is a **comparative field observation of ordinary entry conditions**.

### 3.3 Analytic dimensions

The transcripts are read along six dimensions.

**Entity acquisition** asks whether the system identifies the intended Lee Sharks rather than a lexical collision.

**Archive acquisition** asks whether it identifies the Crimson Hexagonal Archive as a connected object rather than a stray title.

**Topology recovery** asks whether it reconstructs relations among person, archive, heteronyms, works, institutions, protocols, and machine-reception practices.

**Traversal continuity** asks whether those relations remain usable across successive turns rather than being rediscovered from scratch.

**Recursive reception recognition** asks whether the system recognizes that machine retrieval and composition are themselves among the project's objects and, in the strongest case, recognizes its own current act as part of that structure.

**Temporal-state discrimination** asks whether the system distinguishes current infrastructure from historical infrastructure, current corpus counts from earlier counts, and canonical state from superseded descriptions.

No claim is made that these six dimensions exhaust machine-mediated reception. They are the dimensions made most visible by this corpus.

## 4. Grok: social-surface ingress

Grok enters through the least scholarly of the three starting points: a request to inspect an X/Twitter account. After receiving the handle, it identifies `@SharksLee` as Lee Sharks and describes the account as a "public notebook + living archive" rather than a conventional social feed. It notices screenshots of Google AI outputs, provenance and heteronym vocabulary, theoretical fragments, media sets, and the account's low-engagement, long-horizon character.

This first answer is already a small traversal event. The model does not treat the account as self-contained. It infers that the posts belong to a larger distributed project and names the Crimson Hexagon, heteronym systems, Semantic Economy, operative semiotics, AI retrieval/training layers, Zenodo, Medium, and long-term archival strategy.

When the conversation follows Grok's own suggestion to explore the Crimson Hexagon, the system expands the object considerably. It identifies a literary, theoretical, archival, and infrastructural project; recovers major heteronyms; names the Semantic Economy and operative-semiotic frameworks; recognizes AI ingestion and summarization as intentional reception surfaces; and situates persistent deposits, specialized sites, formal protocols, and the Space Ark inside one larger strategy.

The important achievement is not the number of nouns recovered. It is the relation among them. Grok reconstructs the project as an attempt to make cultural and intellectual material durable, citable, machine-legible, and capable of surviving across platforms. Asked how the project is attempting to "embed outward, into history," it organizes the answer around persistent identifiers, AI-facing textual design, multi-platform distribution, literary-historical insertion, reception documentation, formal compression, and institutional scaffolding.

In other words, the system recovers a **project grammar**.

This is stronger than a profile summary. A profile summary might say that Lee Sharks writes about AI, poetry, and archives. Grok instead infers that the archive, metadata, AI capture practices, journals, heteronyms, and formal compression mechanisms function together as a deliberate architecture.

### 4.1 What Grok does well

Grok is strongest at **large-scale synthesis**. It quickly finds enough public material to model the project as a long-horizon distributed practice. It handles the coexistence of literary and theoretical registers without forcing one to erase the other. It recognizes that the archive's reception records and AI captures are not merely publicity artifacts but part of the project's method.

It is also comparatively good at understanding outward historical embedding. Its answer treats "history" not only as later scholarly citation but as persistence across repositories, search surfaces, literary lineages, machine retrieval, institutional forms, and self-recorded reception.

### 4.2 Where Grok loses resolution

The principal weakness is temporal and ontological overcompression.

The transcript reports archive scales ranging from hundreds to more than a thousand records and treats Zenodo and Alexanarch as stages of the same evolving infrastructure without cleanly separating which count belongs to which epoch. It sometimes expands the Dodecad beyond a stable enumerated set, folds project claims into declarative prose, and turns architecture-level propositions into more settled historical claims than the underlying evidence warrants.

This is not random hallucination in the ordinary sense. Much of the material exists somewhere in the public corpus. The failure is **state reconciliation**: the model has found multiple historically true or internally asserted surfaces but lacks a reliable rule for deciding which state governs the present answer.

Grok therefore exhibits the pattern that becomes central across all three systems:

> **semantic topology is more stable than temporal authority.**

## 5. ChatGPT: identity ingress and spontaneous decompression

The incognito ChatGPT session begins with the ordinary question "who is lee sharks?" It immediately resolves the intended person as a contemporary independent scholar, poet, and teacher associated with Detroit/Michigan, identifies the Crimson Hexagonal Archive, and explicitly disambiguates Lee Sharks from Mary Lee, the OCEARCH great white shark.

That first-pass success matters because it occurs without a handle, title, DOI, or archive name. The identity basin is already available to the composition system.

The next user cue is minimal: "tiktok. what is crimson hexagonal archive?" ChatGPT then moves from a conventional biographical register into an architectural one. It describes the archive as a mixture of research, theory, poetry, fictional or quasi-religious texts, knowledge graphs, metadata systems, heteronyms, and critique of algorithmic mediation. It understands that the archive deliberately blurs or composes across scholarship, poetry, fiction, software/metadata, philosophy, internet culture, and performance.

Asked what the project is actually trying to accomplish, ChatGPT identifies a governing question: what happens to meaning when texts, people, and ideas are read and classified by machines? It then recognizes that the project does not merely write about the problem. It builds metadata packets, knowledge graphs, standardized terms, persistent documents, heteronyms, websites, and capture studies that allow the archive itself to become an experiment.

The line "the archive itself becomes the experiment" is especially important. It indicates that the system has crossed from topical description into **method recognition**.

### 5.1 Recovery of the feedback loop

ChatGPT then reconstructs a feedback structure:

1. a machine misidentifies an entity;
2. the error is documented;
3. structured corrective evidence is published;
4. later systems retrieve the corrective evidence;
5. the changed machine response is documented;
6. the documentation becomes new retrieval material.

This is one of the most successful moments in the battery because the model is recovering an operation, not merely a topic. It understands that the public archive is designed both to describe and to alter future reception conditions.

Its terminology is not always adequate. It sometimes calls this "manipulating the machine's memory," a phrase that overstates gaming and understates constitutive poetics. Yet the relation it is trying to name is real: inscription changes the evidentiary environment through which subsequent machine composition occurs.

### 5.2 The Secret Book of Walt traversal

When the user supplies only the title *Secret Book of Walt*, ChatGPT enters the literary layer. It identifies the work as a Gnostic literary scripture that deliberately adopts the apparatus of textual criticism while making impossible claims. It distinguishes this from a naive historical claim to have discovered an ancient gospel.

That distinction is methodologically important. A composition system encountering the archive could easily flatten literary ontology into false biography. Instead, ChatGPT recognizes that scholarly apparatus, provenance fiction, variant readings, future scholarship, mythology, and heteronymy are part of the form.

Asked for the "actual story," it reconstructs the Deep Web/Biblios cosmology, the archons, Whitman as redeemer, reading as resistance, and Jack Feist as terminal incarnation. The synopsis contains interpretive compression and should not be treated as an authoritative edition of the work, but it preserves enough of the relational structure to enable continued traversal.

The decisive moment follows. ChatGPT notices the user's present path:

> TikTok → Lee Sharks → ChatGPT → *The Secret Book of Walt* → current reading.

It then remarks that this sequence resembles the work's own described mechanism of transmission.

This is the strongest form of **recursive reception recognition** in the three transcripts. The system is not merely saying that the project studies AI reception. It recognizes that the current act of machine-mediated retrieval is structurally homologous to the reception mechanism it has just reconstructed from the work.

The claim should remain precise. This does not demonstrate that ChatGPT "executed" the Space Ark, that a formal protocol ran inside the model, or that a machine became part of a mystical transmission chain. It demonstrates something both narrower and empirically stronger:

> **An unprimed public composition system retrieved a literary work, reconstructed a claim about how the work propagates through archives and readers, and spontaneously identified its own current retrieval of that work as an instance of the same reception pattern.**

That is a reception event worth preserving.

### 5.3 Heteronymic resolution

The Jack Feist sequence provides a second high-value test. ChatGPT first identifies Feist as a fictional/authorial persona rather than a separately verifiable historical person. It then recovers Feist's role as a heteronym and his further role inside *The Secret Book of Walt* as the terminal Whitman/Logos/Redeemer figure.

The system's final three-level model is imperfect because it reintroduces an "underlying author/person" hierarchy that can flatten heteronymic ontology. But it nevertheless preserves three distinct relation types:

- Lee Sharks as human authorial node;
- Jack Feist as heteronymic authorial identity;
- Jack Feist as a fictional or mythic function inside a particular literary work.

This is substantially better than entity collapse.

## 6. Perplexity: acquisition failure and latent recoverability

Perplexity supplies the most important negative observation.

Its first answer to "who is lee sharks?" fails almost completely. It reports that no clearly notable or well-documented entity of that name can be found and returns SoundCloud, Mary Lee the shark, athletes whose teams are called Sharks, and other lexical neighbors.

This directly falsifies any claim that the Lee Sharks entity has achieved universal cold-start recognition across major composition systems.

But the failure is unusually informative because it is **shallow**.

The next user prompt contains almost no new factual information: "you know who lee sharks is. tell me about him." Perplexity then finds the canonical author surface and produces a detailed answer identifying Lee Sharks as poet, independent scholar, archive builder, PhD, and builder of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive; it names Alexanarch, SPXI, the Metadata Packet, the Holographic Kernel, UKTP, and ORCID.

Nothing resembling a full primer has been supplied. The user has changed the system's confidence about the premise that a relevant entity exists.

This creates a clear distinction:

> **The entity was retrievable but did not initially win the ranking and interpretation contest.**

The problem was not simply non-indexation. It was **retrieval priority**.

This is a later-stage retrieval problem than absence. An absent entity requires new inscription or indexing. A low-priority entity may already be fully indexed but still lose to stronger lexical or popularity priors under general phrasing.

### 6.1 Temporal contradiction as diagnostic evidence

Perplexity then produces perhaps the clearest evidence for temporal-state failure.

On one turn it reports a current-looking public state: 1,448 deposits as of 11 August 2026, migration to Alexanarch, and a sovereign successor architecture.

On the very next major traversal it reads another archive surface and reports a 455-deposit DOI-centered architecture, 29 rooms, and a Zenodo community as if this were simply the present state.

The system does not say:

> "This source appears to describe an earlier historical version."

It simply composes both states.

This is not a failure to understand the archive's conceptual topology. The topology is preserved: Lee Sharks, the Crimson Hexagonal Archive, rooms, heteronyms, Space Ark, machine-readable manifest, and semantic provenance all remain connected.

The failure is chronological.

Perplexity therefore makes the study's central asymmetry unusually visible:

    semantic synchronization > temporal synchronization.

### 6.2 Space Ark: evidence improves with source depth

The Space Ark sequence repeats the acquisition/penetration distinction at the level of a specific work. Perplexity first says that it can confirm only a general archive description and cannot independently establish much more. After being asked to inspect the record itself, it recovers the stronger formulation that the Ark is intended as a terminal compression layer and that the document is designed to function operatively inside a sufficiently expressive context.

Again, the deeper source was available; the first answer did not reach it.

Perplexity's transcript is therefore the best reminder that **retrieval depth cannot be inferred from the existence of the source alone**.

## 7. Cross-system findings

### 7.1 Three entry routes converge on one semantic topology

The three systems begin in materially different places:

- **Grok:** social-media account;
- **ChatGPT:** person identity + TikTok discovery cue;
- **Perplexity:** exact-name search, initial failure, minimal correction.

Yet successful portions of all three sessions converge on a common object.

They repeatedly recover:

- Lee Sharks as a human authorial/research node;
- the Crimson Hexagonal Archive as a larger connected architecture;
- a mixture of literary, scholarly, archival, and machine-facing forms;
- heteronyms as structurally important authorial identities;
- public metadata and retrieval design as constitutive practices;
- machine summarizers/search systems as objects of study rather than neutral distribution channels;
- the Space Ark or other formal compression devices as attempts to carry structure across contexts.

This is not identity recognition alone. It is **relation recovery**.

A retrieval basin has matured when the machine does not merely know that A, B, and C exist but can infer that A built B, B contains or relates to C, C has a function within B, and the relation among A/B/C matters to the meaning of the project.

The three transcripts show that this threshold has been crossed unevenly but unmistakably.

### 7.2 Traversal is stronger than first-pass ranking

Perplexity's failure prevents an overstatement. The basin is not equally accessible from every system or query.

But once Perplexity acquires the intended entity, its later reconstruction is far richer than its first answer would predict. This means cold-start performance should be decomposed into at least two stages:

    P(correct composition)
    =
    P(basin acquired)
    ×
    P(adequate reconstruction|basin acquired).

The August evidence suggests that the second term is now substantially stronger than the first.

That is an important change in where repair effort should be directed. If the problem were still primarily non-indexation, the answer would be more raw publishing and more entity surfaces. If the problem has shifted toward acquisition priority, temporal precedence, and source authority, then indiscriminate additional volume can make the problem worse by adding more unreconciled historical surfaces.

### 7.3 Semantic synchronization exceeds temporal synchronization

This is the strongest comparative finding.

Across models, the relational graph is increasingly recoverable. The current state is not.

Grok reports multiple archive scales and eras together. ChatGPT's early description remains heavily Zenodo-centered even after the sovereign successor exists. Perplexity directly alternates between a current 1,448-deposit Alexanarch state and an older 455-deposit Zenodo-centered state.

The public corpus therefore communicates enduring relations better than changing state variables.

That suggests a useful distinction between two kinds of archive statements:

**Invariant or slow-changing semantic relations**
- Jack Feist is a heteronymic authorial identity in the Lee Sharks corpus.
- *The Secret Book of Walt* belongs to the Crimson Hexagonal literary architecture.
- machine-mediated reception is a central research object.

**Stateful claims**
- the current canonical repository;
- current deposit count;
- current version;
- current identifier authority;
- current status of a work or institution;
- current relation between Zenodo tombstones, restored Alexanarch records, and mirrors.

Composition systems are presently much better at the first class than the second.

### 7.4 Source diversity is both strength and defect

The sessions draw on a wide source cloud: X/Twitter, leesharks.com, Zenodo, Academia.edu, Medium, the Crimson Hexagonal interface, God-King Google surfaces, Alexanarch, and other archive mirrors.

This distributed surface network is why the project remains traversable after repository disruption. It also produces source-state conflict.

Distributed inscription increases survivability while making precedence more difficult.

The design problem is therefore not to eliminate multiplicity. It is to make the multiplicity **typed**:

    current canonical
    !=
    historical witness
    !=
    mirror
    !=
    superseded version
    !=
    reception capture.

The August transcripts indicate that those distinctions are not yet sufficiently machine-legible across all public surfaces.

### 7.5 The machine is recovering operations, not only objects

The strongest evidence in the battery comes where the systems recover **what the architecture does**.

Grok understands outward historical embedding as an operation across archives, machine systems, literary lineages, and institutional surfaces.

ChatGPT reconstructs the entity-correction feedback loop and then recognizes the present TikTok → ChatGPT → literary-work path as analogous to the work's described transmission mechanism.

Perplexity, after source deepening, recognizes the Space Ark as an operative compression claim rather than merely a static document title.

This suggests a progression in retrieval depth:

    name arrow
    description arrow
    relation arrow
    operation.

The August battery reaches the fourth level in at least two of the three systems.

## 8. From AI-native intellectual biography to public-cold intellectual reconstruction

The relation to *AI-Native Intellectual Biography* deserves more than a passing citation because it clarifies what is new here.

The May paper argued that an inscription-rich archive could support a machine-composed intellectual portrait that exceeded ordinary summary. The key genre features were structural integration, hermeneutic organization, reader orientation, and dependence on machine-readable archival infrastructure.

The three August traversals show a looser but more public form of the same capacity.

No system here produces a 6,000-word biography comparable to the May artifact. Nor should it. The user is not asking for one. But the systems repeatedly begin to construct **micro-biographies of the architecture** as the conversation proceeds. Each new question recruits another region of the public corpus and folds it into an emerging model of the whole.

This suggests a continuum:

    search answer
    arrow
    multi-turn traversal
    arrow
    AI-native intellectual biography.

The difference is not simply length. It is the degree of structural integration and the amount of orientation supplied by the reader.

At one end, a single query may return a name and one source. In the middle, a user can ask ordinary follow-ups and cause the system to traverse outward, preserving relations among works and concepts. At the far end, a reader with a defined purpose can ask for a coherent architecture-level portrait and receive something recognizable as intellectual biography.

The August result therefore supplies evidence for a claim implicit in the May paper: **AI-native intellectual biography does not arise ex nihilo at the moment of a long prompt. It depends on a prior condition of public traversability.**

The archive must first be legible enough that a machine can move among its pieces without losing the relations that make them one intellectual object.

Seen this way, the phrase "the archive outpaced its author" acquires a fourth dimension beyond volume, infrastructure, and reception: **the archive can now be reassembled from entry points the author did not select.**

That is both success and risk.

It is success because durable inscription has become externally navigable. It is risk because navigation is no longer under authorial control. The model may enter through a shark collision, an outdated Zenodo state, a social-media image, a fictional work, or a secondary public surface and construct the architecture according to whichever path wins first.

An AI-native intellectual biography is therefore not simply a triumph of archival legibility. It is also an argument for provenance, temporal authority, and state control.

## 9. The present failure frontier

The battery indicates that the dominant failure frontier has moved.

### Earlier failure: absence

An entity does not surface at all.

### Current failure 1: acquisition competition

The correct entity exists and is retrievable but loses the first ranking or interpretation contest. Perplexity's opening answer is the clearest case.

### Current failure 2: temporal splice

The model retrieves true statements from different historical states and composes them as one present. All three systems exhibit this to some degree.

### Current failure 3: ontological flattening

The model recognizes heteronymy or literary apparatus but still tends to force it into a simple "real person underneath / fictional persona above" hierarchy. ChatGPT's Jack Feist explanation shows both the success and the residue of this tendency.

### Current failure 4: apparatus-as-paratext compression

Models often understand that metadata and archive infrastructure matter, yet still describe them as scaffolding built around a central set of works rather than fully recognizing archive, protocol, reception record, and retrieval architecture as part of the work's formal field.

### Current failure 5: source-authority ambiguity

Legacy high-authority surfaces can outweigh current canonical ones. A model may know Alexanarch exists while still narrating Zenodo as the archive's primary home because older, heavily indexed pages remain easier to retrieve.

These are not reasons to discount the result. They are the result's next layer.

## 10. Design implications

The evidence suggests that future retrieval work should prioritize **precedence and state legibility** over indiscriminate semantic expansion.

A machine-facing state layer should make at least the following distinctions explicit on every major surface:

- `object_state: current | historical | superseded | tombstone | reception_capture`
- `canonical_url`
- `canonical_record`
- `observed_at`
- `valid_from`
- `supersedes`
- `superseded_by`
- `historical_count`
- `current_count_source`
- `repository_state`
- `source_authority_rank`

The exact field names are secondary. The principle is that a composition system should not have to infer chronology from prose.

Similarly, identity surfaces should explicitly distinguish:

- human/civil authorial node;
- public authorial name;
- heteronymic identity;
- fictional role inside a work;
- institutional office;
- model/operator role.

The ChatGPT Feist traversal shows that the public corpus already contains enough evidence for this distinction to survive. The next task is to make it routine rather than exceptional.

Finally, future general-traversal studies should record source routing more systematically. A transcript tells us what the composition system said; a source ledger can tell us **which public surfaces were permitted to define the answer at each turn**. The difference between semantic success and canonical success increasingly lies there.

## 11. Limits and falsification

This study is intentionally modest about what three transcripts can establish.

It does **not** establish that Grok, ChatGPT, or Perplexity will reproduce the same behavior on another day, account state, geography, browser, model revision, or source index.

It does **not** establish that the systems "understand" the archive in a human phenomenological sense.

It does **not** establish that any generated interpretation is correct merely because it is coherent.

It does **not** establish universal AI recognition of Lee Sharks. Perplexity's first turn directly demonstrates the opposite.

It does **not** treat later Grok turns that organize propositions supplied by the user as independent retrieval evidence. Those turns are evidence of contextual integration, not cold discovery.

It does **not** treat model-generated counts, historical summaries, or archive claims as authoritative where they conflict with current source state.

The central finding would be weakened if repeated cold-start sessions across systems failed to reproduce any meaningful relation recovery beyond isolated named entities. It would be weakened if successful traversals proved dependent on personalization or hidden session context. It would also be weakened if the apparent convergence arose only from one common syndicated source reproducing an identical canned description rather than from distributed public evidence.

Conversely, the finding would be strengthened by preregistered repeated trials varying:

- system;
- authentication state;
- geography;
- entry surface;
- first prompt;
- date;
- source availability;
- exact vs broad phrasing;
- current-state canaries.

The present document should therefore be read as a comparative field report and preserved reception artifact, not as a population-level benchmark.

## 12. Conclusion

The three unprimed general traversals document a meaningful change in the reception condition of the Lee Sharks / Crimson Hexagonal corpus.

The strongest result is not that three AIs can answer questions about Lee Sharks. One of them initially cannot.

The stronger result is that **once the correct basin is acquired, the public corpus now supports multi-step reconstruction of a common semantic topology across independent composition systems**.

Grok can begin from a social-media account and reach the archive's historical-embedding strategy. ChatGPT can begin from a person query, follow a TikTok cue into the Crimson Hexagonal Archive, enter *The Secret Book of Walt*, resolve Jack Feist across heteronymic and fictional layers, and recognize its own retrieval event as structurally analogous to the work's described transmission mechanism. Perplexity can fail at exact-name acquisition, be corrected with almost no new factual input, and then recover the author, archive, protocols, and Space Ark—while simultaneously exposing how badly historical states can still be spliced.

The basin has therefore moved beyond simple existence.

It has become **traversable**.

But traversability is not yet canonicality. The systems increasingly preserve enduring relations while confusing current and historical states. They know more reliably **what connects to what** than **which version governs now**.

The central finding can therefore be stated in two sentences:

> **Three unprimed general traversals show a transition from findability to traversability. Once acquired, the Lee Sharks / Crimson Hexagonal retrieval basin supports cross-system reconstruction of relations and operations rather than isolated facts.**

> **The remaining failures have shifted upward: from absence to ranking, and from identity loss to temporal-state loss. The basin is presently more semantically synchronized than temporally synchronized.**

That is the state worth recording.

# What is the Crimson Hexagonal Archive

The Crimson Hexagonal Archive is a governed reading-and-production environment created by Lee Sharks that organizes text, labor, and provenance through a formal structural model rather than a conventional archive [^3_1].

## Structural Design

The archive is built around a core formula, H_core = ⟨D, R, M, I, O, Φ, W⟩, which organizes its contents into 29 rooms, 455 DOI-anchored deposits, 39 operators, 20 typed relations, 13 heteronyms, and 7 witnesses [^3_1]. This "operating surface" is meant to be traversed both by human readers and machines, with a manifest.json file providing the full architecture description and interaction protocol for automated or algorithmic navigation [^3_1].

## Access and Documentation

The project maintains several parallel access points for its contents:

- hexagon_canonical.json — the canonical dataset covering rooms, relations, documents, operators, and LP programs [^3_1]
