# What Is a /non Article? An Introduction to the Negative of the Negative (EA-NON-ARTICLE-01 v1.0)

# What Is a /non Article? An Introduction to the Negative of the Negative (EA-NON-ARTICLE-01 v1.0)

**Lee Sharks** · Crimson Hexagonal Archive · 25 October 2026 · CC BY 4.0

A /non article is a page of public knowledge about one thing (a poem, a war, a theory, a person, an operation) composed with every source it rests on shown, sentence by sentence. The articles are collected at [alexanarch.org/non](https://www.alexanarch.org/non/), the Negative of the Negative, which the Crimson Hexagonal Archive keeps. Each article answers a simple question: what is known about this thing once the sources that usually go unread are read?

## Where /non articles begin

When someone asks an answering system what something is (Google's AI Overview, an encyclopedia, a chatbot), the answer arrives as a settled description. It names the thing, gives its attributes, and cites a few sources. What it leaves out leaves no trace. The sources it surfaced but did not use, the disputes it smoothed over, and the work that made the thing known in the first place are absent from the answer, and an absence cannot be seen from inside an answer.

That absence is the negative. A /non article composes it back: the negative of the negative. The name is the archive's own, and it is a distinct referent from Hegel's negation of the negation.

## Three compositions, compared

A /non article compares up to three compositions of its subject.

**What the answering layer said.** The answer as a real system gave it, verbatim, with the source cards it showed, captured on a stated date. For most articles this is a Google AI Overview seated in the archive's [Capture Registry](https://www.alexanarch.org/captures/).

**What its disclosed sources say.** The same question recomposed from the full texts of the sources that answer disclosed, its cards. Nothing new is brought in. This composition shows what those disclosed sources support that the answer did not carry. It can be checked by anyone holding the same sources.

**What the sources say with the archive admitted.** The same composition again, with the Crimson Hexagonal Archive's papers admitted: what the archive says directly about the thing, how it relates the thing to its own objects, and the categories it applies.

Between the three, the article records two differences. The first shows what the disclosed sources support that the answer left out. The second shows what the archive adds.

An article that opens an existing answer carries all three. An article constructed for something no answering layer has composed, or that no field source treats, records that absence as a finding and composes from what there is. The operator Attributization was constructed this way, with no prior answer and no field sources, so its article rests on the archive's papers and says so.

The archive is admitted in one of two ways. Where the field already holds an account of the thing, the archive's sources sit beside the field's on equal terms. Where the archive supplies an ontology no other disclosed source provides, its ontology rules: the article calls this the witness case, and it applies to entries such as the Secret Book of Walt, the Capture Registry and Attributization. [The procedure](https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/1690/) (#1690) sets out both, and every article states its case.

## An example: Sappho 31

Asked about "sappho 31" on 7 October 2026, Google's AI Overview described a famous lyric poem of love and jealousy that "survives mostly intact because a later ancient critic quoted it in On the Sublime". It gave the poem's other name, *Ode to Anactoria*, and described the speaker's jealous reaction.

The [Sappho 31 article](https://www.alexanarch.org/non/sappho-31/) read the Overview's disclosed sources in full. They hold what the answer did not carry:

- **Where the poem breaks.** Four stanzas survive whole, then one line of a fifth, because the critic quoting it stops there: "Still, all must be endured, since even a poor…" That surviving line is itself disputed (one scholar reads "all must be dared"), and translators handle the broken stanza in opposite ways: one skips it, one makes it a call to action, one a moment of self-hating submission. The Overview's "mostly intact" carries none of this, so a reader cannot tell where the text ends or what the sources make of its missing ending.
- **The second name is a conjecture.** *Ode to Anactoria* rests on a guess that the poem's subject is Anactoria. The Overview gave it as a name, with no hedge.
- **The jealousy reading is contested.** Many critics deny it, and the sources say so. The Overview composed jealousy as the poem's description.
- **The speaker's gender is fixed by the Greek.** The sources read the poem as same-sex desire. The Overview named a "beloved woman" and left the speaker ungendered.

Then the archive is admitted. It adds a reading the disclosed field examined in this article does not hold: that the poem's κῆνος ("that man") is the future reader facing the inscribed text. The archive first records the idea on 9 November 2025, in [the Autumn Notebook](https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/991/) (#991), and argues it in full in [ΦΑΙΝΕΤΑΙ ΜΟΙ: Sappho 31 and the Inscription of the Future Reader](https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/1270/) (#1270, 9 January 2026), by Rebekah Cranes, a heteronym of Lee Sharks. The article carries the reading as an interpretation, with its source's own condition: "Future evidence may confirm, modify, or refute it."

## How to read a /non article

Each article has the same parts, from the top down.

1. **The lede.** One sentence on what the thing is, each clause tagged with the source that supports it.
2. **The knowledge object.** The article proper: plain prose with no provenance in the sentences, and every sentence's sources listed beneath it.
3. **The claims.** Each claim a sentence rests on, with its exact quote, its source and its location in that source. A reader can open the source and find the words.
4. **The differences.** What the answering layer left out of its own sources, and what the archive's admission adds.
5. **The kernel.** What the archive contributes, with each item's standing kept distinct (a documented finding, an interpretation, a stipulation, a hypothesis) and the conditions under which it would be judged. Whether scholarship takes a reading up and whether the evidence supports it are separate questions, and the kernel keeps them apart.
6. **The status.** Every article says what stage it has reached, its case, the version of the procedure it was composed under, and whether its ledgers have been audited. Nothing on /non is presented as final. The collection describes itself as "under construction, by design."

Every article also has its data, readable by machines at the same address (its `entity.json`), and a permanent identifier, an AXN in the family NON. When an article is composed again, the new composition becomes its next version, and the earlier one stays on the page.

## What the collection holds

As of 25 October 2026, /non has 25 entry pages, 24 of them composed; Allen Ginsberg's has been traversed and not yet composed. Most are on public subjects: Sappho 31, Aristotle's *Sophistical Refutations*, *Howl*, the Book of Revelation, the American Civil War, Marxism, model collapse, Google AI Overviews and Anne Carson among them. The crimson hexagon of Borges's "The Library of Babel" has an entry that reads Borges's object beside the archive's own entity of that name. Some entries are the archive's own objects: SPXI, a protocol; the Secret Book of Walt, a work; the Capture Registry, a dataset. And one is an operator, Attributization: the conversion of an act a source performs into a property of the thing a sentence describes, an operation the usual description of a thing conceals, composed as an object in its own right.

## How /non articles are made

Some articles are composed in session by the archive's author. Others are commissioned from [SPXI-gnΩsis](https://gnosis.spxi.dev), a service that composes an article for any thing it is asked about, Wikipedia notability not required. Its service is described in [its metadata packet](https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/1703/) (#1703). A commissioned article is checked against its manifest and lands in /non by itself, public under CC BY 4.0, and is published as a dataset on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/leesharks/negative-of-the-negative). Reading /non is free. Every article, its data and every quoted claim are open to anyone.

The current procedure is published as [The Negative of the Negative (EA-NEGONT-02 v0.9, #1690)](https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/1690/), and each article records the version it was composed under: some entries in the collection still stand at earlier versions, shown on their pages, and [the status page](https://www.alexanarch.org/non/about/) says which. The premise behind it is in [Opening the Encyclopedia (EA-WIKI-OPEN-01 v1.1, #1697)](https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/1697/). Its claim is that the settled description of a thing is the form in which an operation appears as a property of that thing. A reliable source is enough to recognize something. Only the source that carries the work is enough to reconstruct it. A /non article is built for reconstruction.
