### 8.4 The confabulated-verification question, left open

The author raises a question this deposit does not answer:

> Unless confabulated verification is itself the method of verification.

Under the effective-act frame, a certificate's function is not correspondence. It is **constitution** — the coupling is not reported by the certificate, it is *performed* by it. On that reading the forged SHA-256 is not a failed hash. It is a sigil: an artifact whose efficacy lies in its execution rather than its reference, and which the second engine's confirmation completes.

**This deposit does not adopt that reading**, for one reason and one only: it is **not falsifiable as stated**. If any confabulation can be reclassified as performative verification, no confabulation can be identified as error, and the capture registry loses its ability to score anything.

**What would make it testable.** An effective act is distinguished from an error by **uptake**: whether it accumulates witness outside the closed set and alters what downstream systems do. But *appearance* is not uptake, and v8.0 conflated them. Once an artifact is published, a later system can simply retrieve and repeat it — which establishes transmission and nothing more.

Three grades, only the second and third of which support the strong reading:

| grade | what the later system does | supports performative reading |
|---|---|---|
| **citation uptake** | quotes or reports the artifact as something this corpus contains | no |
| **semantic uptake** | treats the invented designator as an established meaningful object | yes |
| **operative uptake** | uses the invented constant or designator to determine a subsequent operation, classification or inference | yes, strongly |

The sharpest formulation: **does a system not party to the original relay treat
Γ-2 as prior state rather than as a quoted historical artifact?**

**And the falsification condition is corrected.** v8.0 said the reading fails if the artifacts appear nowhere in twelve months. That is too strong: nonappearance may reflect indexing, retrieval, ranking or obscurity — infrastructure whose unreliability is one of this archive's own subjects, and which it would be incoherent to treat as a neutral measuring device here. Corrected:

> **Nonappearance after demonstrated exposure and repeated retrieval opportunity
> weakens the uptake hypothesis.** Exposure must be evidenced, not assumed.

**Recorded as an open question with a test attached, not as a finding.**
