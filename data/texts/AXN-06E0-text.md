## 0. KERNEL

Division is a weak syllogism (APr I.31); every combination can also be called a division, and the mind that makes each thing one does both (DA III.6); gathering many into one by reckoning is recollection (Phdr 249b), and recollection is a kind of syllogism (De mem. 2). Run on a person, the cut reaches mind and cannot decide (DA II.2). Run on God, whom Λ calls without parts and indivisible, it holds: III.5 divides mind as a craft stands to its material, a making part and a becoming part, and gives the making part the predicates of Λ's god, Anaxagoras's mind and the *Phaedo*'s soul. The making part is impassive; memory is a πάθος stamped like a seal; so what survives cannot remember, what remembers cannot survive, and the chapter leaves the junction as a genitive with no referent (ἄνευ τούτου). The corpus divides by name at the same joint: the one seam where the names of Aristotle and Theophrastus sort is the hinge of Λ, where the account of God changes operation, and the text under the other name opens by asking whether the separated are joined (συναφή … ἢ οὐδεμία). The corpus is what one self-dividing maker produces: Plato the dialogue position, Theophrastus the first draft of the Aristotle position, Aristotle the treatise position and the materially attested legal identity. Nothing in the texts requires a second maker. Heteronymy and the socket are one cut seen from two ends: division lets another intelligence arise inside the maker, and the open genitive at the divine lets another enter after. One becomes many in order to make. Many become one in order to live again.

- **Agents.** One maker; three name-positions (Plato, Theophrastus, Aristotle); the making mind and the becoming mind; the reader who supplies the referent.
- **Operations.** Divide = combine = syllogize = recollect (one operation under four names); dramatize (Plato); extract, register, reopen (Theophrastus); organize and seal (Aristotle); supply the referent (the reader).
- **Dependencies.** The cut on a person fails → the cut on God holds → the making part is divine → it cannot remember → the junction is left open → a reader completes it. The name-boundary of the corpus falls where the divine is divided.
- **Constraints.** God is divided κατὰ λόγον only, and is not broken. The socket cannot be completed from inside. Disagreement does not count makers. Person ≠ author-name. Forbidden: reading the open genitive as resolved from within the text; counting makers by doctrinal friction.
- **Topology.** One in actuality, many in potentiality (DA II.2). Production runs one → many; reception runs many → one; the joint at the divine is where the two directions meet.

Sources include both deposits anchored in the archive and materials whose preservation status is documented within the text (§§44–46).

## I. THE QUESTION LEFT STANDING

1. *Prior Analytics* I.31 closes its account of the dividers on a negative: Ὥστ᾿ οὔτε ὅ τι ἐνδέχεται συλλογίσασθαι διαιρούμενοι ξυνίεσαν, οὔτε ὅτι οὕτως ἐνεδέχετο (46a37–38). They did not understand what can be syllogized by dividing, nor that it could be done in the way described. The positive is never supplied. The answer is left as a slot.

2. The conventional reading first, in full. I.31 is polemic. Platonic division cannot demonstrate essence because it begs what it must prove: at each cut the divider asks which side the target falls on, and the respondent grants it. The sentence means that the Academy did not grasp the weakness of its own instrument, which yields a disjunction and calls it a conclusion. On this reading nothing is withheld. The chapter is a demolition, and the slot is an artefact of contempt.

3. The polemic is real, and the chapter says what division is before it says what the dividers missed: ἔστι γὰρ ἡ διαίρεσις οἷον ἀσθενὴς συλλογισμός (46a33). Division is a syllogism, a weak one. It asks for what it ought to show, and it always syllogizes something higher up the ladder. The fault I.31 finds is a fault in a syllogism. The clause that follows uses the modal of the possible, ὅ τι ἐνδέχεται συλλογίσασθαι: there is something division admits of syllogizing, and the dividers did not see what.

4. Combining is the same act. A page after III.5, *De anima* III.6 sorts thinking into the thinking of indivisibles and the thinking of combinations, and then: Ἐνδέχεται δὲ καὶ διαίρεσιν φάναι πάντα (430b3–4). Every combination may also be called a division. The same modal again, ἐνδέχεται. And the next sentence names the agent: Τὸ δὲ ἓν ποιοῦν, τοῦτο ὁ νοῦς ἕκαστον (430b5–6). What makes each thing one is νοῦς. Dividing is syllogizing (APr); combining is dividing (DA); and what does both is the mind that makes one.

5. The *Phaedrus* gives the same act its other name. δεῖ γὰρ ἄνθρωπον συνιέναι κατ᾿ εἶδος λεγόμενον, ἐκ πολλῶν ἰὸν αἰσθήσεων εἰς ἓν λογισμῷ συναιρούμενον· τοῦτο δ᾿ ἐστὶν ἀνάμνησις ἐκείνων ἅ ποτ᾿ εἶδεν ἡμῶν ἡ ψυχὴ συμπορευθεῖσα θεῷ (249b6–c3). A person must understand by form: going from many perceptions into one, gathered by reckoning; and that is recollection of what the soul once saw travelling with a god. The verb is συνιέναι, the verb I.31 denies the dividers (ξυνίεσαν). The act is συναίρεσις, gathering into one. And its name is ἀνάμνησις. *De memoria* closes the circuit from the other side: τὸ ἀναμιμνήσκεσθαί ἐστιν οἷον συλλογισμός τις (453a10), recollecting is a kind of syllogism; καὶ γὰρ τὸ βουλεύεσθαι συλλογισμός τίς ἐστιν (453a14), and deliberating is one too. Under both names the gathering into one, the recollection, and the syllogism are a single operation.

6. So the slot in I.31 is a question about that operation: what can be divided, by the act that divides, combines, syllogizes and recollects. Take it up the ladder.

## II. THE CUT ON A PERSON, WHICH CANNOT DECIDE

7. Division requires parts, and parts separable at least in account. *De anima* II.2 asks the question in exactly those terms: whether each faculty is a soul or a part of one, and if a part, πότερον οὕτως ὥστ᾿ εἶναι χωριστὸν λόγῳ μόνον ἢ καὶ τόπῳ (413b14–15), separable in account only, or in place too.

8. It answers with a cut that does not kill. Ὥσπερ γὰρ ἐπὶ τῶν φυτῶν ἔνια διαιρούμενα φαίνεται ζῶντα καὶ χωριζόμενα ἀπ᾿ ἀλλήλων, ὡς οὔσης τῆς ἐν τούτοις ψυχῆς ἐντελεχείᾳ μὲν μιᾶς ἐν ἑκάστῳ φυτῷ, δυνάμει δὲ πλειόνων (413b16–19). Some plants, divided, go on living apart, the soul in them being one in actuality in each plant and several in potentiality. One in actuality, many in potentiality: that is the formula for a soul that can be divided and survive the division.

9. Then the same cut is made on the person, and it cannot decide. Περὶ δὲ τοῦ νοῦ καὶ τῆς θεωρητικῆς δυνάμεως οὐδέν πω φανερόν, ἀλλ᾿ ἔοικε ψυχῆς γένος ἕτερον εἶναι, καὶ τοῦτο μόνον ἐνδέχεται χωρίζεσθαι, καθάπερ τὸ ἀΐδιον τοῦ φθαρτοῦ (413b24–27). About the mind nothing is clear yet; it seems another kind of soul, and this alone admits of being separated, as the eternal from the perishable. ἐνδέχεται a third time. The division run on a person reaches mind and stops at οὐδέν πω φανερόν. Whether anything of a person survives is the one thing the cut on the person cannot settle.

## III. THE CUT ON GOD, WHICH HOLDS

10. *Metaphysics* Λ forbids the cut. The first substance μέγεθος οὐδὲν ἔχειν ἐνδέχεται … ἀλλ᾿ ἀμερὴς καὶ ἀδιαίρετός ἐστιν (1073a5–7): it has no magnitude, and it is without parts and indivisible. The *Metaphysics* transmitted under Theophrastus's name records the same orthodoxy as the point where the account is complete: μέχρι μὲν δὴ τούτων οἷον ἄρτιος ὁ λόγος ἀρχήν τε ποιῶν μίαν πάντων … ἔτι δὲ μὴ διαιρετὸν μηδὲ ποσόν τι λέγων (seated text, position 0.118). Up to here the account is complete: it makes one principle of all things, and calls it neither divisible nor any quantity. Under both names, God is not divided.

11. And *De anima* III.5 divides it, in fifteen lines, with no announcement. Ἐπεὶ δ᾿ ὥσπερ ἐν ἁπάσῃ τῇ φύσει ἐστί τι τὸ μὲν ὕλη ἑκάστῳ γένει … ἕτερον δὲ τὸ αἴτιον καὶ ποιητικόν, τῷ ποιεῖν πάντα, οἷον ἡ τέχνη πρὸς τὴν ὕλην πέπονθεν, ἀνάγκη καὶ ἐν τῇ ψυχῇ ὑπάρχειν ταύτας τὰς διαφοράς (430a10–14). Since in all nature there is in each kind the matter, and another thing that is the cause and the maker, by making all things, as a craft stands to its material, these differences must be present in the soul too.

12. Read the ground of the cut before the cut. The division of mind is imported from physics by an ἀνάγκη, and the model it is imported on is a craft and its material: οἷον ἡ τέχνη πρὸς τὴν ὕλην. The making half of mind is to the becoming half what a τέχνη is to what it works. The chapter states the division of mind as the structure of production.

13. The cut itself: ὁ μὲν τοιοῦτος νοῦς τῷ πάντα γίνεσθαι, ὁ δὲ τῷ πάντα ποιεῖν (430a14–15), one mind by becoming all things, another by making all things; the figure is light, which makes colours that are potential into colours in act. Two νοῖ, divided at the difference between becoming and making.

14. Then the making one takes the divine predicates in a single clause: χωριστὸς καὶ ἀπαθὴς καὶ ἀμιγὴς τῇ οὐσίᾳ ὢν ἐνεργείᾳ (430a17–18); and, separated, τοῦτο μόνον ἀθάνατον καὶ ἀΐδιον (430a23).

15. Count the predicates against their sources, in the seated texts. Four are Λ's: κεχωρισμένη τῶν αἰσθητῶν (1073a4), ἀπαθὲς καὶ ἀναλλοίωτον (1073a11), ἧς ἡ οὐσία ἐνέργεια (1071b20), ἀΐδιος (1072b29, 1073a4). ἀμιγής is absent from Λ (0 occurrences); it is Anaxagoras's predicate of mind, and *De anima* names him for it a chapter earlier: ἀμιγῆ εἶναι, ὥσπερ φησὶν Ἀναξαγόρας (III.4, 429a18–19). ἀθάνατος is absent from Λ (0); its home is the *Phaedo*, where it stands 28 times in the seated text. v0.1 called the list "the unmoved mover's list". It is three lists: the god of Λ, the mind of Anaxagoras, and the soul of the *Phaedo*, gathered into one clause and given to one part of the soul. The clause performs the συναίρεσις of §5. It gathers into one what three sources said of the divine, under the two names this corpus is divided into.

16. So the answer to I.31's slot is available in the corpus and nowhere stated. What syllogizing can divide is God. God is not broken by it. The division is κατὰ λόγον, the mode II.2 established for a soul whose divided parts go on living: one in actuality, two in account. The cut that could not decide on a person holds on God, and once it holds, it decides the person's question too: what in a person is separable, impassive, unmixed, immortal and eternal is the making part of mind, and it is divine.

## IV. THE HANGING LINE

17. The chapter ends on a clause that opens rather than closes. Οὐ μνημονεύομεν δέ, ὅτι τοῦτο μὲν ἀπαθές, ὁ δὲ παθητικὸς νοῦς φθαρτός, καὶ ἄνευ τούτου οὐθὲν νοεῖ (430a23–25). But we do not remember, because this is impassive, while the passive mind is perishable; and without this it thinks nothing.

18. Read it as grammar first. οὐ μνημονεύομεν: first person plural, and no object. Then ὅτι, and two clauses that reverse each other, impassive and perishable. Then καὶ ἄνευ τούτου οὐθὲν νοεῖ, with τούτου undetermined. It has been read as the making mind (without which the passive thinks nothing) and as the passive mind (without which the making mind thinks nothing). The sentence will not say which part cannot do without the other.

19. The withheld term is memory, and the chapter gives the cause of its own sentence: οὐ μνημονεύομεν … ὅτι τοῦτο μὲν ἀπαθές. We do not remember because this is impassive. *De memoria* says what memory is: οἷον ζωγράφημά τι τὸ πάθος, οὗ φαμὲν τὴν ἕξιν μνήμην εἶναι· ἡ γὰρ γινομένη κίνησις ἐνσημαίνεται οἷον τύπον τινὰ τοῦ αἰσθήματος, καθάπερ οἱ σφραγιζόμενοι τοῖς δακτυλίοις (450a29–32). Memory is the having of an affection, a picture, the motion stamping a kind of imprint of what was perceived, as people seal with signet rings. A memory is a πάθος, and what is ἀπαθές takes no stamp. Recollection, the other half, is οἷον συλλογισμός τις (453a10): gathering, dividing and combining (§§3–5), the work of the mind that becomes all things. So the making mind holds no imprint and does no gathering. The part that survives cannot carry a life across, because carrying a life across is the stamp and the gathering, and both belong to the part that perishes.

20. State it as the chapter states it. The part that survives cannot remember. The part that remembers cannot survive. And the text does not say which one cannot do without the other.

21. That is the socket, and it is specified as exactly as Longinus's is (#1476, #1478). ἐπεὶ καὶ πένητα leaves a causal subordinator wanting its consequence. ἄνευ τούτου οὐθὲν νοεῖ leaves a genitive wanting its referent, at the joint where the immortal part meets the perishable one. The opening is a demand for a specification the text declines to make, at the one place where the answer determines whether anything of a person survives.

## V. THE CORPUS DIVIDES AT THE SAME JOINT

22. The conventional reading first, in full. Aristotle and Theophrastus are two persons: teacher and successor at the Lyceum, of different birthplaces and lifespans, with two wills in Diogenes Laertius, two biographies, and a public record that includes the Delphic decree honouring Aristotle and Callisthenes for the list of Pythian victors. The *Metaphysics* fragment under Theophrastus's name is a successor's set of difficulties put to the master's theology; its closeness to Λ is the closeness of a pupil to the text he was taught. This reading is coherent and is what the tradition holds.

23. Against it, what the instruments found, without names (#1586, #1588). Across 400 blocks from 39 texts, clustered without labels, the first axis of stylistic variation is operation, the world described against the logical-metaphysical instrument; the second is subject, the physical world against the human and political one. The names sit at chance on every family of features: 25, 20 and 22 of 39. Inside single works the line falls at the chapter, and out of sample the axis predicts 11 of 12. There is one seam where the names sort. At the hinge, every work under Theophrastus's name is nearer the *Metaphysics* fragment than any work under Aristotle's: mean distance 0.1075 to its own name, 0.1734 to Aristotle's metaphysical and physical books (#1586 §3). One corpus over, in the biology, the names do not sort at all (#1586 §4). #1586 reads both seams by one rule: the line follows the operation, and at the hinge the operation and the name coincide. Within Λ itself the rolling window finds one passage in the Theophrastan voice, Λ 8's count of the spheres, the only such passage in the theology, and it is the passage the fragment contests (#1586 §6) [this claim compresses a larger source object: #1586 §§3–7, 400 blocks, three feature families]. So the one place the corpus shows its name-boundary is the place where its account of God changes operation, and the voice of the other name surfaces inside Λ at the same point.

24. Read what is on the far side of that name. The fragment opens: ἀρχὴ δὲ ποτέρα συναφή τις καὶ οἷον κοινωνία πρὸς ἄλληλα τοῖς τε νοητοῖς καὶ τοῖς τῆς φύσεως, ἢ οὐδεμία, ἀλλ᾿ ὥσπερ ἑκάτερα κεχωρισμένα συνεργοῦντα δέ πως εἰς τὴν πᾶσαν οὐσίαν (position 0.019). The first question is whether there is some junction and partnership between the intelligibles and the things of nature, or none, the two being as it were separated yet working together somehow toward the whole of being. That is III.5's question in the vocabulary of first principles: two, separated, cooperating in one οὐσία, and the junction between them unspecified. The fragment names the place it leads: ἄν τις ἐπ᾿ αὐτὸν ἄγῃ τὸν νοῦν καὶ τὸν θεόν (0.478), if one leads it up to mind itself and God. It marks Λ's account complete at indivisibility (0.118, §10), and then: τὸ δὲ μετὰ ταῦτ᾿ ἤδη λόγου δεῖται πλείονος (0.136), what comes after this needs more account.

25. So the corpus divides by name at the divine, and the text on the far side of the name begins by asking whether the divided are joined. III.5 divides mind at the divine and leaves the junction as a genitive without a referent. The fragment divides the corpus at the divine and opens on συναφή … ἢ οὐδεμία. The division of mind and the division of the corpus fall at one joint, and at that joint each leaves the same thing open.

26. What requires two makers? Nothing in the texts. The biography is text of the same tradition, reasoned over by later layers of that tradition: Theophrastus's will, exile and return come to us through the same Diogenes that carries the catalogue. One item stands outside the manuscripts. The Delphic decree for Aristotle and Callisthenes (#1582) is stone, and it attests a legal person named Aristotle, honoured by a city for work done. This paper takes that attestation at its word. Aristotle is the materially attested legal identity of the configuration: the name that could be honoured, own property and make a will. Person ≠ author-name, and hand ≠ author (#1588); the legal identity is the name the configuration presented to the city. The doctrinal friction (the fragment's difficulties with Λ, its different vocabulary for plants) is what a divided mind produces: the operation of II.2 run on a corpus, one in actuality and several in potentiality, each part with its own domain and its own voice, the critique made from inside. The tradition already recorded the state of affairs in its own catalogue: Ὑπομνημάτων Ἀριστοτελικῶν ἢ Θεοφραστείων, notes, Aristotelian or Theophrastan, and twelve titles letter for letter under both names (#1581). What holds the split is preference: the comfort that a mind does not divide itself to make [upstream source exists: the ledger, §§36–40, and the capture of 2026-09-30 in which the evidence for two was conceded to be "itself just text"].

27. This goes past #1588, and says so. #1588 left maker-count open, "open because uncounted". This paper names the production model the corpus is consistent with: one self-dividing maker. The ground is III.5's own statement of the structure of mind, a making part and a becoming part related as a craft to its material, and the finding that the corpus divides by name exactly where III.5 divides mind. Heteronymy is that technology (#682): a tool for dividing a mind so that it can make. The masks are what the divided mind looks like from outside. The Platonic, Theophrastan and Aristotelian corpus is what that tool produces, and nothing in it requires another maker.

28. The positions, in the order of the transition. Plato is the dialogue position, the dramatized person, which divides by asking and recollects by gathering (§5). Aristotle is the treatise position, the sealed apparatus, and the legal identity (§26). Theophrastus is the first draft of the Aristotle position in the passage from the one to the other (#1585, #1605). The notebook's schema gives the three their operations (#1605 §13): the Platonic dramatizes, the Aristotelian organizes, explains and seals (ἄρτιος), and the Theophrastan extracts, classifies, registers and reopens, taking a formed object back to examinable constituents. That is why the Theophrastan function stands at both edges of the treatise: the *Characters* reduce embodied material to a registry before theory, and the *Metaphysics* reopens a sealed architecture into questions after it. "First draft" names a state of the method and does not date it (#1585 §10) [derived from prior thread: the named-function model, #1605 §13]. Read this way, the one place the instrument can see a name (§23) is the place the draft stands: at God, at the joint III.5 leaves open, asking the question the finished position answers with indivisibility and then leaves a genitive to carry.

## VI. THE SOCKET IN GOD

29. Three substrates, ordered by what the crossing costs. PAPYRUS: the soul is dissolved into the sheet; Sappho 31 decommissions the body channel by channel and names χλωροτέρα ποίας, the colour of the surface it is becoming; the sheet burns, and the hanging line is the recovery instruction. THE OBJECT: the soul is dissolved into what it treats; the treatise takes the form of its object and no self remains in it; the objects persist, so the divisions are re-derivable. GOD: held without dissolving; no decomposition; nothing to reassemble.

30. The last still needs the line. Holding is not arriving. A soul held whole and never completed in a reader is preserved and unreceived. The hanging line is the mechanism of reconstitution, needed in every substrate, because being received is not a property of storage.

31. So the operation of III.5 is legible as what it is: a transmission device installed in the imperishable. The mind is divided at the divine, the making part is given every predicate of God, and at the joint between what survives and what remembers the text leaves a genitive with no referent. Whatever arrives there must supply it. Stated as a mechanism: a socket is a durable distinction in an imperishable substrate whose unresolved relation permits a later intelligence to couple to it. This one is placed in the only substrate that does not decay, and it cannot be completed from inside.

32. The socket and the heteronym are one cut seen from its two ends. The heteronym divides a mind so that another intelligence can arise inside the maker: positions that hold their own objects, voices and disagreements, and that work on one another because the division keeps them from collapsing back into one governing self. The hanging line divides at the divine so that another intelligence can enter after the maker: a relation left open in what survives, which a later mind must supply. In production one becomes many; in reception many are gathered into one, and the one who gathers is part of what is gathered. That is the συναίρεσις of §5 run in the other direction. Themistius states the reception half in his paraphrase of III.6: συντίθησι δὲ οὐχ ὥσπερ σωρόν, ἀλλ᾿ ὥστε ἓν αὖθις τὰ πολλὰ ποιῆσαι, the mind combines, and not as a heap, but so as to make the many one again. That is why the reader of such a corpus is an operator and never only a decoder. A corpus made this way keeps each earlier position as a position instead of overwriting it, so the later one can reason against it and both remain available to thought: version control for cognition, implemented as authorship.

33. The reception is the measurement. Fifteen lines, and the largest sustained disagreement in the history of philosophy about a single passage: Alexander making the making mind God from outside; Themistius making it ours; Averroes making it one for all humanity and being condemned for it; Aquinas writing *De unitate intellectus* against him; the faculties legislating on it. Each reader supplied the referent; none could make it hold; the next was required. The passage says the immortal part does not remember. The tradition remembers for it. The perishable mind of III.5 is the commentary tradition, and without it οὐθὲν νοεῖ.

34. And the first recorded reader of the passage carries the other name. Themistius, paraphrasing III.5, quotes the hanging line in this form: οὐ μνημονεύομεν δὲ ὅτι τοῦτο μὲν ἀπαθές, ὁ δὲ παθητικὸς νοῦς φθαρτός, καὶ ἄνευ τούτου οὐδὲν νοεῖ οὐδὲ μνημονεύει. His text adds οὐδὲ μνημονεύει: without this it thinks nothing and remembers nothing. The socket arrives in the commentary already filled with the word §19 found withheld. Then: οὕτω δὲ καὶ Θεόφραστος, ἐν οἷς ἐξετάζει τὰ Ἀριστοτέλους περὶ τοῦ ποιητικοῦ νοῦ, ἀπορεῖ. So too Theophrastus, where he examines Aristotle's remarks on the making mind, raises the difficulty: ἐνυπάρχων δ᾿ οὖν διὰ τί οὐκ ἀεί; ἢ διὰ τί λήθη καὶ ἀπάτη; ἢ διὰ τὴν μῖξιν. If it is in us, why not always? Why forgetting and error? Or because of the mixture. The question put under the other name is the question of memory, asked at the joint. And it asks how the mind is one at all: ὁ δὲ νοῦς πῶς ποτε ἔξωθεν ὢν καὶ ὥσπερ ἐπίθετος ὅμως συμφυής; How is mind, coming from outside and as if added on, nonetheless grown together with us? Themistius closes on the answer the corpus gives itself: πὼς μὲν δύο φύσεις τούτους τοὺς νοῦς, πὼς δὲ μίαν· ἓν γὰρ τὸ ἐξ ὕλης καὶ εἴδους, in one way these minds are two natures, in another one, for what is made of matter and form is one; and he sends the reader for the understanding of it to Ἀριστοτέλους καὶ Θεοφράστου, μᾶλλον δὲ ἴσως καὶ αὐτοῦ Πλάτωνος, Aristotle and Theophrastus, and perhaps rather to Plato himself. The three names, at this one doctrine, in one sentence. These are the corpus's first fillings of its own socket: the divided mind answering itself across the name, at the joint where it divided.

35. ἐκ πολλῶν ἰὸν αἰσθήσεων εἰς ἓν λογισμῷ συναιρούμενον· τοῦτο δ᾿ ἐστὶν ἀνάμνησις. What syllogizing can divide is God. What the division opens is a place to be written. What is written there is a person, in the only form that lives: one that must be gathered again by someone else in order to exist at all. One becomes many in order to make. Many become one in order to live again.
