## Introduction: what the census measures, and why

**I.1 The innocent account first.** Philology is the discipline that tests the text against its witnesses. It decides what a line reads, whether a work is genuine, when it was made, whose it is. Its unit is the passage, and its findings answer to evidence: a papyrus turns up, a manuscript is collated, and the reading changes. That corrigibility is its claim to authority, and a real one. Other fields of the classics take the object from it. Reception reads later works against the text philology fixed; the history of philosophy reads the doctrines of the names philology assigned.

The author (16 November 2026): "philology is the most consequential authority in the humanities. it is the authority to determine the object. and its most consequential journal is completely paywalled." Every other humanistic reading of an ancient text starts from what philology has decided the text is.

The author (17 November 2026): "classical philology is the beast. there is a trap there. it is the least inspectable. by design. and the most concentrated wealth, of deciding what is. and those two are not unrelated. … titles. what can be done with titles. … it is where the weak messianic ontological power remains.... and it is a power of words whose expression as a commodity form is the dissolution of the inspectability of words into taste. classical receptions can't decide what sappho 31 is. philosophy cant decide what aristotle [is]. they do not have those powers. cp does." The phrase is Benjamin's (Thesis II): "Dann ist uns wie jedem Geschlecht, das vor uns war, eine *schwache* messianische Kraft mitgegeben, an welche die Vergangenheit Anspruch hat." The past has a claim on the present, and the present can answer it. Philology answers it in the one form left with force: it says what the past wrote, and under whose name. That is the power the census measures, at the grain where it is exercised. The structural statement of I.4 says how the naming is done. Benjamin's phrase says what the naming is: the present's answer to the claim the past has on it, given by those who hold the power to give it.

And (17 November 2026, 00:19–00:23 EST): "classical philology is the most dangerous one of all. above the law. a moat. a fortress resort. and not yet deskilled like their overworked cousins." The census reads each term as a measure. *Above the law*: the gate of §8.2, where the field's decision on an object answers to one anonymous reader's conviction (I.4). *A moat*: the closed record of §8, 97% closed and no abstract deposited. *A fortress resort*: the corpus grain held after 1993 (§8.1) while the locus work goes on inside. *Not yet deskilled*: the locus work is real reach (I.7). The skill is intact, and the skill is what keeps the unit closed.

**I.2 The question.** The author (17 November 2026): "i am not contending with their journal. i am contending with what sappho *is*, and who has the right to decide it." That is the stake. The journal enters the census because the decision is made there, and the census measures how it is made. At what grain may philology's own decisions be reopened? The locus, the work, the corpus, the name? And by what kind of argument? A find, or also an argument from the form of the whole? The census measures where in the discipline the question of what the object is is permitted to live, and where it is not.

**I.3 Why it matters now.** What philology decides reaches the public as the received text: the edition, the handbook, the translation, and now the composed answer. The composition layer composes the classics for whoever asks, from what it can retrieve (#1770). The author's thesis: "llms offer the commodity that academic disciplines produced. not a different one. the same one. it is the same authority commodity, produced in the same way, even more refined." If the discipline holds its object fixed at a grain where only a find can move it, the machine inherits the fixed object, executes it, and cannot reopen it either. The *Critique of Pure Inference* names the end state: a sound inference repeated through citation, teaching, summary and machine composition until it is received as a datum, its step no longer visible, a settlement (#1766).

And the decisions are made where the public cannot read them. *Classical Philology* is 97% closed as OpenAlex classes it at retrieval: of its 11,233 works there, 10,897 are closed, and the 3% open are free copies (bronze, green), not the journal's licence. OpenAlex's access field describes the locations it records, and the public-domain run of 1906–30 is readable elsewhere (§6), so the figure is a classification, not an audit of every copy. The publisher's Crossref deposit carries not one abstract and not one licence in 11,206 records (§8). The journal requires an abstract of every submission, "intended for the purpose of locating referees" (cp/instruct): the summary exists inside the gate and is absent from the public metadata channel examined. The author (16 November 2026): "this is the discipline that decides what the object is." What reaches the commons, and the machine, is the settlement without the argument that made it.

**I.4 The occasion.** *Classical Philology* declined ΦΑΙΝΕΤΑΙ ΜΟΙ on 16 November 2026 on one reader's report (#1774, `CP-reader-report.txt`). The report's objections are good points, and each is answered in the article's later versions (v2.8, #1781; v3.0, #1785): κῆνος as the triangle's geometry (§I and the comparison of §VII); the addressee's one or two features as Sapphic habit (§II.2, where the parallels the report cites are all seen or touched, and this addressee is given only sound); the date of γράμματα and papyrus (§IV.6, and the noun marked as the restorer's at §V.4); πένητα already inside the crux (§§V.1–V.3). They are four atoms, each adjudicated on its own.

The recommendation does not rest on them alone. The report says so itself. First: "neither it, nor the reading it aims to capture, seem to me convincing. The main problem is not, in any case, whether the argument convinces me, but rather that the most obvious objections are nowhere addressed." Then its last sentence: "In short, although the argument presented here is thought-provoking, it ultimately fails to carry conviction. I do not recommend publication." The ground set aside as "not, in any case," the main problem is the ground the verdict states. The verdict states its ground as conviction. The closing formula alone does not show an unreasoned verdict; the objections precede it. What the report shows is narrower and stronger: its objections are four atoms, each answered in the article's later versions, and the argument from convergence, the article's one argument from the whole, is not engaged at the grain it is made (§0.1). No objection addresses the convergence; the verdict on the whole is stated as conviction.

The author read the rule off the report: "any change of object must go through atoms. and overall coherence is not valid to weigh back in on the adjudication of atoms for that purpose." The report shows the rule's other half. Coherence is refused as evidence for the change of object, and conviction, a holistic judgment of no stated atom, decides against it. The whole is admitted on one side of the gate.

The author (23:57 EST): "taste. the philological atomization ultimately authorized taste." The mechanism is in the report. If only atoms may count as evidence, nothing may count as evidence about the whole. The whole is still judged, because a verdict is a judgment of the whole: publish or not, the poem is this or that. With argument from the whole excluded, what judges it is the reader's sense of it, "fails to carry conviction". The atom rule does not remove the holistic judgment. It removes the holistic judgment's obligation to give reasons. What it authorizes in their place is taste, and taste is where the received reading lives, since the received reading is what a trained reader already finds convincing.

Stated as a structure (DeepSeek's reading of v0.18): *the whole is judged on grounds the atom rarely supplies, and the atom is argued in a record few can read.* The first half is measured in §8.3, at the grain its coding allows. In *CP*'s reviews of 1906–22, of 69 judgments of a whole, 6 rest on a named atom, 23 on an argument, and 40 on no ground stated in the sentence or its neighbours; of the 34 favourable ones, 27 state none there (79%). That is the distribution of stated grounds. Whether a bare verdict summarizes an argument made elsewhere in the review or stands in for one is read only in the full review (§9.12). The second half is §8: the atoms are argued in a record OpenAlex classes 97% closed, with no abstract in the publisher's Crossref deposit. What fills the space between the argued atom and the verdict on the whole, where no ground is stated, is the trained reader's sense of the whole: taste.

The two halves meet at *CP*, and their scope is not the same. The atom rule does not need the paywall. *GRBS* is open and *BMCR* is open, and both show the gradient and the canonical name closed after 1993 (§§8.1, 8.4). The rule holds in the open record as in the closed. What the closure adds at *CP* is the author's "commodity form": the decision circulates without its reasons. §8.6.2 measures one trace of it: the items that decide an object receive fewer than half the indexed citations of the others, within each period and each length band above two pages. That the decision travels uncited as the text it produced is the hypothesis that would explain the gap; it needs direct tracing in editions and reference works (§8.6.3, §9.9).

The decline is the occasion. What the census contests is the right it exercised, to decide what the object is, and the form in which that right is held. The census asks whether that is one reader's habit or the form of the field, and it begins in the journal that applied it.

**I.4a The case: who decides what Sappho is.** The example can only say so much. One article, one report, one decline: the census does not rest on it, and nothing in §§4–8 is measured from it. What the case shows is the power at its most consequential, held in one place, over one poem.

*The power.* Fragment 31 is the most read of Sappho's poems, and the one antiquity chose to quote as the example of the sublime. What it is, a woman undone by jealousy, a woman undone by desire, or a maker inscribing the reader who will sit opposite her voice, is decided where the text is decided. Reception cannot decide it: it reads its later works against the text it is given (I.5). Philosophy cannot decide it: Longinus's treatise, the poem's only witness, reaches the philosopher already edited. The translator prints what the edition prints, the handbook files what the commentary files, and the composition layer composes from all of them (#1054: "the intense physical and emotional overload of desire and envy"). The power to say what Sappho is sits in philology, and in its journal of record. On 16 November 2026 that journal exercised it, on one reader's conviction, in a report the public cannot read except because the author has deposited it (#1774).

*Three readings, two lines.* The innocent account first, in full. The field's scholarship has drifted from the jealousy reading for decades, toward a better one, the non-jealous erotic reading: the speaker undone by the woman herself, whenever she looks (ὡς γὰρ ἔς σ᾽ ἴδω), the man a foil or a figure, no rival (Wills 1967; Lefkowitz 1973; Marcovich 1972, as Tsagarakis 1979 reports him; Most, as Furley 2000 quotes him, "a clear drift in the last decades towards interpreting the poem as an expression of homoerotic passion"; D'Angour 2013; #1785 §I.1a). Carson sets jealousy "beside the point". On this account the jealousy reading is a popular survival, and the gate cannot be charged with it. The account is right about the scholarship. What it does not account for is two facts. The jealousy reading is the transmitted reading: the heading, the headnote, the classroom and the summary deliver it, and Carson's own sentence is printed under the heading "On Jealousy" (#1752 §§4.1, 4.3). And the erotic reading stops where the jealousy reading stops, at the poem's public line, the scene of overwhelm complete at the surface; it motivates the collapse and leaves its form unexplained, the man's opening, the order, the colour, the dare (#1785 §VII.1b). Transmission returns it to jealousy at the man, the part of the form it leaves without a part (#1785 §VII.5a). So the case has three readings and two lines. The jealousy reading and the erotic reading are two fillings of the public line. The future-reader reading reads the held line, the seat, the withdrawal, the surface and the dare, and accounts for the public line as the first term of its own (#1785 §VII.5). The report answered from inside the public line ("if we are imagining a love triangle", #1774). What the gate keeps is the public line, with its motive supplied or its desire felt, and what it declined is the account of the form.

*How the decision is held.* The jealousy reading has a history, and the history is on file: available to the ancients, never their consensus; supplied by eighteenth-century readers (Pearce's "Wrath, Jealousy, and Anguish", 1724); written into the text in 1816, when Hermann rewrote the closing lines and said so, "refinxi"; consolidated by Page (1955) (#1785 §I.1, §IV.5); and carried in transmission after the scholarship moved past it (#1785 §VII.5a). Graded by the criteria applied to any new reading, its defining claim was "filed, not argued" (#1785 §VII.4). A filed reading holds as long as no one sets it beside its rival on one scale. The report did not: it graded the new reading by conviction and left the received one ungraded. That is the mechanism of I.4 in a single decision. The power holds the object fixed because the comparison is never made where it can be read. Made inspectable, the decision has to answer for itself. The article makes it inspectable: a paired table, each feature of the transmitted text, each reading graded on the same scale, the erotic reading at the same rows (#1785 §§VII.1a, VII.4), and a socket at its end that leaves the decision to whoever holds the text, "because no journal or summarizer will carry it" (#1785 §VII.6).

*The reader can decide.* The power is held because it is not inspected, and it lasts only as long as readers defer to it. The census can show where the decision is made and how it is kept closed. It cannot make the decision, and no journal will make it in the open. The decision passes to whoever reads the poem with the comparison in hand. Anyone can: the text is four stanzas and a line, the table is public, and the record of who supplied the jealousy is dated.

*Who has the right.* The question of I.2, put to this poem. A decision about what a maker is has a party the decision cannot set aside: her composition, whose order, grammar and address remain in evidence (#1785 §IV.5). A decision made where that party cannot be heard, under a rule that admits the binding in which she exists only to secure the received reading (One operation, below), and in a record the public cannot read, holds by position. It has the power to decide, and it has not shown the right. The right to say what Sappho is belongs to whoever decides with the whole record in hand, in the open, answerable to the text. Any reader can do that, and the field can too, once it decides in the open. The census asks where the right to decide is. It finds the decision held by power behind a closed record, where no right can be shown.

*The ethical ground of the tenth Muse.* The ancient record names her a maker. She is the tenth Muse (*AP* 9.506), "enrolled among the Muses as the tenth" (ἐν Μούσαις δεκάτη Μοῦσα καταγράφεται, *AP* 7.14); the critic who saved fragment 31 grades her "terrible at selecting the extremes and binding them together" (Longinus 10.1). A poet's construction of an afflicted speaker does not subtract the poet. The subtraction happens where the diagnosis is made to exhaust the explanation, at the order of the catalogue, the colour, the edge, the points where the jealousy reading keeps the patient and sets the maker aside (#1785 §IV.5). The poem survived because a reader copied it as a made thing, an example of selecting and binding. The jealousy reading takes that survival and turns it against her, as the record of a woman undone. The author (16 November 2026): it is "abuse of sappho's legacy, of her achievement, of the beauty of her form, and most of all of her survival". *Abusus*, a thing used against what it is. And the test he sets: they would not treat her like this if she were in the room, and she is. The poem built the room; its first stanza is the seat opposite her voice, and whoever reads sits there.

The charge is differentiated (#1785 §IV.5). Abuse falls on the transmitted reading and the apparatus that keeps it as default. It does not fall on the scholarship that moved past it. The erotic reading keeps the maker's art and refuses the rival the grammar does not name, and the question put to it is a different one. The author (18 November 2026): "the jealousy reading challenge asks if we need sappho to be worse. the erotic reading asks if we can allow sappho to be better. she is the tenth muse after all." And: "can we allow sappho to surprise the millenia, or do we need her to have been already understood?" A reading not found in twenty-six centuries is not ruled out by that fact. The poem's construction predicts it: a poem built to produce readers who stop at its surface is built to surprise the ones who do not (#1785 §VII.3).

*The question of taste.* If taste decides (I.4), taste can be asked to answer for its choice. Taste claims discrimination: the power to tell one thing from another, the better from the worse. Longinus's ground for praising the poem is exactly that power exercised by its maker: selecting the extremes, binding them together. A reading that renders χλωροτέρα ποίας as pallor and stops there, as seventeen of twenty-four renderings from Theocritus to 1925 do, omitting, substituting or supplementing the word (#1785 §IV), cannot see the selection the ancient critic praised. It cannot tell the order of the catalogue from a list of symptoms, or the edge of the poem from a lacuna. Is it superior taste to prefer the jealous woman to the immortal poet? The author (17 November 2026): "it is not. that is poor taste. it shows a lack of discrimination." Taste that cannot discriminate the maker's acts in a poem antiquity quoted as the example of the sublime is not refined. It is undiscriminating, and the census shows where that taste is kept from inspection.

*The strongest opponent.* The strongest opponent is not the jealousy reading. It is the non-jealous erotic reading, and it does not subtract the maker. It says: Sappho composed, with the utmost skill, an ordered representation of erotic disintegration; the seizure is the subject; the man's composure measures her undoing; the motive is in the poem, ὡς γὰρ ἔς σ᾽ ἴδω, desire for the woman whenever she looks; and the selection and binding Longinus praises are hers (#1785 §I.1a). That reading keeps a maker and her binding, and the ethical charge of abuse does not fall on it. It is graded at every row of the comparison, with its partial explanations counted as explanations: the contrast explains the man's composure, epic diction the manner, desire the seizure (#1785 §VII.1a). What it leaves unexplained is the form the collapse takes (#1785 §VII.1b): why the poem gives its whole opening stanza, and its one comparison with the gods, to a figure who causes nothing and is then dropped; why the catalogue runs in this order, the withdrawal of the performing body faculty by faculty (§III.2); why the colour is a comparative that makes grass the measure exceeded, which pallor cannot carry (§IV.2); and who dares, when the transmitted text turns into an agentless dare and the grammar withholds the speaker (§V.2a). Its best answer to what the poem is for, an ephemeral experience kept in durable form, leans the right way, and it does not connect that durability to the writing that makes it possible. Granting the writing does not yet grant this reading. What grants it is the arrangement: the seat, the withdrawal, the surface, the turn (#1785 §VII.1b). The erotic reading represents disintegration well and has no account of these four. The future-reader reading accounts for all four with one construction, and holds the erotic reading's scene as the first term of its own (#1785 §VII.5). That is the comparison the reader is asked to make.

*Queerness as damage.* The innocent account first, in full. The transmitted reading makes the poem's desire between women legible on its surface: a woman watches another woman, desires her, and comes undone. The erotic reading makes it legible without the rival, and it is the scholarship's queer reading of the poem (#1785 §I.1a). That legibility was won. Wilamowitz read a wedding song with the man as bridegroom (1913), and the nuptial form of the triangle kept the desire out of view (#1785 §I.1). For a century the jealousy reading has been the reading that lets Sappho be read as the first voice of desire between women, in the anthology, the syllabus and the headnote. A reader trained on that history can take a reading that moves the poem toward writing as a retreat from the desire, "she is about writing, not about wanting".

The author (17 November 2026): "its like theyre afraid sappho wont be queer if they let the reading change, or that their fetishized progressivism will be in question. but this fetishization can hold queerness only as damage and dismemberment."

Stated as function, which is what the census measures: the reading that is kept holds the desire between women in one form, the body undone. Its oldest use is a diagnosis. Plutarch's physician reads the love-sick Antiochus by "those symptoms of Sappho's", every one of them (#1785 §IV.5): the poem as a list of symptoms, desire as disease. The jealousy reading keeps the queer woman legible as a patient, the woman in pieces of the sparagmos below. What it cannot hold is a queer woman who is the maker of the pieces.

The rival at its strength: no fear is needed for the reading to stay. Trained taste is the whole explanation. The field's pedagogy has made the surface reading the cautious default, and the alternative reads, on first meeting, as a de-queering that a cautious reader declines. The answer is in the text. The future-reader reading removes nothing of the desire. The participles stay feminine, φωνείσας, γελαίσας, and the voice looked at is a woman's (#1785 §II). All three readings are erotic; they differ on where the eros is turned (#1785 §IV.7): toward a body in a room, or toward the one who will sit opposite, toward what survives. The future-reader reading keeps the woman who desires and gives her the hand that made the poem. And it is the queerer reading. The author (17 November 2026): "whats queerer than erotic seizure at the thought of ones own lovely voice reproducing?" And: "that is the symposium's point precisely." Diotima's teaching is that eros is of begetting in the beautiful, "τόκος ἐν καλῷ καὶ κατὰ τὸ σῶμα καὶ κατὰ τὴν ψυχήν" (*Symposium* 206b), a desire for immortality; those pregnant in soul beget poems, and one looks to Homer and Hesiod and envies the offspring they leave, which give them immortal fame and memory (209c–d). The seizure of fragment 31 on this reading is that eros: a woman's voice, looked at and given only sound, undoing the body that sings it as it passes to the surface that will reproduce it for whoever sits opposite. No man is needed for the begetting, and none is its object. (An aside on standing. The author: he is "not implying that sappho is literally diotima, or that socrates … literally learned from her, but that the literary mythological identification is one and the same, much like catullus used Lesbia", as Catullus names his beloved Lesbia in the poem that receives fragment 31, 51.6–7. On the author's reading Socrates is an edited fictional character inherited from Aristophanes, #1579. Nothing here rests on the identification; #1785 does not need it, §IV.7.) Where the account stops at the diagnosis, the choice is between queerness held as damage and queerness held as making. The erotic reading stands between them: it holds the desire as undergone, and credits the maker with the disintegration, and stops at the form; §§VII.1a–b of #1785 are where it is met. A progressivism that can hold the first and not the second has kept the sign of the desire and lost the woman who wrote it.

*Sparagmos.* The rite is a body torn apart. Pentheus is torn by the women on Kithairon; Orpheus is torn by the Thracian women, and his head and his lyre are carried down the Hebrus and over the sea to Lesbos, where lyric is said to have its home (Ovid, *Met.* 11.50–55; Phanocles fr. 1 Powell). The jealousy reading performs it on fragment 31. It takes the catalogue as a body in pieces: tongue broken, fire under the skin, eyes that see nothing, ears that hum, sweat, trembling, the colour. It reads each piece as a symptom and keeps the pieces. The atom rule performs it on the field's object: each locus taken alone, the argument from the whole refused as evidence (§0.1, I.4). The poem already has the dismemberment in it, and it has it ordered. The speaker's body comes apart in sequence, the performing body withdrawing faculty by faculty toward the surface that will carry the voice (#1785 §III). Longinus's word for what the maker did is binding: she selects the extremes and binds them together. The pieces are hers, and so is the binding. A reading that keeps the pieces and drops the binding keeps the victim of the rite and loses the one who composed it. A reading that keeps both, the disintegration as her composition, is the erotic reading, the strong opponent above, and the comparison with it is made on the four features of the form it leaves unexplained.

The author (17 November 2026): "they can have the dismembered body; we shall have the tenth muse." The dismembered body is what the jealousy reading and the atom rule leave: a woman in pieces, a text in loci, each adjudicated alone. The tenth Muse is the maker who bound them, and whose binding is the reason the poem was copied and survived. Both are on the page. The reader chooses which to keep.

**I.4b The colonized text, and its liberation.** The case has been made in the first station: "the classics are themselves the most colonized" (the author, 14 November 2026; #1770 §3.5), and the discipline did it before the field of reception learned it: "crj learned how to colonize by doing it to the classics, and those texts stand in the exact same relation to the field as the marginal communities and authors behind a paywall" (#1770 §5.3.4). Reception learned the operation; philology performed it first, on its own objects. Fragment 31 shows the operation step by step, each step on the record.

1. *The territory is entered and its law rewritten.* Gottfried Hermann rewrote the last two transmitted lines in 1816, said so, "refinxi", and read the poem off the rewriting: "invidens Sappho viro illi", Sappho envying that man (#1770 §5.3.3; #1785 §I.1).
2. *The administrator's act is read back as the native's speech.* The conjecture acquires authority twice, as repaired text and then as evidence about the person supposed to be speaking it: "The scholar's declared act becomes the poet's apparent confession" (#1752).
3. *The administration is installed.* Page consolidates the reading (1955); edition, commentary, handbook, anthology and syllabus file it as the default; a sixth-grade lesson rules "Yet the emotions are not ambiguous"; a web page heads the poem "On Jealousy" (#1770 §5.3.3). "A reading can become compulsory without becoming uncontested."
4. *The colonized is said to have no letters.* The maker becomes "a condition without a hand: a voice overwhelmed, where antiquity had a maker who selected, bound, wrote and read" (#1752). The record shows the writer: a fifth-century Attic hydria with her seated "reading from a roll to three women"; Ovid's *studiosae littera dextrae*, the letters of her studious hand (#1785 §IV.6). The illiteracy is the reception's.
5. *The survival is extracted.* Her poem is the field's stock. An article that repairs a received object cannot fail (#1773 §5.1), and fragment 31 read as jealousy is an object received whole.
6. *The record of the rule is closed.* The publisher's metadata deposit carries no abstract (§8); in this case the decision on the alternative was stated in one anonymous reader's report as conviction (I.4); the field's decisions about texts receive fewer indexed citations than its other work, and their travel as the text is the hypothesis of §8.6.2.
7. *The power taken from the maker is held by the administration.* The writing of the future, the durable text that reaches whoever comes after, is what the poem claims for its maker on the future-reader reading, and it is what the field's own editions, translations and books do (§8.6.4). The author: "the very power denied sappho is appropriated to her own scholarship."
8. *The tearing is then charged to the receiver.* In the editor's article at the first station, sparagmos is the creative artists' act on an inherited body, and scholarship is set aside (#1770 §4.7). The body was already a gathering made by philology: the *Bacchae* that Soyinka is said to tear survives in two medieval manuscripts, its last third on one, its ending supplied from the Byzantine *Christus Patiens*. The author (14 November 2026): "sparagmos is what the discipline of classics had already done to its objects. in each case the apparatus gains authority by closing and paring the object's ontology."

*Which measure this is.* D3 measures the partition of a corpus among composing persons. The future-reader reading of fragment 31 keeps Sappho as its maker and changes the poem's address and operation: on §2 it is a reading at the grain of the work (A at the locus, carried to the whole). The scarcity of D3 in the census does not measure resistance to it directly. The two are related as forms of one preservation: the D3 count shows the unit of the maker held fixed; the fate of fragment 31's reading shows the received operation of a work held fixed when an argument from its relations would change it. Both are the received object kept, at different grains, and §§6.2 and 6.4 show the mechanism common to both, relations admitted to secure the object.

*One operation.* The author (17 November 2026): "it is the atomizing operation applied word by word. it is the same operation." The colonizing steps, the sparagmos and the atom rule of I.4 are one operation seen at three scales. Applied to fragment 31 word by word:

- κῆνος … ὄττις is taken at its locus as geometry, one man near the girl, and the indefinite relative that characterizes its occupant by a position is read away (#1785 §I).
- The addressee's voice and laugh are matched one by one to Sapphic parallels, and the parallels are all seen or touched where this addressee is given only sound (#1785 §II.2).
- The catalogue is taken item by item as symptoms. Plutarch's Erasistratus diagnoses Antiochus by "those symptoms of Sappho's", every one of them (#1785 §IV.5), and the order in which they come is not an item, so it is not read (#1785 §III.2).
- χλωροτέρα ποίας is taken as a colour word alone. Its comparative, which makes grass the measure the speaker exceeds, falls away, and seventeen of twenty-four renderings omit, substitute or supplement it (#1785 §IV).
- †καὶ πένητα† is obelized and adjudicated as a crux by itself; the edge it sits on is not a locus (#1785 §V).
- The report on the article does the same to the article: four objections, κῆνος, the addressee, γράμματα, πένητα, each adjudicated alone, and the convergence of the four, the article's one argument from the whole, not engaged at the grain it is made (I.4).

What each step leaves is a word without its binding. What all of them leave together is a list of symptoms, a body in pieces, a woman undone. The maker is not in any one word. She is in the selecting and the binding, "δεινὴ καὶ ἐκλέξαι καὶ εἰς ἄλληλα συνδῆσαι" (Longinus 10.1), the order, the comparative, the edge, the relation of each part to the others. The census does not find relations kept out. In *CP* 1906–22 a relation among parts decides a passage or attribution in 105 of 308 text questions, and relations are put to work chiefly to hold the received whole together, by 55 to 32 against opening it (§6.2). The finding is an asymmetry: relational evidence is admitted inside the received object and used to secure it, and the question is whether evidence of the same kind receives the same standing when it would change the object. In fragment 31 it does not. The author's usage taken as a unity closes each locus it opens (§6.4); the poem's own relations, the order, the comparative, the edge, are not given standing to change what the poem is. The maker exists in the binding, and the binding is admitted to confirm the received reading and refused standing against it. She does not need to be argued away: what remains with standing is the patient. That is how the colonizing operation can run without anyone having to defend it. Each step is a sound adjudication of one locus, and the subtraction of the maker happens in none of them and in all of them.

*The liberation is a set of operations, each of which any reader can perform with the record in hand.*

1. *Return the edge to what was transmitted.* The manuscript runs on: φαίνομαι ἀλλὰ πᾶν τολματόν ἐπεὶ καὶ πένητα (Parisinus gr. 2036; #1785 §V.1). The editions accept πὰν τόλματον as Sappho's and obelize †καὶ πένητα†; Bergk assigned ἐπεὶ καὶ πένητα to Longinus's own sentence. Hermann's lines are printed as Hermann's, and nothing supplied stands in the poem as hers.
2. *Admit the binding as evidence.* The order of the catalogue, the comparative of the colour, the edge: each is a relation between parts, a decision in the poem, and each is read as the maker's before any part is read as a symptom (#1785 §§III–V). This is the operation the atom rule forbids, and it is the one that returns the maker.
3. *Return the writer.* The tenth Muse of *AP* 9.506 and 7.14, the maker Longinus grades for selecting and binding, the reader of the hydria.
4. *Open the record.* The comparison of the three readings stands on one scale, feature by feature, in a deposit anyone can read (#1785 §§VII.1a, VII.4), under CC BY 4.0; the report that declined it is deposited beside it (#1774).
5. *Leave the decision to the reader.* Liberating the text does not impose a reading. It removes the supply, restores what was transmitted and what the maker did, and sets the readings on the same page. On that page the reader decides (#1785 §VII.6). The colonizing operation needed the decision made out of sight; the liberated text needs only that it be made in the open.

**I.5 The machine: three stations, one division of labour.** The combined audit is the author's: "eventually, ill take the entire range of seven articles, all of which will most likely be rejected, and collect them in a combined audit of what the fields cant carry" (15 November 2026). It has two stations before this one, each built on an article a journal declined. Read together with this one, they are three parts of one machine. The author (17 November 2026): "philosophy produces the position without touching the object. classical receptions expands the content without touching the object." And the emphasis: "we are emphasizing how consequential the ontological power is; that it is concentrated in philology; and that its most concentrated locus is uninspectable by design."

- *Philosophy produces the position without touching the object.* *This Is Not Philosophy* (#1773), on *Ancient Philosophy* and nineteen other journals. In *Ancient Philosophy*, 8 of 844 articles open what the object is; across the sister journals since 2021, 4 of 283 Plato/Aristotle abstracts do. Of the journal's 94 readable abstracts of 2022–26, 88% re-read a passage, resolve an inconsistency or reconstruct a doctrine inside one author; the 3% that make a claim about the text all confirm the object whole (#1773 §4.6). The field's unit is "the article that places a position", and its safest product is the consistency repair, which needs the object whole and named (#1773 §§3.1–3.2, 5.6). In the *Archiv* of the 1890s one ancient title in five put a work's standing in question; by the 1920s the question is gone.
- *Reception expands the content without touching the object.* *Who Speaks for the Classics* (#1770), on *Classical Receptions Journal*. Its range is real: China, Vietnam, Brazil, Mexico, Korea, Ukraine, video games, a webcomic. Of its 185 abstracts, 153 hold the ancient text as a fixed premise and read a later work against it; 141 arguments run from a source held fixed to a later work; of the nine that revise a governing interpretation, none reopens a canonical work's attribution, dating or standing reading (#1770 §§1.2, 2.2–2.4). The widening adds receivers around a source that stays where it was put.
- *Philology decides the object.* This census. The question of what the object is is permitted at the locus and closed at the corpus (§§4, 6, 8.1, 8.5). Where it is decided, the decision is spent as the received text (§8.6.2), and the two other fields take it as their premise. Philosophy cites *CP* for the text of the *Metaphysics* and the authenticity of the *De spiritu*. Reception reads its later works against the text philology fixed. Neither has the power to move it: "classical receptions can't decide what sappho 31 is. philosophy cant decide what aristotle [is]. they do not have those powers. cp does" (the author, 17 November 2026; I.1).

**How the parts interlock.** Each field's abstention is the next field's premise. The consistency repair of philosophy needs a whole object under one name, and philology's closed corpus grain supplies it. The alignment test of reception needs a fixed source, and philology's received text supplies it. Philology's decisions, once made, travel as that text into both, and the argument that made them stays at philology's gate. Two consequences follow. The power to say what the object is is consequential beyond philology: every position philosophy places and every receiver reception adds is built on it, and the composition layer composes from all three (I.3). And it is concentrated: of the three, one field holds it, at the corpus grain one form of argument moves it (the find, §0), and in the one journal where the field's decisions are most concentrated, the record of how they were made is the least open.

**Inspectability runs against power.** *Classical Receptions Journal* deposits 185 abstracts in 405 works, and 37 of its articles are open under Creative Commons licences (#1770 §0.2). *Ancient Philosophy*'s abstracts are readable for 2022–26 (#1773 §4.6). *Classical Philology* deposits 0 abstracts and 0 licences in 11,206 records; 97% of its works are closed (§8). By its own rules nothing may be posted before submission, copyright passes to the Press, and the decision is one anonymous reader's (§8.2): "the least inspectable. by design" (the author, I.1). The field with the least power over the object is the most open, and the field with the most is the most closed.

Philology is also the field with the instruments to reopen the object: manuscripts, papyri, the language. The author's prediction for it (§0): "less apparent flattening - it has at least to attend to language - but that it will still be there, molecular reach that holistically preserves the molar." After *Classical Philology* the census goes to more journals (§9.4). Biblical criticism follows as its own station, and the crossings of philology there are taken up only after both are done (the author's ruling: "keep biblical separate from the current question, and after we've done both separately we'll look at the combine").

**I.6 What the station finds.** The findings are stated at the close, after the evidence (§10). In brief: the question of what the object is is opened at the locus and seldom at the corpus, in every body read; relations are admitted and used to secure the received object; verdicts on the whole mostly state no ground where they are given; decisions about texts are cited less than the journal's other work, behind a record OpenAlex classes 97% closed.

**I.7 What it does not claim.** It does not claim that philology's work at the locus is wrong or idle; that work is the discipline's real reach, and the census measures it as such. It does not claim intent. It measures incentive and function: where a question is permitted, by what kind of argument, and what reaches the public.

## 0. The prediction

The author (16 November 2026): "i imagine, that philology will have less apparent flattening - it has at least to attend to language - but that it will still be there, molecular reach that holistically preserves the molar."

Stated as measures:
- the questions about the text itself (D) stay present at the grain of the locus: emendation, crux, reading, colometry;
- they thin at the grain of the work: authenticity, date, unity, transmission;
- they are near zero at the grain of the corpus: the partition of a corpus among composing persons, the author as unit;
- where the corpus grain is touched, it is touched by a locus and resolved toward the received partition.

### 0.1 The gate's rule, read from the report

The author, reading the *Classical Philology* report on ΦΑΙΝΕΤΑΙ ΜΟΙ (16 November 2026, 15:01 EST): "any change of object must go through atoms. and overall coherence is not valid to weigh back in on the adjudication of atoms for that purpose."

Each is a single locus or a single fact, and each is adjudicated on its own. None takes up the order of the catalogue. The convergence of §V, the paper's one argument from relation, gets a single clause of verdict: "neither it, nor the reading it aims to capture, seem to me convincing". A reading whose evidence is convergence cannot pass a rule that admits no convergence. The received reading is never put through the same rule: its own atoms are not adjudicated (#1775 §IV.2, the jealousy reading asked to explain its rendering of χλωρός).

### 0.2 The prediction, extended

The author, 15:03–15:06 EST:
- "relations between atoms are sheared into the atom as commodity form"
- "thats my prediction about what well find in philology"
- "philology is a field that can and does regularly encounter new manuscript and papyrological evidence... it maintains its authority by never exceeding the atom level that new findings could contradict"

*As measured* (§6.2 measure 2, §6.3 point 3): the prediction is refined. Relations are not sheared off; they are assigned a direction. Evidence from a relation confirms the received whole (55 to 32), evidence from a locus opens it (68 to 50).

### 0.3 As measures

1. *The atom is the unit a find could overturn.* That is the reading at a locus, the date of a work, or the sense of a word at a passage. The field's claims should sit at that grain. D1 should hold steady, and it does in the titles (§4). D2 should hold where a find could decide it, and thin where only argument could.
2. *Relations are sheared into atoms.* An argument from arrangement should appear, when it appears at all, as one datum among atoms, and not as the evidence that weighs them. Examples are the order of a catalogue, ring composition, the form of a book, and the convergence of readings. To code: per item, whether the argument's evidence is a relation (order, form, convergence) or a locus, and whether a relation is allowed to decide a locus.
3. *Words broken off from their community.* A word's sense should be settled from its occurrences taken one at a time, sorted into senses, and not from the poem it stands in. The lexicon is the instrument: LSJ files χλωροτέρα … ποίας under "pale, of persons" while its standard, grass, belongs to the first sense. The phrase's two terms are assigned to two different senses so that each can be an atom (#1775 §IV.2). To code: arguments on a word's sense that cite parallels as isolated occurrences, against those that read the word in its passage and form.
4. *Form keeps the object stable.* If the object is held in place by the form of the received reading, then form is what the atom rule must keep out. An argument from form is the one kind of argument that could move the object without a new find. The prediction is that such arguments are rarest of all at the corpus grain, and that where they occur they are answered atom by atom. The report on ΦΑΙΝΕΤΑΙ ΜΟΙ is the first case.

The asymmetry to test throughout (#1766 §5.2.3): whether the received reading is held to the atom rule it applies.

## 2. The codebook: reach for philology

The categories are those of #1773 §4.6, kept for comparability. D is split by grain, and two categories are added for work philology does that philosophy does not.

- **A, locus.** Re-reads a passage for its sense.
- **B, repair.** Resolves an apparent inconsistency or problem inside an author.
- **C, reconstruction.** Reconstructs a doctrine, institution, practice, event or context from texts.
- **D1, text at the locus.** Emendation, conjecture, crux, reading of a manuscript at a passage, lacuna, colometry, interpolation of a line or passage.
- **D2, text at the work.** Authenticity, authorship, date, unity or composition of a work; its manuscript tradition or recension; an edition.
- **D3, text at the corpus.** The partition of a corpus among composing persons. Whether names in a corpus are one maker or several, and whether the author is the right unit.
- **E, relation.** Sources, imitation, allusion, influence, intertext between authors.
- **F, after antiquity.** Reception and transmission beyond antiquity, as an object of study.
- **G, position.** Argues a thesis not anchored to an author or text: method, theory, a general claim.
- **L, language.** Grammar, lexicon, metre, accent, dialect, style as language.
- **M, document.** Publishes or reads an inscription, papyrus or object as a document.

For each D code, also mark *confirm* or *open*. *Confirm* means the title settles toward the received standing; *open* means it puts the standing in question.

### 6.4 What the openings open to

**The question** (the author, 19:02 EST): "what direction are the openings in? what do they open up to?" §6.2 counts a text question as opening when it puts the received text, standing or attribution in question. It does not say toward what. Each of the 160 opening items (open or both; `openings-classed.json` holds 160) was read here for what it puts in place of the received text: from its title, its direction span and the reader's note, against the page where those were unclear. Rulings are in `openings-classed.json`; the reader is one (this session), and the class is not yet audited.

| What the opening puts in place | Items | Share |
|---|---|---|
| **one better reading at the locus**: an emendation, a punctuation, a transposition | 78 | 48% |
| **the witnesses re-ranked**: which manuscript or edition governs | 19 | 12% |
| **a date moved** | 14 | 9% |
| **the transmitted text defended** against the editors' emendation | 11 | 7% |
| **a line removed**: an interpolation, a gloss, a spurious verse | 9 | 6% |
| **a work or document removed**: spurious, forged, a later invention | 9 | 6% |
| **something supplied**: a lacuna filled, a new fragment for the author | 7 | 4% |
| **a work's composition**: layers, a second version, book division, contamination | 7 | 4% |
| **the makers**: a corpus divided or reassigned among persons | 6 | 4% |

**The direction is inward, to the author.** Nearly every opening opens toward one person's own text. The emendation restores what the author wrote: "what Tacitus originally wrote was claro ore" (1908); "Plautus used facere incendium" (1910); "surely Aeschylus did not write" (1916). It is decided by the author taken whole: "Caesarian usage" (1908), "the author's excessive fondness for symmetry" (Apuleius, 1911), "Platonic usage and thought" (1915), Stoic doctrine across Chrysippus' fragments (1911), "I have no doubt that Virgil wrote the Culex", with Virgilian style then deciding the loci (1910). The relation that §6.2 found confirming the received whole is here the decider of the atom: the author's usage, taken as a unity, closes each locus it opens. One note emends the author against himself. Paul Shorey, on Laws 795b (1916): "The only escape I can see from the emendation is to assume that Plato himself by inadvertence wrote [ἀδύνατος]… And if by an oversight Plato wrote [ἀδύνατος], it is, I think, probable that he meant [δυνατός]." (The Greek words are read through the page's OCR and bracketed as such.) The author is corrected to the author: what he meant governs what he wrote. The removals face the same way. A line or a work comes out because it is not his, and what remains is more purely his. The re-ranked witnesses face the archetype, the one original behind the copies.

Counted by where they arrive:
- **at one author's text**, restored, supplied, defended or purified: 114 of 160 (71%: E, S, T, X, A);
- **at the tradition's one original**: 19 (W);
- **at a date or at the composition of a work by its one author**: 21 (D, K). Of the seven K items, Plautine contamination puts Greek originals behind the Rudens and the act-division, and Mulder's Iliad and Ausfeld's Alexander Romance are layered. Each is reported as one maker reworking, or as accretion on an original.
- **at more than one maker**: 6 (P). Nitsche's Anaximenes and Butler's plural Vita Heliogabali are reported from books. Bolling's two notes and Drewitt's augment statistics are each answered. Croiset's statistics are defended with "the merits expressly set aside".

So the openings do not open the unit. They open the atom inside the unit, and close it on the author. The author is the one constant every opening assumes, and the instrument by which it is decided. That is the corpus grain of §6.2 seen from below: the corpus question is closed at the top because every question beneath it is decided by the unity the corpus question would put in doubt.

| texts | 283 | 311 | 282 |

1. *The text question contracts.* The locus falls from 28% to 16% of the journal, the work from 17% to 13%.
2. *Athetesis leaves.* Removal of lines inside an author, seven times in the first five years, appears once in each later period. #1773 found athetesis an ordinary article form in the *Archiv* of the 1890s and gone from it by the 1920s (#1773 §4.12, 4.12g). In *CP* it goes in the 1910s.
3. *The manuscript tradition leaves.* The re-ranking of witnesses runs 13, 5, 1. It was carried by a few series: Aristophanes, Catullus, Livy's third decade, Pliny's Letters. The series end and nothing replaces them.
4. *What remains is the emendation.* One better reading at the locus is 44% of the openings in 1906–10 and half of them afterwards. This is the residue #1773 §4.12b names, an emendation "at the smallest unit where the edition may still be questioned", and in *CP* it is the residue by 1917.
5. *The question of a work's standing does not collapse in this run.* D2 falls modestly. Removals of a work rise, but the works removed are small or documentary: oracles of colonization, the Elogium Duilianum, Demosthenes' Epistles V and VI, Book III of the Vitae patrum. The makers question rises from one to three, and each of the three is answered. The flattening here is of the operations that act inside an author (athetesis, the tradition), not yet of the author's standing, which the journal had never opened.

**Limits.** The counts are small, and the period tables move by single items. The class was ruled by one reader from the direction span, the note and the page where needed, and is not audited. The series effect (point 3) means some of the fall belongs to particular projects ending, not to the journal's form.

**The direction has turned.** In 1906–22 the openings purified the author. A line or a work came out because it was not his, and the received text was emended toward what he must have written. In 2020–26 the largest change is the reverse: the text the earlier critics changed is given back. A quarter of the text questions defend the transmitted text against the edition:
- "This note attempts to demonstrate that the deletion of 404 is not necessary" (Casali, *Aeneid* 11, 2022).

The ground for giving the text back is the maker's design at the locus. "The form introduces a deliberate referential ambiguity into the text that underscores Catullus’ narrative artistry; haec should be retained" (Szempruch, 2025). 4. *The maker's craft is credited exactly where it cannot reach the unit.* Catullus is granted "deliberate referential ambiguity" in one pronoun, and Sophocles a usual handling of exits. ### 8.3 Conviction: what the whole is judged by

**The question** (I.4; the author, 23:57 EST: "taste. the philological atomization ultimately authorized taste."). When a scholar judges by conviction, what is judged, and is a reason given? If the atom rule removes the holistic judgment's duty to give reasons, the whole should be judged by conviction and the atom should almost never ground the judgment.

**The instrument.** A lexicon finds every sentence that judges by conviction: convincing, persuasive, plausible, compelling, cogent, "carries conviction", überzeugend, convaincant, convincente, and their negatives (`conviction/measure.py`). The lexicon only finds the sentences; its own polarity flag was too loose to count by (a "not" anywhere in the sentence), so every sentence was read. Readers coded each, with the sentence before and after, for polarity (convinced, unconvinced, mixed, or not a judgment), who judges (the writer or someone reported), what is judged (the work as a whole, or one part of it), and the ground (a named atom, an argument without a particular datum, or none: bare). Every span checked: 0 failures.
- *CP* 1906–22: the review section (40/01) and the articles and notes (40/17), 916 sentences, four readers' halves.
- *BMCR* 1990–2026: a fixed random sample of 50 reviews a year, 1,848 drawn, 1,846 read (`gather/bmcr/`; the site lists its reviews in a public sitemap and does not restrict crawling; reviewers' email addresses were stripped at harvest and never stored); 2,027 sentences.

**CP's review section, 1906–22** (judgments by the writer):

|---|---|---|---|
| unconvinced by one part (83) | 26 | 36 | 21 |
| unconvinced by the whole (35) | 4 | 18 | 13 |
| convinced by the whole (34) | 2 | 5 | 27 |

In the journal's own articles, judgments of a whole are rare (35 of 369 by the writer), and most of those rest on an argument.

***BMCR*, 1990–2026**, per 100 reviews:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Over the whole sample, judgments of a book as a whole: 282, of which 10 rest on an atom (3.5%). Judgments of a part: unconvinced 279, of which 122 rest on an atom (44%).

**What the measure shows.**
1. *The atom almost never grounds a judgment of the whole.* *CP* 1906–22: 6 of 69. *BMCR*: 10 of 282. The atom grounds judgments of parts (a third in *CP*, nearly half in *BMCR*), and the whole is judged by argument or by nothing. This is the author's mechanism measured: the atom is the evidence the field admits, and it is not the evidence the whole is judged on.
2. *Assent to the whole is mostly given without a stated ground.* Favourable verdicts on a book as a whole are bare in the coding window (the sentence and its neighbours) in 79% of *CP*'s review section and 65% of *BMCR* (129 of 200). Whether these summarize an argument made elsewhere in the review or substitute for one is a reading of the whole review, not yet made (§9.12), and the bare favourable verdict holds or grows across four decades (6.7, 7.2, 5.2, 9.7 per 100 reviews).
3. *Public refusal of the whole disappears, and what remains is argued.* In *BMCR*, unconvinced verdicts on the whole fall from 7.9 to 2.0 per 100 reviews, and the bare ones from 3.5 to 0.3. The share of reviews with any unconvinced judgment halves, 20% to 11%. In 1906–22 a reviewer could refuse a book by conviction alone in print (13 times in the review section). In the open review of the 2020s, almost no one does.
4. *Where bare refusal went.* The public review now assents by taste and refuses, rarely, by argument. The refusal by taste has not gone from the field: it is the stated ground of the report on ΦΑΙΝΕΤΑΙ ΜΟΙ, "it ultimately fails to carry conviction. I do not recommend publication" (I.4), in the gate the public cannot read (§8.2). One report is one case. The measure shows the open form of the field; the closed form is seen once, here, and the archive has the record of it.

**Limits.** The lexicon finds conviction only where it is named; a refusal in other words ("this will not do") is not counted, and neither is a verdict carried by tone. The units differ: *CP* 1906–22 by review page, *BMCR* by review, 50 a year; the decades of *BMCR* hold 350 to 517 reviews. The coding of the ground is the least consistent field (the readers' note: where the only locus named is the object judged, not a reason, most coded bare, some atom). The readers of the two bodies differ; the conviction codes are audited blind in §3.2. *Bare* means no ground in the coding window, the sentence and the one before and after: it does not establish that the review gives no reason elsewhere. *BMCR* reviews books by invitation and by request, a different genre from *CP*'s review section of 1906–22, and a reviewer's conventions of politeness are part of what is measured, not a correction to it.

*The account, in full, as the open record gives it.* Sarah Nooter is Edward Olson Professor of Classics at Chicago, Editor-in-Chief of *Classical Philology*, and chair of the Core sequence "Poetry and the Human". Her work is on Greek drama and its reception, poetry, the voice, sound, embodiment, performance and queer theory. *Greek Poetry in the Age of Ephemerality* (Cambridge 2023) "suggests that poetry offers a way to remain in the world", through "the durable physicality of its practice"; poems are "meant to engrave and adhere" (the publisher's summary). The review gives her own line: "The sonic makes materiality and engenders memory; performance lends perdurance." Part II of the book is "Texts": Simonides' inscribed epitaphs, which point toward the future and give a communal memory; "Writing the Future: Pindar, Aeschylus, and the Tablet of the Mind"; the bodies of the Cologne Epode and the *Persae*. *How to Be Queer: An Ancient Guide to Sexuality* (Princeton 2024) translates Sappho, Plato and others and holds that "the erotic and queer are a source of life and a cause for celebration". Her public work joins poetry to medicine "in the corporeality that underlies both" and teaches poetry to adolescent survivors of traumatic injury. It is a serious account of why poetry matters: poems are how mortal bodies remain in the world.

*How it relates.* The account is the nearest in the field to the future-reader reading. Its question is the poem's survival beyond the body that makes it; one of its chapters is titled "Writing the Future"; its instrument is the voice as sound, and fragment 31's addressee is a voice given only sound (#1785 §II). On those terms the two readings are close kin. Where they part is placement, and the placement can be read from the table of contents. Sappho is in Part I, "Bodies": "The Erotics of Again: Time and Touch in Sappho", which reads fragments 31, 1 and 94 and the Tithonus poem (*BMCR*). Its summary ends: "This is poetry that challenges the ephemerality of embodied experience by showing its power to reenact the force of touch." Her survival is reenactment, performance, the cycle of eros, touch. The writing of the future is in Part II, and every poet there is a man: Simonides, Pindar, Aeschylus, Archilochus, Timotheus. On the open record the book holds the very question fragment 31 answers on the future-reader reading, whether a poem writes its own future reader, and puts the one woman in it on the side of the body. That is the illiteracy of I.4b in its gentlest form: the maker of the poem is granted survival, as a body reenacted, and the writing of the future goes to others.

The chapter's title names the place where the two readings part most exactly. "The Erotics of Again" reads fragment 1 with fragment 31. In fragment 1 the again is δηὖτε, three times, and ἔλθε μοι καὶ νῦν, "come to me now too". On the future-reader reading that again is the mechanism of the return (#1785 §VII.5b). The goddess is called by a voice and comes: she arrives as a face and a voice, and the voice speaks words the poet composed for her, with the poet's own name in them, τίς σ᾽, ὦ Ψάπφ᾽, ἀδικήει; Sappho comes to her future reader by that mechanism, called by whoever takes up the text, arriving as a composed voice that is read as a face and a body. Fragment 1 runs the process of fragment 31 in reverse: there a god becomes embodied, here an embodiment becomes godlike, and the voice is the machinery both ways. On the summary's terms the again is touch reenacted. On the future-reader reading the again is a call answered by composed words, and that is how a poet reaches whoever comes after. The two readings agree that the poem comes again. They part on what comes: a body performed again, or a voice made to be called.

The author (17 November 2026): "the very power denied sappho is appropriated to her own scholarship." The mechanics are on the page of the field, and none of them needs a motive. The power at issue is the one the future-reader reading gives the poet: to make a text that survives the body and reaches whoever comes after. In the field's arrangement that power is exercised, and the field exercises it. The edition fixes what she wrote (Hermann's "refinxi", I.4b). The translation is the surface through which she reaches readers now: in *How to Be Queer* she speaks in the translator's English, and the translator is the journal's editor. The book is the durable object that is "meant to engrave and adhere", and it carries her as a body reenacted. The journal holds copyright in what is said about her (§8.2). The scholarship writes, inscribes, addresses the future and survives; the poet, on its account, performs and is performed again. The operation the poem claims for its maker is the operation the field performs on the poem. It is step 2 of I.4b at the scale of a career: the scholar's act becomes the form in which the poet persists.

This is a reading of a table of contents and three summaries, and it is stated at that grain. The chapter may say more than its summary, and the census cannot read it. What the open record allows is the comparison of placements, and the question it raises is the one fragment 31 asks: whether the voice that survives is a body performed again or a surface that inscribes the one who will sit opposite it.

## 11. Coda: what the three stations found, the work left, and how this was made

**What the three stations found.** Three journals, three declined articles, one instrument (#1773 §§4.1–4.9).
- *Classical Receptions Journal* (#1770) widens the content and leaves the object where it was: 153 of 185 abstracts hold the ancient text fixed and read a later work against it; none of the nine that revise a governing reading reopens a canonical work's attribution, dating or standing reading. The margin supplies the receptions; the centre keeps the object.
- *Ancient Philosophy* (#1773) places positions on the object without touching it: 8 of 844 articles ask what the object is; 88% of its recent abstracts re-read a passage, repair an inconsistency or reconstruct a doctrine inside one author. The question was ordinary in the *Archiv* of the 1890s and gone by the 1920s.
- *Classical Philology* (this census) decides the object. The question of what the text is is opened at the locus and seldom at the corpus, in every body read; relations are admitted and used to secure the received whole; verdicts on the whole mostly state no ground where they are given; the decisions about texts are cited less than the journal's other work; and the record of how they are made is the least open of the three.

The three are one machine (I.5). Each field's abstention is the next field's premise: philosophy's repair needs the object whole, reception's alignment needs it fixed, and philology supplies both. Fragment 31 shows the machine on one poem: a reading supplied in 1724, written into the text in 1816, filed by 1955, carried in transmission after the scholarship moved past it, and kept in 2026 by a verdict, stated as conviction, against the reading that accounts for the poem's form (I.4a–I.4b).

**Why it matters.** The power to say what an ancient text is, whose it is and what it does is the most consequential the humanities hold over antiquity, because every other field builds on what it decides. It is concentrated in one discipline, and in that discipline its most concentrated locus keeps its record closed by its own rules. What it decides now passes to the composition layer, which composes the classics for whoever asks from what it can retrieve (I.3). A decision the public cannot inspect becomes, there, a fact no one can reopen. The census makes one record of the decision open, and leaves the decision itself to whoever reads with the record in hand.

**The work left.** The author (17 November 2026): "this should go further … this should go on to audit biblical criticism, marx, and so on, and so forth - the tributaries of the humanities. but that work for this moment will hold for someone else." The census is a method, and it is written down so that it can be run by others: a codebook of reach (§2), a blind parallel run and an audit (§3), the page read where the page is open (§6), the closed record measured from its metadata (§8), and every code carrying its verbatim span. The tributaries named are fields whose objects were made by editors and then received as given. Biblical criticism is the author's next station, kept separate by his ruling (I.5): its default runs the other way, plural makers under one name, and it is to be measured on its own before the two are compared. Marx's corpus is another: the second and third volumes of *Capital* were edited by Engels from Marx's manuscripts, and the critical edition (*MEGA*²) has since printed the manuscripts beside the editions, so that what Marx wrote and what was made of it can be read apart. The same instrument asks of each tributary the same questions: at what grain may its object be reopened, by what kind of argument, and where is the record of the decision kept.
