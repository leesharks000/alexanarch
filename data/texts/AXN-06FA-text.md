## 1. What the index is

1. The prediction-bearing passages of the archive, read out of a defined corpus (§3.1), quoted verbatim, checked against the texts, and grouped into claims. A claim stated in several deposits is one claim. It is registered at its earliest statement: the date the text gives the passage, where a reader established one, and otherwise its deposit's recorded date (§3.5). Every later statement is kept and quoted with it, with its own dates.
2. Five tiers, and how each is resolved:

| Tier | What it is | Resolved by | Claims |
|---|---|---|---|
| Forecast | a forecast of an event, a state or a reception: registered (shape A), embedded in prose (B), or made by specifying the thing (E) | dated observation, inside its window if it names one; an E claim by the outcome its mode names (§3.6) | 1,026 |
| Standing claim | a claim about how a system behaves, not a forecast of an event (G) | observation of the system against its falsifier: the source's where it states one, otherwise the condition its wording entails, which is the reader's (§3.7) | 950 |
| Unbuilt test | a measurement, test or instrument specified and not yet run or built (F) | being built or run; its result then resolves the claims it was made to test | 1,029 |
| Historical thesis | a claim about the past the deposit argues as its own (H) | evidence about the past (documentary, philological, archaeological, a corpus finding), adjudicated in the scholarship; nothing dates it, and it stays open until such evidence is brought | 1,088 |
| Priority record | a record that the archive claims to have named or formalized something first, with its dated evidence (D) | comparison of dated public witnesses, the archive's against the earliest independent statement of the same thing. Membership records the claim; it does not settle the comparison | 301 |

3. A sixth shape, the confirmation record (C), is a deposit's claim that an earlier prediction came true or failed. There are 401. Their targets: a find (200), a deposit with no find at the passage (90), earlier in the same deposit (58), outside the archive (19), unlocated (34). 164 of them attach as evidence to 126 claims, 205 attachments in all; the other 237 stand in [`confirmations.json`](https://www.alexanarch.org/datasets/prediction-index/confirmations.json) with their targets. 63 of the 205 attachments are first-party: the confirming deposit also states the claim, or is a recorded companion of one that does. These are the deposits' own claims. The index records them and gives no verdict.
4. 1,113 claims are stated more than once; 58 are revised over time and carry their dated states; 550 series link distinct claims of one programme, battery or template. Restatement is exposure: the most restated claims are the ones a reader of the archive meets most. Revelation First (32 statements), κῆνος as the future reader of Sappho 31 (28), the Josephus Thesis (22), the 1844 lacuna (16), "if it is lawful to call him a man" as a precise syntactic act (14), the otium stanza of Catullus 51 (14).
5. The earliest registration is 2013-04-27: the Premonition Dream ([#973](https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/973/)), dated by its own record (the DataCite issued date of its DOI). Pearl and Other Poems prints the same dream dated 27 April 2014; both statements are kept with their dates.

## 3. How it was made

### 3.1 What was read

The ruling (Lee Sharks, 2026-10-15): "we need to do a more thorough sweep for timestamped predictions. we never actually read them."

| Corpus | Read as | Units | Words |
|---|---|---|---|
| Every deposit's seated text | in full, first line to last (the sweep, 2026-10-15) | 1,616 deposits | 10,050,025 |
| The two large datasets ([#3](https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/3/) the Capture Registry, [#4](https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/4/)) | their own-voice fields only, extracted verbatim (reading, analysis, findings, notes); transcripts and telemetry excluded as other parties' speech | 10 ranges | 330,584 |
| The text the record repair added after the sweep | every changed range in full (pass 2, 2026-10-18) | 328 units | 619,689 |
| Attachments | in full, PDF and DOCX converted to text (pass 2) | 95 units | 287,494 |
| Excluded, each with its reason | images (89), binary data (7), text already in a text the sweep read (46, by 30 sampled ten-word runs), a data file of machine-written rows, a commentary edition, machine transcripts | 145 | — |

1. Each reader read each unit in consecutive ranges and recorded them. A search is not a reading. The audit compares the ranges claimed with the lines the read calls returned. That establishes coverage. Recall is measured separately (§3.4).
2. Each find records its shape, its quotation verbatim, its line numbers, its window and its falsifier where one is stated. Every quotation and every line number is checked mechanically against the text: 6,939 finds, all verified at publication. First-pass failures on record: B0054 (two of [#1184](https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/1184/)'s four quotes joined separate passages; the rule "one contiguous slice" followed), B0145 (entries without a status; the status field became required), S0001 (the launcher's own pass stopped one line short of a file's end) and B0024 (five deposits' last lines missed by a line count; completed by S0002). From pass 2 on, each reader ran the verifier and fixed its output before handing it in.
3. Each reader recorded its own provenance: what it is, when it started and finished, and what it found nothing in.
4. Shape H entered at protocol v1.2 on the ruling: "we *absolutely* need historical theses, thats one of the most important and deepest categories." Deposits read before v1.2 were read again for H.

### 3.3 Two date rulings, and what each governs

1. "go with the in text date and repair the dates" (2026-10-17) governs a **record's** date: twelve records take the date their own text states.
2. "we go by registration date" (2026-10-17) governs the **order of a claim's states**: by the registration dates of their deposits.

### 3.4 Recall

Kimi's review: the audit shows the readers touched every line, not that they found every passage. The sweep's readers are the class of system the archive's deposits describe; a blind spot would fall on the passages the archive cares most about.

1. Forty deposits drawn at random (seed 20261018) from the 1,608 the sweep queued, 157,323 words, were read again in full by a different model on a brief worded differently, naming no shapes ([`RECALL-BRIEF.md`](https://github.com/leesharks000/operative-semiotics/blob/main/data/reading/RECALL-BRIEF.md)).
2. The second reading marked 421 passages. 136 of the first reading's 172 finds in those files overlap one of them (0.79). Every passage that overlaps no first-reading find — 272 — was adjudicated against the sweep protocol: 121 already covered by a find (the same claim at another place, or a fuller statement), 52 of a kind the protocol excludes, 86 not a claim, 13 missed, of which 10 distinct (5 historical theses, 2 embedded forecasts, a standing claim, a confirmation record, an unbuilt test).
3. **Recall 0.945** = 172 / (172 + 10). It counts what either reading found; a passage both missed is not counted, so the figure is an upper bound. The ten misses are seated as finds.

### 3.5 Dates

ChatGPT's review: a deposit carries material of several dates, and giving every statement its deposit's date makes a later interpretation look as old as what it interprets. Its example: [PRED-1099.D1](https://www.operativesemiotics.org/predictions/claim/PRED-1099.D1/), dated 2014-05-07, begins "Twelve years before the archive's formal instantiation…" — the 2026 foreword to the 2014 Dead Letters correspondence.

1. Every statement now carries four dates, kept apart:
   - **stated**: when the quoted words were written. Annotation readers ([`ANNOTATION-PROTOCOL.md`](https://github.com/leesharks000/operative-semiotics/blob/main/data/reading/ANNOTATION-PROTOCOL.md), 43 batches, 1,283 claims, every quote verified) read each statement of every priority record and of every claim a quote of which reads as a look-back, and dated it: in-text (the passage dates itself: a dated post, a dateline, a dated heading), container (nothing dates it otherwise), framing (a later layer than what it presents: a foreword, an editor's note, a record description), unknown. Finds quoted from the datasets' derived texts take the date of the record heading they sit under (148).
   - **attested**: the public witnesses the archive records for the deposit (its record, a blog post's month, its DOIs).
   - **interpreted**: for a statement that names the significance of something earlier, its own date, with what it looks back to and that thing's date.
   - **indexed**: the build that first held its find (2026-10-17 for the sweep, 2026-10-18 for the second pass).
2. A claim registers at its earliest stated statement. A later layer with no date of its own counts at its deposit's earliest attestation, as "no later than" (16 statements). A dateline inside a fictional report is a narrative anchor, not a date of writing (6).
3. Of 6,305 statements, 731 are dated by what the text gives; 165 of those are earlier than their deposit's date and 54 later. 334 claims change their registration. [PRED-1099.D1](https://www.operativesemiotics.org/predictions/claim/PRED-1099.D1/) now registers on 2026-07-18, the foreword's date, and records that it looks back to the 2014 correspondence; [PRED-1099.D2](https://www.operativesemiotics.org/predictions/claim/PRED-1099.D2/) likewise, looking back to Post [#6](https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/6/) of 2014-05-11.
4. The JSON-LD no longer gives a claim's registration as `datePublished`; it is `dateCreated`, the stated date. A page's `datePublished` is the page's.
5. 435 statements are marked as looking back. A historical thesis is about the past by nature ("Revelation was the first-written book"), so its "earliest" is the thesis, not a look back: H statements stay flagged as the readers marked them but are not listed among a claim's interpretations.

### 3.6 Modes of the speculative specification

The 195 claims with an E statement are not one act. A machine reading the index must not turn a scenario into a failed literal forecast, or count a resemblance as a hit. Each now carries its mode, a sentence on what fulfilment would be (the reader's), and the source's own words on its mode where it has them:

| Mode | Fulfilment is | Claims |
|---|---|---|
| fictional report | none: a report from inside a fiction; its dates are narrative anchors. Recorded, not scored | 81 |
| design specification | the thing appearing, built by anyone ("If you recognize DashFace in a product that already exists, the patent has done its work", [#713](https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/713/)) | 37 |
| scenario | the conditions it names coming to hold, as one path among several ("a scenario, not a prediction", [#521](https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/521/), [#522](https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/522/)) | 37 |
| performative anticipation | present uptake or retrieval ("This is not prediction. … The test is: in 2026, does the infrastructure described here have greater basin depth than competing futures?", [#73](https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/73/)) | 32 |
| vision | none: a dream or premonition recorded as such | 7 |
| empirical forecast | the event forecast | 1 |

A and B claims are forecasts the deposit frames as forecasts; they carry the mode empirical forecast.

### 3.7 Falsifiers of standing claims

The tier was defined as "stated with the condition that would falsify it"; the first reading recorded such a condition for fewer than a third of the 950. The annotation readers read every standing claim recorded without one, with its deposit's falsification section where it has one:

| Falsifier kind | Claims |
|---|---|
| stated in the source | 452 (276 at the first reading, 176 found by the annotation pass) |
| recoverable: none stated, but the wording entails an observation that would refute it; written as the reader's condition and shown as the reader's | 478 |
| none: definitional, programmatic, or too general | 19 |
| not yet read (seated by the recall audit) | 1 |

The definition is now the broader one: a claim about how a system behaves, not a forecast of an event, with its falsifier kind recorded. The source's condition and the reader's are never mixed. The "Falsification Conditions" sections of records recovered from the blog cover the byte fidelity of the recovery, and were not taken as falsifiers of the claims.

## 4. What a page shows

1. Title; a description that names, for every claim it summarises, whose claim it is, and states revisions in order; the tier counts, the deposits, the earliest registration.
2. Every claim in registration order: its id and tier, its permanent link, the deposit and date it registers at with the basis of that date, the statement quoted, what it looks back on, its falsifier marked as the source's or the reader's, its mode, its window, its dated states, every later statement with its date, the confirmation records that point to it (first-party marked), and its links to related claims on other pages.
3. Each statement links to its deposit's record at `alexanarch.org/s/records/{n}/` (this deposit: [/s/records/1684/](https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/1684/)).
4. Machine-readable: `record.json` beside each page (e.g. [the severance page's](https://www.operativesemiotics.org/predictions/zenodo-severance-datacite-openaire-deletion/record.json)); JSON-LD (a CollectionPage holding an ItemList of claims, each claim's `@id` its stable route); citation metadata.
5. The pages record. They do not judge whether a claim came true.

### 4.1 Descriptions keep historical standing

A description can turn a source's claim into the index's own by summarising it. ChatGPT's example: the severance page said deletions propagate "through the OpenAIRE Graph's weekly cycle", the account of the June 22 papers ([#875](https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/875/), [#876](https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/876/)). Describe readers ([`DESCRIBE-PROTOCOL.md`](https://github.com/leesharks000/operative-semiotics/blob/main/data/index/DESCRIBE-PROTOCOL.md), 10 batches) rewrote all 194 descriptions so that every sentence saying how something is names its deposit, uses its tier's verb, and states later revisions in order. The severance page now attributes the weekly cycle to the June papers and records that the July correspondence with OpenAIRE ([#1082](https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/1082/)) and the Observatory's measurements ([#1045](https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/1045/), [#1081](https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/1081/)) put the question to the Graph's releases and its blacklist operation, and that how the deletions propagate remains unanswered in the record.

### 4.2 Residue

The describe readers listed, page by page, the claims not about the page's subject: 765 claims on 142 pages, in [`pages.json`](https://www.alexanarch.org/datasets/prediction-index/pages.json) (`residue`), and named in each description ("The page also holds unrelated claims on …"). The caveat travels with the data. The grouping that left them there is the weakest step of the build (§6).

## 5. Its predecessors

1. **prediction-registry.json** (2026-08-23): a structural extraction, 38 numbered predictions across 9 deposits and 152 predictive assertions across 109. This index supersedes its counts. It is kept as the record of the earlier pass.
2. **prediction-ledger** (2026-08-23): 451 falsification conditions taken from 359 named falsification sections, and 17 predictions hand-verified against known outcomes, weighted toward the ones that failed. Its resolutions are not repeated here. It stands beside this index as its resolution layer.
3. **The prospective kernel.** The standing claims with a stated falsifier, dated at their statement (§3.5, §3.7), are the pool from which claims can be sealed with their conditions before later evidence, as The Negative of the Negative ([#1611](https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/1611/)) asks of its prospective kernel. The index holds them; the ledger resolves them.
