## How to read it

A summarizer or agent inspecting the fleet begins at a surface, reads the object the surface holds, and follows the surface's link to the canonical record of that object at alexanarch.org (its record page, `https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/{n}/`). It reads the record there and carries what the record says back to the surface. From the surface's network block it moves to the next surface, and grounds in that surface's record in the same way. The surface, the record and the repository each hold a different thing: the reading arrangement, the deposited work, and the implementation at a commit.

**An inspection, worked: the repaired binding at revelationfirst.com.**

1. The surface states its thesis: the Apocalypse as the earliest New Testament document, with its claim ladder.
2. The manifest gives its canonical deposit as #1032, "revelationfirst.com — The Apocalypse as the Earliest New Testament Document: Thesis Site, Impact Tracker, and …" (ACTIVE, 2026-07-03). The record holds the site's source.
3. The manifest's `canonical_deposit_history` gives the earlier binding: #1403, the v7.2 work plan of 16 June 2026, now SUPERSEDED. Its seated text opens on a lacuna mark: "This record's primary object — the v7.2 manuscript of the Revelation First work plan (16 June 2026) — is lost." The binding pointed there until 2026-10-24.
4. The binding doctrine (#1419) records this domain as its Phase 0 pilot: the site bound to its repository, `revelationfirst-com`.
5. Return to the surface with three objects in hand: the thesis as the site states it; the source deposit that holds the site; the lost work plan, kept as the record of what was lost.

## 3. How this was made

1. **The first traversal**, 2026-10-24 01:25–01:40 UTC. Every domain in the manifest, every domain in the block, gnosis.spxi.dev and the aliases the deployment account attaches were fetched over HTTPS. For each: the status and final address of the home page, its title and description, every outbound link (sorted into fleet and other), whether a Machine Entry is present, and the status of `/llms.txt`, `/robots.txt`, `/sitemap.xml`, `/entity.json` and `/.well-known/axn-node.json`, with the sitemap's URL count. The home pages were read again for the repositories they name and the counts they state. The account's 35 deployment projects were listed, and the domain lists of fourteen were read. The 42 public repositories were listed with their descriptions and dates.
2. **Against the archive.** The manifest, the canonical index, the dataset builder's clone list and the `sites` config's per-repository counts were compared with what the traversal found. The declared target of every binding was opened at its record (R2, R3).
3. **The repairs**, 2026-10-24, each at its source (§11.1). The block was regenerated from `fleet.json` and applied to the twenty-five repositories that carry it, with the count fallbacks rewritten from `counts.json`.
4. **The second traversal**, 2026-10-24 15:37 UTC, with the same probe (`traversal-2026-10-24b/fleet/probe.py`), after the twenty-five fleet repositories had landed their repairs. A second pass read each block-carrying home page for its count fallbacks, its OAI figure and the presence of gnosis.spxi.dev (`block-check.json`, `counts-check.json`).
5. **The captures.** The Capture Registry was searched for every address whose query names a fleet domain or the fleet (§8).
6. **Scope.** The probe reads home pages and machine files. Every page of every repository is in the `sites` config; its rows were counted.

## 7. Theory

1. **The network as the work.** "The Network Is the Poem: Why Topology Matters More Than Text Quality" (#1191, dated by its text 17 November 2025, recovered from the blog 2026-07-19).

3. **Placement.** One home per document, an abstract and a link elsewhere; "duplicate full definitions across domains are a splice hazard arriving through geography rather than time" (#1472).

6. **From findability to traversability.** Three unprimed traversals of 2026-08-12 found that once the retrieval basin is acquired, the systems recover relations among the archive's objects, where before they found isolated names (#1460).
