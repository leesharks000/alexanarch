# PART III · PAPYROLOGY AND AI — THE REFRAMING PASS

**The Leiden Conventions (1931; Wilcken 1932).** Standard sigla for the condition of a
papyrological text: `[abc]` restored · `[...]` lacuna of known extent, one dot per
letter · `[— — —]` extent unknown · `ạḅ` damaged or unclear · `⟨ab⟩` scribal omission
supplied · `{ab}` present but judged erroneous. Harmonised for online publication as
**Leiden+**.

> **A ninety-five-year-old standardised notation for declaring exactly what could not
> be read and how confident the editor is. That is the Lacuna Protocol, arrived at
> independently and much earlier — and continuity with a pre-computational discipline
> is a stronger position than novelty.**

**"Mind the Gap"** (arXiv 2407.00250) — trains TrOCR to **emit** Leiden Conventions
with a loss that penalises failure to bracket a lacuna,
`L_weighted = L × (n_brackets + 1)`. *A model punished for not marking what it could
not read.* The most citable external work for the lacuna argument.

**Ithaca** (DeepMind, *Nature* 2022) — restoration plus geographical and chronological
attribution; I.PHI, 78,608 inscriptions. **Ithaca alone 62% · historians alone 25% ·
historians with Ithaca 72%.** Location 71%; dating within 30 years. Open-sourced;
authors note applicability to papyrology, numismatics, codicology.

**Vesuvius Challenge** — **PHerc. 1667 fully unwrapped and read, June 2026**; ~1.5m of
text across 20 columns; phase-contrast µCT on the ESRF BM18 beamline. *All open* —
tomographic data, surfaces and transcriptions under CC, code on GitHub. Papyrologists
(Nicolardi's team, Federico II) as readers; **machine recovers the surface, human
reads it.**

**Infrastructure** — papyri.info / Duke Databank · EpiDoc (XML/TEI) · SEG Online.

# PART IV · EXTERNAL — CRYPTOGRAPHIC PROVENANCE *(new in v3.0)*

**The competitive landscape v2.0 missed.**

**C2PA / Content Credentials** — the industry standard; Adobe, Meta, Leica, Sony,
Google, Samsung; 6,000+ member organisations. A **C2PA Manifest** carries provenance
plus a digitally signed, tamper-evident claim.

> **The stated limitations are the archive's opening.** Trust is extended to many
> entities by assumption and opt-in; **validation is not required by the
> specification**; signature coverage of the entire file is not required; and, in the
> literature's own phrase, *"trusted does not necessarily imply trustworthy."*
> C2PA is **platform-centric** — provenance asserted by the capture device and the
> editing software. AXN is **creator-centric** — identity computed from the bytes by
> anyone, verifiable without the platform. **This is the sharpest available
> differentiation and it is against the actual incumbent.**

**ARCHANGEL** (UK National Archives, 2019) — blockchain-recorded checksums for
decade-to-century integrity of scanned and born-digital records, peer-distributed
"without the need for a trusted third party."

**Provenance Marks** (Blockchain Commons, 2025) — a chain of small marks, each
verifying the previous and committing to the next, **represented as a sequence of
natural-language words.**

> **Structurally adjacent to the six-glyph seal**: a human-transcribable
> representation of a cryptographic chain. Independent arrival at the same design
> instinct — that a verification token must be sayable by a person. Strong citation.

**DFIP** (2026) — binding human biometric to AI computational fingerprint in one
attestation. Relevant to Assembly Chorus substrate disclosure.

**Fixity practice** — OAIS (ISO 14721) · FADGI 3rd ed. (2023) · PREMIS · Dublin Core ·
LOCKSS/CLOCKSS · BagIt/RO-Crate.

**Adjacent threats mapped by the external passes:** metadata stripping in
platform pipelines; born-digital accessibility crisis; format-based text
steganography and whitespace channels.

# PART VII · THE NICHE

> **Papyrology reads ancient hands and spent a century building conventions for
> declaring what it could not read. HTR converts contemporary hands into text. C2PA
> attests what a device captured. The archive proposes the fourth thing: contemporary
> hands entering machine canons as themselves — citable, verifiable, unconverted, and
> warranted by a living author who can sign, date, attest, and consent.**

**vs. HTR:** they convert; the hand is discarded. Here the hand is the payload.
**vs. Vesuvius/Ithaca:** those recover artifacts whose authors cannot consent or
attest. **Living authorship is the evidentiary asset they structurally cannot have.**
**vs. C2PA:** platform-centric attestation versus creator-centric computation;
validation optional there, arithmetic here.
**vs. DOI/ARK:** naming and resolution, no bytes, no object.
**vs. writer-ID forensics:** establishes who wrote it, not how to cite it.

# PART IX · THE ENLI CORPUS

**Offered repeatedly. Not yet accepted or specified.** Paper 198 (`AXN:05AD`) remains
the only artifact held.

**Why it is load-bearing:** the forensic defence against handwriting synthesis works
at **corpus scale and fails at page scale** — a single page proves nothing generation
cannot match, while a dated, chained, attested sequence in one hand changes the cost
asymmetry. Writer-ID methods are validated on multi-sample sets. It is a
**non-synthetic reserve** whose scarcity increases monotonically. And it is
**Japanese hand**, where benchmarks are thinnest and the register hazard of #1406 is
sharpest.

**To settle in correspondence before acceptance** — consent questions before research
questions: scope and selection · depositor, co-author, or subject · published versus
sealed · name in public plans · withdrawal terms · and **research dataset versus
archive holding**, which are different instruments and must not be conflated.

**Sequence:** terms → corpus instrument → FADGI capture → stamp and register → study.
