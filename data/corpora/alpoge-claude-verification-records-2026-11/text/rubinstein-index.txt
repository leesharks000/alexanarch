Alpöge/Claude proof of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis

On 20 November 2026 Levent Alpöge announced a proof,
found with Anthropic's Claude, of the Riemann Hypothesis and of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for
Dirichlet L -functions. The proof was released as a formal proof in the Lean proof assistant:
175,187 files, about 17 million lines. This page links to my own check of that Lean proof and to checks made
by others.
— Michael Rubinstein

The proof explained for the Riemann zeta function

The Alpöge–Claude proof of the Riemann hypothesis explained for the
Riemann zeta function (PDF, 164 pages; working draft, 21 November 2026)

A working guide to the proof, specialised to the Riemann zeta function, written with Claude, Codex and
Gemini as an aid to studying it. It aims at a human-readable account in the style of classical analytic number
theory. It deduces the Riemann hypothesis from one inequality between weighted sums over primes, called (M),
and two estimates for Dirichlet characters, and proves the two estimates from theorems in the literature.
The inequality (M) is not proved there: its proof is taken from the Lean development. The paper writes up
parts of that proof and states exactly which inputs it takes from the Lean development. It is a work in
progress.

Lean verification

mrubinst.github.io/grh-lean-verification

That page records a check of the Lean proof run with Claude Code on University of Waterloo compute
servers, starting from the archive Alpöge published. It shows the five theorems exactly as they are stated
in Lean, with an explanation of why they say what RH and GRH for Dirichlet L -functions say, and a scan
of all the source files for anything that could introduce an unproved assumption. It then reports each
machine check with its status and output: a full compile of all 175,187 modules, the list of axioms the
theorems depend on, a replay of every module through Lean's kernel, and a check by a second, independently
written kernel (nanoda).

Result (21 November 2026). All four checks finished with no errors. All 175,187 modules compile.
The five theorems depend only on Lean's three standard axioms. A replay of every module through Lean's kernel
accepted all 175,187. The independent kernel nanoda checked the 1,484,729 declarations that the theorems
depend on, with no typechecker errors.

The page also describes how the checks were run, and the repository contains the scripts, so that anyone
can repeat them. A dated log records each step, including the problems met along the way.
