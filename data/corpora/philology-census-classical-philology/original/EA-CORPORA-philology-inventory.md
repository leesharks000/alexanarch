## 2. What is missing

The archive holds philology's *applications*: the authenticity criticism of Plato and of the Gospels, and the editing of Sappho. It does not hold philology's own constitution:
- the documents in which it defined itself and its method;
- the quarrels in which it drew its boundary;
- its own journals as a run.

On this search, none of the following is in any seat. Each is public domain and in its original language:
- **The founding demonstrations of the standing question.**
  - Valla, *De falso credita et ementita Constantini donatione*: 1440, printed 1517.
  - Casaubon, *De rebus sacris et ecclesiasticis exercitationes XVI*, 1614: the dating of the Hermetica, which dissolved an ancient author.
  - Bentley, *A Dissertation upon the Epistles of Phalaris*, 1699: archive.org has the 1699 printing.
- **One name into many.** F. A. Wolf, *Prolegomena ad Homerum*, 1795: archive.org has the 1795 printing.
- **The discipline defined.**
  - Wolf, *Darstellung der Altertums-Wissenschaft*, 1807.
  - Boeckh, *Encyklopädie und Methodologie der philologischen Wissenschaften*, 1877: archive.org has 1877. It defines philology as "Erkenntniss des Erkannten".
  - Usener, *Philologie und Geschichtswissenschaft*, 1882.
- **The method.**
  - Lachmann's Lucretius commentary, 1850: archive.org has 1855 and 1866 printings.
  - Madvig, *Adversaria critica*, 1871–84.
  - Housman, the preface to *Manilius* I, 1903, and "The Application of Thought to Textual Criticism", 1921.
- **The boundary drawn: the quarrel over *Die Geburt der Tragödie*.**
  - Nietzsche, *Homer und die klassische Philologie*, 1869.
  - Wilamowitz, *Zukunftsphilologie!*, 1872 and 1873.
  - Rohde, *Afterphilologie*, 1872.

  The archive holds the *Geburt* only in its new edition of 1886 (27/03). The quarrel is the field refusing a book as not philology, the gate of #1773 one century earlier.
- **The doxography that made Theophrastus.** Diels, *Doxographi Graeci*, 1879. It is the construction behind every report of the Presocratics "from Theophrastus", and it sits at the centre of the Theophrastus line (#1580–#1605). archive.org has a copy with no date in its metadata, which needs checking.
- **The field's history of itself.**
  - Sandys, *A History of Classical Scholarship* I–III, 1903–08: archive.org has 1903.
  - Wilamowitz, *Geschichte der Philologie*, 1921: public domain in the United States, and still to be located.

## 3. What a seating should gather

These are proposals, in order of what the census needs first.

**3.1 *Classical Philology*, 1906–1928, whole.** The volumes are in the United States public domain. archive.org holds them article by article as JSTOR's Early Journal Content (1,082 items in that date range, identifiers `jstor-NNNNNN`).

Seated, they let the census read D on the page and not only in the title, as #1773 §4.12g did for the *Archiv*. That is the measure the title-only pass cannot make (notebook §4, limits).

**3.2 The constitution of philology.** These are the texts that state what the discipline is, what its method is, and where its boundary lies:
- Wolf 1795 and 1807;
- Boeckh 1877;
- Usener 1882;
- Lachmann 1850;
- Housman 1903 and 1921;
- the 1869–1873 quarrel (Nietzsche, Wilamowitz, Rohde).

This seat is the counterpart of 39/02–09 for philosophy: the construction of the object, here for the philological object.

**3.3 The standing question's canon.** Valla, Casaubon and Bentley: the three demonstrations by which philology showed that a received author or document was not what it was received as. They are the precedents for the field's own D at the work grain, against which the census's D can be read.

**3.4 Diels, *Doxographi Graeci* 1879.** This one serves the Theophrastus line as much as the census.

**3.5 The self-history.** Sandys I–III, and Wilamowitz's *Geschichte* once located.

**3.6 The other journals, for later stations.** *American Journal of Philology*, *Transactions of the American Philological Association*, *The Classical Review*, *Hermes*, *Rheinisches Museum* and *Mnemosyne*, through 1928.

**3.7 Declared absences, to add to 31/11.**
- Pfeiffer, *History of Classical Scholarship*, 1968 and 1976.
- Timpanaro, *La genesi del metodo del Lachmann*, 1963.
- Reynolds and Wilson, *Scribes and Scholars*.
- Grafton, *Defenders of the Text* and *Forgers and Critics*.
- Maas, *Textkritik*, 1927. It is public domain in the United States, but its author died in 1964; it is to be declared unless the author rules otherwise.

**The ruling owed.** Whether this is one seating (EA-CORPORA-40, *Philology Constituted*) with 3.1 as its first seat, or two: the CP run as the census's seat, and the constitution as a seating of its own.
