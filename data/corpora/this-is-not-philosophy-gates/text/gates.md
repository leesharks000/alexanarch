## A. The journal editor's responses to the author's submissions (*Ancient Philosophy*, Ronald Polansky, editor)

The author's ruling (15 November 2026): the correspondence is with the editor in his official capacity, on the substance of his field, and is institutional, not private. Its scope: the editor's responses to the submissions and near-submissions, and what he looks for in submissions, are quoted; his statements of his own views are not. Read from the author's mailbox in four threads and 41 messages, 23 July – 9 September 2026, with quoted replies stripped.

0A.2 **The specification, in the editor's words.** Polansky's demands, in order:
- **Vocabulary.** "I frequently request that authors rid papers of philosophical jargon" (23 July). Later: "Avoid off-putting technical vocabulary beyond that of the passages you are considering" (31 August), and "Do not have any words that are not easily recognized in the abstract" (2 September).
- **The object.** "My recommendation... is instead to do some close analysis of an ancient text rather than such broad stroke sort of writing" (28 August). "I would much rather see close analysis or one or two" (2 September).
- **The frame.** "It should begin with a discussion of the present state of thinking about the relationship of the two works, i.e., coverage of available secondary discussion" (9 September).
- **The size.** "Anything over 40 pages is problematic" (29 July). "33 or so pages single-spaced, i.e., crazy long" (2 September).
- **The claim.** "If the paper is not about Aristotle and Theophrastus being one person, but instead that the two works mentioned are closely connected, this could be O.K." (9 September). "Could we see a tamer version of this paper" (2 September).
- **The register.** "PLEASE STOP WRITING IN THIS ECCENTRIC WAY" (31 August). "WHY NOT, THEN, PLAY THE SORT OF GAME NEEDED TO BE ACCEPTED FOR PUBLICATION RATHER THAN GAMES THAT YOU INVENT?" (31 August). "Do the sorts of things that you may resist, but that I might welcome" (31 August).
- **The stake.** Of the closing section of *Many and One*, the count of makers, which returns a null in either direction: "I saw the stuff from the other paper about one body or two bodies. We really can do without any of this. Who cares besides you, and can you really offer them much reason to care? It seems quite questionable. So, could we see a tamer version of this paper" (2 September; in the same letter: "I have not read more than a few sentences of this paper"). Of *Socrates as Orthonym*: "We seem able to arrive at the same results without the hypothesis" (28 August), and "Why bother to claim one guy did it all?... So, what really is the point other than to entertain an unlikely story?" (28 August).

The decline of 28 August 2026, its grounds as the editor stated them: "One of them found a version of your paper already on Academia... So, there is an issue about whether there should be multiple publications of versions of a paper. Also, there is a question regarding whether papers composed by A.I. participation should be considered as regular submissions." And: "I think that we should decline it for reasons of length and lack of clarity."

## B. Journal and publisher policies on AI

- *Philosophy & Public Affairs* (Wiley), policy announced August 2026, as quoted by Daily Nous (24 August 2026, https://dailynous.com/2026/08/24/after-experiment-journal-decides-to-prohibit-ai-authored-content/): "Our policy is therefore to prohibit the submission of AI-authored content to Philosophy & Public Affairs." "We define AI-authored content as papers that are substantially written by AI." "We will use the leading AI detection software at the time to verify the truthfulness of the former declaration." "AI detection software, including updated models, may be used throughout the review process, up to and including after publication." "...the paper will be rejected or retracted, and the author will be banned from further submission".
- The occasion: Simon Goldstein, "Epistocracy and the Commitment Problem", *Philosophy & Public Affairs*, August 2026, "written mostly by Claude"; the editor-in-chief called its publication "an experiment rather than as an expression of a settled PPA policy" (Daily Nous, 13 August 2026, https://dailynous.com/2026/08/13/philosophy-journal-publishes-largely-ai-authored-article-on-purpose-guest-post/).
- *Ethics* (University of Chicago Press), AI policy as reported by Daily Nous (https://dailynous.com/?p=55833): "AI tools cannot author or even co-author a submission." "Ethics will not consider submissions that have been authored or co-authored by an AI." (The journal's own policy page, https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/journals/et/ai-policy, refused the session.)
- De Gruyter Brill, AI policy for authors (https://degruyterbrill.com/en/ai-for-authors): "such tools cannot take responsibility for the submitted work and therefore cannot be considered as authors." "We do not accept papers (journal articles, book chapters, etc.) that are generated by Artificial Intelligence (AI)." "The use of AI tools must be disclosed when their output has significantly contributed to any part of your manuscript." Covers *Apeiron*, *AGPh*, *Elenchos*, *Rhizomata*, *Phronesis* and *Research in Phenomenology*.
- Peer review: *Ergo*, "We require our referees not to use AI in any form for reviewing of articles"; *Ethics*, "The editors of Ethics have agreed not to upload submissions to such external servers nor to use AI tools to assess them"; the *Journal of the History of Philosophy* reported by readers to prohibit AI in submitted papers, wording not seen (Daily Nous, 28 September 2026, https://dailynous.com/2026/09/28/ai-in-peer-review-practices-policies/).
- The coordinated response: the Marc Sanders Foundation conference at NYU, 3–4 April 2027, for a "coordinated response to the presence and use of AI-tools", with the editors-in-chief of the *Journal of Philosophy*, *Philosophical Review*, *Mind*, *Noûs*, *Ergo*, *Philosophy Compass*, *Analytic Philosophy*, *Ethics* and *Philosophy and Public Affairs* (Daily Nous, 26 October 2026, https://dailynous.com/2026/10/26/coordinating-the-philosophy-professions-response-to-the-use-of-ai-in-research/).
