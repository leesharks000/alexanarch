"EMPTY" FROM "FREE" 

In an article entitled "Zur Etymologie einiger Worter fur 'leer'" (IF. 
XXXV, 265 ff.), the Dutch scholar Van Wijk has brought together a number 
of words in which the meaning "empty" is derived from that of "loose, 
free," e.g., Dutch een looze noot "an empty (hollow) nut." He compares 
the occasional German use of frei in the sense of unoccupied as in ein freies 
Zimmer. So in English we may say "I have this hour free." And the 
naive association between "loose" and "free" = " unoccupied" is illustrated 
in a remark recently heard from a colored laundress, that she had "only 
Wednesdays loose." Two striking examples, not mentioned by Van Wijk, 
are furnished by the regular words for "empty" in Modern Greek and in 
English.
