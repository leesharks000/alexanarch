FOR YOUR CLASSES. CLUBS. PROGRAMS. ENTERTAINMENTS

CARMINA LATINA 

Selected and Edited by ROY C. FUCKINGER 

Professor of Greek and Latiii 

Northwestern University 



Most energetic teachers of Latin nowadays recognize the advisability of 
relieving the steady pull of their teaching by some kind of diversion. Some devote 
a few minutes of each recitation to such an object, others set apart a certain portion 
of the week's work, but a majority maintain a Latin club which meets outside of 
school hours. In any case music is one of the teacher's most valuable aids, 
reviving the flagging interest of the pupils and fixing Latin words in their memories. 
The difficulty, however, has been that suitable texts have been accessible only to 
the instructors, who have been compelled to copy the words on the blackboard and 
to have them recopied by the students. What teacher has not wished that the 
songs might be available in some convenient, inexpensive form which could be 
placed in each pupil's hands without waste of time ? 

This need is now fully met by Professor Flickinger's Carmina Latina, a sixteen-page booklet 
wliich sells for ten cents a copy. This contains such indispensable fa%'orites as Gaudeamus Igilur, 
Integer Viiae, Lauriger Horalius, and A deste Pideles. It also presents versions of A merica and The 
Star-Spangkd Banner. There is a hitherto unpublished version of Lead, Kindly Light, as well 
as a hymn attributed to St. Augustine which can be sung to the tune of Glorious Things of Thee 
Are Spoken. Mica, Mica, Parva Stella is already a general favorite but is usually spoiled by an 
unworthy musical setting. The wistfulness and true nobility of the poem are much better brought 
out by the tune which the editor has here proposed. Up to this point we have texts without the 
notes, since the tunes are all familiar and can be found in all hymnals and college songbooks. But 
for the noble hymn of Northwestern University, Quaecumgue Sunt Vera, both words and music are 
given. Who ever heard of a Latin round ? Yet here are words and music for an easy round which 
has already been used with great success at the Chicago Classical Club and in several classrooms. 
Finally comes the Greek text of Tyrtaeus' Marching Song which Professor Flickinger has discovered 
can be sung to the same tune as Columbia, the Gem of the Ocean. 

The convenience and inexpensiveness of this collection, containing both old and new materials, 
with fresh suggestions on every page, insure a widespread demand in classical circles.

A New Edition 

The Greek Theater and Its Drama 

By ROY C. FLICKINGER 

Professor of Greek and Latin, Northwestern University 

THE noteworthy features of ancient drama and its production, which are usually regarded 
as unrelated, have been marshaled by the author under one co-ordinating principle. The 
material is freshened at every point by conclusions of the latest investigators. The range 
of topics discussed is unusually wide; scores of books, magazines, and monographs would 
be required to obtain the same information. Moreover, the results of the author's own researches 
appear on every page. The new illustrative material is profuse, and much of it appears now for 
the first time. The bibliographical references are sufficient to put the reader in immediate 
touch with the latest and most significant works in the field. 

A full General Index, completely revised, makes it easy to reassemble the material and examine it 
from a different point of view. To any serious student of the drama, whether ancient or modern, the 
work is indispensable. It is written in a style attractive to the general reader, and presupposes no knowl- 
edge of the Greek language. 

The complete Index of Passages which is appended to the volume will enable teachers to emphasize 
the salient points of antiquarian interest in any play which they may be reading with a class.
