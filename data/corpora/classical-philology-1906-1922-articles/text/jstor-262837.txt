Jerome's prologue to his translation of Origen's Homilies on Luke, page 245, 
Vail. (Migne, Pair. Lot. 26, 219; Pair. Gr. 13, 1799-1800) reads: 

Quamobrem petistis ut contemptis istiusmodi nugis saltern triginta et novem 
Adamantii nostri in Lucam homilias, sicut in Graeco habentur, interpreter; 
molestam rem et tonnento similem, alieno, ut ait Tullius, stomacho et non suo 
scribere; quam tamen idcirco nunc faciam, quia sublimiora non poscitis. 

I have been unable to place this quotation anywhere in Cicero's works by 
the use of the ordinary special lexica, indexes, etc., and Miiller does not 
appear to list it in his fragments of Cicero (Part IV, Vol. III). The expres- 
sion has a proverbial sound and Cicero has other passages in which stomachus 
occurs in more or less proverbial forms. This one, however, is not found 
in Otto's Sprichworter.
