In his discussion of these words in Classical Philology, XV, 387 ff ., 
Professor Boiling admits only two meanings, a "strong emotion of longing 
or yearning," and "the want, the need, or the lack" of a thing. But between 
these two extremes there are, as for other Homeric words, other shades of 
meaning less strong than the first and stronger than the second. Especially 
there is the feeling the want of a thing, with no implication of intense regret, 
but only of dissatisfaction at, or the inconvenience of, finding a thing is not

quite sure that many will say that this nameless expression is a perfectly
