The scurrile locution sua rS facere (Petronius 47. 4; 66. 2 1 ) — start- 
ing, perhaps, in the jest of some Kabelais, Aristophanes, Petronius of the 
slums (or of the beau monde) — applies the construction of [Terrae?] 
aliqua re [sacrum] facere to one of the euphemistic senses of facere (cf . 
Petron. 62. 4). This low phrase can have nothing in common with 
Plautine tua re feceris (pace Lindsay ad Cap. 296; Buecheler ap. Lowe 
ad Petron. 62, 47, 4), wherein tua re (= abs te) 2 is a simple separative, in 
an archaic locution developed before the syncretism of ablative, instru- 
mental, locative made the addition of differentiating prepositions neces- 
sary. The same idiom survives in med ri fert, and I am entirely in 
accord with Lindsay's rejection (Syntax of Plautus, p. 3) of the other
