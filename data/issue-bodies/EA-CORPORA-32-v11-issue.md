# EA-CORPORA-32 — The Thirty-Second Seating

The author asked for the seating on 2026-11-02, of the texts still owed in §5 of the design Legitimately Many (drafts/LEGITIMATELY-MANY-design-2026-11-01.md): "lets prepare that seating, its ea corpora deposit, and its tll spine and card". Each seat names the research it was made for, by the author's rule of the same day that a seating conducted in the course of active research names that research; the EA-CORPORA-31 seats were given the same field.

Twelve seats. Eleven came in two groups at v1.0, and the twelfth was folded in at v1.1. The first group carries the ancient and early printed witnesses the study reads at its seams. The writings under Archytas's name include a book on the ten categories, which Simplicius (seated at 31/04) reads as the source Aristotle followed; Mullach's 1860 volume holds them in Greek with his Latin. The Aldine Aristotle of 1497 prints Aristotle's Metaphysics and Theophrastus's one after the other, gathers both under one heading in the register, and prints after Theophrastus's the scholion recording that Andronicus and Hermippus did not know the book and that Nicolaus assigned it to Theophrastus; the leaves are held as images, the passages transcribed. Wimmer's Didot Theophrastus of 1866 names Brandis's edition of the two Metaphysics (1823) in its preface. Philodemus's Index Academicorum, in Mekler's edition of 1902, gives the Academy's own succession of heads and pupils. Dionysius's First Letter to Ammaeus dates a corpus by declared evidence, archon years quoted from Philochorus, against a Peripatetic who made Aristotle the source of Demosthenes. Cicero's letter to Atticus of 54 BCE takes the exoteric works, with their prefaces, as Aristotle's form.

The second group carries the modern readers who fixed the order of a corpus as the development of one mind. Schleiermacher's Einleitung of 1804 sets aside the life from Diogenes that the Greek editions printed first and proposes to restore the natural order of the dialogues. Campbell's Sophistes and Politicus of 1867 groups a late set of dialogues by diction. Tennemann's System of 1792 and the second volume of his Geschichte (1799) extract Plato's system from the ordered writings; Hegel's lectures on Plato, in Michelet's edition of 1833, cite Tennemann and fault the extraction, and the Differenzschrift of 1801 locates the need of philosophy in oppositions fixed apart. The modern editions and studies the debts name, still in their term or without an open copy, are declared at 32/11.

Version 1.1 (2026-11-02) folds in a twelfth seat at the author's word ("lets grab nicolaus"; "yes, lets grab them, but fold into 32 and version"). Draft §3 of the study found that the second name enters for Theophrastus's Metaphysics with Nicolaus of Damascus, as the scholion the Aldine prints reports him. His On the Philosophy of Aristotle survives in a Syriac compendium edited in 1965 and is declared at 32/11. Seated at 32/12: his fragments as Müller collected them, where On His Own Life calls him a zealot of Aristotle; Meyer's De plantis of 1841, which restores to Nicolaus the two books on plants transmitted as Aristotle's; and the Basel Theophrastus of 1541, which prints Aristotle's De plantis inside its Theophrastus volume and the Metaphysics among Theophrastus's works, the scholion still after it, then ΤΕΛΟΣ ΤΩΝ ΘΕΟΦΡΑΣΤΟΥ. The ten seats of v1.0 are unchanged and keep their AXNs; 32/11 and 32/12 take theirs from this version.
