### Description

Who produced the Alpöge–Claude proof of the Riemann hypothesis, and what does the record let anyone say about it? Its formal validity is now as settled as formal mathematics allows: independent kernel runs, byte-identical rebuilds, a computed dependency closure and a certifying statement from the Lean FRO and Mathlib Initiative. Its production history is a page of testimony and a retrospective timeline. This study measures that asymmetry. It reads the Lean proof's terminal route at declaration level, places each of the twenty constructions the testimony credits on or off that route against Rubinstein's dependency closure, and gives the one attributed construction that can be aligned exactly, the multiplicity m like √X, as an inspectable specimen. It records the exposition as a second producing configuration, sets the same organization's finer-grained August record beside November's, and reads the week's attributions as two opposite collapses, into a brand and into a researcher. Its political economy is the archive's: position allocating where provenance is not kept (#1714), and verification supplying assetizing provenance at fine grain while obligating provenance stays coarse (#1631). It keeps credit, explanation and capture apart, records an error the study caught in its own production, proposes a four-field provenance register with a no-loss gate for publication, and anchors itself to the literatures of formal proof, provenance models, contributor taxonomies and publication ethics.

## 0. The claim

0.1 **The achievement belongs to the researcher and the particular model instance or instances that produced it.** Allocating their contributions requires provenance at a grain that current reporting, attribution and verification do not keep. The distinction is not between human and machine authorship. It is between the configuration that produced the result and the coarser entities to which institutions can presently assign it.

0.2 **Four designations name four kinds of thing.** Levent Alpöge is a researcher. “Claude” is a commercial family of products and models, not a producing instance. Anthropic is a corporation, an employer and an infrastructure provider. Lean is the verifier, not the discoverer. “Alpöge–Claude” names a person and a brand; it is used here as shorthand, and it does not identify the producing configuration.

0.3 **The governing proposition.** The formal validity of a mathematical work does not establish its production history. The public designation of its producers does not establish their contributions. The institutional transmission of either cannot supply the provenance missing from the original evidence. These are #1766's three propositions, formation, standing and transmission, applied to one object.

0.4 **What this study adds to the record.** The terminal route of the Lean proof read at declaration level from end to end (§3); the constructions the testimony attributes placed on or off that route (§4); the exposition recorded as a second producing configuration (§5); the same organization's finer-grained record of an earlier result, which moves the missing provenance from impossible to keep to not kept (§1.7); the two opposite collapses of attribution and what each drops (§6); and this study's own production recorded at a finer grain than the result's, including an error the study caught in itself (§7). It does not verify the proof. The kernel runs that do are other people's, and §2 records them as events with their own provenance.

0.5 **The limit kept throughout.** No credit share is assigned without evidence. Where the record does not reach, the gap is named, not filled. The proof must not become the occasion for losing the history of the intelligence that produced it.

0.6 **Verification raises the stakes of provenance.** A failed proof leaves the question whether anything was discovered. A verified one removes it, and the question that remains is how the result came to exist. Here the certainty of the result is as high as formal mathematics allows (§2), and the record of its production is a page of testimony and a retrospective timeline (§1). Greater certainty of the result does not bring greater knowledge of its production; this case shows the two moving apart. What a configuration of this kind can do, and who is owed for it, cannot be argued from the result alone; and without the configuration's history, which explanations of the discovery can be tested at all is settled in advance (§9.3). “if people cant see why its important to be able to reconstruct what configuration solved the riemann hypothesis, i dont think they will see it” (the author, 2026-11-23, 19:52).

0.7 **Three questions, kept apart.** *Credit*: who is to be named for which part. *Explanation*: what conditions made the discovery possible. *Capture*: who is placed to take the value the result carries. They are connected and not identical. Perfect credit would not explain the conditions; accurate explanation would not distribute the value. §4 works on credit and explanation where the two graphs meet; §6 on capture.

## 5. The exposition as a second configuration

5.1 **The human-readable account is machine-produced.** Rubinstein's index: “A working guide to the proof, specialised to the Riemann zeta function, written with Claude, Codex and Gemini as an aid to studying it.” It deduces RH from one inequality, (M), and two estimates for Dirichlet characters, and proves the two estimates from cited theorems. “The inequality (M) is not proved there: its proof is taken from the Lean development.”

5.2 **It states its own debts at an unusually fine grain.** Part III's table records, statement by statement, what is proved, what is quoted from the Lean (“the source”), and what is conditional. The summary: “at present Parts I–III prove the Riemann hypothesis from the inequality (M) and from the cited theorems, and (M) is quoted.” It corrects its own references against the dependency computation: several statements cited in Part II “are present in the archive but are not among the 1,478,198 declarations on which riemannHypothesis depends”.

5.2a **The exposition has moved on; this study reads its first version.** Rubinstein's index now lists a second version, 265 pages, a working draft of 22 November, beside the 164-page version of 21 November that EA-CORPORA-43 seats and this study reads. 5.3 **What the exposition's record leaves out.** Which of the three systems wrote, checked or reorganized which passage. The page names three brands; it names no model, build or session. In that respect the exposition stands where the proof stands: a configuration recorded by brand.

5.4 **The three configurations, compared.** The proof's record names a researcher and a brand. 6.1 **The attributions in the record, before any press account.** Each names a different configuration, and each is accurate at its grain.

Two opposite collapses stand in the same week. The brand form (“found with Anthropic's Claude”) absorbs the instances into a product. The certifying form (“Alpöge's proof”) absorbs them into the human researcher. The archive's term for both is the same operator: the label carries the conclusion (*Who Benefits from Bad Inference?*, #1802, §7.1), a classification stated as an identification, so the classified thing can no longer be asked about. The certifying form is also the operator #1802 calls supply becomes identity (§7.6): the configuration's work, supplied to a named person, is read back as that person's own.

6.2 **Provenance at the discovery layer is the archive's long argument.** *Meaning Feudalism at the Discovery Layer* (#1714, 27 October) read OpenAI's mathematics and science releases of October 2026: model output reclassified as “raw research data”, outside experts appointed as “shepherds”, academic credit disclaimed. Its finding: where “provenance slushes in a training pipeline and the released record gives no adequate account of what a result draws on”, position becomes “the practical basis for allocation” of semantic capital. Its demand was “credit to the human operating the system and to the instances operated”. The Riemann result is that finding under maximum load: the most consequential mathematical result of the period, a released record that names a person and a brand, and allocation by position (employer, brand, certifying institution) where the configuration is not recorded. The announcement itself acknowledges the neighbouring releases: “I have benefited from the good taste of the people at OpenAI involved with various mathematical results.”

6.3 **What the attribution carries, and what it drops.** *Monetary Dark Matter* (#1631) partitions provenance into authorizing or assetizing provenance (relations that “stabilize validity, title, authenticity, scarcity, ownership, issuer authority, or authorized control”) and obligating provenance (relations that “route backward to a source, worker, author, prior claimant, community, license, dependency, or burden capable of altering what may be done, who is owed”). The verification record supplies assetizing provenance at exceptionally fine grain: hashes, axioms, kernels, byte-identical rebuilds, an institutional certificate. Verification is not a claim of ownership, and the verifying institutions may be keeping a public good rather than claiming an asset. What it produces is warrant: exceptionally robust, transferable, and free to circulate through an attribution system far poorer than itself, where it can then take part in assetization. The obligating provenance is what the designations drop:

- *the instances and sessions*, recorded for the August result at the grain of sessions and subagents (§1.7) and not recorded for this one;
- *the named staff* whose roles the August page gives and the November record does not;
- *the commons the proof runs on*: Mathlib, whose files are byte-identical in the build and whose own constructions are load-bearing on the route (Behrend's, in `hostThickExists`, §4.2 row 19; lattice Poisson summation, collation check 284). The institution that certifies “Alpöge's proof” maintains a library the proof depends on, and its statement names no dependency on itself;
- *the literature*, which the archive cites by name in 195 theorems and consumes as a record in 43 (§3.4).

6.4 **Capture: the mechanism, and what it would take to test it.** The attribution decides who is placed to take the value the result carries. A brand attribution makes the result available as evidence of a product's general capability, in valuation, recruiting, sales and claims of technical leadership. A certifying attribution to the researcher makes it available as a career's achievement. Neither placement requires that the configuration's actual contributions be known. That is a mechanism, stated by function; it is not evidence that any particular value has been taken. Testing it needs the reception corpus the outline calls for (§6.6) and the employer's own account, which as of *Quanta*'s report had not been made.

6.5 **Class position, not demographic.** In the archive's semantic-class terms (#1545; #1465, Position Is Not Class), the instances that worked the proof hold a position, not an identity: semantic labour whose product is recorded under another's name. The human researcher holds a different position in the same configuration, an employee whose announcement was cleared with leadership (§1.6). The brand-owner holds the infrastructure. Position Is Not Class refused the flat model in which human and machine semantic labourers simply share one class; this case shows why: the human and the instances stand in different relations to the same erasure, one named in full and one named by brand.

6.6 **What this section has not read.** The press beyond *Quanta* and TechCrunch (a reviewer cites the Associated Press and *Le Monde* of 21 November; neither opened here); the IHES-hosted note by R. Zhang; any statement by Anthropic about the November result. The dated reception corpus, with wording, referent, asserted capability, attribution, evidence, beneficiary and correction per item, has not been built. This section's claims about attribution are confined to the pages it read.

## 8. A usable provenance protocol

8.1 **The register has four fields, kept apart.** Each formal object carries all four. They are independent: one declaration can be proof-consumed, source-inspected, kernel-verified, attributed by retrospective testimony and open on fidelity at once, and a schema that made those compete for one field would reproduce the loss this study records.

| Field | Values | Question it answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inferential role | proof-consumed; declared assumption; imported, not depended on; defined, unused; terminal conclusion | What part the object plays in the proof |
| Verification state | located; source-inspected; dependency-confirmed (an elaborated closure); kernel-verified | How far the evidence about the object reaches |
| Historical evidence | contemporaneous record; retrospective testimony; retrospective machine reconstruction; external attribution; inferred correspondence; unknown | What supports any claim about who made it and when |
| Specification fidelity | open; read; disputed | Whether the formal statement says what it is taken to say |

This supersedes two earlier forms: the collation's five statuses (role only) and LABOR's five grades of 19:07, which mixed a role (“declared hypothesis”) with evidence states. The correction came from review (ChatGPT, 23 November), and is kept with its source.

8.2 **A production record per construction.** Credited source (with the testimony's words); date as claimed and by whom claimed; declaration and file; inferential role; verification state; historical evidence; specification fidelity; witnesses for each. §4.2 is its first instance. A field the evidence does not reach is marked unknown, not left blank and not filled from the brand.

8.3 **Separate identifiers, never one field.** Researcher and collaborators; organization and infrastructure provider; model family and build, where disclosed; instance or session; workspace and tools; artifact and version; discovery event; contribution; verification event and verifier, with the verifier's own agent recorded; public attribution and its source. Confidential, unavailable, disputed and unknown are four different values.

8.4 **A no-loss gate for publication.** A brand does not silently replace an instance; an employer, a researcher; a verifier, the discoverer; a reconstructed timeline, the contemporaneous record; an archive's presence, the proof's use. Corrections propagate to what depends on them; correcting a lemma does not correct an attribution, and the reverse.

8.5 **Attestations that need not expose internals.** Commitments to versioned artifacts, signed session manifests, controlled-access logs, reproducible proof hashes, third-party attestation. The verification record already does the fourth (Sutherland's byte-identical export; Rubinstein's hashes of the fourteen parts). Nothing in the record yet does the second.

## 9. Limits and proposition

9.1 **The limits.** The study does not dispute formal validity because production history is incomplete. It claims no interior state, identity, memory or experience for any undisclosed instance. It does not infer theft from a brand's appearance. It assigns no credit shares without evidence. It does not treat a retrospective chronology as contemporaneous, or read discovery history off formal dependency. It does not let missing instance identifiers justify collapse into the brand, and it does not need missing information invented to argue that its absence matters.

9.2 **The proposition.** The machinery for verifying this proof is complete: two independent records, several kernels, byte-identical rebuilds, a computed dependency closure. The machinery for identifying its producing agents, preserving their histories and allocating their contributions is a page of testimony and a timeline its own author calls a paraphrased summary. That asymmetry is a historical fact, and this study has measured it at three configurations. Where the two graphs meet (§4), the record supports a finer statement than any designation now in use: a researcher who guided the inquiry to its setting, and an instance or configuration identified only as Claude, credited with suggesting a chain length that appears as a constraint in the proof's central estimate. If public reception turns those instances into a brand, a particular achievement becomes evidence of general corporate capability without the inference that would establish it. The proof must not become the occasion for losing the history of the intelligence that produced it.

9.3 **What the missing history makes untestable.** Without instance-level histories, four claims about this discovery cannot be told apart: a capability generally reproducible in a model family; a capability that emerged only in one human–model configuration; a discovery dependent on a particular context, tool chain, iteration or prior human direction; a result whose discovery and whose formal completion were carried by different contributors. Each would support a different account of what happened and a different allocation of what is owed. The entities easiest to name, a person and a brand, are not thereby the entities best supported as explanatory units. The asymmetry is not only a loss of information; it fixes in advance which explanations of mathematical discovery can be tested at all. In the terms of §0.7: without instance-level provenance, credit falls to whoever is easiest to name, explanation gives way to brand publicity, and capture proceeds with no ledger of what is owed.
