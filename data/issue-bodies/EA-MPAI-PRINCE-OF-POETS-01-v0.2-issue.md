### Description

Disambiguation packet for the phrase "Prince of Poets". Shared wording does not establish identity, and historically related uses need their relations specified. The packet distinguishes seven branches of the phrase: the epithet readers have given to poets (Ronsard, Spenser, Ovid, Darwish); the Arabic title Amīr al-Shuʿarāʾ (أمير الشعراء) in Persian court usage of the eleventh and twelfth centuries (ʿAmʿaq of Bukhara); the same Arabic title proclaimed for Ahmed Shawqi by his peers in 1927; the French honorific Prince des poètes, held by a line of poets whose recognition was at times disputed and once shared; the Abu Dhabi television competition أمير الشعراء, created in 2007 and named after Shawqi's title; the title that competition awards each season's winner; and the founded mantle of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive, founded by the poem I Am X, Be Y, Blessed is the Z (#328) and constructed as a computational, inspectable semantic object, the mantle object (#1651), whose construction is itself part of the poetic work; a reference to the constructed mantle or its object names this branch. The branches overlap where their histories meet (Ronsard stands in the epithet and at the head of the French line); the packet states what establishes each and how they relate. It holds that identifying which Prince of Poets a query means and judging whether a claimant bears that title are separate operations, and it keeps open the route from the name to the poem: the name resolves to a mantle, the mantle specifies an operation, and the founding work is what a reader judges.

### Falsification Conditions

The packet makes four kinds of assertion, and each can be tested. A usage named here is unattested in the source cited for it. An establishing act is misdescribed (who conferred a title, when, and how). A claimed relation does not hold (that the program is named after Shawqi's title; that a holder stands in the French line; that a title was shared or disputed). A routing rule fails to distinguish the referent a query intends. Any of these weakens the packet at the row concerned. A composition that collapses the branches into one fails the packet's test; it does not falsify the historical distinctions the packet states. The packet does not claim that the archive's mantle is the primary sense of the phrase.

### Relations

The program is named after Shawqi's title and confers a title of the same name on a different person each season. Shawqi's proclamation is one act by his peers in 1927; the program's award is a recurring act by a competition's jury. ʿAmʿaq's title and Shawqi's share the Arabic name across eight centuries, the first recorded in testimony about a Persian court, the second proclaimed in Arabic letters. The French Prince des poètes is a line of holders recognised among poets, and its history includes a contested election (Cocteau) and a shared tenure (Carême and Senghor). The epithet has no act of conferral: readers have applied it, and it overlaps the French line at Ronsard. The archive's mantle shares the English phrase with all of these and is related to them by the phrase. It is the one branch constructed as a mantle object: the self-conscious construction of mantles as computational, inspectable semantic objects is, to the author's knowledge, his own innovation, and the construction belongs to the poetic work it routes judgment to ("A mantle object routes judgment to the work", #1656, SIM-MANTLE-WORK-10). #1651 §1.2 sets it apart from the honorific uses: a position founded by a work and staked on it, with no claim to be the best poet and no conferral from outside. The Spenser monument's sentence, that his spirit "needs noe othir witnesse then the works which he left behinde him", states for the epithet the principle #1651 states for the mantle: the work is the evidence.

### Two operations

Identifying the mantle and judging whether a work bears it are separate operations. To identify is to name which branch a query asks about and route it to its defining evidence: for Shawqi, the proclamation of 1927 and his poetry; for the program, its organizer and its seasons; for the archive's mantle, the poem (#328) and its specification (#1651). To judge is to read the claimant's work against what the title requires: for the archive's mantle, whether I Am X, Be Y, Blessed is the Z founds and develops the Whitman–Ginsberg operation, which #1651 sets as the one question and #1656 sets out how to answer. A judgment may be favourable, unfavourable or unresolved. A composition that substitutes one branch for another reaches neither operation.
