# EA-CORPORA-36 — The Thirty-Sixth Seating

The author asked for the seating on 2026-11-07: "i think we need a sappho reception history ea-corpora, whatever's public. translators, scholars. theres some ground work in the archive, notes towards a future reader i believe. the seating can include everything thats publicly available as well as declared absences and pointers for material behind pay- and access- and rights-walls"; and then: "sappho reception history + future reader frameworks and related".

The research occasion is twofold. The revision of Closing the Fragment (v7, a draft of 2026-11-07 for Alexanarch) controls a public composition's closure of fragment 31 against the translators who closed it before: Hall's Longinus ends its four stanzas on "Yet ſince I'me wretched I muſt dare." (1652); Boileau ends his three quatrains on the fifth stanza's opening, that all must be hazarded (in the 1683 printing of the Traité, first published 1674); Philips's version in the Spectator ends on "I fainted, sunk, and dy'd away." (1711); Merivale ends at the failing of "Both sight and sound" (1813); Vivien's prose carries "Mais il faut oser tout..." (1903); Wharton's literal version carries "But I must dare all, since one so poor ..." (1908), the line the January composition's closing aligns with. The Sappho Future Reader framework, whose groundwork is Research Notes Towards the Future Reader (#1548), maps the future reader as a position with a history in twelve strata, from the epigraphic address through Thucydides' possession for all time, Ovid's legar, the scriptural writing for a generation to come, Dante's lettor and Shakespeare's eyes not yet created, the Romantic culture of posterity, Whitman's and Baratynsky's readers in posterity, to the WIPP marker studies' message for readers ten thousand years out.

The seating holds what is public. Part A (seats 36/01–36/18) holds the reception of Sappho from 1652 to 1925: the Longinian translators (Hall, Boileau), the eighteenth century's English Sappho (the Spectator, Pope's Ovid, Fawkes), the nineteenth century's question of her character (Welcker, Mure) and its poets and critics (Merivale, Swinburne, Higginson, Symonds), and the editions and renderings of the twentieth century's first quarter (Vivien, Carman, Wharton, O'Hara and Stacpoole, Wilamowitz, Robinson, Cox). Part B (36/19–36/27) holds the future-reader strata the library did not hold: Ennius in Cicero, Dante, Chaucer, Shakespeare's sonnets, Milton, the Romantic sentences (Wordsworth, Keats, Shelley, Stendhal), Dickinson as Todd and Higginson printed her, Baratynsky with Mandelstam's bottle quoted beside him, and the Sandia report of 1993. Part C enters by reference the strata the library already held (Homer, Herodotus, Thucydides, the Phaedrus, Horace, Ovid, the Hebrew Bible, Nietzsche, Whitman) and the witnesses of fragment 31 (Longinus, Catullus, the diplomatic layer). Part D declares the rest: 70 works of the reception and of the frameworks, each with the wall that keeps it out.

27 seats, 31 works, 472,458 lines, 116 files, 59 loci verified in the text layers; 22 cross-references verified in seated texts; 70 declared absences.

## How the seats were made

Every seat was fetched in session and hashed into its MANIFEST.sha256, with a source.json recording edition, origin, licence, normalization, the loci verified in the text layer, and the research the seat was made for. The loci keep their sources' readings: long s in the TCP transcription of Hall, and the OCR's readings in the Archive's text layers (Boileau's long s read as f, Welcker's Fraktur, Higginson's Aegean read as -^gean). Welcker's seat is the Bayerische Staatsbibliothek copy; the Google scan of the same book was fetched first and set aside because its OCR reads the Fraktur as noise. The builders are under rebuild/corpora/ea-corpora-36/: gen_spec36.py writes the specs; build36.py builds each seat (the thirty-fourth seating's builder with one kind added, the Text Creation Partnership's TEI); add_loci36.py enters the loci, each asserted present in its text layer; xref36.py verifies the cross-references in the seats that hold them; absences36.py writes the declared absences; index_seats36.py enters the seats in the index; seat_axns36.py gives them their AXNs once deposited; and body36.py writes this body.
