## FRAGMENT B — v1.7 Movement III rewrite (Bauhaus lyric pedagogy), recovered in two overlapping windows

### B.1 — image caption (v1.9 topographic format) and opening of the rewritten reading:

[IMAGE: `01-profile-grid-opening.jpg` (rightmost pinned tile detail) — The *i love u all* pinned tile: Paul Klee's *[Old Love Song](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Klee#Werkverzeichnis)* (*Altes Liebeslied*, 1924), a Bauhaus-period gouache-and-watercolor showing faceted lyrical figures with a heart on the right-hand figure. The caption *"i love u all"* appears in a speech-bubble at the top of the tile.]

The Bauhaus-era placement of *Altes Liebeslied* is stronger for the tile's compositional argument than the terminal-mode placement earlier passes constructed. Bauhaus Klee is Klee at the moment of *formalizing lyric pedagogy* — treating the love song as visual instruction, affect as diagrammable form, intimacy as compositional grammar. The title itself gives the reading: *an old love song*, rendered not as sung lyric but as visual composition. The painting stages what a love song *would look like* if it were held still and rendered in gouache: faceted figures leaning toward each other, a heart made structurally visible on one of them, the whole scene organized as compositional grammar rather than as narrative.

Under the caption *"i love u all,"* the Klee cover turns the audio tile into a lesson in *love as form.* Not sentiment added to structure. Structure carrying sentiment. This is the exact register Klee's *Pedagogical Sketchbook* would formalize in the year following: line as thought-motion, form as intention, color as affect, all made teachable as one interoperable notational apparatus. The third pin does not cite Klee's failing body. The third pin cites Klee's love-song-as-diagram, at the moment Klee was theorizing the diagram as the primary unit of visual instruction.

Memory-attested lyrics:

> *I love you all, I love you all*
> *enough to fall*
> *all the way down*
> *I love you all*

*(two lines only, repeated and varied)*

Read against the Bauhaus lyric-pedagogy placement, the song reads with different precision than earlier passes gave it. *I love you all* is universal-address, exactly as Klee's *Love* in *Altes Liebeslied* is love-without-specification. *Enough to fall / all the way down* is the specific commitment: the love is *sufficient* to accept the fall, quantified as commitment rather than promised as protection. The song is not sung *from within* a fall that is already happening (as the v1.6 terminal-mode reading claimed); the song *diagrams* the commitment to a fall that may or may not have begun. Klee's *Altes Liebeslied* is the exact structural companion: love as compositional grammar sufficient to accept its own falling motion, diagrammed rather than described.

The tile therefore reads: *the song of universal love sufficient to accept the fall, delivered in the register of Bauhaus lyric pedagogy — love as form that can be taught, diagrammed, and sung, whose compositional grammar makes the commitment legible without requiring narrative or sentiment.* This is a more precise reading than v1.6 gave, and it is a stronger placement for the pinned triptych's methodological signature: the third pin is *the direct thesis-statement of the primer as pedagogy*. Whatever else the primer teaches, it teaches love as form; whatever else its methodological signature is, its methodological signature is Klee's own — that visual instruction can carry affect without diluting it, and affect can be delivered as instruction without domesticating it. The primer is *taking Klee's pedagogical bet as its own ars poetica.*

The Benjaminian *Angelus Novus* frame — which v1.6 imported through the Klee-general-tradition connection — is not available at this dating. *Altes Liebeslied* (1924) predates Benjamin's ninth thesis on the philosophy of history (1940) by sixteen years. The Bauhaus lyric-pedagogy reading is Klee's own contemporary register at the moment of the painting's making. The Benjamin frame was a productive interpretive move in v1.5 and v1.6 but is not the right frame for this particular Klee. What replaces it is stronger: the Klee reading returns Movement III to the direct pedagogical register that connects it to the Klee tradition v1.4's second-pin Klee-lineage-schema tiles already invoked.
