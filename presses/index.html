Presses

Two imprints, divided by form and not by register. Both are specified at #189, The Press Layer of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive.

Pergamon Press

Journals and scholarly working papers

Prints the journal issues — seven chartered journals across 1,098 deposits.

New Human Press

Creative releases and book-length theoretical editions

Prints the books — 47 deposits presently carry the imprint.

The division is not academic-versus-creative. Operative Semiotics: A Grundrisse is Johannes Sigil's founding volume in a numbered pocket series, and a pocket series is a New Human Press object however academic its content. Where a title page prints Pergamon on a book, the imprint is superseded and the title page retained as printed.

Pocket Humans

The heteronyms' own shelf: one founding volume each. Eighteen volumes are recorded — 12 deposited, 0 scattered, 2 to be collected, 4 forthcoming. Only one deposit carries an explicit series number: #49, Logotic Hacking — Pocket Humans 03.

A dot marks a volume whose critical apparatus exists. The dots are sparse because most founding volumes have none — including Combat Scholasticism, gathered as an edition and never given one.

Published books

ISBN and ASIN are kept distinct. An ISBN means the work exists as a book independent of any retailer; an ASIN means it exists as a listing. For an archive whose subject is provenance erasure that is the difference between a book and a page on a store — and three of these exist only as ASINs and are not deposited at all.

Two of the eight are listed under the wrong heteronym, and both wrong names are Jack Feist. Tiger Leap is properly Sigil's; What Was Lost is properly Spellings'. In each case the true author sits one link away on the emergence chain Spellings → Feist → Sigil. The commercial record froze each work at the moment its author had not yet separated from Feist — not a cataloguing error to correct quietly, but evidence of when each heteronym emerged, held by a retailer that had no idea it was recording it.
